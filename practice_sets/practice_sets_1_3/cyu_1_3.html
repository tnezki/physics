1. Use the vector diagram. What two pieces of information does each force arrow communicate?

  1. The arrow’s direction shows force direction, and its length represents the force magnitude/relative strength.
  2. Arrow direction is speed; arrow length is time.
  3. Arrow color is force size; arrow direction is mass.
  4. Arrow length is distance traveled; direction is acceleration only.

2. A physical quantity is described by both a magnitude and a direction, and it can be represented by an arrow. What type of quantity is it?

3. Two students push the same cart in the same direction with 14 N and 11 N. Determine the net force.

\(F_{\text{net}} = F_1 + F_2\)

  1. 25 N in the direction of the pushes
  2. 0 N
  3. the sum of all magnitudes with no direction
  4. the same magnitude but opposite direction

4. A cart has a 20 N force to the right and an unknown force to the left. The net force is 8 N to the right. Determine the unknown leftward force.

\(F_{\text{net}} = F_{\text{right}} - F_{\text{left}}\)

5. Use the vector diagram to identify the diagonal resultant and explain what its direction represents.

  1. The diagonal arrow is the resultant; it points in the combined direction and its length represents the magnitude of the combined force.
  2. The resultant is always the longest individual arrow regardless of direction.
  3. Opposite force arrows must always be added as positive magnitudes.
  4. A resultant has magnitude but no direction.
