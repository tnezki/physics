Section 2.2 — Check Your Understanding

1. A cart travels 35 m in 5 s. Calculate its average speed and include units.

2. A cart travels 28 m in 7 s. Calculate its average speed and include units.

  1. 4 m/s
  2. 35 m/s
  3. 28 m/s
  4. 7 m/s

3. During a 12-minute bicycle trip, the rider’s average speed is 8 m/s. At one instant, the speedometer reads 11 m/s. Distinguish the two measurements.

4. During a 12-minute bicycle trip, the rider’s average speed is 6 m/s. At one instant, the speedometer reads 9 m/s. Distinguish the two measurements.

  1. 6 m/s describes the whole trip average; 9 m/s is the instantaneous speed at that moment.
  2. Both numbers are instantaneous speeds.
  3. Both numbers are average speeds.
  4. The larger value must be velocity because it is larger.

5. A robot moves at a steady 7 m/s east. Explain what makes this a velocity description rather than only a speed description, and state what would make the velocity change even if the speed stayed 7 m/s.

6. A robot moves at a steady 9 m/s west. Explain what makes this a velocity description rather than only a speed description, and state what would make the velocity change even if the speed stayed 9 m/s.

  1. It gives both speed (9 m/s) and direction (west). The velocity changes if the direction changes, even if the speed remains 9 m/s.
  2. Velocity is only the number on a speedometer.
  3. Velocity changes only when speed changes.
  4. Direction is irrelevant to velocity.
