Section 2.2 — Practice Set 1

1. A cart travels 15 m in 5 s. Calculate its average speed and include units.

2. A robot moves at a steady 9 m/s south. Explain what makes this a velocity description rather than only a speed description, and state what would make the velocity change even if the speed stayed 9 m/s.

  1. It gives both speed (9 m/s) and direction (south). The velocity changes if the direction changes, even if the speed remains 9 m/s.
  2. Velocity is only the number on a speedometer.
  3. Velocity changes only when speed changes.
  4. Direction is irrelevant to velocity.

3. A cart travels 18 m in 6 s. Calculate its average speed and include units.

  1. 3 m/s
  2. 24 m/s
  3. 18 m/s
  4. 6 m/s

4. During a 12-minute bicycle trip, the rider’s average speed is 7 m/s. At one instant, the speedometer reads 10 m/s. Distinguish the two measurements.

  1. 7 m/s describes the whole trip average; 10 m/s is the instantaneous speed at that moment.
  2. Both numbers are instantaneous speeds.
  3. Both numbers are average speeds.
  4. The larger value must be velocity because it is larger.
