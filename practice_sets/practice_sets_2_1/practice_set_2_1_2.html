8. A cup sits on a tray inside a bus moving east. The cup does not slide on the tray. Explain how the cup can be at rest in the bus frame but moving in the Earth frame.

9. Tess walks toward the front of a train moving east at 8 m/s. Tess walks at 3 m/s relative to the train. Explain how Tess can be moving relative to the ground while also being described using a different motion relative to the train.

  1. Tess must have the same velocity in every reference frame.
  2. Tess is at rest relative to the ground because the train is the only valid frame.
  3. Reference frames change the object’s mass, which changes its motion.
  4. Relative to the train, Tess moves east at 3 m/s; relative to the ground, Tess moves east at 11 m/s. Motion depends on the chosen reference frame.

10. A passenger on a bus says a phone on the seat is at rest. A person on the sidewalk says the phone is moving east with the bus. Compare the two descriptions and explain why using Earth’s surface is convenient for ordinary travel descriptions.

11. A passenger on a bus says a phone on the seat is at rest. A person on the sidewalk says the phone is moving east with the bus. Compare the two descriptions and explain why using Earth’s surface is convenient for ordinary travel descriptions.

  1. Only the passenger can be correct because the phone is touching the seat.
  2. Only the sidewalk observer can be correct because Earth is the unique true frame.
  3. Both must measure zero velocity because the phone is not sliding.
  4. Both descriptions can be correct because they use different reference frames. The phone is at rest in the bus frame and moving in the Earth/sidewalk frame. Earth’s surface is a convenient shared everyday frame.
