2. Ari walks toward the front of a train moving east at 10 m/s. Ari walks east at 1 m/s relative to the train. Explain how Ari can have one velocity relative to the train and a different velocity relative to the ground.

4. Mina walks toward the front of a train moving east at 8 m/s. Mina walks east at 1 m/s relative to the train. Explain how Mina can have one velocity relative to the train and a different velocity relative to the ground.

5. Mina walks toward the front of a train moving east at 12 m/s. Mina walks east at 1 m/s relative to the train. Explain how Mina can have one velocity relative to the train and a different velocity relative to the ground.

  1. Mina must have the same velocity in every frame.
  2. The train is the only valid frame.
  3. Changing reference frame changes the object’s mass.
  4. Relative to the train, Mina moves east at 1 m/s; relative to the ground, Mina moves east at 13 m/s. Motion depends on the chosen reference frame.

8. A rider on a ferry says a bag on the floor is at rest. An observer standing on the ground says the bag is moving with the ferry. Compare the two descriptions and explain why Earth’s surface is a convenient frame for everyday travel.

  1. Only the rider can be correct.
  2. Only the ground observer can be correct.
  3. Both must measure zero speed.
  4. Both descriptions can be correct because they use different reference frames. Earth’s surface is a stable, shared everyday reference frame for locations and travel.
