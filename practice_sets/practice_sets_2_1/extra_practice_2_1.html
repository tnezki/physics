1. Leo walks toward the front of a train moving east at 10 m/s. Leo walks east at 1 m/s relative to the train. Explain how Leo can have one velocity relative to the train and a different velocity relative to the ground.

2. A rider on a train says a bag on the floor is at rest. An observer standing on the ground says the bag is moving with the train. Compare the two descriptions and explain why Earth’s surface is a convenient frame for everyday travel.

3. Zoe walks toward the front of a train moving east at 10 m/s. Zoe walks east at 1 m/s relative to the train. Explain how Zoe can have one velocity relative to the train and a different velocity relative to the ground.

4. Kai walks toward the front of a train moving east at 12 m/s. Kai walks east at 1 m/s relative to the train. Explain how Kai can have one velocity relative to the train and a different velocity relative to the ground.

  1. Kai must have the same velocity in every frame.
  2. The train is the only valid frame.
  3. Changing reference frame changes the object’s mass.
  4. Relative to the train, Kai moves east at 1 m/s; relative to the ground, Kai moves east at 13 m/s. Motion depends on the chosen reference frame.
