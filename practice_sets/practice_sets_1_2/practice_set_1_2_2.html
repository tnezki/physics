Conceptual Physics Practice 2 1.2

1. A loaded grocery cart is harder to start or stop than an empty cart. Explain this using inertia.

2. A parked car rests on level ground. Explain why “no forces act” is incorrect.

3. A lamp hangs motionless from a cord. Identify the main forces on the lamp and state the net-force condition.

4. A parachutist descends at terminal speed. Relate drag, weight, and net force.

5. A crate has equal opposite horizontal pushes and balanced vertical forces. Determine the net force.

6. A cart rolls at 0.8 m/s while the forces on it balance. Which principle best explains the prediction?

  1. Newton’s First Law
  2. an inertia force
  3. Newton’s Third Law
  4. a continuing forward net force

7. A property of matter resists changes in velocity. Name the property.

  1. inertia
  2. net force
  3. support force
  4. friction

8. A hockey puck moves north while the net force is zero. Which principle best explains the prediction?

  1. Newton’s First Law
  2. an inertia force
  3. Newton’s Third Law
  4. a continuing forward net force

9. A spacecraft drifts in deep space with negligible net force. Predict what happens next and justify with Newton’s First Law.

10. Use the book-on-table diagram. Which statement best describes the vertical forces?

  1. Weight acts downward and support force acts upward; they can balance.
  2. Only weight acts because the book is not moving.
  3. Only support force acts.
  4. Inertia acts upward to cancel gravity.

11. An air-track glider moves left while the net force is zero. Predict what happens next and justify with Newton’s First Law.

12. A force diagram for a sliding cart includes a forward arrow labeled “inertia force.” Critique the diagram.

13. An elevator moves upward at constant speed. Are its vertical forces balanced or unbalanced? Explain.

  1. Balanced; constant velocity means zero net force.
  2. A forward net force is required for motion.
  3. No forces act because speed is constant.
  4. Inertia is an additional balancing force.

14. A toy car moves on a low-friction track with zero net force. Explain the motion using Newton’s First Law.

15. Use the book-on-table diagram to name the interaction partner associated with each vertical force.

16. When a bus stops suddenly, a passenger tends to continue forward. A student calls this a forward “inertia force.” Critique the explanation.

17. Compare a 12 kg cart and a 3 kg cart. Which has more inertia, and why?

18. A foam block floats motionless in water. Identify the main vertical forces and the net-force state.

19. Use the book-on-table diagram to distinguish the individual forces from the net force.

20. A sled moves across level snow at constant speed. What must be true about the net force?

  1. The net force is zero; horizontal and vertical components balance.
  2. A forward net force is required for motion.
  3. No forces act because speed is constant.
  4. Inertia is an additional balancing force.

21. A boat floats at rest. Explain why the correct description is “zero net force” rather than “no forces.”

22. Compare loaded and unloaded carts and explain how inertia changes what you expect when the same interaction tries to change their motion.

23. A sign hangs motionless from a cable. Name the main forces on the sign.

24. A puck slides east on nearly frictionless ice with zero net force. Which principle best explains the prediction?

  1. Newton’s First Law
  2. an inertia force
  3. Newton’s Third Law
  4. a continuing forward net force
