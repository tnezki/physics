Conceptual Physics Practice 1.2

1. Use the book-on-table diagram to distinguish the individual forces from the net force.

2. A force diagram for a sliding cart includes a forward arrow labeled “inertia force.” Critique the diagram.

3. A backpack rests on the floor. Which two vertical forces are most important, and how are they related?

  1. Weight downward and support force upward are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
  2. A forward net force is required for motion.
  3. No forces act because speed is constant.
  4. Inertia is an additional balancing force.

4. A sled moves across level snow at constant speed. What must be true about the net force?

  1. The net force is zero; horizontal and vertical components balance.
  2. A forward net force is required for motion.
  3. No forces act because speed is constant.
  4. Inertia is an additional balancing force.

5. Two carts receive comparable pushes, but one has much greater mass. Use inertia to predict which cart resists a change in motion more.

6. A cart rolls at 0.8 m/s while the forces on it balance. Which principle best explains the prediction?

  1. Newton’s First Law
  2. an inertia force
  3. Newton’s Third Law
  4. a continuing forward net force

7. A property of matter resists changes in velocity. Name the property.

  1. inertia
  2. net force
  3. support force
  4. friction

8. When a bus stops suddenly, a passenger tends to continue forward. A student calls this a forward “inertia force.” Critique the explanation.

9. Use the book-on-table diagram to name the interaction partner associated with each vertical force.

10. A hockey puck moves north while the net force is zero. Which principle best explains the prediction?

  1. Newton’s First Law
  2. an inertia force
  3. Newton’s Third Law
  4. a continuing forward net force

11. Use the book-on-table diagram. Which statement best describes the vertical forces?

  1. Weight acts downward and support force acts upward; they can balance.
  2. Only weight acts because the book is not moving.
  3. Only support force acts.
  4. Inertia acts upward to cancel gravity.

12. A toy car moves on a low-friction track with zero net force. Explain the motion using Newton’s First Law.

13. Use the book-on-table diagram to distinguish “no forces” from “zero net force.”

14. A puck slides east on nearly frictionless ice with zero net force. Which principle best explains the prediction?

  1. Newton’s First Law
  2. an inertia force
  3. Newton’s Third Law
  4. a continuing forward net force

15. Rewrite the statement “Inertia is what keeps an object going forward” so that it uses physics vocabulary accurately.

16. Compare a 12 kg cart and a 3 kg cart. Which has more inertia, and why?

17. A sign hangs motionless from a cable. Name the main forces on the sign.

18. A bowling ball is at rest with zero net force. Explain the motion using Newton’s First Law.

19. A spacecraft drifts in deep space with negligible net force. Predict what happens next and justify with Newton’s First Law.

20. Compare loaded and unloaded carts and explain how inertia changes what you expect when the same interaction tries to change their motion.

21. An air-track glider moves left while the net force is zero. Predict what happens next and justify with Newton’s First Law.

22. An elevator moves upward at constant speed. Are its vertical forces balanced or unbalanced? Explain.

  1. Balanced; constant velocity means zero net force.
  2. A forward net force is required for motion.
  3. No forces act because speed is constant.
  4. Inertia is an additional balancing force.

23. A boat floats at rest. Explain why the correct description is “zero net force” rather than “no forces.”

24. A book rests on a level table while its vertical forces balance. Explain the motion using Newton’s First Law.
