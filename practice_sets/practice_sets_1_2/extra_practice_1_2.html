Conceptual Physics Extra Practice 1.2

1. Two carts receive comparable pushes, but one has much greater mass. Use inertia to predict which cart resists a change in motion more.

2. Use the book-on-table diagram to name the interaction partner associated with each vertical force.

3. A lamp hangs motionless from a cord. Identify the main forces on the lamp and state the net-force condition.

4. A bowling ball is at rest with zero net force. Explain the motion using Newton’s First Law.

5. When a bus stops suddenly, a passenger tends to continue forward. A student calls this a forward “inertia force.” Critique the explanation.

6. Rewrite the statement “Inertia is what keeps an object going forward” so that it uses physics vocabulary accurately.

7. A book rests on a level table while its vertical forces balance. Explain the motion using Newton’s First Law.

8. A crate has equal opposite horizontal pushes and balanced vertical forces. Determine the net force.

9. A backpack rests on the floor. Which two vertical forces are most important, and how are they related?

  1. Weight downward and support force upward are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
  2. A forward net force is required for motion.
  3. No forces act because speed is constant.
  4. Inertia is an additional balancing force.

10. Use the book-on-table diagram to distinguish “no forces” from “zero net force.”

11. A force diagram for a sliding cart includes a forward arrow labeled “inertia force.” Critique the diagram.

12. A parachutist descends at terminal speed. Relate drag, weight, and net force.

13. A sign hangs motionless from a cable. Name the main forces on the sign.

14. A loaded grocery cart is harder to start or stop than an empty cart. Explain this using inertia.

15. A puck slides east on nearly frictionless ice with zero net force. Which principle best explains the prediction?

  1. Newton’s First Law
  2. an inertia force
  3. Newton’s Third Law
  4. a continuing forward net force

16. A foam block floats motionless in water. Identify the main vertical forces and the net-force state.

17. Compare a 12 kg cart and a 3 kg cart. Which has more inertia, and why?

18. A toy car moves on a low-friction track with zero net force. Explain the motion using Newton’s First Law.

19. A cart rolls at 0.8 m/s while the forces on it balance. Which principle best explains the prediction?

  1. Newton’s First Law
  2. an inertia force
  3. Newton’s Third Law
  4. a continuing forward net force

20. An air-track glider moves left while the net force is zero. Predict what happens next and justify with Newton’s First Law.

21. A boat floats at rest. Explain why the correct description is “zero net force” rather than “no forces.”

22. Use the book-on-table diagram to distinguish the individual forces from the net force.

23. A spacecraft drifts in deep space with negligible net force. Predict what happens next and justify with Newton’s First Law.

24. A parked car rests on level ground. Explain why “no forces act” is incorrect.
