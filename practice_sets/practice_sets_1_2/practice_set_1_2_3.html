Conceptual Physics Practice 3 1.2

1. A parachutist descends at terminal speed. Relate drag, weight, and net force.

2. A parked car rests on level ground. Explain why “no forces act” is incorrect.

3. A backpack rests on the floor. Which two vertical forces are most important, and how are they related?

  1. Weight downward and support force upward are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
  2. A forward net force is required for motion.
  3. No forces act because speed is constant.
  4. Inertia is an additional balancing force.

4. Use the book-on-table diagram to distinguish “no forces” from “zero net force.”

5. A book rests on a level table while its vertical forces balance. Explain the motion using Newton’s First Law.

6. Two carts receive comparable pushes, but one has much greater mass. Use inertia to predict which cart resists a change in motion more.

7. A hockey puck moves north while the net force is zero. Which principle best explains the prediction?

  1. Newton’s First Law
  2. an inertia force
  3. Newton’s Third Law
  4. a continuing forward net force

8. Rewrite the statement “Inertia is what keeps an object going forward” so that it uses physics vocabulary accurately.

9. A spacecraft drifts in deep space with negligible net force. Predict what happens next and justify with Newton’s First Law.

10. A toy car moves on a low-friction track with zero net force. Explain the motion using Newton’s First Law.

11. An air-track glider moves left while the net force is zero. Predict what happens next and justify with Newton’s First Law.

12. A crate has equal opposite horizontal pushes and balanced vertical forces. Determine the net force.

13. Use the book-on-table diagram to distinguish the individual forces from the net force.

14. A lamp hangs motionless from a cord. Identify the main forces on the lamp and state the net-force condition.

15. Use the book-on-table diagram. Which statement best describes the vertical forces?

  1. Weight acts downward and support force acts upward; they can balance.
  2. Only weight acts because the book is not moving.
  3. Only support force acts.
  4. Inertia acts upward to cancel gravity.

16. An elevator moves upward at constant speed. Are its vertical forces balanced or unbalanced? Explain.

  1. Balanced; constant velocity means zero net force.
  2. A forward net force is required for motion.
  3. No forces act because speed is constant.
  4. Inertia is an additional balancing force.

17. Compare a 12 kg cart and a 3 kg cart. Which has more inertia, and why?

18. A sign hangs motionless from a cable. Name the main forces on the sign.

19. A foam block floats motionless in water. Identify the main vertical forces and the net-force state.

20. A sled moves across level snow at constant speed. What must be true about the net force?

  1. The net force is zero; horizontal and vertical components balance.
  2. A forward net force is required for motion.
  3. No forces act because speed is constant.
  4. Inertia is an additional balancing force.

21. A property of matter resists changes in velocity. Name the property.

  1. inertia
  2. net force
  3. support force
  4. friction

22. A bowling ball is at rest with zero net force. Explain the motion using Newton’s First Law.

23. Compare loaded and unloaded carts and explain how inertia changes what you expect when the same interaction tries to change their motion.

24. A loaded grocery cart is harder to start or stop than an empty cart. Explain this using inertia.
