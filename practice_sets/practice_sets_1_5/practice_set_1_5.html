Conceptual Physics Practice 1.5

1. A lamp hangs motionless from a single vertical cord. Draw a force diagram for the lamp only.

2. Use the opposing-force diagram. State the condition required for the shown forces to be balanced.

3. A parachutist descends at terminal speed. Draw a force diagram for the parachutist.

4. A book rests on a level table. Draw a force diagram for the book only.

5. A ball is moving upward after release. A student draws an upward “motion force.” Critique the model.

6. A sled is pulled to the right across rough snow and is speeding up. Draw a force diagram for the sled.

7. Equal 20 N forces act left and right on a moving cart. A student predicts the cart must stop. Critique the prediction.

8. Use the supplied mismatch diagram to critique the claim “An object must move in the direction of the largest force arrow.”

9. Use the flawed cart diagram to distinguish a force arrow from an arrow that merely shows the object’s motion.

10. Use the constant-speed box diagram. Explain what the force arrows must show if the motion is truly constant in a straight line.

11. Use the representation-mismatch diagram. What revision is needed before the model can represent zero net force?

  1. Adjust the opposing arrows so the vector sum is zero.
  2. Any two forces automatically balance.
  3. Motion requires a net force in the direction of motion.
  4. A force diagram cannot show balance.

12. A crate is pushed left at constant speed across a rough floor. Draw a force diagram for the crate.

13. Use the constant-speed diagram to connect balanced forces with unchanged velocity.

14. Use the equilibrium force diagram. Which statement best defines balanced forces?

  1. The vector sum of all forces is zero.
  2. Any two forces automatically balance.
  3. Motion requires a net force in the direction of motion.
  4. A force diagram cannot show balance.

15. A cart is moving right while a net force acts left. Predict how its motion changes if that force continues.

16. A sled moves right but is slowing. A student says the net force must point right. Critique the statement.

17. A block rests on a frictionless incline. Draw a force diagram for the block.

18. An elevator moves upward at constant speed. Draw a force diagram for the elevator car.

19. Use the supplied mismatch diagram to distinguish velocity arrows from force arrows and revise a confused explanation.

20. A cart is moving left while 30 N acts right and 12 N acts left. A student says the net force points left because the cart is moving left. Critique the reasoning.

21. A ball is falling after release. Neglect air resistance. Draw a force diagram for the ball.

22. A metal sphere hangs motionless from a vertical string. Draw a force diagram for the sphere.

23. A puck moves left while the net force points right. Predict its motion over time.

24. A sign hangs at rest from two angled cables. Draw a force diagram for the sign.
