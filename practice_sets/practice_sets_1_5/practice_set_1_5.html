Conceptual Physics Practice 1.5

Vocabulary & Concept Review — Equilibrium

1. Match each physics term to the best definition.

  1. net force
  2. equilibrium
  3. static equilibrium
  4. dynamic equilibrium
  5. normal force
  6. tension
  1. The vector sum of all forces acting on an object.
  2. A state in which the net force is zero.
  3. Zero net force while the object remains at rest.
  4. Zero net force while the object moves with constant velocity.
  5. A support force exerted by a surface, perpendicular to that surface.
  6. A pulling force transmitted by a rope, string, or cable.

2. Use the word bank to complete the statements.

balanced forces  •  unbalanced forces  •  resultant vector  •  equilibrium

a. When all force vectors add to zero, the forces are __________ and the object is in __________.

b. The single vector that represents the vector sum of two or more vectors is the __________.

c. When the force vectors do not add to zero, the forces are __________.

3. Classify each situation as static equilibrium, dynamic equilibrium, or not in equilibrium.

  1. A book sits motionless on a desk.
  2. A train moves west at constant speed on a straight track.
  3. A bicycle speeds up while traveling straight ahead.
  4. A car turns a corner at constant speed.

4. A laptop rests on a desk. A student says, “Because the laptop is not moving, no forces are acting on it.” Critique the statement and revise it using the terms weight, normal force, and net force.

5. A car travels east at constant speed on a straight, level road. Explain why the car can be in equilibrium even though it is moving. State what must be true about the total left/right forces and the total up/down forces.

6. An elevator moves downward at constant speed. Its weight is 7200 N downward. What upward cable tension is required? Is this static equilibrium or dynamic equilibrium?

Use the equilibrium rule: \(\sum F_y=0\).

7. A crate moves right at constant speed across a rough, level floor. A 145 N push acts right, and the crate’s weight is 410 N downward.

a. Find the friction force needed for horizontal equilibrium.

b. Find the normal force needed for vertical equilibrium.

c. State the net force on the crate.

8. A skateboarder moves at constant speed but turns through a curved path. Is the skateboarder in equilibrium? Explain using velocity, acceleration, and net force.

Force Diagrams, Balanced Forces & Net Force

9. A textbook rests on a level table. Draw a force diagram for the textbook only. Label each force and show its direction.

10. A lamp hangs motionless from one vertical cord. Draw a force diagram for the lamp only. Label each force and show its direction.

11. A crate is pushed right across a rough floor at constant speed. Draw a force diagram for the crate. Then label the forces as balanced or unbalanced.

12. A sled is pulled right across rough snow and is speeding up. Draw a force diagram for the sled. Make the relative horizontal arrow lengths consistent with the motion.

13. An elevator moves upward at constant speed. Draw a force diagram for the elevator car and identify whether it is in static equilibrium, dynamic equilibrium, or not in equilibrium.

14. A ball has been tossed upward and has already left the thrower’s hand. Neglect air resistance. Draw a force diagram for the ball while it is still moving upward.

15. A cart has 300 N of weight downward and a 300 N normal force upward. A person pushes 85 N right while friction acts 35 N left.

a. Are the vertical forces balanced or unbalanced?

b. Are the horizontal forces balanced or unbalanced?

c. Find the cart’s net force and state the direction of its acceleration.

16. A 640 N box remains at rest on the floor while two people lift upward on it with forces of 220 N and 180 N. The floor still touches the box. What upward normal force must the floor exert for static equilibrium?

Use \(\sum F_y=0\).

17. A cart is moving right when a net force begins acting left. Describe what happens to the cart’s velocity if the leftward net force continues. Be clear about the difference between the direction of velocity and the direction of acceleration.

18. A student says, “If the net force points left, the object must be moving left.” Critique the statement and rewrite it correctly using the terms net force, acceleration, and velocity.

Resultant Vectors from Components
For Questions 19–24: Each vector is written as \(\langle F_x,F_y\rangle\), where +x is right and +y is up. Add x-components together and y-components together. Write each resultant as \(\vec R=\langle R_x,R_y\rangle\). The given vectors are not parallel.

19. Two forces act on an object: \(\vec F_1=\langle 6,2\rangle\text{ N}\) and \(\vec F_2=\langle -1,5\rangle\text{ N}\).

a. Find the resultant vector.

b. Are the forces balanced or unbalanced?

20. Two forces act on a low-friction cart: \(\vec F_1=\langle -4,7\rangle\text{ N}\) and \(\vec F_2=\langle 9,-3\rangle\text{ N}\).

a. Find the resultant vector.

b. Based on the signs of \(R_x\) and \(R_y\), describe the direction of the cart’s acceleration.

21. Two nonparallel forces act on an air-hockey puck: \(\vec F_1=\langle 8,-6\rangle\text{ N}\) and \(\vec F_2=\langle -3,2\rangle\text{ N}\).

a. Find the resultant vector.

b. Which horizontal and vertical directions does the resultant point?

22. Three forces act on an object: \(\vec F_1=\langle 5,4\rangle\text{ N}\), \(\vec F_2=\langle -2,1\rangle\text{ N}\), and \(\vec F_3=\langle -3,-5\rangle\text{ N}\).

a. Find the resultant vector.

b. Are the forces balanced?

c. Name the two possible equilibrium states the object could have, depending on whether its velocity is zero or nonzero.

23. Three forces act on a small robot: \(\vec F_1=\langle 12,0\rangle\text{ N}\), \(\vec F_2=\langle -4,6\rangle\text{ N}\), and \(\vec F_3=\langle -3,-2\rangle\text{ N}\).

a. Find the resultant vector.

b. Are the forces balanced or unbalanced?

c. Sketch the resultant arrow on a small x-y axis in your workspace.

24. Three forces act on an air-hockey puck: \(\vec F_1=\langle 7,4\rangle\text{ N}\), \(\vec F_2=\langle -2,5\rangle\text{ N}\), and \(\vec F_3=\langle -6,-3\rangle\text{ N}\). The puck is currently moving right.

a. Find the resultant force vector.

b. A student says, “The puck will instantly move in exactly the same direction as the resultant force.” Critique the claim. Explain what the resultant force actually tells you about the puck’s motion.
