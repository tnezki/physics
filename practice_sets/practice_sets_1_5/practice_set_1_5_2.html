Conceptual Physics Practice 2 1.5

1. A cart moves right at constant speed. A student says, “The net force must point right because the cart is moving right.” Critique and revise the claim.

2. A puck moves left while the net force points right. Predict its motion over time.

3. Use the constant-speed diagram to connect balanced forces with unchanged velocity.

4. A cart is moving left while 30 N acts right and 12 N acts left. A student says the net force points left because the cart is moving left. Critique the reasoning.

5. Use the supplied mismatch diagram to critique the claim “An object must move in the direction of the largest force arrow.”

6. A sign hangs at rest from two angled cables. Draw a force diagram for the sign.

7. Use the supplied mismatch diagram to distinguish velocity arrows from force arrows and revise a confused explanation.

8. Inspect the representation-mismatch diagram. Identify the force-model mismatch with the stated constant-speed motion and explain the needed revision.

9. Use the supplied mismatch diagram to explain why motion direction alone cannot justify a free-body diagram.

10. A crate is pushed left at constant speed across a rough floor. Draw a force diagram for the crate.

11. A book rests on a level table. Draw a force diagram for the book only.

12. Use the opposing-force diagram. State the condition required for the shown forces to be balanced.

13. A sled is pulled to the right across rough snow and is speeding up. Draw a force diagram for the sled.

14. A foam block floats motionless in water. Draw a force diagram for the block.

15. A block rests on a frictionless incline. Draw a force diagram for the block.

16. A sled moves right but is slowing. A student says the net force must point right. Critique the statement.

17. A car travels to the right at constant speed on a level road. Draw a force diagram for the car.

18. A parachutist descends at terminal speed. Draw a force diagram for the parachutist.

19. Use the flawed cart diagram to distinguish a force arrow from an arrow that merely shows the object’s motion.

20. The cart is described as moving at constant speed. Use the flawed force diagram to decide whether the shown forces are balanced or unbalanced.

  1. Unbalanced: the unequal opposing horizontal arrows give a nonzero net force.
  2. Any two forces automatically balance.
  3. Motion requires a net force in the direction of motion.
  4. A force diagram cannot show balance.

21. An elevator moves upward at constant speed. Draw a force diagram for the elevator car.

22. Equal 20 N forces act left and right on a moving cart. A student predicts the cart must stop. Critique the prediction.

23. Use the constant-speed box diagram. Explain what the force arrows must show if the motion is truly constant in a straight line.

24. A cart is moving right while a net force acts left. Predict how its motion changes if that force continues.
