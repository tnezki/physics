Conceptual Physics Extra Practice 1.5

1. A ball is falling after release. Neglect air resistance. Draw a force diagram for the ball.

2. A ball is moving upward after release. A student draws an upward “motion force.” Critique the model.

3. Use the opposing-force diagram. State the condition required for the shown forces to be balanced.

4. A metal sphere hangs motionless from a vertical string. Draw a force diagram for the sphere.

5. A puck moves left while the net force points right. Predict its motion over time.

6. Use the flawed cart diagram to distinguish a force arrow from an arrow that merely shows the object’s motion.

7. A car travels to the right at constant speed on a level road. Draw a force diagram for the car.

8. A block rests on a frictionless incline. Draw a force diagram for the block.

9. Use the representation-mismatch diagram. What revision is needed before the model can represent zero net force?

  1. Adjust the opposing arrows so the vector sum is zero.
  2. Any two forces automatically balance.
  3. Motion requires a net force in the direction of motion.
  4. A force diagram cannot show balance.

10. A lamp hangs motionless from a single vertical cord. Draw a force diagram for the lamp only.

11. A sled moves right but is slowing. A student says the net force must point right. Critique the statement.

12. The cart is described as moving at constant speed. Use the flawed force diagram to decide whether the shown forces are balanced or unbalanced.

  1. Unbalanced: the unequal opposing horizontal arrows give a nonzero net force.
  2. Any two forces automatically balance.
  3. Motion requires a net force in the direction of motion.
  4. A force diagram cannot show balance.

13. A sign hangs at rest from two angled cables. Draw a force diagram for the sign.

14. A cart moves right at constant speed. A student says, “The net force must point right because the cart is moving right.” Critique and revise the claim.

15. A cart is moving left while 30 N acts right and 12 N acts left. A student says the net force points left because the cart is moving left. Critique the reasoning.

16. Inspect the representation-mismatch diagram. Identify the force-model mismatch with the stated constant-speed motion and explain the needed revision.

17. A book rests on a level table. Draw a force diagram for the book only.

18. Use the supplied mismatch diagram to distinguish velocity arrows from force arrows and revise a confused explanation.

19. Use the supplied mismatch diagram to explain why motion direction alone cannot justify a free-body diagram.

20. Use the equilibrium force diagram. Which statement best defines balanced forces?

  1. The vector sum of all forces is zero.
  2. Any two forces automatically balance.
  3. Motion requires a net force in the direction of motion.
  4. A force diagram cannot show balance.

21. Use the constant-speed box diagram. Explain what the force arrows must show if the motion is truly constant in a straight line.

22. A foam block floats motionless in water. Draw a force diagram for the block.

23. A sled is pulled to the right across rough snow and is speeding up. Draw a force diagram for the sled.

24. Use the supplied mismatch diagram to critique the claim “An object must move in the direction of the largest force arrow.”
