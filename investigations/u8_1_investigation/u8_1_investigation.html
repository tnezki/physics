Physics - Unit 8 - Section 8.1 - Investigation

Map the Invisible Field

Use a compass to build an evidence-based model of magnetic direction and relative strength.

Learning target: Explain magnetic poles, magnetic fields, field-line models, and magnetic domains using observable evidence.
Research Question: How can compass evidence and magnetization evidence support a model for an invisible magnetic field?

1. Predict the Field Map

Sketch what you think a small compass will do at several locations around a bar magnet. Do not draw field lines yet.

2. Map Direction with a Compass

Equipment: bar magnet, small compass, paper, pencil, ruler, optional iron filings sealed in a clear field viewer.
1
Trace the bar magnet on the paper and label its marked N and S ends.
2
Place the compass at 10-12 locations around the magnet. At each point draw a short arrow in the direction the compass north end points.
3
Use neighboring arrows to sketch several smooth field-line paths. Outside the magnet, arrow direction should run from N toward S.

3. Compare Relative Strength

Choose three locations: near a pole, near the side of the magnet, and far away. Rank the field from strongest to weakest using evidence such as compass response and the spacing of your mapped lines.

4. Magnetize a Steel Object

Stroke one end of a steel paper clip repeatedly in one direction with the same pole of the bar magnet. Test whether the clip can attract another clip before and after. Use magnetic-domain language to explain the change.

5. Defend the Field Model

Write a short claim explaining why a magnetic-field diagram is useful even though the lines are not physical objects. Use at least two pieces of evidence from your investigation.
