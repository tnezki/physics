Physics - Unit 8 - Section 8.1 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Map the Invisible Field

Hands-on field mapping - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups2-3
Core toolCompass + bar magnet

Why This Investigation Fits Section 8.1

This directly matches the section targets for pole interactions, field direction, relative strength, and magnetic domains. Students construct the field model from measurements rather than copying a finished diagram.

Materials

  • Bar magnet
  • Small compass
  • Paper and pencil
  • Ruler
  • Steel paper clips
  • Optional sealed iron-filings field viewer

Before Class

  1. Check every compass for free rotation.
  2. Mark magnet poles clearly.
  3. Keep strong magnets separated from student electronics and magnetic storage media.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
4Prediction + setupSeparate predicted pattern from measured evidence.
10Compass field mapRequire local arrows before connecting them into lines.
4Strength comparisonUse evidence and line spacing qualitatively.
5Paper-clip magnetizationConnect behavior to domain alignment.
4-6Field-model defenseEmphasize model vs physical object.

Expected Observations / Evidence

Compass direction changes smoothly around the magnet and is most strongly controlled by the magnet near the poles. A steel clip can become temporarily more magnetic after repeated stroking/alignment.

Watch for: Iron filings can stain or become hazardous if loose; use a sealed viewer if used at all. Avoid the misconception that breaking or magnetizing an object separates north from south into isolated poles.

Strong Responses

PromptWhat to Look For
Field mapCompass arrows tangent to a smooth pattern, with outside direction N to S.
Relative strengthStrongest near pole regions; line density is treated as a model for relative strength, not as literal strings.
DomainsMagnetization is explained as increased alignment of magnetic domains rather than creation of a single isolated pole.

Common Problems

  • Compass sticks or points oddly: Move steel objects and other magnets farther away.
  • Students connect every arrow into one giant loop: Remind them that each compass reading is local evidence and several field lines may be sketched.

Connection Forward

Section 8.2 will ask whether a magnetic field can be produced without a permanent magnet at all.
