Physics - Unit 7 - Section 7.4 - Investigation

Power Is Not Energy

Measure power, then use operating time to decide total electrical energy transfer.

Learning target: Calculate electric power and electrical energy use, compare devices, and evaluate efficiency/energy claims.
Research Question: How do voltage, current, power, and operating time work together to determine electrical energy use?

1. Predict Before Measuring

Two low-voltage devices can draw different current from the same source. Predict which device will transfer electrical energy faster and what measurement you need to decide.

2. Measure Two Devices

Equipment: low-voltage DC source, miniature lamp, small DC motor or second approved load, switch, wires, multimeter(s).

4. Lighting Claim

A 100-W incandescent lamp and a 25-W LED are described as producing similar useful brightness. If each runs for 5.0 h, compare their electrical energy use in Wh. What efficiency claim is supported, and what claim would go beyond the evidence?

5. Solar Evidence Check

Two rooftop panels have the same area. Panel X receives full sun for 4 h; Panel Y is shaded for part of the day but has a higher peak power rating. Explain why peak power alone cannot decide which transfers more energy over the day. List the measurements you would need.

6. Final Rule

Correct this statement using evidence from the investigation: “The higher-power device always uses more energy.”
