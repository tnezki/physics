Physics - Unit 7 - Section 7.4 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Power Is Not Energy

Power and energy-use evidence lab - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups2-3
Core toolV/I measurement + energy audit

Why This Investigation Fits Section 7.4

The investigation directly applies P=IV and E=Pt, then asks students to critique power-only claims about devices, lighting, and solar energy. It matches the notes emphasis that watts are a rate and total energy depends on time.

Materials

  • Low-voltage DC source
  • Miniature lamp
  • Small DC motor or second approved load
  • Switch and wires
  • Digital multimeter(s)

Before Class

  1. Pre-test both loads at the chosen voltage.
  2. Keep measurement roles consistent with earlier circuit labs.
  3. Have calculators available.

Expected Observations / Evidence

Devices with different current at similar voltage will have different power. Equal operating times make higher power correspond to greater energy transfer, but a lower-power device can use more total energy if operated much longer.

Watch for: Power is not brightness and power is not total energy. Avoid converting every efficiency claim into “lower watts is always better” without holding useful output or service constant.

Sample Calculations

If a device operates at 3.0 V and 0.20 A, P = IV = (0.20 A)(3.0 V) = 0.60 W. For 60 s, E = Pt = (0.60 W)(60 s) = 36 J.

Lighting comparison: 100 W × 5.0 h = 500 Wh; 25 W × 5.0 h = 125 Wh. With the stated similar useful light output, the lower-power lamp uses less electrical energy over the same time. The data do not by themselves establish lifetime, purchase cost, or every environmental impact.

Strong Responses

PromptWhat to Look For
PowerCorrect P=IV calculation with watts interpreted as J/s.
EnergyUses both power and time; does not rank energy use from watts alone.
Solar claimRequests time-dependent sunlight/output evidence, not only peak rating.

Safety / Common Problems

  • Motor start-up current varies: Use a steady no-load reading after the motor reaches stable speed.
  • Brightness comparisons: Do not use visual brightness alone as a quantitative power measurement.
