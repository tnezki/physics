Physics - Unit 4 - Section 2 - Investigation

Momentum Before and After

Measure an inelastic cart collision and test a system-level conservation claim.

Learning target: Use conservation of momentum to analyze a collision by choosing a system and comparing total momentum before and after.
Research Question: Does the total momentum of a two-cart system remain approximately constant during a sticking collision?

1. Make a Prediction

Cart A will collide with stationary Cart B and the carts will stick. Predict whether the total momentum of the two-cart system will be greater, smaller, or approximately the same after the collision.

2. Set Up Video Analysis

Equipment: two carts with sticking bumpers, level track, balance, meter stick, iPad/phone, Vernier Graphical Analysis video analysis.
1
Measure the mass of each cart. Place a meter stick in the video plane for scale.
2
Record a side-view collision with the camera fixed and perpendicular to the track.
3
Track Cart A for several frames before impact and the stuck pair for several frames after impact. Determine representative velocities before and after.

4. Analyze the System

Compare total momentum before and after. Calculate the percent difference only if your teacher asks; otherwise describe how close the values are and identify likely experimental causes of the mismatch.

Why is it important that your system includes both carts? Identify one internal interaction and one possible external influence.

5. Conservation Claim

Use your measured before-and-after momenta to defend or revise this statement: When net external impulse is small, total momentum of the cart system remains approximately constant.

Momentum is a vector quantity. Keep a consistent positive direction and include signs on measured velocities.
