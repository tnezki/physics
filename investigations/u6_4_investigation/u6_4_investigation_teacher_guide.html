Physics - Unit 6 - Section 6.4 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Measure a Lens: Focal Length and Magnification

Hands-on quantitative lens lab - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups2-3
Core toolConverging lens + screen

Why This Investigation Fits Section 6.4

Students have learned focal point, real/virtual images, the thin-lens equation, and magnification. Repeated focusing tests whether one focal length describes the same lens while object and image distances vary.

Materials Per Group

  • Converging lens with holder
  • Illuminated arrow/object
  • White screen/card with stand
  • Meter stick or optical bench
  • Small ruler
  • Calculator

Before Class

  1. Determine object positions that yield clear screen images within the available bench length.
  2. Measure/mark the lens center reference.
  3. Use a high-contrast arrow or patterned object so orientation and image height are obvious.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
4Prediction + alignmentStress center-to-center distance measurement.

Expected Evidence

For the same lens, calculated focal lengths should cluster around one value even though do and di change. The projected images should be inverted. Magnification should change with setup geometry.

Sample Reasoning / Calculation

Example: do = 30 cm, di = 15 cm gives 1/f = 1/30 + 1/15 = 3/30 = 1/10, so f = 10 cm. If ho = 2.0 cm and hi = 1.0 cm, the measured size ratio M = hi/ho = 0.50.

Watch for: Do not let students treat focal length as the lens-screen distance for every setup. Also distinguish “larger image” from “more light.”

Strong Responses

PromptWhat to Look For
Focal-length claimUses all three trials and discusses consistency rather than choosing only the best trial.
Real imageCites the fact that a sharp image can be caught on a screen because rays actually converge there.
UncertaintyNames a plausible source such as locating the lens center or deciding the sharpest screen position.

Common Problems

  • No image on screen: Object may be inside the focal length or components may be misaligned; increase object-lens distance and realign.
  • Image too dim: Reduce room light and use a brighter/high-contrast object.
  • f values vary widely: Recheck that do and di were both measured from the lens center.

Safety / Cleanup

Never focus direct sunlight with the lens. Keep glass lenses in holders to reduce drops/chips.
