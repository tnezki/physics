Equilibrium: Still and Moving

Hands-on equilibrium investigation · adapted from the legacy Vector Force Balance Challenge

Time25-30 minutes
Groups3 students
Core toolsStraws + motion data

Why This Investigation Fits Section 1.4

The legacy Vector Force Balance Challenge is unusually well matched to static equilibrium: equal opposite forces, perpendicular resultants, and three-way balance. This revision keeps that physical challenge but adds a short dynamic-equilibrium test so students see that zero net force is compatible with both rest and constant straight-line motion.

Materials Per Group

  • 3 drinking straws
  • 1 ping-pong ball
  • Masking tape, protractor, ruler, timer
  • Wood block or textbook that slides with visible friction
  • Spring scale or Vernier force sensor
  • Vernier motion detector + Graphical Analysis

Room Setup

Give each group a smooth surface for the ping-pong challenge and a 1-2 m path for the sliding-object challenge. If motion detectors are limited, Part B works well as a teacher station while groups complete Part A.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
0-5Level 1 opposite-force balanceEqual opposite vectors → zero net force.
5-9Level 2 perpendicular forcesTwo perpendicular nonzero forces cannot cancel each other.
9-15Level 3 three-way balanceSymmetric directions can create a near-zero resultant.
15-24Dynamic-equilibrium pull + velocity graphConstant speed, not “moving,” is the key evidence.
24-30Compare static/dynamic equilibriumBoth require zero net force.

Expected Observations

  • Level 1: when opposing air effects are approximately equal, the ball remains near the center; stronger force shifts it toward the weaker side.
  • Level 2: perpendicular forces combine into a diagonal resultant rather than canceling.
  • Level 3: approximately equal forces spaced around the ball can produce near-equilibrium.
  • Dynamic challenge: during the flattest velocity interval, the forward applied pull and backward friction/resistance are approximately balanced; support force and weight balance vertically.
Language precision: The air-force challenge is qualitative. Do not present students' blowing strengths as measured equal forces. Use “approximately balanced” and let the ball's behavior serve as evidence.

Dynamic-Equilibrium Force Diagram

Expected model: weight downward, support upward, applied pull forward, friction/resistance backward. During approximately constant straight-line speed, vertical and horizontal pairs balance so net force is approximately zero.

Common Misconceptions

  • “If it is moving, forces cannot be balanced.” Return to the flat velocity interval.
  • “The forward force must be larger because the block is moving forward.” Motion direction and net-force direction are different ideas.
  • “Equilibrium means no forces.” Both parts provide visible examples of multiple forces acting while the net effect is near zero.

Shortening Option

If time is tight, run Levels 1 and 3 only, then make the dynamic-equilibrium pull a teacher demonstration with one projected Graphical Analysis graph. Keep the student comparison table and equilibrium rule.

Connection Forward

Section 1.5 asks students to build and critique complete force models. End by asking: “What evidence would you need before deciding whether a force diagram is believable?”
