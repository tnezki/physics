Equilibrium: Still and Moving

Balance real forces, then test whether equilibrium can exist while an object is moving.

Learning target: Determine whether an object is in mechanical equilibrium and explain how balanced forces relate to rest or constant straight-line motion.
Research Question: What evidence tells us that forces are balanced, and can an object be in equilibrium while it is moving?

Part A. Air-Force Balance Challenge

Materials: 3 drinking straws, 1 ping-pong ball, masking tape, protractor, ruler, and timer. Work on a smooth tabletop or floor area.

Level 1 - Opposite Forces

  1. Mark a center point with tape.
  2. Two students position themselves directly opposite each other, roughly 180° apart.
  3. Place the ball at the center. Both blow through the straws and adjust until the ball stays near the center for at least 5 seconds.
  4. Change one person's force and observe what happens.

Level 2 - Perpendicular Forces

  1. Move the two blowers so their directions are 90° apart.
  2. Try equal-strength blowing first.
  3. Predict and then observe the direction in which the ball drifts.
  4. Decide where a third force would need to point to stop that drift.

Level 3 - Three-Way Balance

  1. Place three blowers about 120° apart around the center.
  2. All three begin together and make small adjustments until the ball remains near center.
  3. Record your longest stable time.

Part B. Dynamic Equilibrium Challenge

Materials: wooden block or textbook, spring scale/force sensor, smooth marked path, Vernier motion detector, and Graphical Analysis. The object should slide with noticeable friction.

Your group will pull the object in a straight line while trying to maintain approximately constant speed for several seconds.
