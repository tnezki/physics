Physics - Unit 5 - Section 3 - Investigation

Trace the Sound Path

Use several pieces of evidence to model source vibration, medium motion, pitch, and loudness.

Learning target: Students will explain sound as a longitudinal mechanical wave that requires a medium and transfers energy through vibrating particles.
Research Question: What must matter do for sound energy to travel from a vibrating source to a receiver?

1. Make a Prediction

A sound source and listener are separated by matter. Predict what the particles of the medium do as sound travels: move all the way from source to listener, or vibrate locally and pass the disturbance onward? Explain.

2. Set Up

Equipment: two tuning forks of clearly different frequencies, rubber striker, shallow cup of water, paper towels, two paper/plastic cups connected by taut string, tabletop, meter stick optional
1
Source evidence: Strike one tuning fork gently, then more firmly. Compare loudness and pitch. Record what changed and what stayed approximately the same.
2
Frequency evidence: Strike two different-frequency tuning forks with similar strength. Compare pitch. Use the labeled fork frequencies as evidence.
3
Visible vibration: Touch a vibrating tine lightly to shallow water. Record how the water makes the source motion visible.
4
Medium evidence: Use the string telephone with the string taut, then slack. Keep the speaking voice similar. Compare how well the sound is transmitted.
5
Build a particle model: Draw several particles in a line and show how a compression and rarefaction can move while each particle only vibrates near its equilibrium position.

3. Collect Evidence

TestWhat changed?What stayed similar?Evidence about source / medium / pitch / loudness

4. Analyze the Evidence

1. Why does the tuning-fork water test support the claim that sound begins with a vibrating source?

2. Use the gentle-versus-firm strike to separate loudness from pitch.

3. Use the two different forks to connect frequency to pitch.

4. Why does the string telephone work best when the string is taut? Explain in terms of a medium carrying vibration.

5. Use your model to explain why sound cannot travel through a vacuum.

5. Defend or Revise Your Model

Write a source → medium → receiver explanation of sound that includes local particle vibration, energy transfer, compressions/rarefactions, pitch, and loudness.

Use your group’s actual observations and measurements. Classroom data may vary; explain variation instead of forcing a perfect result.
