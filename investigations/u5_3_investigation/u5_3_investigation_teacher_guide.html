Physics - Unit 5 - Section 3 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Trace the Sound Path

Hands-on sound evidence stations - designed for 25–30 minutes

Time25–30 minutes
Groups3–4
Core toolTuning forks + string telephone

Why This Investigation Fits Section 5.3

Students have already learned the longitudinal-wave model in the notes. The investigation supplies physical evidence for source vibration, transmission through matter, and the independence of pitch and loudness.

Materials Per Group

  • Two tuning forks with different labeled frequencies
  • Rubber striker
  • Shallow water cup and paper towels
  • Cup-and-string telephone
  • Tabletop

Before Class

  1. Prepare taut string telephones and check they transmit clearly.
  2. Fill shallow water containers only enough to reveal tuning-fork motion.
  3. Review safe tuning-fork handling.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
4 minPrediction + setupReinforce local particle motion versus particle transport.
12–14 minEvidence testsRotate through source, frequency, water, and string-medium tests.
5 minParticle modelDraw compressions/rarefactions and local vibration.
5–7 minAnalysis + conclusionConnect evidence to mechanical longitudinal-wave model.

Expected Evidence

Striking the same fork harder increases loudness much more than pitch; different-frequency forks produce different pitches; water reveals source vibration; taut string transmits cup vibrations better than slack string. Students should infer that matter vibrates locally and transfers the disturbance.

Sample Calculation / Quantitative Check

No required calculation beyond reading labeled tuning-fork frequencies. If students time a sound path, v = d/t and 340 m/s are already allowed, but they are not necessary here.

Watch for: Do not let students put vibrating forks against teeth, faces, or ears. Use shallow water and rubber strikers only. Slack-string comparisons should keep speaking distance/voice as similar as practical.
