Wire, Loop, Coil: Shape the Field

Hands-on compass comparison - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups2-3
Core toolCurrent-carrying conductors + compasses

Why This Investigation Fits Section 8.2

This investigation extends the Oersted evidence into the complete Section 8.2 target: current-produced fields, direction reversal, and straight-wire/loop/coil comparisons.

Materials

  • Low-voltage DC source
  • Switch
  • 10-20 ohm current-limiting resistor
  • Straight insulated wire
  • One-turn loop
  • Multi-turn coil/solenoid
  • 3-6 compasses
  • Connecting leads

Before Class

  1. Verify every circuit is current-limited.
  2. Prebuild or prepare the loop and coil so students spend time observing rather than winding wire.
  3. Mark a consistent current direction on each setup.

Expected Observations / Evidence

Compass patterns show a current-dependent field whose direction reverses with current. The coil should produce the most organized/concentrated field in its central region.

Watch for: Never short a battery or power supply. Keep the switch open except during brief observations. Coil wire may warm if current is too large.

Expected Evidence Pattern

  • With current off, compasses mainly respond to Earth and nearby environmental fields.
  • With current on, a straight wire produces a circular field pattern around the wire.
  • Reversing current reverses the magnetic-field direction.
  • Loop and coil geometry makes field contributions overlap; the central field is more concentrated in the multi-turn coil.

Strong Responses

Students should explain that moving charge remains the source of the magnetic field in every arrangement; geometry changes how contributions combine in space.

Connection Forward

Section 8.3 uses the coil as a controllable electromagnet and adds magnetic force on moving charge and current-carrying wire.
