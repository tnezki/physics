Physics - Unit 6 - Section 6.1 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Light Path Evidence: Materials, Shadows, and Vision

Hands-on evidence investigation - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups2-3
Core toolFlashlight + material/shadow tests

Why This Investigation Fits Section 6.1

After the 6.1 notes, students are ready to use transparent/translucent/opaque categories, ray paths, umbra/penumbra ideas, and the vision model. The investigation asks them to generate evidence rather than repeat definitions.

Materials Per Group

  • Flashlight or bright LED source
  • Clear acrylic or glass sample
  • Tracing paper or wax paper
  • Opaque cardboard
  • Printed target card
  • Small opaque object
  • White screen or cardstock
  • Ruler (optional for consistent distances)

Before Class

  1. Prepare identical material sets.
  2. Check that the translucent sample passes light but blurs the printed target.
  3. Choose a small object and source geometry that gives a visible shadow; a broad source or diffuser should soften the edge.

Expected Evidence

Clear material should transmit a recognizable image, translucent material should transmit light without a clear image, and opaque material should block most visible transmission. Changing source/object geometry should alter shadow size or edge sharpness. Students should connect visibility to light entering the eye.

Sample Reasoning / Calculation

No calculation is required. If students use c = fλ or v = d/t, keep it as a connection to the notes rather than a required part of this investigation.

Watch for: Transparent does not mean “no interaction.” Also listen for the claim that eyes send rays outward.

Strong Responses

PromptWhat to Look For
Material classificationUses observable transmission quality as evidence for each category.
Shadow explanationExplains the shadow as blocked ray paths rather than “darkness traveling.”
Vision modelTraces light from source to object/surface and then into the eye.

Common Problems

  • All samples look similar: Increase contrast behind the sample and reduce room light.
  • Shadow edge change is weak: Increase source size with a diffuser or change object-screen distance more dramatically.

Safety / Cleanup

Use bright LED/flashlight sources rather than lasers for this investigation. Handle glass carefully; acrylic is preferred.

Connection Forward

Section 6.2 asks what happens when light returns from a surface in an organized direction: reflection and mirrors.
