Physics - Unit 6 - Section 6.1 - Investigation

Light Path Evidence: Materials, Shadows, and Vision

Use controlled light paths to classify materials and explain what reaches the eye.

Learning target: Explain how light travels, how materials transmit or block it, how shadows form, and why an object is visible only when light from it enters the eye.
Research Question: How can evidence from transmitted light and shadows reveal the path light takes from a source to your eye?

1. Predict the Material Test

For each sample, predict whether you will see a clear image through it, a bright but blurred patch, or almost no transmitted light.

2. Test Transmission

Equipment: flashlight, printed target card, clear acrylic/glass, tracing paper or wax paper, cardboard, white screen.
1
Place the printed target 20-30 cm behind each material. Shine the flashlight toward the target and look through the material from the opposite side.
2
Classify each sample as transparent, translucent, or opaque. Record what evidence supports the classification.

4. Connect the Evidence to Seeing

A book does not produce its own visible light. Trace the path that lets you see the book when a lamp is on. Then explain why the same book is difficult to see in a completely dark room.

5. Defend a Model

Use at least two observations from your investigation to defend this claim: Light can interact with matter and still continue along a path; what you see depends on which light reaches your eye.
