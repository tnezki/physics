Why This Investigation Fits Section 8.5

The section is explicitly a synthesis/application section. This investigation gives direct transformer evidence, a reversible motor/generator experience, and a structured modern-device/energy critique while using only the formulas allowed by the Unit 8 plan.

Materials

  • Low-voltage isolated AC source
  • Transformer core and known-turn coils
  • AC multimeter
  • Small DC motor
  • Low-voltage DC source
  • Sensitive meter or suitable LED
  • Connecting leads
  • Optional speaker/microphone hardware for demonstration

Before Class

  1. Pretest the transformer voltage ratio.
  2. Keep all student transformer work low voltage and isolated.
  3. Pretest the motor-as-generator response so the meter range is appropriate.

Expected Observations / Evidence

Transformer voltage changes with the turns ratio; real measured values need not be perfectly ideal. The small motor demonstrates reciprocal motor/generator behavior. Device explanations should always include both mechanism and energy pathway.

Watch for: Never use exposed mains voltage. Higher secondary voltage is not higher free power. Do not confuse a magnetic field that mediates a force with an unlimited energy source.

Sample Calculations

Turns ratio: if Np = 100, Ns = 300, and Vp = 6 V, then Vs/6 = 300/100 = 3, so Vs is predicted to be 18 V in the idealized model.

Power: ideal Pout is about 60 W. With V = 20 V, I = P/V = 60/20 = 3 A.

Expected Evidence

  • Measured transformer voltage should broadly follow the turns ratio, with real losses and measurement variation.
  • A DC motor can demonstrate both electrical-to-mechanical motor operation and mechanical-to-electrical generator operation.
  • System explanations should identify an energy input as well as a field/force/induction mechanism.

Strong Responses

TransformerStep-up/down classification from turn ratio and voltage, not from power alone.
Motor/generatorSame hardware can exchange roles with input/output reversed.
Extraordinary claimDemands measured input/output energy or power over time and checks hidden inputs/losses.

Safety / Common Problems

  • Transformer: use only isolated low-voltage AC. Never use exposed mains voltage.
  • Motor as generator: output may be small; spin smoothly and use a sensitive meter if an LED is unreliable.
  • Power calculation: the 60 W example is mathematical only; do not attempt to build a 60 W student transformer circuit.
