Can Your Force Model Survive the Evidence?

Use real observations and measurements to build, test, and revise force models.

Learning target: Create and critique force models for real-world situations involving inertia, support force, net force, and equilibrium.
Research Question: How can we decide whether a force diagram and a motion description are actually consistent with the evidence?

Model Check Routine

At every station, use the same five checks:

  1. Choose the object. What single object are you modeling?
  2. Identify interactions. Every force arrow must come from something interacting with that object.
  3. Draw vectors. Direction and relative arrow length should make physical sense.
  4. Find the net effect. Balanced or unbalanced?
  5. Compare with motion. Does the force model agree with what the object actually does?

Station 3 - Cart Evidence

Use a cart and Vernier motion detector. Give the cart a brief push and release it. Collect a velocity-time graph that includes both the push and a clean post-push interval.

Choose a post-push interval and draw a force model for the cart. Do not draw a force merely because the cart is moving.

Explain how the graph supports or challenges your force model.
