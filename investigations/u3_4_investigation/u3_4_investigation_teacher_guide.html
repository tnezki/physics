Physics - Unit 3 - Section 3.4 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Equal Forces, Different Motion

Hands-on interaction + system investigation - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups3-4
Core toolSpring scales + low-friction carts

Why This Investigation Fits Section 3.4

The investigation separates three ideas students commonly blend together: third-law forces are equal and opposite; they act on different objects; and different masses can have different accelerations under equal-magnitude interaction forces. The final system-boundary prompt directly targets internal versus external forces.

Materials Per Group

  • Two spring scales
  • Two low-friction carts
  • Added cart masses
  • Spring bumper/plunger or strong rubber band
  • Track or smooth surface with end stops

Before Class

  1. Check both spring scales for zero and similar calibration.
  2. Prepare one light cart and one clearly heavier cart.
  3. Test the cart-release method so no hand adds a noticeable extra push.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
0-6Coupled-scale testsStudents record simultaneous equal readings and opposite directions.
6-15Unequal-mass cart releaseObserve that the lighter cart changes motion more.
15-22Object-level force pairRequire A-on-B/B-on-A language.
22-30System boundary reasoningShift to both carts + spring as one system and classify internal/external forces.

Expected Evidence

Coupled scales should read approximately the same force. During the cart release, the interaction forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction; the lighter cart should show the larger acceleration/change in velocity. For the combined two-cart system, the cart-on-cart/spring-mediated interaction is internal.

Watch for: Students may say the forces cancel because they are equal and opposite. Ask: "On which object does each force act?" Cancellation in a net-force sum only makes sense after a system/object has been chosen.

Strong Responses

PromptWhat to Look For
Scale pairReadings are equal within instrument uncertainty; directions are opposite; forces act on different objects.
Different cart motionUses a = Fnet/m from Section 3.1 to explain why smaller mass gives greater acceleration for equal force.
System reasoningInternal interaction forces are inside the chosen two-cart system; external forces such as track friction can affect the whole system.

Common Problems

  • Scale readings differ: Check zero, pull along one line, and avoid rubbing the scale bodies.
  • Heavy cart barely moves: That is useful evidence; ensure the light cart is not so fast it reaches the end stop.
  • Students identify two forces on one cart as a third-law pair: Use object names explicitly: A on B pairs with B on A.

Safety

Use end stops. Secure added masses. Keep fingers clear of compressed springs/plungers and do not stretch rubber bands to failure.

Connection Forward
