Physics - Unit 5 - Section 2 - Investigation Teacher Guide

When Waves Meet and Stand

Hands-on rope/spring interference investigation - designed for 25–30 minutes

Time25–30 minutes
Groups4
Core toolLong rope or Slinky

Why This Investigation Fits Section 5.2

The notes introduce superposition, standing waves, and Doppler conceptually. This activity gives direct mechanical-wave evidence for superposition and standing-wave structure, while the Day 1 moving-source demo supplies the Doppler phenomenon.

Materials Per Group

  • Long rope or Slinky
  • Fixed support or stable partner hold
  • Tape markers
  • Clear floor space

Before Class

  1. Test a rope length and tension that reliably produces one- and two- or three-segment patterns.
  2. Mark a safe wave lane.
  3. Plan which end will absorb/hold pulses so students can clearly see overlap.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
4 minPredictionCommit to overlap shapes.
7 minPulse superpositionRun same-side and opposite-side pulse collisions.
9 minStanding wavesBuild at least two stable segment patterns and mark nodes/antinodes.
6–8 minAnalysisConnect repeated cancellation/reinforcement to the standing pattern and revisit Doppler.

Expected Evidence

Same-direction displacements add temporarily; opposite displacements reduce/cancel temporarily; pulses continue after overlap. Stable standing waves show nearly fixed nodes and maximum-motion antinodes. More segments on the same length correspond to shorter wavelength.

Sample Calculation / Quantitative Check

No new formula is needed. If desired, students may use earlier wavelength ideas qualitatively: more equal segments fitting on the same fixed length means a shorter wavelength.

Watch for: Students often say waves bounce off each other or disappear during cancellation. Have them track each pulse before, during, and after overlap.
