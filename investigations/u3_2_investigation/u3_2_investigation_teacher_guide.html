Physics - Unit 3 - Section 3.2 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Breakaway Force: Static vs. Sliding Friction

Vernier force-graph investigation - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups2-3
Core toolForce sensor + Graphical Analysis

Why This Investigation Fits Section 3.2

The activity directly targets static friction, sliding friction, weight, and net force. The force-time graph makes the static-friction adjustment and breakaway peak visible in a way a single spring-scale reading cannot.

Materials Per Group

  • Block with hook
  • Vernier force sensor
  • Device with Graphical Analysis
  • Rough test surface
  • Added mass
  • Balance or known mass values

Before Class

  1. Tape or clamp the test surface so it cannot move.
  2. Zero the force sensor and test a slow horizontal pull.
  3. Choose added mass that keeps the total pull within the sensor range.

Tool / Data Setup

Display force vs. time. The local maximum immediately before motion is the best estimate of maximum static friction. During a near-constant-speed slide, average the flatter region to estimate sliding friction.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
0-4Prediction and setupCheck that the predicted graph includes a rise before motion.
4-14Base-block trialsStudents identify peak static and sliding plateau/average.
14-21Added-mass trialReinforce mass versus weight while students compute W = mg.
21-30Net-force and evidence explanationsRequire students to distinguish friction force from applied force and net force.

Expected Evidence

The force rises while the block remains at rest, reaches a maximum near breakaway, and usually drops to a lower sliding level. Added mass commonly increases both the maximum static friction and sliding friction on the same surfaces. During near-constant-speed sliding, applied force and friction are approximately balanced.

Watch for: Students often say "friction always equals the applied force." It only adjusts to match the pull while static equilibrium is maintained (up to a maximum) and is approximately equal during constant-speed sliding.

Strong Responses

PromptWhat to Look For
Breakaway explanationStatic friction adjusts to oppose attempted sliding up to a maximum; sliding friction then acts during motion and is usually smaller.
Net-force reasoningSubtracts opposite horizontal forces and connects the sign/direction of net force to acceleration.
Mass/weight statementMass is measured in kg; weight is force W = mg in N and increases when mass increases near Earth.

Common Problems

  • No clear peak: Pull more slowly and horizontally; use a rougher surface if needed.
  • Sliding graph oscillates: Pull over a longer path and use an average over the most stable interval.
  • Surface moves: Tape it down before repeating.

Safety

Pull horizontally, keep fingers away from hooks and moving blocks, and ensure added masses are secure.

Connection Forward

Section 3.3 carries the same net-force thinking into falling motion, where air resistance replaces surface friction as the important resistive force.
