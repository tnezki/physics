Physics - Unit 3 - Section 3.2 - Investigation

Breakaway Force: Static vs. Sliding Friction

Use a force-time graph to see why a block can resist a push before sliding and why sliding can require less force.

Learning target: Students will explain how friction, mass, and weight affect real-world motion.
Research Question: How does the friction force change before and after a block begins sliding?

1. Predict the Force Graph

As you pull harder on a block that is still at rest, sketch what you expect the force reading to do. Then show what you expect immediately after the block starts sliding.

2. Set Up

Equipment: block with hook, Vernier force sensor, Graphical Analysis, rough surface strip, added mass, balance or labeled masses.
1
Place the block on the test surface. Attach the force sensor and zero it with no tension.
2
Pull horizontally and very slowly. Increase the pull until the block just begins to slide, then keep it sliding at nearly constant speed for about 2 seconds.
3
On the force-time graph, identify the highest force just before sliding and the lower, roughly steady force during sliding.

4. Explain the Breakaway

Why can the block remain at rest while the pull increases? Why does the required pull often decrease after sliding begins?

5. Connect Friction to Net Force

Choose one sliding moment when the applied pull is greater than the sliding friction. Use Fnet = Fapplied - Ff to explain the direction of acceleration.
