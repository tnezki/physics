How Far in Each Interval?

Free fall vs. air resistance • Two student-recorded videos • About 35–45 minutes

Research question: Does a falling object travel the same distance during every equal time interval? What changes when air resistance matters?

1. Record Two Short Videos 5–12 min

Materials: small dense ball, one open coffee filter, meter stick, iPad/camera, Vernier Video Analysis, clear drop area.
  1. Place a meter stick upright next to a safe 1.5–2.0 m drop. Film from the side. Keep the iPad still and the whole fall in view.
  2. Record the ball being released without a push. Start filming before release.
  3. Without moving the iPad, record an open coffee filter falling from about the same height. Do not throw it.

Safety: Stay on the floor. Never climb furniture. Keep people and devices out of the drop zone.

2. Video 1 — Ball 8–10 min

  1. Open Vernier Video Analysis and Import Video (ball). Set the scale using the meter stick.
  2. Place the origin at the release point. Point the positive vertical (Y) direction down.
  3. Choose Add. Mark the center of the ball at release and then about every 0.10 s. Read its Y position from Vernier.
  4. Record the distance fallen from the start. Subtract consecutive readings to fill the last column.

4. What Does the Filter Show?

1. As the filter falls, do the interval distances keep growing, become almost equal, or vary unpredictably? Describe the evidence.

2. Look back at the ball data. What is one clear difference between the two distance patterns?

What Do the Intervals Tell Us?

Use your actual measurements—not just what you expected to happen.

Reminder: In free fall, gravity is the only important force. Significant air resistance changes the pattern of motion. Falling objects can still speed up even when air resistance is present.

5. Use Your Evidence 8–12 min

1. Which 0.10 s interval had the greatest distance fallen for your ball? Give the interval and its distance.

2. What does the ball’s changing distance during equal time intervals tell you about its speed?

3. Based on your measurements, which object more closely shows free fall? Support your choice with evidence.

4. How could you tell that air resistance was affecting the other object? Point to a pattern in its data.

5. If a coffee filter begins falling at a nearly constant speed, what would happen to the distances during equal time intervals?

6. Does gravity stop acting when air resistance is significant? Explain why the two falling objects can move differently.
