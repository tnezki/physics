Physics - Unit 2 - Section 4 - Investigation

Full-period ball-drop investigation with video analysis

Use evidence from the activity to test and apply the motion model from Section 2.4.

Learning target: Describe free fall as motion under gravity alone and use simple free-fall relationships to determine speed or distance fallen.
Research Question: How well do real falling objects match the free-fall model g ≈ 10 m/s², and when does air resistance make the model less useful?

1. Make a Prediction

Before collecting evidence, write the result or pattern you expect and give one reason.

2. Set Up and Collect Evidence

Equipment: 2-3 compact balls of different masses, one sheet of paper per group, meter stick, phone/tablet camera or Vernier video capture, Vernier Graphical Analysis, safe drop area
1
Measure and mark a drop height of about 1.5-2.0 m. Keep feet and faces clear of the drop zone.
2
Record a compact ball falling from rest. Use video analysis to obtain position-time and velocity-time data.
3
Estimate the acceleration from a useful interval of the velocity-time data using a=Δv/t. Compare with g≈10 m/s².
4
Use d=1/2 gt² to predict the fall time for your measured height, or use the measured fall time to predict distance. Compare model and observation.
5
Repeat the qualitative drop with a different compact mass. Then compare flat paper with the same paper crumpled tightly to isolate the effect of air resistance.

4. Analyze the Evidence

1. How close was your measured acceleration to 10 m/s²? Identify at least one source of measurement uncertainty.

2. Did compact objects of different mass show meaningfully different free-fall acceleration?

3. Why did flat and crumpled paper behave differently even though their masses were the same?

4. State when the free-fall model is a good approximation and when it is not.

5. Final Model

Write a concise conclusion that answers the research question using specific evidence from your group’s data or observations.
