Physics - Unit 6 - Section 6.5 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Optical Systems on Trial

Four-station synthesis/design investigation - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups2-3
Core toolMirrors, light guide, lens, and glare station

Why This Investigation Fits Section 6.5

Section 6.5 is explicitly a synthesis/application section. Rather than adding new mathematics, this circuit asks students to use earlier Unit 6 models as design tools and to critique claims with evidence.

Materials Per Group

  • Station A: 2 plane mirrors, cardboard periscope tube/box, opaque barrier, target
  • Station B: plastic optical fiber or acrylic light pipe, bright LED source
  • Station C: converging lens + holder, illuminated object, white screen
  • Station D: bright lamp/flashlight, smooth reflective surface or glass, angle/viewing markers
  • Black paper/cloth for blocking stray light where useful

Before Class

  1. Pre-build station tubs and label A-D.
  2. At Station A, provide adjustable mirrors but do not give a finished ray diagram.
  3. At Station B, verify the chosen light guide works for a gentle bend.
  4. At Station C, pre-check object/lens distances that fit the table.
  5. At Station D, arrange source and reflective surface so glare changes clearly with viewing geometry.

Expected Evidence

Periscope success depends on mirror orientation and reflection geometry. A light guide carries most light to the far end by repeated internal reflection but may lose light at tight bends. A converging lens can form a real screen image by redirecting rays to meet. Glare varies with source/surface/viewing geometry, undermining absolute “all angles/all surfaces” claims.

Sample Reasoning / Calculation

No new formula is required. Students may invoke θi = θr for the periscope, critical-angle/TIR reasoning for the light guide, or the thin-lens model for the focused image if it strengthens their explanation.

Watch for: Students may name multiple phenomena without tracing a light path. Require them to say what happens to the ray at each component.

Strong Responses

PromptWhat to Look For
Device explanationNames the correct interaction and traces what light does from source to observer/screen.
Evidence useRefers to a specific station observation rather than only vocabulary.
Claim critiqueReplaces absolute wording with conditions/limitations supported by evidence.

Common Problems

  • Periscope image not visible: Check that both mirrors are near 45° and face each other; verify a continuous line of sight through both reflections.
  • Fiber appears dark: Block stray room light and couple the LED directly into the strand end.
  • Lens station will not focus: Increase object-lens distance and align lens center with object and screen.
  • Glare change is weak: Use a smoother surface and lower the room light.

Safety / Cleanup

Use LED sources at the fiber/glare stations. Secure mirrors and lenses in stands; handle glass carefully. Keep bright sources out of direct eye lines.

Connection Forward

This investigation closes Unit 6 by treating optics as a coherent toolkit: light can be transmitted, blocked, reflected, refracted, guided, and focused depending on material and geometry.
