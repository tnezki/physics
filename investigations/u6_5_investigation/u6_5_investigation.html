Physics - Unit 6 - Section 6.5 - Investigation

Optical Systems on Trial

Gather evidence from four optical systems, then defend which light-interaction model explains each one.

Learning target: Apply reflection, refraction, lenses, total internal reflection, and vision ideas to optical devices and evaluate real-world optical claims using evidence.
Research Question: How do successful optical devices control ray paths, and what evidence distinguishes a workable design from a misleading claim?

1. Initial Design Claim

Choose one statement to defend or challenge before testing: “Any arrangement of mirrors can make a periscope work,” “A fiber guides light even around bends,” “A camera/eye model focuses by changing where rays meet,” or “A reflective coating can eliminate all glare.”

2. Evidence Circuit

Stations: A - periscope mirrors; B - light pipe/fiber; C - lens + screen; D - glare/viewing angle.

Station A - See Around a Barrier

A
Use two plane mirrors and a cardboard tube/box to make a view path around an opaque barrier. Adjust mirror angles until the target is visible. Sketch the successful ray path and mark both reflections.

Station B - Guide Light Around a Bend

B
Shine an LED into a clear light pipe/fiber. Compare a gentle bend with a tighter bend. Record where light exits or leaks and connect the evidence to total internal reflection.

Station C - Focus a Real Image

C
Use a converging lens to focus an illuminated object on a screen. Move the object and refocus. Explain what the lens must do to the incoming rays.

Station D - Test a Glare Claim

D
View a bright lamp reflected from a smooth surface at several viewing angles. Keep the source fixed. Record when glare is strongest and decide whether “eliminates all glare from every angle” is a defensible claim for an ordinary coating.
