Physics - Unit 7 - Section 7.3 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Does Every Device Obey Ohm's Law the Same Way?

Quantitative Ohm’s Law comparison - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups2-3
Core toolV-I measurements + student graph

Why This Investigation Fits Section 7.3

The current notes require Ohm’s Law calculations, cause-and-effect predictions, and interpretation of voltage-current graphs. Comparing a fixed resistor with a filament bulb also reinforces the section statement that resistance can change with temperature.

Materials

  • Adjustable low-voltage DC source or approved battery settings
  • Fixed resistor with suitable power rating
  • Miniature incandescent bulb
  • Switch and wires
  • Two digital multimeters or approved voltage/current sensors

Before Class

  1. Choose safe source settings and resistor power rating.
  2. Pre-test current ranges.
  3. Provide graphing rulers if students are drawing in Notability by hand.

Expected Observations / Evidence

A fixed resistor should show nearly proportional V-I behavior. A filament lamp typically departs from a straight line as its filament heats and resistance changes.

Watch for: Do not describe every real device as “breaking Ohm’s Law.” The useful course claim is that the simple V=IR proportional model assumes approximately constant resistance.

Expected Graph

The fixed resistor should produce an approximately straight I-versus-V line through the origin. The incandescent bulb should curve because the filament heats and its resistance rises as current increases.

Sample Calculation

Example fixed-resistor point: V = 3.0 V and I = 0.030 A gives R = V/I = 100 Ω. A second point near 5.0 V and 0.050 A again gives about 100 Ω, supporting constant resistance.

Strong Responses

EvidenceInterpretation
Resistor R values roughly constantVoltage and current remain proportional.
Bulb graph curvesResistance is changing, plausibly because the filament temperature changes.

Safety / Common Problems

  • Bulb gets hot: Do not touch the glass immediately after high settings.
  • Resistor overheats: Use a power-rated resistor and stay within teacher-approved voltage/current limits.
  • Noisy low-current data: Use a resistor value and meter range that produce measurable current without overheating.
