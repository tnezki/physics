Physics - Unit 5 - Section 5 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Tune by Beats

Quantitative tuning-fork beat investigation - designed for 20–25 minutes

Time20–25 minutes
Groups3–4
Core toolClose-frequency tuning forks + timing

Why This Investigation Fits Section 5.5

After 5.5 notes, students can use beat frequency quantitatively. The Day 1 demo creates the mystery; Day 2 turns the same phenomenon into a measurement tool and distinguishes beats from simple cancellation.

Materials Per Group

  • Three or more close-frequency tuning forks
  • Rubber strikers
  • Stopwatch
  • Quiet listening space

Before Class

  1. Select fork pairs with beat rates roughly 1–8 Hz so students can count them.
  2. Check labels and audibility.
  3. Create station spacing so groups do not overwhelm one another acoustically.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
3 minPrediction + formula checkCompare small versus large frequency differences.
12–14 minThree fork pairsPredict, listen, count 10 s, repeat, calculate observed beat rate.
4 minCompare model to evidenceCheck observed rates against |f1 − f2|.
4–5 minCER + cancellation connectionExplain tuning and distinguish beats from phase cancellation.

Expected Evidence

Observed beat frequency should be close to the absolute frequency difference. Small differences produce slow beats; larger differences produce faster beats. Human counting and fork tolerances create modest error.

Sample Calculation / Quantitative Check

Example: 256 Hz and 260 Hz give fbeat = |256 − 260| = 4 Hz, so students should hear about four loud-soft cycles per second (about 40 in 10 s).

Watch for: Use rubber strikers only. Do not strike forks on desks. Keep forks away from faces and ears. Acoustic interference from neighboring groups can make beat counting difficult; stagger trials if necessary.
