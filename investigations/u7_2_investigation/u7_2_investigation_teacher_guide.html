What Flows? What Changes?

Hands-on circuit measurement lab - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups2-3
Core toolLow-voltage circuit + multimeter

Why This Investigation Fits Section 7.2

This activity directly tests the Section 7.2 distinction between current as charge-flow rate and voltage as energy per charge. Measurements at two current locations confront the “current is used up” misconception without requiring resistance calculations from Section 7.3.

Materials

  • Low-voltage DC battery pack
  • Miniature bulb or approved resistor load
  • Switch
  • Connecting wires
  • 1-2 digital multimeters

Before Class

  1. Pre-check all meter fuses.
  2. Mark the current and voltage input jacks clearly.
  3. Build one reference circuit for troubleshooting.

Expected Observations / Evidence

In a simple one-loop circuit, current measurements before and after the load should be nearly equal. The voltage across the operating load should account for most of the source potential difference in this simple setup.

Watch for: Meter placement is the major safety/content issue: ammeter in series, voltmeter in parallel. Do not introduce Ohm’s Law calculations yet.

Sample Calculation

If I = 0.18 A for 10.0 s, then Q = It = (0.18 C/s)(10.0 s) = 1.8 C. If the source is 1.5 V, interpret that as about 1.5 J of energy per coulomb of charge.

Strong Responses

PromptWhat to Look For
Current comparisonCurrent is approximately the same at both locations in a single series loop.
Voltage meaningVoltage is energy transferred per charge between two points, not a substance moving through the wire.
Misconception correctionCharges circulate; the source transfers energy to them and the load converts electrical energy.

Common Problems

  • Blown meter fuse: Usually caused by placing an ammeter across the battery. Re-teach series placement before replacing the fuse.
  • Negative readings: Swap meter leads or accept the sign as direction information.

Safety

Use only low-voltage classroom DC sources. Keep the circuit open while changing connections. Never short the battery through an ammeter.
