Physics - Unit 4 - Section 3 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Same Work, Different Power

Hands-on lifting investigation - designed for 20-25 minutes

Time20-25 minutes
Groups2-3 students
Core toolSpring scale + stopwatch

Why This Investigation Fits Section 4.3

Section 4.3 distinguishes work from power and introduces gravitational potential energy. Lifting the same mass through the same height slowly and quickly isolates the core idea: similar work/energy transfer, different rate.

Materials Per Group

  • Known 1-3 kg mass with secure handle
  • Spring scale with appropriate range
  • Meter stick
  • Stopwatch
  • Stable lifting area

Before Class

  1. Check that spring scales can safely support the selected mass.
  2. Mark a consistent lifting distance of about 0.40-0.70 m.
  3. Keep lifts below shoulder height and away from feet.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
0-3PredictionStudents distinguish work from power before measuring.
3-12Slow and fast liftsRecord force, distance, and time with approximately constant-speed motion.
12-18Calculate W, P, and PECompare W=Fd and PE=mgh within experimental limits.
18-24InterpretExplain why speed of completing the task changes power more than work.

Expected Evidence

Slow and fast lifts should produce similar work values because force and displacement are similar, while the faster lift has greater power. Measured work and gained PE should be of the same order.

Watch for: A jerky lift makes the spring-scale force vary because the mass accelerates. Ask students to use a steady lift and treat small differences as measurement uncertainty.

Sample Calculation Pattern

If a steady scale reading is 20 N through 0.50 m, then W = Fd = 10 J. If the lift takes 2.0 s, P = W/t = 5 W. A 2.0 kg mass raised 0.50 m gains PE = mgh ≈ 2.0×10×0.50 = 10 J.
