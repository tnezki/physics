Physics - Unit 4 - Section 3 - Investigation

Same Work, Different Power

Measure a real energy transfer and separate the ideas of work and power.

Learning target: Explain work as energy transfer and calculate simple work, power, and gravitational potential energy.
Research Question: How can two lifts transfer similar energy but have different power?

1. Make a Prediction

You will lift the same mass through the same vertical distance slowly and quickly. Predict which trial will involve more work and which will involve more power.

2. Set Up

Equipment: known mass, spring scale, meter stick, stopwatch, stable table/stand.
1
Attach the mass to the spring scale. Choose a vertical lifting distance between 0.40 m and 0.70 m and mark the start and finish heights.
2
Lift at roughly constant speed and record the spring-scale force, distance, and time. Repeat once slowly and once quickly.

4. Connect Work to Energy

Calculate the gravitational potential energy gained using PE=mgh with g≈10 m/s². Compare that value with the work you measured during the lift.

Why can the slow and fast lifts have similar work but different power?

5. Apply the Model

A machine and a person raise the same load to the same height. The machine finishes in half the time. Explain what should be similar and what should be different about work, energy transfer, and power.

Use approximately constant-speed lifts so the spring-scale reading is reasonably steady. Do not jerk the mass or lift above shoulder height.
