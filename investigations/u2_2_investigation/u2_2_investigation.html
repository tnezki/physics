1. Set Up the Ramp

Materials: toy car, four-foot board, tape, stable support, two iPads (stopwatch + slow-motion camera), and a soft car stop.
  1. Raise one end of the board slightly and secure it. Put a soft stop at the bottom.
  2. Mark 0, 1, 2, 3, and 4 ft down the board. The 0-ft mark is at the raised end.
  3. Keep the ramp height and release position the same for every run.

2. Film with Two iPads

  1. iPad 1 — timer: Open the Clock Stopwatch. Stand it beside the ramp, facing the camera.
  2. iPad 2 — camera: Set it to Slo-mo. Frame the whole ramp, all five marks, and the stopwatch screen. Hold it still.
  3. Start the stopwatch and video before releasing the car. Let the car roll from 0 to 4 ft without pushing.
  4. Replay slowly. Pause whenever the front of the car reaches a mark. Read the time showing on the filmed stopwatch.
  5. Record that stopwatch reading at each mark, including 0 ft. Make one complete new run if the video is unclear.
Timing tip: Read the stopwatch in the video, not the video's playback timer. The stopwatch may already show a few seconds at 0 ft. Do not change it to 0.00 s.

4. Calculate Average Speeds

Interval time = later stopwatch reading − earlier stopwatch reading
Average speed = distance traveled ÷ interval time

Example: time for 1–2 ft = stopwatch at 2 ft − stopwatch at 1 ft. Speeds are in ft/s.

5. Look for the Pattern

A. Compare the four one-foot average speeds. Where was the car slowest? Where was it fastest?

B. Is the 0–4 ft average speed the same as the 3–4 ft average speed? Use your numbers.

C. Which gives a better estimate of the car's speed near the bottom: 0–4 ft or 3–4 ft? Why?

D. The car keeps moving down the ramp. Is its velocity constant? Use speed and direction to explain.

Final conclusion: Does one average speed describe the car's motion equally well at every point on the ramp? Support your answer with two measurements.
