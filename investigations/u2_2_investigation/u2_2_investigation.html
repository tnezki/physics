Physics - Unit 2 - Section 2 - Investigation

Hands-on walking data investigation

Use evidence from the activity to test and apply the motion model from Section 2.2.

Learning target: Distinguish speed from velocity and use distance, time, and direction to describe motion.
Research Question: How can two trips have similar speeds but different velocities?

1. Make a Prediction

Before collecting evidence, write the result or pattern you expect and give one reason.

2. Set Up and Collect Evidence

Equipment: meter sticks or tape measure, stopwatch, rubber bands or floor markers, masking tape
1
Mark a straight 8-10 m path. Place rubber-band or tape markers every 1 m.
2
Trial A: walk from start to finish at a steady pace. Record total distance and total time.
3
Trial B: travel the same total distance but include a turn so your final direction differs from Trial A. Record distance and time.
4
Trial C: complete an out-and-back trip that ends at the start. Record total distance and total time.
5
Calculate average speed for each trial using v=d/t. Then write a velocity description for the motion when a single direction is meaningful.
