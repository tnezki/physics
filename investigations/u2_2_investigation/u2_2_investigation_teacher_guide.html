Physics - Unit 2 - Section 2 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Hands-on walking data investigation

Hands-on walking data investigation - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups3-4
Core toolMeasured walking course + markers

Why This Investigation Fits Section 2.2

Distinguish speed from velocity and use distance, time, and direction to describe motion. The activity comes after the notes, so students use the section model to interpret evidence rather than discovering later-unit content prematurely.

Materials Per Group

  • meter sticks or tape measure, stopwatch, rubber bands or floor markers, masking tape

Before Class

  1. Measure the course and place visible markers. Check stopwatches.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
0-5Prediction and setupCheck that the research question and variable choices are understood.
5-18Collect evidencePrioritize clean, interpretable evidence over many trials.
18-25Analyze / comparePress students to cite data, graph features, or observations.
25-30Conclude / modelRequire a conclusion that directly answers the question.

Expected Evidence

Average speed depends on total distance and total time. Velocity descriptions require direction; changing direction changes velocity. An out-and-back path has nonzero average speed because distance traveled is nonzero.

Watch for: Students may confuse total distance with a directional velocity description. Ask them to state direction explicitly rather than introducing displacement formulas.

Strong Responses

FeatureWhat to Look For
Claim / conclusionDirectly answers the research question rather than restating procedure.
EvidenceUses specific measured values, graph trends, or observed relationships.
ReasoningUses only the Section 2.2 physics model and connects evidence to the claim.

Common Problems

  • Messy data: Repeat one clean trial instead of averaging several poor runs.
  • Direction confusion: Have students state the chosen positive direction or reference frame before interpreting signs or motion.
  • Overreach: If students introduce later concepts, redirect them to the representations and formulas currently established in Unit 2.

Safety / Cleanup

Keep walking and drop zones clear, use non-breakable equipment, and reset sensors/carts/materials for the next class. For the free-fall investigation, do not allow students to climb on desks or chairs.

Connection Forward
