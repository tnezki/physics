Does Motion Need a Continuing Force?

Vernier motion investigation · designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups2-3 students
Core toolMotion detector + Graphical Analysis

Why This Investigation Fits Section 1.2

The current section asks students to use Newton's First Law and inertia to decide when motion stays the same or changes, while distinguishing “no forces” from “no net force.” The investigation makes the common misconception visible: the push ends, yet the cart continues moving. A rough-surface comparison then supplies a clear case in which an additional horizontal interaction makes the motion change.

Materials Per Group

  • Dynamics cart
  • Level track or smooth tabletop
  • Vernier motion detector and device running Graphical Analysis
  • Short strip of felt, towel, or other rough material that visibly increases rolling resistance
  • Student investigation in Notability

Before Class

  1. Check the track/table for a region where a released cart can roll for several seconds without leaving the measurement range.
  2. Confirm the motion detector reads the cart cleanly. Use a flat target on the cart if needed.
  3. Choose a rough-surface strip that changes the velocity clearly but does not stop the cart immediately.
  4. Set Graphical Analysis to show velocity vs time. Students do not need acceleration in this section.

Expected Evidence

On a reasonably smooth surface the velocity after release should remain approximately steady for a useful interval, though real rolling resistance and track imperfections may make it drift. On the rougher surface the velocity should change more noticeably after release. The important evidence is not a perfectly horizontal graph; it is that the cart does not require the hand's continued forward push to keep moving.

Do not call inertia a force. Inertia is the tendency of matter to resist a change in motion. Any force arrow must be tied to an actual interaction.

Force-Model Expectations

  • Vertical: weight downward and support force upward.
  • Horizontal on the smooth track after release: no hand force; any rolling resistance is relatively small, so net horizontal force can be near zero.
  • Horizontal on the rougher surface: resistive/frictional interaction opposite the motion gives a nonzero net force and changes the velocity.

Strong Responses

PromptWhat to Look For
No force vs no net forceStudents state that forces can act and still cancel; zero net force does not mean zero individual forces.
Newton's First LawRest or constant straight-line motion persists when net force is zero.
InertiaA property of matter; not a hidden forward push.

Common Problems

  • Detector noise: ignore the first/last noisy region and analyze the clean middle interval.
  • Cart coasts downhill: level the surface before class.
  • Students say “momentum keeps it moving”: save momentum language for Unit 4; return to Newton's First Law and inertia here.
  • Rough strip stops cart too fast: choose a milder surface or reduce its length.

Connection Forward

Section 1.3 names forces as vectors and asks students to combine them. End by asking: “If several forces act at once, how can a diagram tell us whether their combined effect is zero?”
