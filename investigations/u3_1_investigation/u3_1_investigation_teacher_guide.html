Physics - Unit 3 - Section 3.1 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Force, Mass, and Acceleration: Test the Second Law

Vernier cart investigation - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups3-4
Core toolMotion detector + force sensor

Why This Investigation Fits Section 3.1

Students have already studied acceleration in Unit 2 and have now learned Fnet = ma. The investigation makes the direct force-acceleration pattern and inverse mass-acceleration pattern visible in real data before friction is treated explicitly in Section 3.2.

Materials Per Group

  • Low-friction cart and level track
  • Vernier motion detector
  • Vernier force sensor
  • String
  • Added cart masses
  • Device running Vernier Graphical Analysis

Before Class

  1. Level tracks and check that carts roll freely.
  2. Secure added masses so they cannot fall.
  3. Test one pull to identify a clean force interval and a linear velocity interval.

Tool / Data Setup

In Graphical Analysis, display force vs. time and velocity vs. time. Zero the force sensor before data collection. Use a linear fit on the clean velocity segment; its slope is the measured acceleration. Average the force over that same time interval.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
0-4Prediction and setupConfirm students know that acceleration is the slope of the velocity-time graph.
4-14Three force-change runsCoach students to pull steadily rather than jerk the cart.
14-22Two mass-change runsKeep the target force similar while changing total mass.
22-30Compare F/m to measured acceleration and write claimPress for evidence and direction, not just "more force = more acceleration."

Expected Evidence

For fixed mass, larger average horizontal force should produce larger acceleration, approximately in direct proportion. For similar force, larger total mass should produce smaller acceleration. Measured acceleration should be reasonably close to F/m, with differences caused by friction, pull variation, sensor alignment, and graph selection.

Watch for: The measured pull is not literally the full net force if friction is non-negligible. On a good low-friction track the approximation is useful, but do not use poor agreement to imply Newton's Second Law fails.

Strong Responses

PromptWhat to Look For
Force patternLarger force at the same mass gives larger acceleration; students cite specific runs.
Mass patternLarger mass at similar force gives smaller acceleration.
Final claimUses Fnet = ma or a = Fnet/m, cites data, and states acceleration direction follows net-force direction.

Common Problems

  • Noisy velocity graph: Use the smooth middle portion of the run and avoid launch/stop intervals.
  • Force varies too much: Slow the pull and use a longer steady interval; use the average force for that interval.
  • Cart hits detector/end stop: Shorten the run and establish start/stop marks.

Safety

Keep hands and loose clothing clear of moving carts. Use end stops, keep added masses secure, and do not pull toward the edge of a table.

Connection Forward

Section 3.2 complicates the model by adding friction, support force, weight, and the distinction between applied force and net force.
