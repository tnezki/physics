Physics - Unit 8 - Section 8.4 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Move, Hold, Reverse: Induction

Hands-on induction investigation - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups2-3
Core toolCoil + galvanometer

Why This Investigation Fits Section 8.4

This setup directly reproduces the evidence sequence in the 8.4 notes: move, hold, reverse, and compare slow versus fast motion. It also supports the required AC graph interpretation without adding later electromagnetic mathematics.

Materials

  • Bar magnet
  • Multi-turn coil
  • Center-zero galvanometer or sensitive voltmeter
  • Connecting leads
  • Stopwatch

Before Class

  1. Check polarity so opposite motions visibly produce opposite deflections.
  2. Choose a coil/magnet combination that gives a clear but safe signal.
  3. If using a digital meter, verify it responds quickly enough.

Expected Observations / Evidence

Meter deflection occurs during change and reverses with opposite change. Faster motion creates a larger induced response. Repeated reversals support an alternating-voltage model.

Watch for: Neodymium magnets can pinch and affect electronics. Do not connect the coil to powered mains equipment. Treat the meter as a sensor, not a load.

Expected Evidence Pattern

  • A stationary magnet relative to the coil gives little or no sustained induced response.
  • Moving into versus out of the coil produces opposite meter deflections.
  • Reversing the magnet pole also reverses the response for the same motion.
  • Faster motion usually gives a larger peak deflection because magnetic conditions change faster.

Strong Responses

The required Faraday relationship is conceptual: induced voltage increases when the rate of magnetic-field change increases. Students should not invent or use a flux/calculus formula not present in Unit 8.

Common Problems

  • Digital meter misses quick pulses: Use a center-zero analog galvanometer or a meter with a fast bar display if available.
  • Small signal: Use more coil turns, a stronger magnet, or faster controlled motion.

Connection Forward
