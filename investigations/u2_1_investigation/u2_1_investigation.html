Physics - Unit 2 - Section 1 - Investigation

Hands-on reference-frame investigation

Use evidence from the activity to test and apply the motion model from Section 2.1.

Learning target: Explain that motion is relative and describe an object’s motion using an appropriate frame of reference.
Research Question: How does changing the reference frame change the way the same motion is described?

1. Make a Prediction

Before collecting evidence, write the result or pattern you expect and give one reason.

2. Set Up and Collect Evidence

Equipment: meter sticks or floor tape, 6-8 position markers, masking tape, one movable chair or rolling stool, stopwatch (optional)
1
Create a straight floor line with numbered position markers. Choose the floor as Reference Frame A.
2
One student walks steadily from marker 0 to marker 5 while a floor observer records beginning and ending positions.
3
Repeat while a second observer walks alongside the walker at approximately the same speed. Treat that observer as Reference Frame B.
4
Repeat once more with the observer walking in the opposite direction. Describe the walker from both frames without changing what the walker actually does.

4. Analyze the Evidence

1. For each trial, state whether the walker was moving or at rest relative to each frame.

2. Explain why two different motion statements can both be correct.

3. Why is Earth’s surface a convenient reference frame for most classroom motion descriptions?

5. Final Model

Write a concise conclusion that answers the research question using specific evidence from your group’s data or observations.
