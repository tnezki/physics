Who’s Really Moving?

Reference Frames Investigation • Groups of 3 • About 20 minutes

Name: Group: ________
Learning targets
  • I can explain why an object can be moving relative to one reference frame but not another.
  • I can identify the reference frame used in a motion description.
  • I can compare motion from two different points of view and explain why everyday motion is usually described relative to Earth’s surface.
Key idea: A reference frame is the point of view—and the objects treated as “not moving”—used to describe motion. Every motion description uses one, even when it is not stated.

Roles and Materials

Part C + Analysis

What About Earth?

Reference frames are useful choices—not a statement that one frame is “truly” at rest.

Earth is moving too. Near 44° N latitude, the ground moves east at roughly 1,200 km/h (about 750 mi/h) because Earth rotates. Earth also orbits the Sun at about 30 km/s.

1. Shared motion

You, the cart, the ball, the air, and the floor all share Earth’s motion. How is this similar to the rider and ball sharing the cart’s motion in Part B?

2. Why use Earth’s surface?

Why does a car speedometer, a speed-limit sign, or a weather report of wind speed usually use Earth’s surface as the reference frame?

Analysis • about 4 minutes

Name the Frame

For each statement, write the reference frame being used.

Motion statementReference frame
“The ball went straight up and came straight back down.”
“The ball moved in an arc and traveled about 1 m forward.”
“The rider moved 6 m in 5 seconds.”
“The rider stayed right next to the pusher the whole time.”
“The car is going 55 mi/h.”

3. Two correct paths

Both observers were right about the ball in Part B. Explain how two different paths can both be correct.

4. Why didn’t the ball fall behind?

The cart kept moving while the ball was in the air. Explain why the ball still returned to the rider’s hands.

Synthesis

Claim, Evidence, Reasoning

Use evidence from both parts of the investigation.

Question: Can an object be both moving and not moving at the same time?

Claim

Answer the question clearly.

Evidence

Use at least one measurement from Part A and one observation from Part B.

Reasoning

Explain why your evidence supports the claim. Use the words reference frame and relative to.

Exit question: Why do we usually describe everyday motion relative to Earth’s surface instead of relative to the Sun?
Final check: A complete motion statement should make clear what object is moving and relative to what reference frame.
