Physics - Unit 2 - Section 1 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Hands-on reference-frame investigation

Hands-on reference-frame investigation - designed for 25 minutes

Time25 minutes
Groups3-4
Core toolFloor number line + moving observer

Why This Investigation Fits Section 2.1

Explain that motion is relative and describe an object’s motion using an appropriate frame of reference. The activity comes after the notes, so students use the section model to interpret evidence rather than discovering later-unit content prematurely.

Materials Per Group

  • meter sticks or floor tape, 6-8 position markers, masking tape, one movable chair or rolling stool, stopwatch (optional)

Before Class

  1. Have the floor line taped before class. Keep the walker’s path identical while changing the observer/reference frame.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
0-5Prediction and setupCheck that the research question and variable choices are understood.
5-18Collect evidencePrioritize clean, interpretable evidence over many trials.
18-25Analyze / comparePress students to cite data, graph features, or observations.
25-30Conclude / modelRequire a conclusion that directly answers the question.

Expected Evidence

Relative to the floor, the walker changes position. Relative to an observer moving alongside at nearly the same velocity, the walker may change position very little. Opposite-moving frames make the relative change appear larger.

Watch for: Do not turn this into a speed-calculation lab; Section 2.1 is about motion relative to a frame.

Strong Responses

FeatureWhat to Look For
Claim / conclusionDirectly answers the research question rather than restating procedure.
EvidenceUses specific measured values, graph trends, or observed relationships.
ReasoningUses only the Section 2.1 physics model and connects evidence to the claim.

Common Problems

  • Messy data: Repeat one clean trial instead of averaging several poor runs.
  • Direction confusion: Have students state the chosen positive direction or reference frame before interpreting signs or motion.
  • Overreach: If students introduce later concepts, redirect them to the representations and formulas currently established in Unit 2.

Safety / Cleanup

Keep walking and drop zones clear, use non-breakable equipment, and reset sensors/carts/materials for the next class. For the free-fall investigation, do not allow students to climb on desks or chairs.

Connection Forward

Section 2.2 will add speed and velocity language to motion descriptions.
