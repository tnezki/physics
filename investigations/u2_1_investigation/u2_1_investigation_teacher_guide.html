Physics - Unit 2 - Section 1 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Who’s Really Moving?

Reference Frames Investigation • designed for about 20 minutes • groups of 3

Time20 minutes
Groups3
Core toolRolling cart/chair + ball + two phone videos

Learning Targets

  • I can explain why an object can be moving relative to one reference frame but not another.
  • I can identify the reference frame used in a motion description.
  • I can compare motion from two different points of view and explain why everyday motion is usually described relative to Earth’s surface.

Why This Investigation Fits Section 2.1

The activity gives students two simultaneous descriptions of the same physical event. The rider/cart frame makes the tossed ball appear to move mostly straight up and down; the floor frame shows the ball continuing forward while it rises and falls. Part A first establishes the simpler idea that the same object can have zero displacement in one frame and nonzero displacement in another.

Materials Per Group

  • rolling office chair, hallway cart, or lab cart
  • tennis ball or other soft ball
  • two phones: one rider view and one stationary floor view
  • masking tape, meter stick, student handout, pencil

Before Class

  1. Clear a straight lane about 6 m long.
  2. Lay a tape line along the floor with marks every 1 m, labeled 0 through 6.
  3. Mark a floor-observer spot about 2 m to the side of the 3 m mark.
  4. Test one cart/chair and ball setup. Choose a soft ball and a cart that can move smoothly at walking speed.

Roles

RoleJobReference frame
RiderSits on cart, holds/tosses ball, records rider-view videoMoves with cart
PusherPushes at a steady walking speedMoves with cart (approximately)
Floor observerTimes Part A and records stationary floor videoFloor / Earth’s surface

Suggested Flow

MinutesSegmentTeacher Focus
0–2Roles, safety, key ideaRequire students to name the frame before saying “moving” or “at rest.”
2–7Part AConfirm 0 m relative displacement in cart frame and about 6 m in floor frame.
7–15Part BPrioritize one clean, steady-speed toss and two useful videos.

18–20Analysis + exitCER may be finished as homework if needed.

Safety

Walking speed only. Rider remains seated with feet up. No tosses higher than about 1 m. Keep the lane and observer area clear. Use a soft ball only.

Teacher Tips

  • The cart must move as steadily as possible. If it speeds up or slows down during the toss, the ball may land ahead of or behind the rider. That is useful later for accelerating-frame discussion, but rerun the trial for this investigation.
  • The floor video works best if the observer holds the phone still and lets the cart cross the frame.
  • The rider should brace the phone against their chest or torso so the camera shares the cart frame as closely as possible.
  • No carts available? A student walking steadily while tossing the ball can substitute, although the visual contrast is less dramatic.
  • Do not frame one observer as “correct.” Both descriptions are valid within their stated reference frames.

Expected Evidence / Answer Key Highlights

ItemExpected idea
Part ABall displacement is about 0 m relative to the cart and about 6 m relative to the floor. Floor-frame speed is typically around 1–1.5 m/s. Rider is approximately at rest relative to the pusher but moving relative to the floor.
Part B: rider viewBall appears to move mostly straight up and down and returns to the rider’s hands.
Part B: floor viewBall follows a forward-moving, curved/parabolic-looking path while rising and falling.
Why ball returnsWhen released, the ball already shares the cart’s forward motion. With little horizontal net force while airborne, it continues that forward motion while also moving vertically.
Name the framerider/cart; floor/Earth; floor/Earth; rider or pusher/cart; road/Earth’s surface.
Part C / exitEverything nearby shares Earth’s large-scale motion. Earth’s surface is the useful everyday default because roads, buildings, classrooms, and other familiar landmarks are fixed relative to it and measurements are ordinarily made against them.
CER claimYes—an object can be moving relative to one frame and at rest relative to another. The words “moving” and “at rest” are incomplete without a reference frame.

Common Misconceptions to Listen For

  • “The floor observer is right and the rider is wrong.” Both descriptions can be correct within different frames.
  • “The ball should fall behind the rider.” This usually reflects the misconception that continued motion requires a continued forward push.
  • “The ground is truly at rest.” The ground is at rest relative to the Earth-surface frame; Earth itself is rotating and orbiting.
  • “Reference frame means where the observer is standing.” More precisely, it is the coordinate/point-of-view system treated as fixed for the description. The observer is useful because they usually share that frame.
