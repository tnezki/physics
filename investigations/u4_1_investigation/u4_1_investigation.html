Physics - Unit 4 - Section 1 - Investigation

Same Stop, Different Force

Use force-time evidence to connect contact time, impulse, and force.

Learning target: Explain impulse as force acting over time, connect impulse to change in momentum, and explain why increasing contact time can reduce collision force.
Research Question: How can changing stopping time change the force when the cart undergoes a similar momentum change?

1. Make a Prediction

A cart will hit the same force sensor twice: once against a hard stop and once with a foam pad. The cart starts from as nearly the same motion as possible. Predict how contact time and peak force will compare.

2. Set Up the Test

Equipment: dynamics cart, level track, Vernier force sensor, Graphical Analysis, rigid bumper, foam pad, release marker.
1
Mount the force sensor securely at the end of the track. Zero the sensor before collecting data.
2
Mark one release point. Use the same cart and release method for every trial so the incoming momentum is as similar as possible.
3
Collect a force-time graph for a rigid collision. Repeat with the foam pad between cart and sensor.

3. Collect Evidence

Use the graph to estimate the duration of the force pulse and record the largest force shown. Do not calculate impulse from graph area; use the graph as evidence about time and force.

4. Analyze the Evidence

For the same cart brought from similar incoming motion to rest, the momentum change is similar. Explain how your contact-time evidence helps explain the difference in force.

Which trial better models an airbag, gym mat, crumple zone, or bent-knee landing? Explain.

5. Defend a Claim

Claim: Increasing collision time can reduce the force needed for the same overall momentum change. Use at least two pieces of evidence from your graphs.

Keep the cart, release point, and incoming direction as consistent as possible. The comparison is strongest when the incoming motion is similar.
