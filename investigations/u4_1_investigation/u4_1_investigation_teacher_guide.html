Physics - Unit 4 - Section 1 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Same Stop, Different Force

Vernier force-time investigation - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups2-3 students
Core toolVernier force sensor + Graphical Analysis

Why This Investigation Fits Section 4.1

This investigation follows Section 4.1 notes and gives students direct evidence for the contact-time argument behind airbags, crumple zones, padding, and bent-knee landings. It uses force-time pulse shape qualitatively and avoids requiring graph-area integration.

Materials Per Group

  • Dynamics cart and level track
  • Vernier force sensor connected to Graphical Analysis
  • Rigid bumper/contact surface
  • Foam pad
  • Tape for a consistent release point

Before Class

  1. Secure the force sensor so it cannot slide during impact.
  2. Test a moderate cart speed that stays within the sensor range.
  3. Set a release marker and prepare equal-condition rigid/foam trials.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
0-4Prediction + setupRequire a force/time prediction before graph collection.
4-14Rigid and foam trialsKeep cart and incoming motion as consistent as practical; repeat poor trials.
14-22Sketch/compare pulsesFocus on longer contact time and lower peak force, not graph-area calculus.
22-28Impulse explanation + applicationConnect similar momentum change to different force-time histories.

Expected Evidence

A foam stop should generally lengthen the force pulse and reduce the peak force compared with a rigid stop when incoming cart motion is similar. Exact peak values will vary with padding and sensor mounting.

Watch for: If the cart rebounds strongly in one condition but not the other, the momentum changes are no longer as similar. Reduce speed or adjust the stop so the comparison is mainly stopping rather than rebound.

Common Problems

  • Sensor clips at maximum: reduce cart speed.
  • Noisy pulse: check mounting and use a cleaner release.
  • Large rebound: adjust bumper/padding so both trials end closer to rest.

Strong Responses

PromptWhat to Look For
Why can foam reduce force?Similar momentum change spread over a longer interaction time; graph evidence cites longer pulse and smaller peak.
Safety applicationExplicitly connects padding/crumple zone/bent knees to increased stopping time rather than “removing momentum.”
