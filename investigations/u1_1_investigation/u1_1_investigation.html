2. Collect Data

Materials: bouncy ball · meter stick · iPad in slow motion · hard floor

Keep it fair: same ball, same floor, no downward push, meter stick beside the bounce.

Procedure

  1. Stand the meter stick vertically beside the bounce.
  2. Record in slow motion with the ball and meter stick visible.
  3. Drop from the listed height. Release without pushing.
  4. Replay and pause at the highest point after the first bounce.
  5. Read the rebound height at the center of the ball.

Use the Evidence

5. Claim - Evidence - Reasoning

Claim: State the relationship that is best supported by your data. Name the type of relationship.
Evidence: Give at least two specific pieces of evidence from your table, graph, or Desmos model.
Reasoning: Explain why your evidence supports your claim. Connect the pattern in the data to the formula.

6. Discussion

  1. Was your original claim supported? Explain.
  2. Which model best described your data? What made you choose it?
  3. What does your formula tell you about the relationship between drop height and rebound height?
  4. Why did your points probably not land perfectly on the model?
  5. What could make one group's results different from another group's?
  6. How could your formula help predict the rebound height for a drop height you did not test?
Before you finish: Your claim should match your evidence, and your reasoning should explain why the formula is a useful model of the measured relationship.
