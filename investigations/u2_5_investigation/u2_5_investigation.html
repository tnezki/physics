Physics - Unit 2 - Section 5 - Investigation

Vernier motion-model synthesis investigation

Use evidence from the activity to test and apply the motion model from Section 2.5.

Learning target: Use velocity vectors, motion diagrams, and simple graphs to model and explain the motion of objects.
Research Question: Can your group create several motion representations that all tell the same motion story?

1. Make a Prediction

Before collecting evidence, write the result or pattern you expect and give one reason.

2. Set Up and Collect Evidence

Equipment: Vernier motion detector, Graphical Analysis, meter stick or floor markers, open walking lane
1
Choose one motion story from the teacher: steady motion, speeding up, slowing down, moving away then returning, or another approved pattern.
2
Predict the position-time and velocity-time behavior before collecting data.
3
Perform the motion and collect detector data. Repeat once if needed for a readable trace.
4
Select four equal-time snapshots. Draw a motion diagram and velocity vectors that match those moments.
5
Trade with another group. The other group identifies one representation that would become inconsistent if a specific feature were changed.

3. Record Your Evidence

4. Analyze the Evidence

1. Explain how your graph, motion diagram, velocity vectors, and written story agree.

2. Identify one place where velocity changes and cite evidence from at least two representations.

3. Critique one plausible incorrect model and revise it so all representations agree.

5. Final Model

Write a concise conclusion that answers the research question using specific evidence from your group’s data or observations.

Use the physics ideas from the completed Section 2.5 notes. Do not add explanations from later sections unless your teacher specifically asks you to.
