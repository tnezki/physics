Engineer a Stronger Electromagnet

Electromagnet engineering test - designed for 30 minutes

Time30 minutes
Groups2-3
Core toolCoil + current-limited DC

Why This Investigation Fits Section 8.3

The assessment plan explicitly calls for electromagnet-strength comparisons and design tasks after 8.3. This investigation makes turns, current, and core material measurable while preserving the section formula limit for magnetic force.

Materials

  • Insulated magnet wire
  • Soft-iron nail/bolt
  • Air-core tube
  • Low-voltage current-limited DC supply
  • Switch
  • Ammeter or multimeter
  • Identical paper clips or steel washers
  • Optional spring scale/force sensor for a more quantitative strength metric

Before Class

  1. Prepare at least two coil-turn options or winding stations.
  2. Set a safe current ceiling and short on-time.
  3. Standardize the steel test objects.

Expected Observations / Evidence

Turn count, current, and an iron core should all influence measured strength. Exact clip counts vary, so trends and controls matter more than identical numbers.

Watch for: Coils can heat quickly. Use low voltage, current limiting, and brief switch closures. Do not let students exceed the teacher-set current limit.

Sample Calculation

For B = 0.30 T, I = 4.0 A, and L = 0.50 m: F = BIL = (0.30)(4.0)(0.50) = 0.60 N. Doubling current doubles the force to 1.20 N if B and L are unchanged and the wire remains perpendicular.

Expected Evidence

  • More turns generally strengthen the coil field when geometry and current are comparable.
  • Increasing current generally strengthens the field, within heating and supply limits.
  • An iron core usually strengthens the electromagnet because the coil field aligns magnetic domains in the core.
  • At high magnetization the iron-core contribution approaches saturation, so gains need not continue linearly forever.
