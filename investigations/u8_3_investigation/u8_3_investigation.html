Physics - Unit 8 - Section 8.3 - Investigation

Engineer a Stronger Electromagnet

Test turns, current, and core material, then connect the controllable field to force on current-carrying wire.

Learning target: Analyze electromagnet strength and apply the perpendicular magnetic-force model for current-carrying wires.
Research Question: Which design changes make an electromagnet stronger, and how does the same field-current interaction create force?

1. Engineering Question

Your team must build the strongest practical electromagnet using the available low-voltage supply. Predict which variable will matter most: number of turns, current, or iron core.

2. Establish a Fair Strength Test

Equipment: insulated magnet wire, iron nail/bolt, air-core tube, low-voltage current-limited source, switch, ammeter or multimeter, paper clips or identical steel washers.

Choose one response measure and use it consistently: maximum number of identical clips lifted, maximum washer mass lifted, or maximum separation at which one washer is attracted.

3. Test Design Variables

Change one variable at a time. Keep coil geometry, test object, distance, and on-time as consistent as possible.

4. Build an Evidence-Based Design Rule

Use your data to write three rules: one about turns, one about current, and one about an iron core. If your evidence is noisy, say what is supported strongly and what remains uncertain.

5. Magnetic Force Check

For a 0.50 m wire carrying 4.0 A perpendicular to a 0.30 T magnetic field, calculate the magnetic force using F = BIL. Then predict what happens to force magnitude if current doubles while B and L stay fixed.

6. Direction and Energy Check

A current-carrying wire in a magnetic field is pushed sideways. Explain why reversing current reverses the force direction. Then explain why this magnetic force can produce motion in a motor without implying that a magnetic field supplies unlimited energy.
