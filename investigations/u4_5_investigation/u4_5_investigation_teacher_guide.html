Physics - Unit 4 - Section 5 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Energy Decision Board

Evidence-centered comparison investigation - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups3-4 students
Core toolScenario evidence matrix

Why This Investigation Fits Section 4.5

Section 4.5 asks students to distinguish energy sources from carriers, compare options using multiple criteria, and critique “free energy” claims. This investigation uses a fictional scenario so students can practice evidence-based tradeoff reasoning without pretending one universal ranking exists.

Materials Per Group

  • Student investigation pages
  • Calculator optional
  • Highlighters or pens for evidence coding

Before Class

  1. Decide whether groups must choose one source or may propose a mixed strategy.
  2. Emphasize that the evidence table is a fictional classroom scenario, not a universal real-world ranking.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
0-5Source/carrier classificationCheck whether students trace energy origins rather than labeling every stored form a source.
5-15Evidence matrixGroups rate options using the stated scenario only.
15-23RecommendationRequire at least three criteria and one acknowledged drawback/tradeoff.
23-28Free-energy claim checkDemand complete energy accounting and reproducible measurements.

Expected Evidence

Different groups may make different defensible recommendations depending on how they weight criteria. Strong answers cite scenario evidence, state tradeoffs, and avoid claims that any option is always best.

Watch for: Do not reward a preferred energy technology independent of reasoning. Assess quality of evidence use, system thinking, and acknowledgment of tradeoffs.

Strong Responses

PromptWhat to Look For
Source vs carrierHydrogen/electricity described as carrying or transferring energy supplied from a source; sunlight/wind/geothermal/fuel treated with appropriate origin reasoning.
RecommendationUses availability, reliability, environmental impact, efficiency/useful output or infrastructure; explicitly names tradeoffs.
Free-energy critiqueRequests measured input/output energy, complete system boundary, repeatable tests, and accounts for thermal losses.

Common Problems

  • One criterion dominates: require at least three categories.
  • Universal claims: redirect students to “in this scenario.”
  • Hydrogen called a source automatically: ask what supplied the energy used to produce it.

Connection Forward

This closes Unit 4 by applying conservation and energy accounting to decisions beyond a single classroom mechanical system.
