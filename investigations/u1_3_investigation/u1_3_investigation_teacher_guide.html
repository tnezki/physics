Tip-to-Tail Resultant Lab

Six perpendicular unequal two-vector situations plus two three-vector challenges. Every vector endpoint is on a grid intersection and every student diagram uses 1 square = 1 N.

Time40-50 minutes
GroupsPairs
Check toolPhET Vector Addition

Purpose and Section Alignment

Learning Target: Students will represent forces as vectors and determine resultant/net force in simple force situations.

  • U1-MG03-IC01: use vector arrow direction and length to represent force direction and relative magnitude.
  • U1-MG03-IC03: determine direction and relative magnitude of the resultant/net force from a simple force-vector diagram.
Teacher goal: repeated practice with the tip-to-tail method. Students read each vector from a scaled grid, redraw the vectors tip-to-tail, draw the resultant, use the Pythagorean Theorem for magnitude, and use inverse tangent plus quadrant reasoning for direction. PhET is the check, not the starting point.
Scope note: The current 1.3 Notes include perpendicular vector geometry, the Pythagorean relationship, and direction reasoning. Keep the work concrete and graphical: read Δx/Δy from the grid, use the right-triangle relationship for R, then use inverse tangent for the resultant direction. Do not expand this into a formal component-trig lesson.

Materials and Launch

  • Printed investigation or iPad/Notability copy.
  • Pencil and straightedge.
  • One device per pair for https://phet.colorado.edu/sims/html/vector-addition/latest/vector-addition_en.html.

Launch with one board example showing only the mechanic of translating a vector without rotating or resizing it. Then draw the resultant from the original tail to the final head. Do not solve one of the printed situations.

Student Routine

  1. Count squares to read each vector's Δx and Δy. Only A/B/C labels appear on the student diagram.
  2. Translate A, then B (and C in the challenges) onto the blank grid tip-to-tail.
  3. Draw R from the original tail to the final head.
  4. Read ΔxR and ΔyR from the grid.
  5. Use the Pythagorean Theorem for magnitude.
  6. Use inverse tangent for a reference angle, then use the quadrant to report direction counterclockwise from +x.
  7. Check in PhET and record the input-vector magnitude/direction readouts and the resultant.

Teaching Notes by Situation

SituationsWhat to watch for
1-4Axis-aligned perpendicular inputs with resultants in all four quadrants. Establish the complete routine and insist that the drawing agrees with the calculated direction.
5-6Diagonal lattice vectors. Every endpoint is on a grid intersection, so students can still count integer Δx/Δy values. The vectors are perpendicular and unequal, so the same tip-to-tail and right-triangle reasoning applies.
7-8Three-vector challenges. Require A → B → C to remain unchanged while translated. The single resultant runs from the first tail to the final head.

Common Errors and Press Questions

  • Vector changes while moving: “Did you translate the arrow, or did you rotate/resize it?”
  • Wrong resultant endpoints: “Where did the first vector start? Where did the last vector end?”
  • Inverse tan used as final direction: “What quadrant is the resultant actually in?”
  • Arrow length out of proportion: “If 1 square = 1 N, how many squares should that change be?”
  • Checking too early: have students complete their own drawing and calculation before opening the PhET check.

Suggested Closing

Compare one case where the calculator's inverse-tangent output is not yet the final direction. Ask: Why is the tip-to-tail drawing useful even after you know how to calculate the magnitude and angle? Strong responses connect the picture to direction, quadrant, scale, and a visual reasonableness check.

Success criterion: students move vectors without changing them, construct the resultant geometrically, and make the numerical magnitude/direction agree with the drawing before relying on PhET.
