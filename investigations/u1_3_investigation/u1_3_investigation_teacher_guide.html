Build the Resultant

PhET Vector Addition investigation · adapted from the legacy Online Vectors Lab

Time20-25 minutes
GroupsPairs
Core toolPhET Vector Addition

Why This Investigation Fits Section 1.3

The legacy Online Vectors Lab is a strong simulation resource, but its component calculations and angle work exceed the current Section 1.3 target. This revision keeps the simulation and the experimental spirit while narrowing the task to direction, relative magnitude, simple one-dimensional combination, and qualitative two-dimensional resultants.

Learning Target Connection

  • Force is represented as a vector with direction and magnitude.
  • Same-direction forces add; opposite-direction forces subtract.
  • The resultant points toward the larger unbalanced effect in 1D.
  • In 2D, the resultant reflects both input directions and is pulled more toward the larger vector.
  • Arrow length is part of the model, not decoration.

Materials

  • One device per pair with internet access
  • PhET Vector Addition: https://phet.colorado.edu/sims/html/vector-addition/latest/vector-addition_en.html
  • Student investigation in Notability

Keep DOK honest. Students are not expected to calculate components, angles, or Pythagorean magnitudes in this section. The simulation is being used to make vector direction and relative magnitude visible.

Design Challenge Examples

  • Right resultant with one left input: 8 right + 3 left → 5 right.
  • Zero resultant: any equal, opposite pair.
  • Mostly right, slightly up: a long right vector plus a shorter up vector.

Common Misconceptions

  • Adding all magnitudes regardless of direction: have students physically point along each arrow before predicting.
  • Believing the resultant is a third independent force: clarify that the resultant is a single vector representation of the combined effect of the inputs.
  • Ignoring arrow length: ask whether a 10 N and 2 N force should look equally strong in a model.
