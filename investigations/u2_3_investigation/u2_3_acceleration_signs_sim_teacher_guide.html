Physics · Unit 2.3 · Teacher guide

Which Way? Which Speed?

20-minute simulation investigation · Four signed motion combinations

Student recording: Each run has two matching 0–15 second time lines. Students pause every second and write the actual signed velocity number on top and actual signed acceleration number below. The marks are times, not velocity or acceleration scales. No dots, graphs, or separate data tables.

Before class / directions

  1. Open Walter Fendt — Motion with Constant Acceleration. Demonstrate Reset, numeric entry (press Enter), Start/Pause; slow motion is optional. Do not tell students to change vector settings.
  2. Show where the site displays velocity vs. time and acceleration vs. time. Model recording a signed v and a at one time. Students should read numerical signs, not just the arrows.
  3. Students work through all four runs, then answer six follow-up questions. No acceleration calculation is assigned.

Four scenarios

RunPositionInitial vAccelerationExpected motion
A (+,+)0 m+1 m/s+0.2 m/s²Right, speeds up.
B (+,−)0 m+5 m/s−1 m/s²Right, slows; turns at 5 s; then speeds up left.
C (−,+)+10 m−1 m/s+0.2 m/s²Left, slows; turns at 5 s; then speeds up right.
D (−,−)+10 m−1 m/s−0.2 m/s²Left, speeds up.

Runs C and D intentionally start at +10 m, not 0 m, so the car begins to the right and can initially travel left. The simulation may not keep the car onscreen for the entire 15 s; use its v–t and a–t diagrams to inspect values. Exact-second pausing can be approximate.

Suggested 20-minute pacing

0–2: Open and demonstrate.   2–5: A.   5–9: B.   9–12: C.   12–15: D.   15–20: Six questions and discussion.

Follow-up answers

  1. A: rightward and increasing speed; v +1 at 0 s to +3 at 10 s.
  2. B: zero at 5 s; slows right first, then speeds up left.
  3. C: initially left and slowing; opposite signs.
  4. D: left and speeding up; same negative signs.
  5. B after turn travels left (negative v); C after turn travels right (positive v).
  6. v +3, a −1: right and slowing, then stops, turns left, speeds up if acceleration continues.
