Physics - Unit 2 - Section 3 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Vernier Graphical Analysis cart investigation

Vernier Graphical Analysis cart investigation - designed for 30 minutes

Time30 minutes
Groups3-4
Core toolCart + gentle ramp + Vernier video analysis or motion detector

Why This Investigation Fits Section 2.3

Explain acceleration as the rate at which velocity changes and identify acceleration from motion descriptions, data, and graphs. The activity comes after the notes, so students use the section model to interpret evidence rather than discovering later-unit content prematurely.

Materials Per Group

  • dynamics cart, track or smooth ramp, books/blocks for a gentle incline, Vernier Graphical Analysis with video analysis or motion detector, meter stick

Before Class

  1. Test the ramp angle and sensor/video view. Aim for a smooth 1-2 s run with no push at release.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
0-5Prediction and setupCheck that the research question and variable choices are understood.
5-18Collect evidencePrioritize clean, interpretable evidence over many trials.
18-25Analyze / comparePress students to cite data, graph features, or observations.
25-30Conclude / modelRequire a conclusion that directly answers the question.

Expected Evidence

A rolling cart on a fixed gentle incline should show velocity changing steadily enough for a roughly linear velocity-time trend. The calculated Δv/t should have the same sign and similar magnitude across reasonable intervals.

Watch for: A noisy graph can make students chase exact values. Emphasize the trend and Δv/t over a useful interval.

Strong Responses

FeatureWhat to Look For
Claim / conclusionDirectly answers the research question rather than restating procedure.
EvidenceUses specific measured values, graph trends, or observed relationships.
ReasoningUses only the Section 2.3 physics model and connects evidence to the claim.

Common Problems

  • Messy data: Repeat one clean trial instead of averaging several poor runs.
  • Direction confusion: Have students state the chosen positive direction or reference frame before interpreting signs or motion.
  • Overreach: If students introduce later concepts, redirect them to the representations and formulas currently established in Unit 2.

Safety / Cleanup

Keep walking and drop zones clear, use non-breakable equipment, and reset sensors/carts/materials for the next class. For the free-fall investigation, do not allow students to climb on desks or chairs.

Connection Forward

Section 2.4 applies constant downward acceleration to free fall.
