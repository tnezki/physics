Ramp Acceleration

Vernier Dynamics Cart + Track • Guided trial, then independent trial

Name: Group: __________
Learning target: Explain acceleration as the rate at which velocity changes and identify acceleration from motion descriptions, data, and graphs.

Research question: What evidence shows that a cart on a gentle ramp is accelerating rather than moving at constant velocity?

1. Set Up and Film — Trial 1 (Together)

Materials: Vernier dynamics cart and track with built-in tape measure, stable support, iPad camera, Vernier Video Analysis, and a soft stop.

  1. Raise one end of the track slightly and secure it. Release the cart from the same starting position every run, without pushing.
  2. Film one good trial from the side. Keep the built-in tape measure visible in the video for setting the distance scale.
  3. Do not move the filming iPad during the recording. Keep the whole cart path visible and stop the cart safely.

2. Track the Cart With Your Teacher

  1. Import the video into Vernier Video Analysis. Use the track's tape measure to set the distance scale. Set the origin and positive direction down the track.
  2. Open Advanced Video Options → Advance Frames → 5 frames.
  3. Choose Add, then mark the same spot on the cart (such as its front axle) as the video advances.
  4. Open the velocity–time graph/data table. Copy five points with both time and velocity shown; use points spread across the run.

3. Record Trial 1 Data

Record only time and velocity. Vernier already keeps the position values. Use actual time from Vernier, not the slowed video playback clock.
