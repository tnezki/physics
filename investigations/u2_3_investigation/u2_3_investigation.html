Physics - Unit 2 - Section 3 - Investigation

Vernier Graphical Analysis cart investigation

Use evidence from the activity to test and apply the motion model from Section 2.3.

Learning target: Explain acceleration as the rate at which velocity changes and identify acceleration from motion descriptions, data, and graphs.
Research Question: What evidence shows that a cart on a gentle ramp is accelerating rather than moving at constant velocity?

1. Make a Prediction

Before collecting evidence, write the result or pattern you expect and give one reason.

2. Set Up and Collect Evidence

Equipment: dynamics cart, track or smooth ramp, books/blocks for a gentle incline, Vernier Graphical Analysis with video analysis or motion detector, meter stick
1
Build a gentle ramp and mark a release point. Keep the ramp angle fixed for all trials.
2
Collect position and velocity data as the cart rolls down the ramp. Do not push the cart.
3
Repeat until the velocity-time graph is smooth enough to interpret.
4
Choose two points separated by a useful time interval. Record initial velocity, final velocity, and elapsed time.
5
Calculate Δv=v_f-v_i and acceleration a=Δv/t. Compare that calculation with the overall velocity-time pattern.

4. Analyze the Evidence

1. What graph evidence shows that velocity changed?

2. Was the acceleration approximately positive, negative, or zero using your chosen positive direction? Explain.

3. Would a steeper or flatter velocity-time trend mean a larger or smaller acceleration? Defend with your data.

5. Final Model

Write a concise conclusion that answers the research question using specific evidence from your group’s data or observations.
