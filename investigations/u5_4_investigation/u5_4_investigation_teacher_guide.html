Physics - Unit 5 - Section 4 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Find the Resonance Window

Hands-on forced-vibration/resonance investigation - designed for 25–30 minutes

Time25–30 minutes
Groups3–4
Core toolSpring–mass oscillator + timed driving

Why This Investigation Fits Section 5.4

The 5.4 notes define forced vibration, natural frequency, and resonance. This experiment turns those ideas into a controllable frequency-response test without introducing spring-force equations or later mathematics.

Materials Per Group

  • Spring or Slinky and sturdy support
  • Small securely attached mass
  • Stopwatch and meter stick
  • Metronome/device pacing

Before Class

  1. Build and test one safe spring–mass setup per group or station.
  2. Choose a mass that oscillates visibly but does not overstretch the spring.
  3. Mark a maximum safe amplitude and practice driving the top with small motion.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
5 minNatural timingMeasure 10 cycles, compute T and f.
12 minThree driving trialsRun slow, near-natural, and faster conditions with similar driver amplitude.
5 minCompare evidenceIdentify the largest response and check control variables.
5–8 minExplain resonanceConnect data to forced vibration, natural frequency, and demo.

Expected Evidence

The largest response should occur when driving is closest to the system’s natural frequency, though hand driving and damping make the peak broad. Off-frequency trials should generally produce smaller steady amplitudes.

Sample Calculation / Quantitative Check

Example only: if 10 oscillations take 8.0 s, T = 0.80 s and f = 1/0.80 = 1.25 Hz. Useful drive comparisons are about 0.6 Hz, 1.25 Hz, and 1.9 Hz, adjusted to convenient pacing.

Watch for: Secure the mass and support. Keep amplitudes small. Stop immediately if the mass loosens or the spring approaches its stretch limit. Students must not pull the mass toward faces.
