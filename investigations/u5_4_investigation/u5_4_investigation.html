Physics - Unit 5 - Section 4 - Investigation

Find the Resonance Window

Measure a system’s natural timing, then drive it too slowly, near that timing, and too quickly.

Learning target: Students will explain how sound reflects, refracts, and causes resonance in real-world situations.
Research Question: How does vibration amplitude depend on how closely the driving frequency matches a system’s natural frequency?

1. Make a Prediction

A hanging mass–spring system has a preferred vibration rate. Predict which driving condition will create the largest response: much slower than that rate, near the same rate, or much faster.

2. Set Up

Equipment: spring or long Slinky suspended from a sturdy stand, small securely attached mass, stopwatch, meter stick, metronome/device pacing, safety glasses as locally required
1
Pull the mass down a small distance and release it. Time 10 complete oscillations. Compute period T = total time/10 and natural frequency f = 1/T.
2
Let the system settle. Drive the top of the spring with a small, consistent vertical motion at about half the natural frequency for 10–12 cycles. Record the largest steady response amplitude.
3
Repeat with a driving frequency near the measured natural frequency. Keep the driving motion itself about the same size.
4
Repeat at about 1.5 times the natural frequency. If pacing is difficult, use the closest convenient metronome setting and record the actual value.
5
Compare the three responses. Explain which trial best demonstrates forced vibration and which best demonstrates resonance.

4. Analyze the Evidence

1. Which driving frequency produced the largest response? How close was it to the measured natural frequency?

2. Why is a large resonant response not simply evidence that the driver moved farther? Refer to the controlled driving motion.

3. Connect the investigation to the tuning-fork demo: what quantity had to match?

4. Use the section notes to distinguish forced vibration from resonance in one or two precise sentences.

5. Defend or Revise Your Model

Use your data to defend or revise this claim: “The largest response occurs when the driving frequency matches a natural frequency of the system.”

Use your group’s actual observations and measurements. Classroom data may vary; explain variation instead of forcing a perfect result.
