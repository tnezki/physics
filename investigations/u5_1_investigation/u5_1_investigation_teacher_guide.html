Physics - Unit 5 - Section 1 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Measure a Traveling Wave

Hands-on rope + slow-motion/video analysis - designed for 25–30 minutes

Time25–30 minutes
Groups3–4
Core toolRope, metronome pacing, video timing

Why This Investigation Fits Section 5.1

After the 5.1 notes, students can legitimately use period/frequency and v = fλ. This investigation makes those relationships measured rather than purely symbolic while reinforcing that medium motion and wave travel are different.

Materials Per Group

  • 4–6 m lightweight rope
  • Tape measure and two floor markers
  • Slow-motion camera or Vernier Graphical Analysis video analysis
  • Metronome or paced counting

Before Class

  1. Mark a clear rope lane away from traffic.
  2. Choose a repeatable rope length/tension that produces visible pulses.
  3. Practice the 1 Hz and 2 Hz pacing so one complete source cycle is clear.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
4 minPrediction + setupStress same rope and approximately same tension.
8 minPulse-speed trialsHelp students time the same feature of the pulse at both markers.
8 min1 Hz and 2 Hz wave trainsKeep trials short so reflected waves do not dominate.
6–8 minAnalysis + modelCompare v from d/t with fλ and explain the inverse f–λ pattern.

Expected Evidence

For fixed rope conditions, pulse speed should be approximately similar across trials. Increasing source frequency should generally shorten wavelength. Classroom values will not match perfectly because hand driving, tension, frame selection, and reflections vary.

Sample Calculation / Quantitative Check

Example only: if a pulse travels 2.0 m in 0.50 s, v = 2.0/0.50 = 4.0 m/s. At 1.0 Hz the model predicts λ ≈ 4.0 m; at 2.0 Hz it predicts λ ≈ 2.0 m. Students should use their own measured values.

Watch for: Do not treat disagreement as failure. Ask whether tension changed, whether the same point on the pulse was timed, or whether reflected waves contaminated the periodic pattern.
