Physics - Unit 5 - Section 1 - Investigation

Measure a Traveling Wave

Use video evidence to connect source timing, wavelength, and the speed of a disturbance.

Learning target: Students will describe vibrations and waves using amplitude, wavelength, frequency, period, and wave speed.
Research Question: When rope tension stays about the same, how do frequency and wavelength work together to describe wave speed?

1. Make a Prediction

Keep the same rope and approximately the same tension. If the source frequency doubles, predict what will happen to wavelength and to wave speed. Explain your reasoning before collecting data.

2. Set Up

Equipment: 4–6 m lightweight rope, two floor markers 2.0 m apart, meter stick/tape measure, timer or slow-motion video (Vernier Graphical Analysis video analysis is optional), metronome/device pacing, bright tape marker
1
Stretch the rope along a clear lane. One student keeps the far-end tension as steady as possible; do not wrap rope around hands.
2
Place two floor markers 2.0 m apart along the rope. Send one clean pulse and record slow-motion video. Determine the time for the pulse to travel between the markers. Repeat for three trials.
3
Calculate pulse speed for each trial using v = d/t, then find a representative speed for your group.
4
Keep tension unchanged. Make a short train of waves at about 1.0 Hz. Use a video frame to estimate crest-to-crest wavelength before a reflected wave returns strongly.
5
Repeat at about 2.0 Hz with the same tension. Estimate the new wavelength. Stop the trial if reflections make the pattern hard to interpret.

4. Analyze the Evidence

1. Compare the single-pulse speed with fλ for each periodic-wave condition. Are they reasonably consistent for classroom data?

2. When frequency increased, what happened to wavelength? Use your evidence, not just the formula.

3. Name two experimental factors that could make your measured speeds disagree.

4. Explain why the rope pieces did not need to travel from source to receiver for the wave to transfer energy.

5. Defend or Revise Your Model

Write a short evidence-based model connecting frequency, wavelength, and wave speed for a wave traveling on the same rope under approximately constant tension.

Use your group’s actual observations and measurements. Classroom data may vary; explain variation instead of forcing a perfect result.
