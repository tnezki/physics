Physics - Unit 7 - Section 7.1 - Investigation

Tape Charge Detectives

Use attraction and repulsion as evidence to infer relative charge and test a qualitative distance pattern.

Learning target: Explain charge interactions, conservation of charge, and how electric force changes with separation.
Research Question: What can tape interactions tell us about charge type, neutral-object attraction, and the effect of distance?

1. Predict the Charge Pattern

You will create two identical tape stacks. Predict which tape pairs will attract, repel, or show little interaction after the stacks are separated.

2. Make Two Tape Pairs

Equipment: clear tape, smooth tabletop, scissors, ruler, small paper bits, pencil.
1
Make two tape stacks. In each stack, press a lower strip labeled B to the table and place an upper strip labeled T directly on top. Fold small handle tabs at the ends.
2
Pull each T+B stack from the table together, then quickly separate T from B. Hold tapes only by the tabs.

4. Build a Charge Model

Without assigning an absolute + or - sign, use your evidence to decide which tapes have the same type of net charge and which have opposite types. Explain why T vs B is stronger evidence about sign relationship than tape vs neutral paper.

5. Test Distance

Choose one repelling pair. Start far apart and slowly decrease separation without touching. Describe how the electric interaction changes as distance decreases. Connect your observation to the distance pattern in Coulomb's law; do not claim a numerical inverse-square result from this qualitative test.

6. Conservation Check

The two tapes began nearly neutral and separated with opposite charge types. Write a particle-level explanation that is consistent with conservation of charge.

Important: this setup reveals relative charge relationships. It does not, by itself, tell you whether T is positive or negative.
