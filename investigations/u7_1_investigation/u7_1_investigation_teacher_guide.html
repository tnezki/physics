Physics - Unit 7 - Section 7.1 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Tape Charge Detectives

Hands-on electrostatics evidence lab - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups2-3
Core toolClear-tape charge pairs

Why This Investigation Fits Section 7.1

The activity uses only Section 7.1 ideas: attraction/repulsion, neutral-object attraction, charge transfer, conservation, and the qualitative distance dependence of electric force. It deliberately avoids assigning absolute sign from ambiguous triboelectric materials.

Materials

  • Clear tape
  • Smooth tabletop
  • Scissors
  • Ruler
  • Small neutral paper bits

Before Class

  1. Make one sample pair so students can see the T/B labeling convention.
  2. Use fresh tape and dry surfaces.
  3. Keep a second tape roll available if one brand gives weak effects.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
4Prediction + tape preparationCheck T/B labels and handle tabs.

Expected Observations / Evidence

T-T and B-B pairs should repel; T-B should attract. Both charged tapes commonly attract neutral paper bits. Repulsion becomes easier to observe at smaller separations.

Watch for: Attraction alone does not prove opposite net signs because a charged object can attract neutral matter. Do not treat tape deflection as a quantitative Coulomb-force measurement.

Strong Responses

PromptWhat to Look For
Relative charge modelT tapes share one charge type; B tapes share the opposite type; T-B attraction supports opposite signs.
Neutral paperAttraction is explained by redistribution/polarization; it does not identify absolute sign.
ConservationElectrons transfer between materials; total charge of the isolated tape system is not created or destroyed.

Common Problems

  • Weak effects: Use fresh dry tape, reduce humidity if possible, and avoid touching charged surfaces.
  • Tapes twist together: Hold them by tabs and compare one suspended tape with one slowly approaching tape.

Connection Forward
