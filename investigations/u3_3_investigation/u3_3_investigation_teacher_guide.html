Coffee-Filter Drag and Terminal Speed

Video-analysis falling investigation - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups3-4
Core toolVideo analysis / Graphical Analysis

Why This Investigation Fits Section 3.3

Section 3.3 is about the transition from gravity-only reasoning to net-force reasoning with air resistance. Coffee filters have a high drag-to-weight ratio and often approach a steady falling speed over a classroom-scale distance, making the force balance visible without requiring a full terminal-velocity equation.

Materials Per Group

  • 4 identical basket-style coffee filters
  • Meter stick or vertical scale
  • Tape
  • Phone/iPad/camera
  • Device with Vernier Graphical Analysis video analysis
  • Optional balance for filter mass

Before Class

  1. Choose a drop location with 1.5-2 m of clear vertical space if possible.
  2. Tape a meter stick in the video plane.
  3. Test one filter to confirm it falls stably rather than tumbling.

Tool / Data Setup

In video analysis, set the scale from the meter stick and track one consistent point on the filter. A velocity-vs-time display is ideal. Students need only identify whether the later velocity is still changing or is approximately constant; no drag equation is required.

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus

Expected Evidence

A single open filter should fall slowly; three nested filters, with similar frontal area but greater weight, generally fall faster. A stable filter may show a late-fall velocity that changes little, consistent with air resistance approaching weight. Reducing frontal area/streamlining generally increases fall speed.

Watch for: Do not guarantee that every classroom drop reaches exact terminal speed. If the velocity is still changing noticeably, describe the object as approaching terminal speed. Also avoid treating the folded-filter comparison as a pure area experiment because shape changes too.

Strong Responses

PromptWhat to Look For
Terminal-speed force modelWeight downward equals air resistance upward; net force and acceleration are zero while downward velocity remains nonzero.
One vs three filtersGreater weight requires greater air resistance for balance, so the heavier stack generally reaches a higher steady speed.
Free vs nonfree fallCoffee filters are nonfree fall because air resistance is significant.

Common Problems

  • Filter tumbles: Use fresh filters, release without spin, and restart with a centered nested stack.
  • Velocity never plateaus: Use the last portion as evidence of approach; do not claim exact terminal speed.
  • Poor video scale: Keep the meter stick in the same plane as the falling filters and camera perpendicular to motion.

Safety

Use a clear drop zone. Do not climb on unstable furniture; use a safe platform or teacher-controlled release point if extra height is needed.
