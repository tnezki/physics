Physics - Unit 3 - Section 3.3 - Investigation

Coffee-Filter Drag and Terminal Speed

Use slow-motion/video data to connect air resistance, weight, frontal area, and terminal speed.

Learning target: Students will distinguish free fall from nonfree fall and explain how air resistance affects falling motion.
Research Question: How do weight and frontal area affect falling motion when air resistance is significant?

1. Predict the Falling Pattern

Identical coffee filters have almost the same frontal area when nested. Predict which will reach the greater steady falling speed: one filter or three nested filters. Explain using weight and air resistance.

2. Set Up Video Analysis

Equipment: 4 identical coffee filters, meter stick, tape, phone/iPad or camera, Vernier Graphical Analysis video analysis.
1
Tape a meter stick vertically in the background. Keep the camera fixed and perpendicular to the drop path.
2
Use a clear drop distance of about 1.5-2 m if available. Release the filters without pushing them.
3
Analyze the video and estimate velocity during the later part of the fall. Look for a region where velocity changes very little from frame to frame.

4. Read the Forces

For a filter whose speed becomes nearly constant, describe the relationship between weight and air resistance. Then explain why the heavier nested stack must generally move faster before air resistance can balance its greater weight.

5. Frontal-Area Check

Use one filter open, then fold an identical filter so it presents a noticeably smaller frontal area. Drop them separately from the same height and compare their motion. Record a qualitative result; shape as well as area changes in this comparison.

6. Conclusion

Use your evidence to distinguish free fall from nonfree fall and to explain terminal speed. If your drop was too short to show a clear constant-speed region, say that the object was approaching terminal speed rather than claiming it reached terminal speed.
