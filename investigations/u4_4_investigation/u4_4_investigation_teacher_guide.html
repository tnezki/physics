Pendulum Energy Accounting

Vernier video-analysis energy investigation - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups2-3 students
Core toolGraphical Analysis video analysis

Why This Investigation Fits Section 4.4

Section 4.4 explicitly targets the work-energy theorem, energy transformations, system boundaries, and energy diagrams. A pendulum lets students compare gravitational potential energy at release with kinetic energy near the bottom using only Unit 4 formulas.

Materials Per Group

  • Pendulum bob with known mass
  • String and secure clamp stand
  • Meter stick
  • iPad/phone with Vernier Graphical Analysis video analysis

Before Class

  1. Build a secure pendulum with a clear swing path.
  2. Mark the lowest point and a safe release point.
  3. Place a meter stick in the video plane and keep the camera fixed side-on.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
0-4Prediction + systemChoose the zero-height reference and identify the pendulum/Earth system.
4-10Measure and recordMeasure release height and capture a clean swing without pushing.

Expected Evidence

At release, energy is mostly gravitational PE; near the bottom, energy is mostly KE. Mechanical-energy totals should be reasonably similar but not identical. Real losses and measurement limits prevent perfect equality.

Watch for: Do not instruct students to “fix” numbers until PE equals KE. Differences are part of the analysis. Keep the bob swing away from faces and fragile equipment.

Common Problems

  • Bad height measurement: measure vertical rise above the lowest point, not string length or arc distance.
  • Pushed release: release from rest without a shove.
  • Video blur: increase light and keep camera perpendicular.

Strong Responses

PromptWhat to Look For
Energy barsPE decreases as KE increases; any “other” category is justified with real dissipative mechanisms.
Conservation claimStates total energy is conserved even if mechanical energy is transferred to thermal/sound or surroundings.
