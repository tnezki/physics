Physics - Unit 4 - Section 4 - Investigation

Pendulum Energy Accounting

Track gravitational potential and kinetic energy in one mechanical system.

Learning target: Apply conservation of energy to explain transformations among gravitational potential, kinetic, and thermal energy and interpret energy diagrams.
Research Question: As a pendulum moves, how can energy forms change while total energy remains conserved?

1. Make a Prediction

A pendulum bob is released from rest above its lowest point. Predict how gravitational potential energy and kinetic energy will compare at release and near the bottom.

2. Set Up the Pendulum

Equipment: pendulum bob of known mass, string, clamp stand, meter stick, iPad/phone, Vernier Graphical Analysis video analysis.
1
Measure the bob mass. Identify the lowest point as the zero-height reference.
2
Raise the bob and measure its vertical height above the lowest point. Release without pushing.
3
Record from the side with a meter stick in the video plane. Use video analysis to estimate the bob speed near the lowest point.

4. Analyze Conservation

Compare the mechanical-energy totals. If they are not identical, identify realistic places energy could be transferred or transformed and identify measurement limitations.

Sketch energy bars for release and bottom using PE, KE, and a small “other/thermal/sound” category if your evidence supports it.

5. Defend the Conservation Model

Explain why a decrease in one energy form does not violate conservation of energy. Use your pendulum evidence and identify the system you are analyzing.

A real experiment will not give perfectly identical PE and KE totals. Treat differences as evidence to analyze, not numbers to hide.
