Physics - Unit 6 - Section 6.2 - Investigation Teacher Guide

Test the Law of Reflection

Quantitative ray-tracing lab - designed for 25-30 minutes

Time25-30 minutes
Groups2-3
Core toolLaser/ray box + protractor

Why This Investigation Fits Section 6.2

This directly tests the section relationship θi = θr and reinforces the required habit that both angles are measured from the normal. A short surface comparison connects the law to specular and diffuse reflection.

Materials Per Group

  • Plane mirror with stand
  • Low-power laser or ray box
  • White paper
  • Ruler and protractor
  • Tape
  • Broad flashlight
  • Crumpled aluminum foil or rough matte paper

Before Class

  1. Set laser/ray boxes below eye level.
  2. Pre-test the mirror stands so they remain vertical.
  3. Prepare a rough reflective sample that visibly spreads the reflected light.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
4Prediction + setupCheck that normals are perpendicular before light turns on.
10Four angle trialsStudents mark rays first, then measure with light off.
5Analyze θi vs θrUse differences to discuss measurement uncertainty.
4Surface comparisonSeparate organized reflection from local angle law.
3-5Diagram checkRequire correct labels and angle reference.

Expected Evidence

Each trial should give incident and reflected angles that agree within a few degrees. Rough surfaces spread outgoing rays because different local surface orientations have different normals, while each local interaction still follows the same law.

Sample Reasoning / Calculation

Example: if θi = 35° and θr = 37°, the difference is 2°. The small mismatch is reasonable experimental error; the pattern still supports θi = θr.

Watch for: The most common error is measuring angle from the mirror surface. Also watch for students drawing the normal along the mirror.

Strong Responses

PromptWhat to Look For
Law conclusionCites several measured angle pairs and acknowledges small error.
Normal lineShows it perpendicular to the mirror and measures both angles from it.
Diffuse reflectionExplains many local normals rather than claiming rough surfaces violate the law.

Common Problems

  • Reflected ray is hard to mark: Dim the room and use a white screen/card to locate the beam before marking the paper.
  • Angles disagree by more than ~5°: Check that the ray strikes the normal/mirror intersection and that the mirror is aligned with the traced line.

Safety / Cleanup

Keep lasers below eye level and pointed only at the paper/mirror test area. Do not use highly curved or hand-held shiny objects.

Connection Forward
