Physics - Unit 6 - Section 6.2 - Investigation

Test the Law of Reflection

Measure incoming and reflected ray angles and decide whether one rule survives repeated trials.

Learning target: Use the law of reflection to interpret ray diagrams and explain reflected light from smooth and rough surfaces.
Research Question: Across several mirror orientations, what relationship exists between the angle of incidence and the angle of reflection?

1. Predict the Pattern

If the incoming ray angle increases, predict what the reflected ray angle will do. State what line you will measure angles from.

2. Set Up the Ray Test

Equipment: plane mirror, low-power laser/ray box, white paper, ruler, protractor, tape.
1
Trace the mirror line on the paper. Choose a point where the ray will strike and draw a normal line perpendicular to the mirror at that point.
2
Aim the incoming ray at the marked point. Mark two points along the incident ray and two along the reflected ray. Turn the light off before drawing the ray lines.

3. Collect Four Trials

Measure both angles from the normal, not from the mirror surface.

4. Analyze the Evidence

Does your data support θi = θr? Use your measurements and discuss the size of experimental error.

5. Smooth vs. Rough Reflection

Shine a broad flashlight beam at a plane mirror and then at crumpled foil or rough paper. Compare how organized the reflected light is. Explain why diffuse reflection does not mean the law of reflection stopped working.

6. Diagram Check

Sketch one successful trial. Label incident ray, reflected ray, normal, θi, and θr.
