Electromagnetism in Modern Technology

This unit moved from magnets and magnetic fields (Section 8.1), to the connection between electricity and magnetism (Section 8.2), to electromagnets and the forces they exert (Section 8.3), and finally to generators and alternating current (Section 8.4). This section applies those ideas to the technologies that depend on them.

An electric motor and a generator are, in a sense, the same machine run in opposite directions. A motor uses current in a magnetic field to produce a force that turns a loop of wire — the electromagnet-and-force idea from Section 8.3. A generator does the reverse: mechanical motion turns a loop through a magnetic field, and that changing field induces a current — the induction idea from Section 8.4. A transformer uses induction differently again, using a changing current in one coil to induce a voltage in a separate coil, which is how household electricity gets stepped up for long-distance transmission and back down for safe use in a home.

Other devices combine these same ingredients. A loudspeaker uses a current-carrying coil in a magnetic field to push a paper cone back and forth, turning an electrical signal into sound. A microphone runs that process in reverse, using sound to move a coil in a magnetic field and generate a small current. A maglev train uses strong electromagnets both to lift the train above the track and to push it forward, avoiding the friction of wheels on rails. An MRI machine uses a powerful magnetic field together with radio-frequency energy to produce detailed images of the body's interior.

Every one of these devices obeys energy conservation: none of them creates energy from magnetism alone. A generator only produces electrical energy because mechanical energy is put in to turn it; a motor only produces motion because electrical energy is put in to run it. A claim that a magnetic device generates power "for free" is a claim that violates this principle and should be evaluated skeptically — the question to ask is always where the energy going into the system is coming from.

Section 8.5: Modern Electromagnetic Systems

Textbook Sections: synthesis/application

Learning Target: Students will apply magnetism, electromagnets, magnetic forces, and electromagnetic induction to real-world technologies and systems.

  • I can explain how electromagnetic systems use energy transformations.
  • I can describe how generators, motors, transformers, speakers, microphones, maglev systems, MRI, and electric meters use electromagnetism.
  • I can explain why energy input is required for electrical energy output.
  • I can critique claims that a device produces energy from magnetism alone.
  • I can analyze a modern electromagnetic system using fields, forces, current, induction, and energy conservation.
  • I can use evidence to defend whether a proposed electromagnetic model makes sense.
