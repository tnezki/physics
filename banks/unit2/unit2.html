2.1 — Motion and Reference Frames

Learning Target: Students will explain that motion is relative and describe an object’s motion using an appropriate frame of reference.

I Can Statements

  • I can explain why an object can be moving relative to one reference frame but not another.
  • I can identify the reference frame used in a motion description.
  • I can compare motion from two different points of view and explain why everyday motion is usually described relative to Earth’s surface.

A student stands inside a bus moving east. A backpack rests on the seat while roadside signs pass the window.

  1. Name two useful reference frames in the situation.
  2. For each frame, state whether the backpack is moving or at rest.
  3. Use your answer from (b) to explain why the two descriptions do not conflict.
  4. Write one complete motion statement that explicitly names its reference frame.
Show your reasoning here.
Answer: Bus/passenger and Earth/road frames; the backpack is at rest in the bus frame and moving east in the road frame; both are valid relative descriptions.

Kai walks toward the front of a train moving east at 9 m/s. Kai walks at 1 m/s relative to the train. Explain how Kai can be moving relative to the ground while also being described using a different motion relative to the train.

Answer: Relative to the train, Kai moves east at 1 m/s; relative to the ground, Kai moves east at 10 m/s. Motion depends on the chosen reference frame.
Solution: Use one reference frame at a time. The train-frame motion is the walking speed; the ground-frame motion combines the train motion and walking motion because both are east.

A passenger on a bus says a phone on the seat is at rest. A person on the sidewalk says the phone is moving east with the bus. Compare the two descriptions and explain why using Earth’s surface is convenient for ordinary travel descriptions.

Answer: Both descriptions can be correct because they use different reference frames. The phone is at rest in the bus frame and moving in the Earth/sidewalk frame. Earth’s surface is a convenient shared everyday frame.
Solution: Motion is relative. Changing the frame changes the measured position/velocity, not the physical object. Earth’s surface is commonly used because it is stable and shared for everyday locations.
