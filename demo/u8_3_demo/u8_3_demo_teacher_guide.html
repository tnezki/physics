Physics - Unit 8 - Section 8.3 - Demo Teacher Guide

A Magnet With an Off Switch

Switchable electromagnet hook - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatCoil + iron core
Core ideaControllable magnetism

Why This Demo Fits Section 8.3

The section begins by strengthening a current-carrying coil with turns, current, and an iron core, then connects magnetic force to motors, speakers, meters, and moving charges. The switchable lifting effect is immediate evidence that an electromagnet differs from a permanent magnet.

Materials

  • Large iron nail or soft-iron bolt
  • Insulated magnet wire coil
  • Low-voltage current-limited DC source
  • Switch
  • Paper clips

Before Class

  1. Prepare a coil with about 40-80 turns around the iron core.
  2. Test that it lifts several clips without excessive heating.
  3. Use a current-limited source and brief on-times.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
1PredictionRequire on and off predictions.
2Switchable liftLet students focus on controllability.
1Repeat with air-core comparison if quickShow the role of iron qualitatively.
1-2Design-variable predictionAsk how turns/current could be tested fairly.
1-2BridgeMove from field strength to magnetic force and devices.

Expected Observations / Evidence

With current on, the coil/core behaves as a strong electromagnet. With current off, most of the current-produced effect disappears and the clips fall, though the core may retain weak residual magnetism.

Watch for: Limit current and duty cycle. The coil can heat. Do not use mains voltage. Residual magnetism in steel may prevent a perfectly clean release; soft iron is preferable.

Transition Into Notes

The notes add domain alignment, saturation, magnetic-force direction, F=qvB, F=BIL, and real devices.
