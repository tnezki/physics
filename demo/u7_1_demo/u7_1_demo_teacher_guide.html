Physics - Unit 7 - Section 7.1 - Demo Teacher Guide

Same Prep. Two Different Behaviors.

Hands-on discrepant event - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatBalloon electrostatics
Core ideaCharge interaction + polarization

Why This Demo Fits Section 7.1

The section begins with positive/negative/neutral objects, electron transfer, conservation of charge, and charging processes. Two like-prepared balloons repel, while a prepared balloon can attract a neutral wall through charge redistribution. The hook creates the need for a particle model without naming every process in advance.

Materials

  • 2 latex balloons
  • Wool cloth or clean dry hair
  • Dry wall or other broad neutral surface

Before Class

  1. Inflate and tie both balloons.
  2. Test the room humidity; electrostatics is weaker on humid days.
  3. Rub both balloons with the same material immediately before class.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
1-2PredictionGet a public attract/repel/nothing vote.
2Balloon-balloon observationSuspend or hold one balloon lightly so repulsion is easy to see.
1-2Balloon-wall observationDo not explain polarization yet; collect competing explanations.
1-2Model choice + bridgeAsk what must be happening inside matter.

Expected Observations / Evidence

Like-prepared balloons usually repel. A charged balloon is attracted to a neutral wall and may stick. Students often notice that “neutral” cannot mean “contains no charge.”

Watch for: Do not use balloon-wall attraction to assign the balloon a positive or negative sign. Attraction to a neutral object does not identify the sign. Humidity may reduce the effect.

Transition Into Notes

Use the notes to track electrons, distinguish net charge from neutrality, and explain how a neutral object can still respond to a nearby charge.
