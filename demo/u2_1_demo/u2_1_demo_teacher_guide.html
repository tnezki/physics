Physics - Unit 2 - Section 1 - Demo Teacher Guide

500 Miles, Almost Zero?

NASCAR displacement paradox / viewpoint reveal - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatNASCAR displacement paradox / viewpoint reveal
Core ideaMotion depends on the reference frame used to describe it.

Why This Demo Fits Section 2.1

This hook happens before the notes and creates a need for the section idea without teaching the whole section. It stays within what students can notice or predict before formal instruction.

Materials

  • Projected slides; optional race-track image or simple lap sketch; no student materials required.

Before Class

  1. Have a simple oval/lap route ready to sketch if students need a concrete picture.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
0-1Opening phenomenonSet up the situation and require a prediction before explanation.
1-4Prediction / observationRun the live phenomenon or scenario. Ask for evidence, not vocabulary recitation.
4-6Compare claimsSurface competing interpretations and the misconception named below.
6-8Bridge questionStop before fully resolving the physics; carry the question into notes.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

Motion depends on the reference frame used to describe it. Students should be able to point to something they observed or inferred, even if they do not yet have precise vocabulary.

Watch for: Students may treat “moving” as an absolute property rather than a statement relative to a frame.

Reveal / Withhold

Reveal the physical result or graph evidence needed for the hook. Withhold a polished definition, formula derivation, or complete model until the guided notes.

Transition Into Notes

How can the same object be moving in one description and at rest in another without either observer being wrong?
