Physics - Unit 8 - Section 8.4 - Demo Teacher Guide

The Slow-Falling Magnet

Copper-pipe induction phenomenon - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatDiscrepant drop
Core ideaChanging magnetic conditions cause electrical response

Why This Demo Fits Section 8.4

The PM identifies the falling-magnet copper-pipe phenomenon as the strongest legacy hook for 8.4. The current notes require induction from changing magnetic conditions and an energy-conversion model. The demo deliberately does not require Lenz-law equations or eddy-current calculations.

Materials

  • Thick-walled copper pipe, about 0.5-1 m
  • Neodymium magnet that fits freely inside
  • Similar-size nonmagnetic metal or plastic object
  • Catch cup or towel

Before Class

  1. Verify the magnet moves freely and cannot stick mechanically.
  2. Place a soft catch at the bottom.
  3. Keep strong magnets away from electronics and students with relevant medical devices.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
1PredictionEstablish that copper is not behaving like an ordinary permanent magnet.
2DropsRun the comparison twice.
1Observation languageSeparate what was seen from explanation.
1-2Energy questionReject a free-energy interpretation.
1-2BridgeAsk what changing magnetic conditions can do to a conductor.

Expected Observations / Evidence

The neodymium magnet falls much more slowly through conductive copper than a nonmagnetic comparison object. The effect occurs during motion/change and converts some mechanical energy to thermal/electrical effects in the conductor.

Watch for: Strong magnets can pinch and can affect magnetic storage, compasses, and some medical devices. Use a catch so the magnet does not strike a hard floor.

Transition Into Notes

The notes formalize electromagnetic induction, rate of magnetic-field change, generators, AC cycles, and frequency.
