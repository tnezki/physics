Physics - Unit 3 - Section 5 - Demo Teacher Guide

Drop or Launch: Which Lands First?

Paired drop / horizontal launch - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatPaired drop / horizontal launch
Core ideaHorizontal motion does not change the fall time in the ideal model

Why This Demo Fits Section 3.5

Students enter projectile motion with prior knowledge of constant horizontal velocity and gravitational acceleration. The demonstration creates the need to separate horizontal and vertical components before the notes provide the model and equations.

Materials

  • Two identical small balls
  • Table or lab bench
  • Optional ramp or short track for the horizontal ball
  • Optional slow-motion camera

Before Class

  1. Clear the landing area.
  2. Place both balls at the same launch height.
  3. If using a ramp, set it so the ball leaves horizontally.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
0-1PredictionAsk whether horizontal motion changes time in the air.
1-3Simultaneous releaseDrop one and launch/roll the other horizontally as close to the same instant as practical.
3-5Replay or repeatListen for impacts or replay slow motion.
5-7BridgeAsk which direction gravity changes and which direction remains unaffected in the ideal model.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

The balls should reach the floor at nearly the same time when released from the same height, even though one travels horizontally.

Watch for: Do not oversell imperfect simultaneity. If the impacts are not clear, use slow motion or repeat. The physics claim is about the ideal model with equal initial vertical motion and negligible air resistance.

Safety

Keep the landing zone clear and use soft catch boxes or floor mats if needed to prevent rolling balls from becoming trip hazards.

Cleanup / Reset

Collect both balls and reset the ramp/track.

Transition Into Notes

Transition into separating projectile motion into independent horizontal and vertical components.
