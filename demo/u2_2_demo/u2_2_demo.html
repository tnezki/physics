Same Speed, Different Motion

How can two people move equally fast but have different velocities?

Today's challenge: Watch, measure, predict, and explain.

0-5 min | Walk 1: Measure

How fast was the walk?

A volunteer travels from A to B: 900 cm (9 m).
Average speed = distance ÷ time
900 cm ÷ 4 s = 225 cm/s

Use the actual class time. The 4-second starting value is an example, not a predicted result.

Walk A: Straight

Steady speed, same direction.

Which walk could have constant velocity? Explain using speed AND direction.
15-18 min | Class predictions

One finger = constant velocity. Two = changing velocity.

1. A car travels east at a steady 30 mph.

1 finger: Constant velocity. Neither speed nor direction changes.

2. A car rounds a circular track at a steady 30 mph.

2 fingers: Velocity changes because the direction changes.

3. A runner moves north, increasing from 2 m/s to 4 m/s.

2 fingers: Velocity changes because the speed changes.

18-20 min | Independent exit check

Explain it without help.

1. A student travels 12 m in 6 s. What is their average speed?

2. A speedometer reads 5 m/s. Instantaneous or average speed?

3. Can an object have constant speed but changing velocity? Give an example.

Speed tells how fast. Velocity tells how fast and in which direction.
