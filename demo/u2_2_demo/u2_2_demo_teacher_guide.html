Physics | Unit 2 | Section 2 | Teacher guide

Same Speed, Different Motion

18 minutes | teacher walks | every student times and calculates

SetupMark A (0 cm) and B (900 cm).
Each studentStopwatch, paper, pencil.
FocusAverage speed versus average velocity.

Preparation

Place two tape marks 900 cm apart and clear a safe walking path. Set positive direction from A to B. On paper, students create four numbered rows and record total distance, initial position, final position, elapsed time, average speed, average velocity for every walk. Every student operates a timer; if individual timers are not available, provide an accessible shared option but still have every student calculate independently.

Measure consistently: Start timers when the teacher begins moving, stop at the end of the described route. Students use their own elapsed times, so small differences due to reaction time are expected.

Formulas to use

Average speed = total distance / total elapsed time. Change in position = final position - initial position. Average velocity = change in position / elapsed time. A to B is positive; B to A is negative. If the final position equals the initial position, whole-trip average velocity is exactly zero (for nonzero elapsed time).

Demonstration sequence

TimeDo
0-3 minSlide 1: Identify A = 0 cm and B = 900 cm. Set the timing rule. Students set up four rows for measurements and calculations.
3-6 minSlide 2: Walk 1, A to B. Walk at a mostly steady pace, then stop at B. Each student times, records 900 cm, 0 cm, 900 cm, and calculates both averages.
6-9 minSlide 3: Walk 2, A to B. Walk slowly for about 300 cm, quickly for 300 cm, pause briefly, then cover the last 300 cm. Aim for about the same total time as walk 1, but do not force a match. Students measure again and compare. During the pause, instantaneous speed = 0; similar whole-trip averages do not imply the same motion at each instant.
9-13 minSlide 4: Walk 3, A to B to A. Travel 1800 cm in all, beginning and ending at x = 0 cm. Students time the entire round trip and calculate. This is the main contrast: average speed is positive; average velocity is 0 cm/s.
13-15 minSlide 5: Walk 4, B to A. Walk steadily back toward A. Students time and calculate. Distance = 900 cm, change in position = -900 cm. Compare with walk 1: both average speeds positive, but the average velocities have opposite signs.
15-16 minSlide 6: Compare all four results. Have students check the sign of velocity and the distance versus change in position. Keep their own times for numeric averages.
16-18 minSlide 7: Discuss three evidence-based questions; use think-pair-share or a few brief responses.

Critical clarification: Do not say that the round-trip walker had zero speed or zero velocity at every instant. The average velocity over the complete trip is zero because the final and initial positions match; the walker was moving during parts of the trip. In walk 4, negative velocity means direction toward A, not negative speed.

Three discussion questions: expected responses

  1. How could walks 1 and 2 have similar average speeds but different speeds during the trip? Both covered 900 cm in roughly the same total time, but walk 2 included speeding up, slowing down, and a stop. Its instantaneous speed was not constant. The average hides that variation.
  2. Why was average speed above zero but average velocity zero for the round trip? Distance was 1800 cm, so 1800/t3 is positive. Change in position was 0 - 0 = 0 cm, so whole-trip average velocity = 0/t3 = 0 cm/s.
  3. How could walks 1 and 4 have similar average speeds but opposite average velocities? Both cover 900 cm; if the times are similar, the average speeds are similar. Walk 1 changes position +900 cm and walk 4 changes it -900 cm; their average velocities point in opposite directions.

Closing sentence: Speed tells how fast an object travels along its path. Velocity describes a change in position per unit time, including direction. For a whole trip, distance and displacement can tell very different stories.
