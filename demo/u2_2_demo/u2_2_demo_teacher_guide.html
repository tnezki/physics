Physics - Unit 2 - Section 2 - Demo Teacher Guide

Fast Trip, Zero Direction?

Round-trip speed/velocity paradox - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatRound-trip speed/velocity paradox
Core ideaSpeed describes how fast; velocity adds direction.

Why This Demo Fits Section 2.2

This hook happens before the notes and creates a need for the section idea without teaching the whole section. It stays within what students can notice or predict before formal instruction.

Materials

  • Projected slides; board space for a quick round-trip sketch.

Before Class

  1. Be ready to sketch school → friend → school and distinguish total path from direction language without introducing displacement formulas.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
0-1Opening phenomenonSet up the situation and require a prediction before explanation.
1-4Prediction / observationRun the live phenomenon or scenario. Ask for evidence, not vocabulary recitation.
4-6Compare claimsSurface competing interpretations and the misconception named below.
6-8Bridge questionStop before fully resolving the physics; carry the question into notes.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

Speed describes how fast; velocity adds direction. Students should be able to point to something they observed or inferred, even if they do not yet have precise vocabulary.

Watch for: Students may say average speed is zero because the trip ends where it started, or treat speed and velocity as synonyms.

Reveal / Withhold

Reveal the physical result or graph evidence needed for the hook. Withhold a polished definition, formula derivation, or complete model until the guided notes.

Transition Into Notes

When does “how fast” describe enough, and when must direction be included?
