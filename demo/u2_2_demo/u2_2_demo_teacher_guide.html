Same Speed, Different Motion

Whole-class walking demonstration • 18-20 minutes • Grade 9

Time18-20 minutes
FormatStudent volunteers + projected prompts
Core ideaSpeed is magnitude; velocity includes direction.

Learning Targets

  • I can calculate average speed from distance and time.
  • I can distinguish instantaneous speed from average speed.
  • I can explain that velocity includes both speed and direction and compare constant speed, constant velocity, and changing velocity.

Materials & Before-Class Setup

  • Two tape marks or cones labeled A and B, placed 900 cm (9 m) apart; use an open hallway if the classroom is too short.
  • Stopwatch/phone; whiteboard; large paper arrow; one volunteer walker and one timer.
  • Project u2_2_demo.html. Slides use Back / Next or left / right arrow keys. The walk time on slide 2 is editable.
Safety: Clear the path of bags and furniture. Walking only, never running. For a circle, use a roomy path and maintain a comfortable pace.

Minute-by-Minute Demonstration

TimeSlidesTeacher Action & Discussion
0-5 min1-2Tell students the walker will travel 900 cm from A to B. Ask what information is needed to describe speed. Time the walk, enter the actual seconds in the slide, and have the class calculate average speed = total distance / total elapsed time. Example ONLY: 900 cm / 4 s = 225 cm/s = 2.25 m/s. Normal measured times will vary.
5-10 min3-4Have the student walk slowly for the first 300 cm, quickly for the next 300 cm, stop for 2 seconds, then cover the last 300 cm. Time the whole trip, including the stop. Ask: speed at the stop? 0 cm/s. Whole-trip average speed? Greater than 0 because total distance is 900 cm. A car's speedometer shows approximately instantaneous speed.
10-15 min5First have a student walk in a straight line at an approximately steady pace, holding the arrow in the travel direction. Then have them walk in a circle, trying to keep a similar pace while rotating the arrow to follow the path. Ask: which walk could have constant velocity? The straight one, if both speed and direction stay constant. The circular walk has changing velocity even if the speed stays constant.
15-18 min6One finger = constant velocity; two fingers = changing velocity. Reveal each answer only after a whole-class vote. Ask students to justify with the exact thing that changed: speed or direction.
18-20 min7Independent exit check. Three brief responses; collect on paper or ask for verbal justifications.

Teacher Answer Key

PromptExpected Thinking
Average speed of 900 cm in 4 seconds?900 / 4 = 225 cm/s (2.25 m/s). The 4 seconds is illustrative; calculate from actual measured time in class.
Instantaneous speed at a full stop?0 cm/s, although average speed over the entire trip can remain positive.
Straight line vs circle, each at steady speed?Constant straight-line velocity only when both speed and direction stay fixed. Circular velocity changes continuously because direction changes.
Challenge 1: Car east at steady 30 mph.Constant velocity.
Challenge 2: Car on circle at steady 30 mph.Changing velocity due to direction.
Challenge 3: Runner speeds up north 2 to 4 m/s.Changing velocity due to speed.
Exit 1: 12 m in 6 s.2 m/s.
Exit 2: Speedometer shows 5 m/s.Instantaneous speed.
Exit 3: Constant speed / changing velocity possible?Yes; steady-speed motion around a curve or circle changes direction.

Misconceptions to Listen For

  • Average means the speed was the same throughout. Counter with the 2-second stop: instantaneous speed reaches zero while overall average remains positive.
  • Steady speed always means steady velocity. Counter with the circular path: direction matters.
  • Stopping time doesn't count. It counts in total elapsed time for average speed.
  • Speed requires a direction. Direction is required to specify velocity, but not speed.

Optional Extension (only if time allows)

Have a volunteer walk A → B → A. Their total distance is 1,800 cm (18 m), but their displacement is 0 cm. This sets up future discussion of average velocity; do not add it if it disrupts the exit check.

Takeaway: Speed tells how fast. Velocity tells how fast and which direction.
