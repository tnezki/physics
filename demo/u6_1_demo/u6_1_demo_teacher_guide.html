Physics - Unit 6 - Section 6.1 - Demo Teacher Guide

The Beam You Cannot See

Discrepant light-path demonstration - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatDiscrepant light-path demonstration
Core ideaStudents confront the difference between light traveling through a region and light from that region actually reaching their eyes. This creates a need for the Section 6.1 ray/vision model without front-loading electromagnetic-wave vocabulary.

Why This Demo Fits Section 6.1

Students confront the difference between light traveling through a region and light from that region actually reaching their eyes. This creates a need for the Section 6.1 ray/vision model without front-loading electromagnetic-wave vocabulary.

Materials

  • Low-power classroom laser pointer or ray box
  • Stable clamp or support so the beam does not move
  • White target card
  • Small white index card used as a movable detector

Before Class

  1. Aim the beam horizontally at the white target well below eye level.
  2. Mark a safe student viewing area to the side of the beam path.
  3. Darken the room slightly if needed, but keep enough light for safe movement.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
1Opening puzzleShow the bright target spot; ask whether light exists in the apparently empty space before the target.
1-2PredictionCommit students to competing models without resolving them.
2Detector testSlide the small card through several positions. Each position reveals a spot.
1Remove detectorRestore the original target spot and ask what changed: the light path or the light reaching the eye?
1-2BridgeCarry the unresolved seeing question into the notes.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

Students should infer that the beam travels through the intervening space even when they cannot see a glowing path. The inserted card redirects some light toward their eyes, making that location visible.

Watch for: Do not say “you can only see scattered light” as the student-facing conclusion before notes. Also avoid implying that the card creates the light.

Safety / Reset

Use only a low-power classroom laser. Keep the beam below eye level, never aim toward students, shiny metal, or glass, and clamp the source so it cannot sweep the room.

Transition Into Notes

Ask: “If light can be present without being visible from the side, what has to happen for light from an object or surface to enter your eye?”
