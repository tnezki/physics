Physics - Unit 7 - Section 7.3 - Demo Teacher Guide

Same Battery. Same Bulb. Different Brightness.

Resistive-wire brightness demonstration - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatLive low-voltage circuit
Core ideaResistance changes current

Why This Demo Fits Section 7.3

Section 7.3 defines resistance, examines length/thickness/material/temperature, and introduces Ohm’s Law. The brightness change creates a visible consequence of changing circuit opposition while the source stays fixed.

Materials

  • Low-voltage battery pack
  • Miniature incandescent bulb
  • Switch and wires
  • Short copper jumper
  • Long nichrome/resistance-wire segment

Before Class

  1. Choose resistance wire that dims the bulb noticeably without heating excessively.
  2. Keep exposed resistance wire mounted where students cannot grab it.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
1-2Baseline circuitEstablish the bulb brightness with copper.
1PredictionStudents commit to brighter/dimmer/same.
2Swap in resistive wireObserve brightness; optionally compare long vs shorter segment.
1-2BridgeAsk what quantity in the path changed.

Expected Observations / Evidence

Adding a sufficiently resistive wire segment reduces current and dims the incandescent bulb. A shorter segment of the same resistance wire should produce less reduction than a longer segment.

Watch for: Resistance wire can warm. Use low voltage, short observation times, and do not let students touch the wire immediately after the demo. Do not state Ohm’s Law on the opening slides.

Transition Into Notes

The notes formalize resistance and use Ohm’s Law to connect voltage, current, and resistance quantitatively.
