Can One Fork Wake Up Another?

Sympathetic tuning-fork resonance - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatSympathetic tuning-fork resonance
Core ideaA driven object can respond dramatically when the driving pattern matches one of its preferred vibration rates.

Why This Demo Fits Section 5.4

Students will explain how sound reflects, refracts, and causes resonance in real-world situations. The projected sequence intentionally withholds the formal vocabulary until the notes.

Materials

  • Two matching tuning forks on resonance boxes (preferred)
  • Rubber striker
  • Optional mismatched fork/box for comparison

Before Class

  1. Place matching forks/boxes facing each other with a small gap.
  2. Test that striking Fork A can drive Fork B enough to remain audible after A is damped.
  3. If available, place a mismatched fork nearby for a comparison trial.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
1 minSet the puzzleEmphasize that B will never be directly struck.
1 minPredictionCollect silence-versus-continued-sound predictions.
2 minMatching-fork trialStrike A, wait, damp A, then listen to B.
1 minMismatch comparisonIf available, repeat with a different-frequency fork.
1–2 minBridgeAsk why timing/frequency matching changes the response.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

With a well-aligned matching pair, Fork B vibrates and can still be heard briefly after Fork A is damped. A mismatched fork responds much less.

Watch for: The effect can be subtle. Test the setup beforehand and keep the room quiet. Do not reveal “resonance” on the opening slide; let the phenomenon create the need for the term.

Transition Into Notes

What property of Fork B determines whether repeated driving from Fork A builds a large vibration?
