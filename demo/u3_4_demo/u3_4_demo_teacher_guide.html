Physics - Unit 3 - Section 4 - Demo Teacher Guide

The Balloon Pushes What?

Balloon rocket - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatBalloon rocket
Core ideaForces come in simultaneous interaction pairs

Why This Demo Fits Section 3.4

Section 3.4 asks students to identify the two objects in an interaction and understand why equal and opposite third-law forces act on different objects. A balloon rocket creates a visible recoil/propulsion interaction without requiring formulas.

Materials

  • Balloon
  • Drinking straw
  • 3-5 m string
  • Tape
  • Two supports for the string

Before Class

  1. Thread the straw onto the string and pull the string taut.
  2. Tape the inflated balloon to the straw with the opening pointing backward.
  3. Do not tie the balloon; pinch it closed until release.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
0-1Opening model choiceAsk what the balloon pushes on.
1-3ReleaseStudents identify the balloon and expelled air as interacting objects.
3-5Reverse the wordingAsk for "balloon on air" and "air on balloon" directions.
5-7BridgeAsk why the forces do not simply cancel.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

Students should identify a backward force on expelled air and a forward force on the balloon. The two forces occur at the same time and belong to the same interaction.

Watch for: Students may say the balloon "pushes on the room" or that the reaction happens after the action. Keep the focus on the balloon and expelled air and on simultaneity.

Safety

Keep the string above eye level or along a wall, and keep faces away from an overinflated balloon.

Cleanup / Reset

Remove tape and balloon; leave the string setup if it will be reused.

Transition Into Notes

Use the final slide to introduce Newton's Third Law and the importance of assigning each force to the object it acts on.
