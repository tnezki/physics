The Compass Moves When the Switch Closes

Oersted-style compass demo - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatCurrent + compass
Core ideaCurrent produces magnetic field

Why This Demo Fits Section 8.2

The current notes explicitly use a compass near a wire as evidence that moving electric charge creates a magnetic field. Reversing current adds strong evidence that the field direction depends on current direction.

Materials

  • Low-voltage DC source
  • Insulated straight wire
  • Current-limiting resistor (about 10-20 ohms)
  • Switch
  • Compass

Before Class

  1. Build a current-limited circuit with the straight wire passing just above the compass.
  2. Test the deflection and the reversed-current deflection.
  3. Plan to energize the wire only briefly.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
1Open-circuit predictionAsk what could change without moving the wire.
1Close switchCollect observation without naming the field yet.
1-2Reverse current predictionUse direction as the key variable.
1Second observationCompare needle direction.
1-2BridgeAsk what moving charge adds to the space around the wire.

Expected Observations / Evidence

The compass deflects while current flows and returns close to its original Earth-field orientation when the circuit opens. Reversing current reverses the wire-produced field direction.

Watch for: Use a current-limiting resistor and brief switch closures. Do not directly short a battery through bare wire.

Transition Into Notes

The notes generalize the evidence from a straight wire to loops, coils, and solenoids.
