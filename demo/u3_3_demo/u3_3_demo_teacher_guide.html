Physics - Unit 3 - Section 3 - Demo Teacher Guide

Same Paper, Different Fall

Simultaneous paper drop - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatSimultaneous paper drop
Core ideaAir resistance can dominate a fall

Why This Demo Fits Section 3.3

The notes distinguish free fall from nonfree fall and later connect air resistance to speed, shape, frontal area, and terminal speed. Flat-versus-crumpled paper makes the role of air visible without implying that different masses have different gravitational acceleration.

Materials

  • Two identical sheets of paper
  • Optional phone/iPad slow-motion video

Before Class

  1. Use sheets of the same paper.
  2. Choose a drop location away from fans or strong drafts.
  3. Prepare one sheet flat and one tightly crumpled.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
0-1PredictionRequire a reason, not just a choice.
1-3Flat vs crumpled dropRelease together from the same height.
3-5Matched-shape comparisonCrumple both similarly and repeat.
5-7BridgeAsk when a falling object can be treated as gravity-only.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

The crumpled sheet should reach the floor first in the first trial. When both are similarly crumpled, their motion should be much more similar.

Watch for: Avoid concluding "heavier falls faster." The useful difference is air interaction caused mainly by shape and frontal area in this comparison.

Safety

Use a clear drop zone and keep students from standing directly under the drop.

Cleanup / Reset

Recycle or save the paper for later use.

Transition Into Notes

The notes can now formalize free fall as gravity-only and nonfree fall as motion in which air resistance matters.
