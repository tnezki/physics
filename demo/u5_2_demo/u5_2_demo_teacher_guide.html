Physics - Unit 5 - Section 2 - Demo Teacher Guide

The Tone That Changes Without Changing

Moving tone-source Doppler hook - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatMoving tone-source Doppler hook
Core ideaRelative motion can change what a listener detects even when the source produces a steady tone.

Why This Demo Fits Section 5.2

Students will explain how waves interact through interference, standing waves, and the Doppler effect. The projected sequence intentionally withholds the formal vocabulary until the notes.

Materials

  • Small battery buzzer or phone playing one steady tone
  • Low rolling cart
  • Tape/strap to secure the tone source
  • Clear aisle

Before Class

  1. Secure the tone source firmly to the cart; never roll an unsecured phone.
  2. Choose a straight aisle with students seated clear of the path.
  3. Verify the source produces one steady tone while at rest.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
1 minSteady-source setupLet students hear the stationary tone first.
1 minPredictionCollect a fast vote about pitch before/after.
2 minPass-by observationRoll the secured source past the class at a safe steady speed; repeat once.
1 minStationary recheckShow the source setting never changed.
1–2 minBridgeAsk what wave spacing could explain the observation.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

Listeners should hear a slightly higher pitch while the source approaches and a lower pitch after it passes. The source itself remains set to one frequency.

Watch for: Do not tell students “Doppler effect” on the opening slide. Also do not say sound travels faster in front of the cart; the notes will separate wave speed from observed frequency.

Transition Into Notes

If the source did not change its setting, what about the arriving wave pattern changed when source and listener moved relative to one another?
