Physics - Unit 6 - Section 6.2 - Demo Teacher Guide

Hit the Hidden Target

Mirror target challenge - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatMirror target challenge
Core ideaA real beam and mirror make students need a geometric reflection rule. The projected prompts withhold the formal angle names until the notes.

Why This Demo Fits Section 6.2

A real beam and mirror make students need a geometric reflection rule. The projected prompts withhold the formal angle names until the notes.

Materials

  • Low-power classroom laser or ray box
  • Small plane mirror on a stand
  • White card target placed off the direct beam path
  • Tape for marking source and mirror positions

Before Class

  1. Clamp the light source below eye level.
  2. Place the target so the direct beam cannot hit it.
  3. Pre-test a mirror angle that sends the beam to the target within a few seconds of adjustment.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
1ChallengeShow source, mirror, and off-axis target; ask if a single flat mirror can hit it.
1-2PredictionStudents point or sketch an expected outgoing path.
2TestRotate the mirror slowly until the spot reaches the target.
1Model comparisonReject “always bounces straight back” using the observed path.
1-2BridgeAsk what angle relationship would let us predict the spot before testing.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

Students should notice that small mirror rotations produce large changes in the outgoing beam direction and that the beam does not generally retrace its incoming path.

Watch for: Do not reveal the law of reflection or name the normal on the projection before the notes. Keep attention on geometry and prediction.

Safety / Reset

Keep the beam below eye level and away from shiny unintended surfaces. Use a stable mirror stand and never let students hand-wave the mirror through the beam.

Transition Into Notes

Ask: “What line could we draw at the mirror so the incoming and outgoing paths can be compared consistently?”
