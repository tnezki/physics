Physics - Unit 5 - Section 1 - Demo Teacher Guide

The Pulse That Leaves the Rope Behind

Rope pulse discrepant event - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatRope pulse discrepant event
Core ideaA disturbance can travel while pieces of the medium only move locally.

Why This Demo Fits Section 5.1

Students will describe vibrations and waves using amplitude, wavelength, frequency, period, and wave speed. The projected sequence intentionally withholds the formal vocabulary until the notes.

Materials

  • Long rope or Slinky
  • Small bright tape flag attached near the middle
  • Clear 4–6 m floor space

Before Class

  1. Lay the rope nearly straight with one end held by a student or fixed support.
  2. Attach a bright tape flag to one point near the middle so its motion is easy to track.
  3. Keep the far end controlled so the rope cannot whip into people or equipment.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
1 minOpening challengeFocus attention on the tape marker and the pulse as two separate things.
1 minPredictionCommit students to a particle-travel model or disturbance-travel model.
2 minSingle pulseSend one clean pulse; repeat once if needed without explaining vocabulary.
1–2 minRepeated motionMake several gentle cycles at the source and ask what repeats locally.
1 minBridgeLeave the energy-transfer question unresolved for the notes.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

The pulse travels along the rope, while the marked point moves mostly transverse to the rope and returns near its original position. Students often initially track the moving shape as if the material itself were traveling.

Watch for: Do not define amplitude, wavelength, transverse wave, or wave speed before the notes. The hook works best if students first describe what they actually see.

Transition Into Notes

If the rope itself does not travel down the room, what is moving—and how can we measure the repeating motion and the traveling disturbance?
