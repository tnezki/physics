Physics - Unit 5 - Section 3 - Demo Teacher Guide

Make the Invisible Vibration Visible

Tuning-fork vibration phenomenon - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatTuning-fork vibration phenomenon
Core ideaA sound source vibrates, and that local motion can transfer a disturbance into surrounding matter.

Why This Demo Fits Section 5.3

Students will explain sound as a longitudinal mechanical wave that requires a medium and transfers energy through vibrating particles. The projected sequence intentionally withholds the formal vocabulary until the notes.

Materials

  • Tuning fork and rubber striker
  • Shallow clear cup/bowl of water
  • Solid tabletop or resonance box
  • Paper towel

Before Class

  1. Fill the bowl only shallowly to limit splashing.
  2. Place paper towels beneath the bowl.
  3. Use a rubber striker; never strike a tuning fork on a hard edge.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
1 minOpening questionStudents propose evidence for invisible vibration.
1 minPredictionVote on water response.
2 minWater revealStrike safely and touch one tine lightly to water; repeat if needed.
1 minTabletop clueTouch stem to tabletop/resonance box and compare loudness.
1–2 minBridgeAsk how motion/energy gets from source to ear without matter traveling across the room.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

Water visibly splashes or ripples when contacted by the vibrating tine. Contact with a large solid surface often makes the sound more noticeable because more surrounding material is driven to vibrate.

Watch for: Do not say that particles travel from the fork to the ear. Also avoid defining compression/rarefaction before the notes.

Transition Into Notes

The fork’s motion is local. What chain of particle motion allows the disturbance and energy to reach a listener?
