Physics - Unit 7 - Section 7.2 - Demo Teacher Guide

Can One Wire Light a Bulb?

One-wire circuit puzzle - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatBattery + bulb challenge
Core ideaComplete path + source

Why This Demo Fits Section 7.2

The section introduces voltage as energy per charge, current as charge-flow rate, and the requirement for a complete conducting path. The one-wire puzzle makes “complete circuit” a geometric idea rather than a memorized two-wire rule.

Materials

  • 1.5-V cell or low-voltage battery
  • Miniature flashlight bulb
  • One short insulated wire with exposed ends

Before Class

  1. Verify the bulb and battery work.
  2. Choose a bulb whose base and side shell contacts are clearly visible.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
2PredictionAsk students to identify the two bulb contacts and two battery terminals.
2Attempt + revealShow at least one working one-wire arrangement.
1Break one contactMake the gap visibly tiny.
1-2Explain what the puzzle requiresHold back formal voltage/current definitions until notes.

Expected Observations / Evidence

The bulb lights when there is a continuous conducting route from one battery terminal through the filament and back to the other terminal. Any gap in that sole route stops steady current.

Watch for: Do not say the battery “contains current” or that the bulb “uses up current.” Keep the bridge as a question about what the source maintains and what moves through the path.

Transition Into Notes

The notes formalize voltage as energy per charge and current as charge flow per time, then distinguish charge flow from energy transfer.
