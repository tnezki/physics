The Diagram on Trial

A cart is moving to the right after the teacher’s hand has already let go.

Which force arrows actually belong on the cart?

Evidence first

Watch what the cart does after release.

Motion

Does its speed stay nearly constant, or does it noticeably slow?

Interactions

What objects are actually touching or pulling on the cart?

Do not draw a force just because the cart is moving that way.

Put the model on trial

A force diagram must agree with the physical situation.

Check the arrows

Can you name a real interaction that produces each force?

Check the motion

Does the net force agree with whether the motion is changing?

A diagram can look neat and still be physically wrong.
Question to carry into the notes

How do we build a force model we can defend?

Choose one object. Identify its interactions. Draw force vectors. Find the net force. Check the model against the observed motion.
