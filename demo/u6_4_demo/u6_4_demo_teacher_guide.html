Physics - Unit 6 - Section 6.4 - Demo Teacher Guide

Catch an Image on a Card

Real-image projection demonstration - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatReal-image projection demonstration
Core ideaA converging lens produces a sharp projected image on a screen, creating a concrete need for focal point, real image, and ray-diagram reasoning without naming them on the opening slide.

Why This Demo Fits Section 6.4

A converging lens produces a sharp projected image on a screen, creating a concrete need for focal point, real image, and ray-diagram reasoning without naming them on the opening slide.

Materials

  • Converging lens or large magnifying glass
  • White card/screen
  • Bright distant object, illuminated arrow, or window scene
  • Lens holder (preferred)

Before Class

  1. Choose an object far enough away that a sharp projected image is easy to find.
  2. Darken the projection area slightly.
  3. Pre-find the approximate lens-screen spacing.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
1Opening challengeAsk whether transparent curved glass can put an image on a blank card.
1PredictionCommit to image/no-image and orientation.
2Find imageHold lens fixed; move card to focus.
1Move lensShow that the focus location changes.
1-2BridgeAsk for a ray-path model that predicts image location.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

A converging lens can form a sharp inverted image on the screen when the object is outside the focal length. Moving the lens changes the screen location required for focus.

Watch for: Do not label the image “real” or introduce the thin-lens equation on the student projection before notes.

Safety / Reset

Do not use the Sun as the bright object; a converging lens can focus sunlight strongly enough to damage eyes or materials.

Transition Into Notes

Ask: “Where must the rays go after the lens if a sharp picture can appear on the card?”
