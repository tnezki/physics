The Tension Trap

5-8 minute phenomenon hook for Equilibrium and Balanced Motion

Time5-8 minutes
GroupingWhole class + 2 volunteers
Student fileProjection only

Purpose

This demo gives students a surprising equilibrium result: the same hanging mass can remain at rest while the individual tension readings change substantially when the support directions change. It creates a need for the equilibrium rule and reinforces that zero net force refers to the vector sum, not to forces disappearing or all individual forces becoming equal.

Keep it qualitative. Students do not need trigonometric component calculations here. The useful observation is that changing force direction changes how much tension is needed to maintain the same equilibrium.

Materials & Setup

  • 2 spring scales with matching ranges
  • 1 light mass, ideally about 100-200 g so the scales remain well below their maximum rating
  • Short cords and a small ring/key ring so both tensions meet at one central point above the mass
  • 2 volunteers or two adjustable support points
  • Project u1_4_demo.html full screen

Connect the mass below the center ring. Attach one spring scale to each side of the ring. Start with both scales angled steeply upward. The system should be symmetric enough that the two readings are similar.

Safety/equipment: Do not pull the scales close to horizontal. Tension rises rapidly as the supports become flatter and can exceed the scale rating. Use a small mass and stop well before the scales approach their maximum reading.

Run of Show

TimeProjectionTeacher MoveWhat to Listen For
0:00-1:30Slide 1Show the mass supported by two scales. Ask what students expect each scale to read and why.“Half the weight each” is the productive prediction to surface.
1:30-3:00Slide 2Hold the scales steeply upward and let the mass settle. Read both scale values aloud.The mass is at rest, so students should connect the situation with zero net force even though three forces act.
3:00-4:30Slide 3Have volunteers slowly spread the scales farther apart while keeping the mass supported and roughly centered. Require a prediction before students look at the readings.Many will expect no change because the mass did not change.
4:30-6:30Slide 4Compare the larger tension readings with the unchanged state of rest. Ask how both statements can be true at once.Students should notice that direction matters: less of each angled tension points upward when the supports become flatter.
6:30-8:00Slide 5Transition into notes without calculating components. Ask what condition equilibrium must impose on the combined forces.Net force equals zero.

Suggested Teacher Language

“The object never stopped being in equilibrium, but the individual force readings changed. That tells us equilibrium cannot mean ‘all forces are the same.’ It has to describe what the forces do when they are combined with their directions.”

Misconceptions to Surface, Not Fix Yet

  • “At rest means no forces act.” Weight and two tensions are present throughout the demonstration.
  • “Two supports must always split the load in half.” Direction matters; angled tensions can each exceed half the weight.
  • “Equilibrium means every force has an equal opposite partner.” Several vectors can combine to zero without forming simple one-to-one pairs.
  • “Zero net force means zero force readings.” Individual forces can be large while their vector sum is zero.

Transition Into Guided Notes

Move into Section 1.4 by returning to the final question: What does equilibrium actually require? The notes distinguish static and dynamic equilibrium and use hanging people, scaffolds, support forces, and constant-speed motion to show how several nonzero forces can combine to zero net force.
