Physics - Unit 3 - Section 2 - Demo Teacher Guide

Why Did the Pull Get Easier?

Block + spring scale - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatBlock + spring scale
Core ideaStatic friction can adjust before sliding

Why This Demo Fits Section 3.2

Students are about to distinguish static from sliding friction and explain how friction changes net force. The rising force reading followed by a smaller sliding reading creates a useful puzzle before the vocabulary is formalized.

Materials

  • Wooden block with eye hook or string
  • Spring scale
  • Smooth tabletop
  • Rough surface strip such as sandpaper or shelf liner

Before Class

  1. Check that the scale reads near zero before pulling.
  2. Arrange the surface strip so it will not slide.
  3. Make sure the block can move without striking students or equipment.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
0-1Opening predictionAsk what the scale will do before motion starts.
1-3Slow pull to breakawayIncrease the pull gradually and let students watch the scale.
3-5Steady slideContinue at roughly constant speed; compare the reading with the peak.
5-7Surface comparison / bridgeRepeat briefly on the second surface and ask what hidden force is changing.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

The pull can increase while the block remains at rest; the largest reading usually occurs just before sliding, and the steady sliding reading is often lower.

Watch for: Do not say friction is always equal to the applied force. That is only true while the block remains at rest with zero net force and static friction is able to match the pull.

Safety

Pull horizontally and keep fingers away from the moving block and scale hook.

Cleanup / Reset

Return the block, scale, and surface strip.

Transition Into Notes

Transition to the notes by naming the need to distinguish the friction before sliding from the friction during sliding.
