Physics - Unit 5 - Section 5 - Demo Teacher Guide

Two Steady Notes. One Pulsing Sound.

Audible beats discrepant event - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatAudible beats discrepant event
Core ideaTwo steady but slightly different frequencies can combine into a repeating loud–soft pattern.

Why This Demo Fits Section 5.5

Students will analyze sound interference and beats using wave superposition and frequency differences. The projected sequence intentionally withholds the formal vocabulary until the notes.

Materials

  • Two tuning forks with close frequencies (for example 256 Hz and 260 Hz)
  • Two rubber strikers
  • Quiet room or listening area

Before Class

  1. Choose a fork pair close enough to create countable beats.
  2. Test that beats are audible from the front of the room.
  3. Do not reveal the labeled frequencies until after the first listening trial.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
1 minSingle tonesEstablish that each fork alone is steady.
1 minPredictionVote steady versus pulsing.
2 minBoth forksSound both close-frequency forks; have students count pulses.
1 minRestate the puzzleEmphasize that source amplitudes are not intentionally changing.
1–2 minBridgeAsk how two frequencies could repeatedly reinforce and oppose.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

Students hear periodic loud-soft variation (beats) when the close-frequency forks sound together. Faster frequency difference gives faster beat rate.

Watch for: Do not state fbeat = |f1 − f2| before the notes. Keep the first experience phenomenological.

Transition Into Notes

If each fork stays steady, what relationship between their cycles could make the combined sound alternate between stronger and weaker?
