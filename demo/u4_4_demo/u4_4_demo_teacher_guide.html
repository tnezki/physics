Physics - Unit 4 - Section 4 - Demo Teacher Guide

The Double-Ball Surprise

Stacked-ball energy-transfer phenomenon - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatBasketball + tennis-ball drop
Core ideaEnergy can transfer within a system while total energy is conserved

Why This Demo Fits Section 4.4

Section 4.4 applies conservation of energy and the work-energy theorem to transformations and systems. The stacked-ball rebound is dramatic because the tennis ball can gain energy while another part of the system loses it.

Materials

  • Basketball
  • Tennis ball
  • Open ceiling space and clear floor area

Before Class

  1. Check ceiling fixtures and establish a clear drop zone.
  2. Practice the stacked alignment so the tennis ball rests centered above the basketball.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
0-2Single-ball baseline + predictionDrop the tennis ball alone, then ask for stacked-drop predictions.
2-4Stacked dropDrop both; repeat once if needed.
4-6Energy accounting claimsAsk where the tennis ball’s extra kinetic energy could come from.
6-8BridgeShift from “energy of one object” to energy transfer inside a system.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

The tennis ball often rebounds much higher than its starting height while the basketball rebounds less strongly. Students may describe the high rebound as “extra” or “created” energy.

Watch for: Keep faces and hands clear of the rebound path. Do not promise an exact rebound height; the demonstration depends on alignment and ball inflation.

Transition Into Notes

Ask: “How can one object gain energy without the system creating energy?”
