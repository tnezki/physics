Physics - Unit 6 - Section 6.3 - Demo Teacher Guide

The Coin That Appears

Disappearing/reappearing coin phenomenon - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatDisappearing/reappearing coin phenomenon
Core ideaAdding water changes the path light can take from the coin to the observer, creating a clean need for refraction and apparent-depth reasoning.

Why This Demo Fits Section 6.3

Adding water changes the path light can take from the coin to the observer, creating a clean need for refraction and apparent-depth reasoning.

Materials

  • Opaque cup or bowl
  • Coin
  • Water in a pitcher
  • Towel for spills

Before Class

  1. Place the coin behind the far rim of the empty cup.
  2. Choose and mark an observer position where the rim just blocks the coin.
  3. Rehearse pouring water without moving the cup or coin.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
1SetupLock the observer position and verify the coin is hidden.
1PredictionStudents decide whether adding water can change visibility.
2Pour and observeAdd water slowly; keep the observer fixed.
1Rule out motionEmphasize that objects did not move.
1-2BridgeAsk what changed about the light path.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

The coin becomes visible after water is added because rays from the coin refract at the water-air boundary and can reach the observer.

Watch for: Do not pre-explain “bends away from the normal” on the student projection. Also avoid saying the coin physically rises.

Safety / Reset

Wipe spills promptly and keep water away from electrical equipment.

Transition Into Notes

Ask: “If the path changes at the boundary, what changed about light inside the water compared with light in air?”
