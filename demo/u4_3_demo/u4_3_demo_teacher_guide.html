Physics - Unit 4 - Section 3 - Demo Teacher Guide

The Marshmallow Range Challenge

Quick launcher phenomenon - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatLauncher demonstration
Core ideaForce acting through distance transfers energy

Why This Demo Fits Section 4.3

Section 4.3 defines work as energy transfer when a force acts through a displacement and introduces power as the rate of doing work. The launcher creates a visible energy-transfer difference without requiring spring-energy formulas that are not part of the Unit 4 formula set.

Materials

  • Plastic spoon or craft-stick launcher secured safely
  • Rubber bands
  • Mini marshmallows or soft pom-poms
  • Meter stick or floor markers

Before Class

  1. Set a safe launch lane away from faces and fragile objects.
  2. Mark a consistent launch direction and two repeatable pullback positions.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
0-2PredictionAsk which pullback will send the projectile farther and why.
2-4Two launchesUse the same projectile and direction; change only pullback distance as consistently as practical.
4-6Work vs effort contrastBriefly compare moving an object with pushing on an unmoving wall.
6-8BridgeCarry the class into the precise physics meaning of work and later power.

Expected Observations / Student Thinking

Students should connect a larger pre-launch interaction with more motion after launch, while recognizing that everyday “effort” is not enough to define mechanical work.

Watch for: Do not introduce elastic potential-energy equations; they are outside the Unit 4 formula limit. Treat the launcher only as a qualitative energy-transfer hook.

Transition Into Notes

Ask: “What must a force do to transfer energy mechanically, and how do we describe how fast that transfer happens?”
