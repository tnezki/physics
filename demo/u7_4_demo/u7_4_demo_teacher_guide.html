Physics - Unit 7 - Section 7.4 - Demo Teacher Guide

Which One Uses More Energy?

Prediction + energy-use paradox - designed for 5-8 minutes

Time5-8 minutes
FormatWhole-class vote
Core ideaPower is a rate; time matters

Why This Demo Fits Section 7.4

The notes explicitly distinguish power from total electrical energy and warn that a high-power device used briefly can use less energy than a lower-power device used much longer. The demo surfaces that misconception before formulas are introduced.

Materials

  • Projected demo only
  • Optional two appliance/device labels for visual context

Before Class

  1. Have the two example ratings and times ready.
  2. Do not put P=IV or E=Pt on the opening slides.

Suggested Flow

MinutesProjected PhaseTeacher Focus
2Initial voteRequire one-sentence reasoning, not arithmetic.
1Reveal rating/time detailsAsk what watts might mean.
2Second claimIntroduce the equal-brightness/lower-power lamp idea qualitatively.
1-2BridgeAsk what information a fair energy claim needs.

Expected Observations / Evidence

Students commonly choose the higher-watt device automatically. The useful tension is that watts describe rate, while total energy also depends on operating time.

Watch for: Do not imply that low power always means high efficiency; useful output matters. Keep solar/efficiency claims evidence-based rather than assuming one technology is always better.

Transition Into Notes

The notes define electric power, calculate P=IV, then connect energy use to E=Pt and real device/solar claims.
