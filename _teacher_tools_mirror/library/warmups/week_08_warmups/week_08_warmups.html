1Free Response2.2 · Comparing speed and velocity

A cyclist rides north at 4 m/s, turns around, and then rides south at 4 m/s. Compare the cyclist’s speed and velocity before and after the turn. Which one changes?

2Free Response1.5 · Balanced horizontal forces

A storage bin slides right across a rough floor at constant velocity while a student pushes it right. Sketch only the horizontal forces on the bin. How do their arrow lengths compare, and why?

3Multiple Choice2.2 · Instantaneous versus average speed

A scooter’s speedometer reads 8 m/s at one instant. Over a 15 s trip, it travels 90 m. Which statement is correct?

  1. Average speed = 8 m/s; instantaneous speed = 8 m/s
  2. Average speed = 6 m/s; instantaneous speed at that moment = 8 m/s
  3. Average speed = 8 m/s; instantaneous speed = 6 m/s
  4. Average speed = 90 m/s
  5. The average speed cannot be calculated

1Free Response2.3 · Acceleration from velocity data

An RC car moves east. At 0 s its velocity is 2 m/s; at 2 s it is 6 m/s; at 4 s it is 10 m/s. Calculate its average acceleration from 0 to 4 s. What pattern in the data supports your answer?

2Free Response1.4 · Equilibrium while moving

Two students pull on a cart with equal forces of 18 N in opposite directions. The cart is already moving steadily to the right. Find the net horizontal force and predict whether it must stop. Explain.

  1. Its direction, and therefore its velocity, keeps changing.
  2. Its speed must increase around every curve.
  3. It is accelerating only when it passes the starting line.
  4. An object must stop to accelerate.
  5. Circular motion has no velocity.
