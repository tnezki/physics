1Free Response2.1 · Comparing reference frames

Lena walks beside a motorized cart in the school hallway, matching its speed. She says, “The cart stays beside me.” A student standing in the doorway says, “The cart moves east.” Name each student’s reference frame and explain why both descriptions can be correct.

2Free Response1.3 · Finding the net force

A small sled slides across the gym floor. A rope pulls it with 26 N east, a second rope pulls with 9 N west, and friction acts with 7 N west. Find the net horizontal force, including direction. Draw one arrow representing the resultant force.

3Multiple Choice1.2 · Newton’s First Law

After one quick push, a puck glides right across nearly frictionless ice at a constant speed. Which statement best describes the puck’s horizontal net force after the hand is no longer touching it?

  1. The hand must still be pushing the puck to the right.
  2. An inertia force pushes the puck forward.
  3. A moving puck must have a larger rightward force.
  4. The horizontal net force is approximately zero.
  5. Gravity provides the forward force.

10/13 · During 2.22.2 Speed & Velocity
1Free Response1.4 · Two-support equilibrium

A hanging laboratory object weighs 36 N. It is motionless while two vertical spring scales support it. One scale reads 14 N upward. What should the other scale read? Explain using the total upward and downward forces.

2Free Response2.1 · Motion depends on the observer

A student stands still on a moving escalator. Describe the student’s motion relative to the escalator step and relative to the building floor. Why is the phrase “the student is not moving” incomplete?

3Multiple Choice2.1 · Identifying a reference frame

A tiny camera is attached to a rolling robot. In its video, a sticker on the robot stays in the same location even as the robot crosses the classroom. Which reference frame explains why the sticker stays in one place?

  1. A student watching from the doorway
  2. The camera moving with the robot
  3. The classroom floor
  4. A stationary camera mounted on a wall
  5. A student standing beside the robot’s path

1Free Response2.2 · Average speed and units

During a lab trial, a cart rolls 720 cm along a straight track in 12 seconds. Calculate its average speed in cm/s and in m/s. Explain why this average does not tell us the cart’s speed at every instant.

2Free Response2.1 · Evidence from reference frames

One lab partner measures a moving robot’s position using tape marks on the classroom floor. Another measures the distance from the robot to a sticker attached to its top. Which measurement can show the robot’s motion relative to the room? Explain why.

3Multiple Choice2.2 · Instantaneous versus average speed

A swimmer travels 120 m in 30 s. A speed sensor reads 5 m/s just as the swimmer finishes. Which statement is correct?

  1. The average speed is 5 m/s and the finish-line speed is 4 m/s.
  2. Both speeds are 5 m/s.
  3. The average speed is 4 m/s and the finish-line speed is 5 m/s.
  4. The average speed is 120 m/s.
  5. No average speed can be calculated from these measurements.

1Free Response2.2 · Speed compared with velocity

An RC car travels east at 3 m/s. It turns a corner and travels north at 3 m/s. Compare its speed before and after the turn. Compare its velocity, and explain your reasoning using direction.

2Free Response1.3 · How changing a force changes net force

A person pushes a shopping cart to the right with a force of 22 N while friction acts left with 8 N. Draw the horizontal forces and calculate the net force. If friction increased to 22 N while the push stayed the same, what would the new net force be? Would the cart necessarily stop immediately? Explain.

3Multiple Choice2.2 · Comparing average speeds

Jay and Mei each travel 50 m in a straight-line skateboard run. Jay takes 10 s and Mei takes 8 s. Which statement is supported by the measurements?

  1. Jay averages 5 m/s and is faster than Mei.
  2. Jay averages 8 m/s.
  3. Mei averages 6.25 m/s, but Jay is faster.
  4. Their average speeds are equal because the distances are equal.
  5. Mei averages 6.25 m/s and is faster than Jay, who averages 5 m/s.

1Free Response2.2 · Distance and average speed

A dog runs 40 m east to fetch a ball in 8 s, then returns 40 m west in 12 s. Find the total distance, total time, and average speed for the whole trip. Explain why returning to the start does not make the average speed zero.

2Free Response1.2 · Inertia and unbalanced forces

A cyclist stops pedaling on a rough road, and the bicycle gradually slows. A friend says, “It slows because it runs out of motion.” Explain what actually causes the change in motion, and predict how the bicycle would behave on a surface with much less friction.

3Multiple Choice2.2 · Constant speed, changing velocity

Which motion is an example of constant speed but changing velocity?

  1. A robot travels around a circular path at a steady 2 m/s.
  2. A robot travels straight north at a steady 2 m/s.
  3. A robot speeds up while traveling straight north.
  4. A robot remains parked beside a wall.
  5. A robot travels straight east while its speed increases.
