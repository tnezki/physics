Find Someone Who — Set 1 — Section 7.5: Complex Electric Circuits

1. How many current paths does a simple series circuit have?

2. What circuit condition is required for current to continue around a loop?

3. In a branched circuit, where does current split and where does it recombine?

4. In ideal parallel branches, is source voltage divided among branches or shared across them?

5. Name one device that protects household wiring by opening the circuit when current becomes too large.

6. Use the parallel-circuit figure to identify the separate current paths and explain why one branch can remain complete if another branch opens.

7. A sensor box contains a 5 Ω resistor followed by an 11 Ω resistor in the same single path. Find the resistance the source sees from those two components together.

8. Two identical 9 Ω heating elements connect across the same two nodes. Calculate the equivalent resistance of the pair.

9. A circuit has 2 A in one branch and 0.8 A in another. Calculate the total current in the common line.

10. A 9 V battery supplies two ideal parallel branches. One branch is opened. Explain what happens to the voltage across the remaining intact branch.

11. A classmate says current is used up by the first bulb in a series loop, so less current must reach the second. Critique using steady-state charge conservation and energy transfer.

12. A circuit designer wants each lamp to keep working if another lamp fails open. Compare series and parallel topologies and defend the better design.

13. A string of lamps is wired so current has only one route. Which circuit type is this?

A. series
B. parallel
C. mixed
D. short
E. open by definition

14. Two lamps connect on separate paths across the same pair of junctions. This is

A. series
B. shorted
C. open
D. parallel
E. single-path

15. A 7 Ω resistor and a 5 Ω resistor are in one series path. Their equivalent resistance is

A. 2 Ω
B. 12 Ω
C. 35 Ω
D. 6 Ω
E. 7 Ω

16. Two identical 12 Ω resistors are connected in parallel. Their equivalent resistance is

A. 12 Ω
B. 24 Ω
C. 144 Ω
D. 3 Ω
E. 6 Ω

17. In a one-path series loop at steady state, which statement about current is correct?

A. Current is divided at every resistor.
B. Current is largest at the battery and zero after the first bulb.
C. The same current passes each component.
D. Each component receives the full source current plus extra.
E. Current equals voltage numerically.

18. A lamp branch connects directly across an ideal source. What happens to its voltage if a different parallel branch is opened?

A. It remains the source voltage as long as the lamp branch stays connected.
B. It becomes zero automatically.
C. It doubles in every case.
D. It is split with the opened branch.
E. It turns into current.

19. Why can two parallel appliances operate independently?

A. One appliance stores current for the other.
B. Parallel branches use up voltage separately.
C. Opening a branch creates charge in the others.
D. Each appliance has its own complete branch between the supply nodes.
E. Every appliance is actually in series.

20. A breaker trips after several high-power appliances are turned on. Which explanation is most accurate?

A. The appliances create extra voltage.
B. The added parallel loads increase total source current; excessive current can overheat wiring, so the breaker opens the common path.
C. The breaker stores extra electrons.
D. Parallel loads always raise equivalent resistance.
E. The breaker acts by shorting the source.
