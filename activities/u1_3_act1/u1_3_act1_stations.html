Question 1

1. Define vector and give one force example that includes both magnitude and direction.

Work / rationale: A vector has both magnitude and direction. Example: a force of 25 N east.

Question 2

2. State the rule for combining two forces that point in the same direction.

Work / rationale: Add the magnitudes and keep the common direction.

Question 3

3. State the rule for combining two forces that point in opposite directions.

Work / rationale: Subtract the smaller magnitude from the larger and use the direction of the larger force. Equal opposite forces give a zero resultant.

8. Use the right-angle vector image. Identify the two component vectors and the diagonal resultant. Explain how the diagonal summarizes their combined effect.

Work / rationale: The component vectors point right and up. The diagonal arrow points up and right and is the resultant, representing the single vector with the same combined effect as the two perpendicular components.

Question 9

9. Object A has 18 N right and 6 N left. Object B has 30 N left and 20 N right. Compare their net-force magnitudes and directions.

Work / rationale: Object A: 12 N right. Object B: 10 N left. A has the larger net-force magnitude by 2 N, and the net forces point in opposite directions.

Question 10

10. A force diagram shows one 24-N arrow east and one 10-N arrow north. Before finding any numerical resultant, explain what you can conclude from the directions and relative magnitudes.

Work / rationale: The resultant must point between east and north (northeast). Because 24 N east is larger than 10 N north, it points closer to east than to north; its magnitude is greater than 24 N but less than 34 N.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A classmate says, “If two force arrows are the same length, they always cancel.” Critique the statement and give one example where equal-length arrows cancel and one where they do not.

Work / rationale: Equal-length arrows cancel only when they are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. Example that cancels: 10 N east and 10 N west. Example that does not: 10 N east and 10 N north, which combine to a diagonal resultant.

Question 12

12. A modeler draws a resultant arrow that points opposite both original same-direction forces. Evaluate the model and describe how the resultant should be revised.

Work / rationale: The model is incorrect. Same-direction force vectors add, so the resultant must point in that same direction and have a magnitude equal to the sum of the original magnitudes.

Question 13

13. Which description is a vector?

  1. 12 kg
  2. 6 seconds
  3. 25 N south
  4. 4 liters
  5. 18°C
Answer: C. 25 N south.
Rationale: A vector needs both magnitude and direction. “25 N south” gives a force magnitude of 25 N and a direction of south.

Question 14

14. What does the magnitude of a force vector describe?

  1. Its size or strength
  2. Its source only
  3. Its direction only
  4. The object’s speed
  5. The object’s mass
Answer: A. Its size or strength.
Rationale: Magnitude tells how large or strong a vector is. Direction is represented separately by the arrow’s orientation.

Question 15

15. Forces of 11 N left and 7 N left act on the same object. The net force is

  1. 4 N left
  2. 18 N left
  3. 18 N right
  4. 4 N right
  5. 0 N
Answer: B. 18 N left.
Rationale: Both forces point left, so their magnitudes add: 11 N + 7 N = 18 N left.

Question 16

16. Forces of 22 N right and 9 N left act on an object. The net force is

  1. 31 N right
  2. 13 N left
  3. 13 N right
  4. 31 N left
  5. 0 N
Answer: C. 13 N right.
Rationale: The forces oppose each other, so subtract magnitudes: 22 N − 9 N = 13 N. The larger force points right, so the net force is 13 N right.

Question 17

17. What is the resultant?

  1. The sum of only magnitudes
  2. A single vector representing the combined vectors
  3. The longest original arrow
  4. A scalar answer
  5. The object’s direction of motion
Answer: B. A single vector representing the combined vectors.
Rationale: The resultant is the one vector that has the same combined effect as the original vectors.

Question 18

18. Which pair of perpendicular forces could have a diagonal resultant?

  1. Two forces on the same straight line
  2. One east and one north
  3. One right and one left only
  4. Two forces both west
  5. Two equal downward forces
Answer: B. One east and one north.
Rationale: Perpendicular east and north components combine to make a resultant pointing diagonally between them.
