Problem 13

Two horizontal forces act: 18 N right and 7 N left. Predict the resultant direction before calculating.

Answer: Right, because the force in that direction is larger.

Problem 14

Use the vector diagrams to explain when one-dimensional force magnitudes should be added and when they should be subtracted.

Answer: Add magnitudes when forces point in the same direction. Subtract magnitudes when they point in opposite directions, with the resultant pointing toward the larger force.

Problem 15

A student combines 12 N right and 5 N left and reports 17 N right. Diagnose the error.

Answer: The forces oppose, so subtract magnitudes: 7 N right.

Problem 16

Without drawing the resultant, state the resultant direction from the scaled arrows.

Answer: Left.

Problem 17

Rank the three force models by net-force magnitude, greatest to least.

Answer: Net-force magnitudes are A=8 N, B=0 N, C=5 N; ranking A > C > B.

Problem 18

The given forces are 4 N right and 10 N left. A student draws the shown resultant. What should be corrected?

Answer: The correct resultant is 6 N left; the resultant arrow must have that direction and corresponding scale length.

Problem 19

Model this verbally as a 1-D vector situation: a wagon has 9 N east and 4 N west. What arrows would you draw?

Answer: Draw a 9 N arrow east and a 4 N arrow west from the wagon.

Problem 20

An object’s force diagram has a net force to the right. What does that tell you about the direction of its acceleration or motion change?

Answer: The acceleration/velocity change is toward the right.

Problem 21

A cart has 17 N right and 6 N left. Determine the net force.

Answer: 11 N right.

Problem 22
