Problem 13

Two horizontal forces act: 9 N right and 14 N left. Predict the resultant direction before calculating.

Answer: Left, because the force in that direction is larger.

Problem 14

Use the vector diagrams to explain when one-dimensional force magnitudes should be added and when they should be subtracted.

Answer: Add magnitudes when forces point in the same direction. Subtract magnitudes when they point in opposite directions, with the resultant pointing toward the larger force.

Problem 15

A student combines 9 N left and 4 N right and reports 13 N left. Diagnose the error.

Answer: The forces oppose, so subtract magnitudes: 5 N left.

Problem 16

Without drawing the resultant, state the resultant direction from the scaled arrows.

Answer: Right.

Problem 17

Rank the three force models by net-force magnitude, greatest to least.

Answer: Net-force magnitudes are A=8 N, B=6 N, C=0 N; ranking A > B > C.

Problem 18

The given forces are 9 N left and 2 N right. A student draws the shown resultant. What should be corrected?

Answer: The correct resultant is 7 N left; the resultant arrow must have that direction and corresponding scale length.

Problem 19

Model this verbally as a 1-D vector situation: a crate has 6 N west and 2 N east. What arrows would you draw?

Answer: Draw a 6 N arrow west and a 2 N arrow east from the crate.

Problem 20

An object’s force diagram has a net force to the left. What does that tell you about the direction of its acceleration or motion change?

Answer: The acceleration/velocity change is toward the left.

Problem 21

A cart has 14 N left and 9 N right. Determine the net force.

Answer: 5 N left.

Problem 22

A crate has 7 N right and an unknown force left. The resultant is 6 N left. Find the unknown force.

Answer: 13 N left.

Problem 23

On a scale of 1 cm = 2.5 N, the diagram shows a 4 cm arrow left and a 1 cm arrow right. Determine the resultant magnitude and direction.

Answer: 10 N left and 2.5 N right give 7.5 N left.

Problem 24

Stage 1: a cart has 16 N left and 7 N right. Determine the resultant. Stage 2: an additional 3 N force right is added. Use the Stage 1 result to determine the new net force.

Answer: Stage 1: 9 N left. Stage 2: 6 N left.
