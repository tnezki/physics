Problem 1

A student reports only “the force is 12 N.” Explain what information is missing for a complete vector description.

Solution: Direction is missing. A complete force vector needs both magnitude (12 N) and direction.
Problem 2

Use the diagram to explain how resultant direction and magnitude depend on the component arrows.

Solution: Add the component vectors with direction included. The resultant’s direction follows the vector sum, and its magnitude depends on both component magnitudes and their directions.

Problem 3

Describe a reliable method for finding a resultant from a simple force-arrow diagram.

Solution: Choose a positive direction, read each arrow’s magnitude and direction, add same-direction forces and subtract opposite-direction forces, then report the resultant magnitude and direction.
Problem 4

Rank the displayed force arrows by magnitude and state the visual evidence used for the ranking.

Solution: Rank the arrows from longest to shortest; equal lengths tie. Arrow length is the visual evidence for magnitude.

Problem 5

Use the opposing-force diagram to determine the resultant direction from the relative arrow lengths.

Solution: The resultant points toward the longer/larger-magnitude opposing arrow; equal opposing lengths would give zero resultant.
Problem 6

Use the diagram to explain what happens when two equal-magnitude forces point in exactly opposite directions.

Solution: They cancel, producing a resultant force of 0 N.
