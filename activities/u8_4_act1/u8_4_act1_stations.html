Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. State the condition required for induction rather than just the presence of a magnet and coil.

Answer: Induction requires a change in the magnetic conditions through a conductor/coil (a changing magnetic flux), not merely a stationary magnet beside a stationary coil.

Question 2

2. What part of a simple generator is moved to keep magnetic conditions changing?

Answer: In a simple generator, the coil or magnet is rotated/moved relative to the other so the magnetic conditions through the coil keep changing.

Question 3

3. A generator converts mechanical input mainly into what output energy form?

Answer: A generator converts mechanical input energy mainly into electrical output energy.

Question 4

4. What does one complete cycle mean on a repeating AC voltage graph?

Answer: One cycle is one complete repetition of the AC voltage pattern, returning to the same phase/point in the repeating waveform.

Question 5

5. How is a motor’s main energy transformation opposite a generator’s?

Answer: A motor converts electrical energy to mechanical motion; a generator performs the reverse main conversion, mechanical energy to electrical energy.

Question 6

6. Use the generator source diagram to trace mechanical input, changing magnetic conditions, induced voltage, and electrical output in order.

Answer: Mechanical input turns/moves the generator → the magnetic conditions through the conductor change → electromagnetic induction produces a voltage → current/electrical energy can be delivered to the external circuit.

Question 7

7. A student rotates a generator coil twice as fast while the field and coil stay the same. Predict the qualitative effect on how frequently the voltage pattern repeats.

Answer: The voltage pattern should repeat more frequently. If the coil completes rotations twice as fast, comparable waveform cycles occur in less time, so frequency increases (ideally about doubles).

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. An AC graph shows 5 complete cycles during 2.5 s. Calculate its frequency.

Work: $f=\text{cycles}/t=5/2.5=2\text{ Hz}$. Answer: $2\text{ Hz}$.

Question 9

9. Two trials use the same magnet and coil. Trial A changes magnetic conditions rapidly; Trial B changes them slowly. Which should produce the larger induced-voltage magnitude and why?

Answer: Trial A should produce the larger induced-voltage magnitude because its magnetic conditions change at a greater rate.

Question 10

10. A generator is connected to a lamp. Explain why the lamp’s electrical energy ultimately traces back to the mechanical work that keeps the generator turning.

Answer: The lamp’s electrical energy is supplied by the generator, and the generator receives mechanical work from the agent turning it. The magnetic field enables induction; it does not create the output energy.

Question 11

11. A student sketches an AC generator graph that never drops below zero. Explain why that is inconsistent with a simple alternating output and describe the missing feature.

Answer: A simple AC output must reverse sign/polarity. The sketch is missing the negative portion below zero (and the zero crossings between positive and negative halves of the cycle).

Question 12

12. Two generator designs have the same magnet and coil, but one changes the magnetic conditions through the coil faster. Explain why the output should differ and what measurement would test the claim.

Answer: Faster change in magnetic conditions should increase induced-voltage magnitude. Test by controlling the magnet/coil and measuring peak or RMS voltage while the rate of motion/change is varied.
