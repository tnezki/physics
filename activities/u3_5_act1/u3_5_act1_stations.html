Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. After a ball leaves a launcher and air resistance is ignored, why is it considered a projectile?

Answer / rationale: After launch, the object is no longer supported or pushed by the launcher; with air resistance neglected, gravity is the only significant force, so it is a projectile.

Question 2

2. Which component of ideal projectile velocity is unaffected by gravity?

Answer: The horizontal component of velocity is unaffected by gravity in the ideal projectile model. Rationale: With air resistance neglected, gravity acts vertically, so the horizontal net force and horizontal acceleration are zero.

Question 3

3. Which component of velocity changes continuously because of gravity?

Answer: The vertical component of velocity changes continuously. Rationale: Gravity produces a nearly constant downward vertical acceleration throughout the flight.

Station 2 Answers

Question 4

4. At the top of the path, is the projectile’s acceleration zero or downward?

Answer: Downward. Rationale: At the top, vertical velocity is momentarily zero, but gravity still acts, so vertical acceleration remains about $g$ downward.

Question 5

5. What does it mean to say horizontal and vertical components are independent?

Rationale: Independence means horizontal motion does not determine the vertical acceleration or fall time, and vertical motion does not change the ideal horizontal velocity. Both components occur simultaneously over the same time.

6. A projectile has horizontal velocity 6 m/s for 5 s. Calculate horizontal range over that interval.

Work: $x=v_xt=(6\text{ m/s})(5\text{ s})=30\text{ m}$.

Question 7

7. Use the dropped-versus-projected source image. Compare the vertical positions at equal times and explain what this shows about the independence of horizontal and vertical motion.

Rationale: At equal times, the dropped ball and horizontally launched ball have the same vertical positions. Their horizontal motions differ, but their vertical motion is the same, showing that horizontal velocity does not change the gravitational fall.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. A ball starts with zero vertical velocity and is in the air 1.5 s. Use $gpprox10 ext{ m/s}^2$ to estimate its vertical drop.

Work: $y=\tfrac12gt^2=\tfrac12(10)(1.5)^2=11.25\text{ m}$. Answer: About $11.3\text{ m}$ downward.

Question 9

9. Use the height-versus-horizontal-position graph. Estimate where the projectile reaches its greatest height, then state what is true about its vertical velocity and vertical acceleration there.

Answer / rationale: The graph peaks at about $x=5\text{ m}$ (height about $10\text{ m}$). At that point $v_y=0$, but $a_y$ is still downward at about $10\text{ m/s}^2$.

Question 10

10. A ball is launched faster horizontally from the same table height. Compare its fall time with that of a slower horizontal launch and compare their horizontal ranges.

Rationale: From the same height with the same initial vertical motion, both projectiles have the same ideal fall time. The faster horizontal launch covers more horizontal distance during that same time, so it has greater range.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A student says the projectile is momentarily at rest at the top because vertical velocity is zero. Explain what component the student forgot and state the acceleration there.

Rationale: The student forgot the horizontal velocity component. At the top, only $v_y=0$; $v_x$ remains nonzero, so the projectile is still moving. Its acceleration is still downward at about $g$.

Question 12

12. A drawn path becomes horizontal after the highest point. Critique the path by using the continuing downward acceleration and constant horizontal velocity.

Rationale: After the highest point, gravity continues to produce downward acceleration, so the vertical component becomes increasingly downward while horizontal velocity remains constant. The path must curve downward rather than become horizontal.

A. zero

E. increasing
Answer: A — zero
Work / rationale: With air resistance ignored, there is no horizontal net force, so horizontal acceleration is zero.

Answer: A — horizontal range but not ideal fall time

Question 20

B. Gravity reduces horizontal speed in the ideal model.
C. Horizontal speed determines vertical acceleration.
D. Vertical motion supplies a forward force.

Work / rationale: Gravity changes the vertical component while ideal horizontal velocity stays constant because the components are independent.
