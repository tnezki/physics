Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. What does a watt measure in an electric device?

Answer / rationale: A watt measures power, the rate of energy transfer. $1\text{ W}=1\text{ J/s}$.

Question 2

2. In $P=IV$, what three quantities are related?

Answer / rationale: $P=IV$ relates power $P$, current $I$, and voltage $V$.

Question 3

3. What does watt-hour measure: power or energy?

Answer / rationale: A watt-hour is a unit of energy, because it is power multiplied by time.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. Use the current–time figure to distinguish direct current from alternating current, then explain why either type can still transfer electrical energy to a device.

Answer / rationale: Direct current keeps one direction/sign with time, while alternating current repeatedly reverses direction. Either can transfer energy because an electric field can do work on charges in a device even though AC charges reverse drift direction each cycle.

Question 9

9. A 1000 W microwave runs for 0.20 h and a 100 W lamp runs for 3 h. Compare their electrical energy use.

Work: Microwave: $(1000\text{ W})(0.20\text{ h})=200\text{ Wh}$. Lamp: $(100\text{ W})(3\text{ h})=300\text{ Wh}$. Answer: the lamp uses 100 Wh more energy in these intervals.

Question 10

10. A device uses 360 Wh while operating at constant 90 W. Determine its operating time.

Work: $t=E/P=360\text{ Wh}/90\text{ W}=4\text{ h}$. Answer: $4\text{ h}$.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A classmate says “high power means high energy use no matter how briefly the device runs.” Use a short numerical counterexample and $E=Pt$ to critique the claim.

Work / rationale: $E=Pt$ depends on both power and time. For example, a 1000 W device used for 0.10 h uses 100 Wh, while a 100 W device used for 2 h uses 200 Wh. The lower-power device can therefore use more total energy.

Question 12

12. Two bulbs provide similar visible light but draw different electrical power. Explain what additional evidence would be needed to make a defensible efficiency claim rather than only a power comparison.

Answer / rationale: To claim greater efficiency, compare useful light output with electrical energy input under the same conditions/time. Power alone gives input rate; efficiency requires evidence about the useful output per input energy.

Question 19

19. An LED and incandescent lamp give similar useful light. The LED draws much less power. What is the strongest supported claim?

A. The LED must have a shorter life.
B. Their energy use is identical for all times.
C. Wattage directly measures visible brightness.

Question 20

20. A solar array is labeled 5 kW peak. Why is that not enough to predict daily energy?

A. Actual power varies with conditions and energy also depends on operating time.
B. Peak power already equals daily energy.
C. Solar angle never matters.
D. Energy cannot be measured from power.
E. Panels always operate at peak rating.
Answer: A — Actual power varies with conditions and energy also depends on operating time.
Work / rationale: Daily energy depends on actual power over time. A peak 5 kW rating does not specify how long or how often the array actually produces that power.
