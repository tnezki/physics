7. A lens has $f=15$ cm and $d_o=45$ cm. Calculate $d_i$.

Work: $1/f=1/d_o+1/d_i$. So $1/d_i=1/15-1/45=2/45$, giving $d_i=22.5\text{ cm}$. Answer: $22.5\text{ cm}$.

Unit 6.4 · Activity 1 · Lenses and Optical SystemsSolution 9
Solution 9
DOK 2

9. A converging lens forms an upright enlarged view when the object is inside the focal length. Explain why this image is virtual rather than projectable.

Rationale: With the object inside the focal length, the outgoing rays from a converging lens remain diverging. The eye back-traces them to an enlarged upright location on the object side, so no real rays meet there and the image is virtual.

10. A camera is out of focus on a nearby object. Explain why changing lens-to-sensor or effective lens position can restore the meeting point of rays on the detector.

Rationale: For a nearer object, the thin-lens geometry changes the image distance. Moving the lens relative to the fixed sensor (or changing the effective lens position) places the sensor at the new ray-convergence plane, restoring sharp focus.

11. A classmate claims a diverging lens can make parallel rays meet at a real focal point on the far side. Critique the claim with a ray model.

Rationale: A diverging lens does not bring parallel rays to a real point on the far side. The rays spread after refraction; only their backward extensions meet at the near-side virtual focal point.

12. Two projector diagrams are proposed: one has rays actually meeting on the screen, the other only has backward extensions meeting. Decide which can form the wall image and justify.

Rationale: A projector screen needs real rays to actually meet on the screen. Therefore the diagram with physical ray convergence can form the wall image; a backward-extension intersection represents a virtual image and cannot be projected there.
