Answer / rationale: A diverging lens spreads an initially parallel bundle apart.

Question 2

2. What is the principal axis in a simple lens diagram?

Answer / rationale: The principal axis is the central reference line through the lens/optical center and focal points in the simple thin-lens model.

Question 3

3. Distinguish object distance from image distance.

Answer / rationale: Object distance $d_o$ is measured from the lens to the object. Image distance $d_i$ is measured from the lens to the image location (with sign conventions distinguishing real/virtual cases).

Question 4

4. Can a virtual image be projected onto a screen at its apparent location?

Answer / rationale: No. A virtual image cannot be projected onto a screen at its apparent location because the actual rays do not converge there.

Answer / rationale: A simple magnifying glass uses a converging lens with the object placed inside its focal length.

Station 3 Answers

Question 6

6. Use the diverging-lens figure to explain how the outgoing rays behave and where their backward extensions appear to originate.

Rationale: After a diverging lens, initially parallel rays spread apart. Their backward extensions on the object side of the lens appear to originate from the near focal point, producing a virtual focus/image construction.

Question 7

7. A lens has $f=15$ cm and $d_o=45$ cm. Calculate $d_i$.

Work: $1/f=1/d_o+1/d_i$. So $1/d_i=1/15-1/45=2/45$, giving $d_i=22.5\text{ cm}$. Answer: $22.5\text{ cm}$.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. An image is 3 times the object height. State the magnification magnitude and explain what the ratio means.

Work / rationale: $|m|=|h_i/h_o|=3$. Answer: magnification magnitude $3$; the image height is three times the object height.

Question 9

9. A converging lens forms an upright enlarged view when the object is inside the focal length. Explain why this image is virtual rather than projectable.

Rationale: With the object inside the focal length, the outgoing rays from a converging lens remain diverging. The eye back-traces them to an enlarged upright location on the object side, so no real rays meet there and the image is virtual.

Question 10

10. A camera is out of focus on a nearby object. Explain why changing lens-to-sensor or effective lens position can restore the meeting point of rays on the detector.

Rationale: For a nearer object, the thin-lens geometry changes the image distance. Moving the lens relative to the fixed sensor (or changing the effective lens position) places the sensor at the new ray-convergence plane, restoring sharp focus.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A classmate claims a diverging lens can make parallel rays meet at a real focal point on the far side. Critique the claim with a ray model.

Rationale: A diverging lens does not bring parallel rays to a real point on the far side. The rays spread after refraction; only their backward extensions meet at the near-side virtual focal point.

Question 12

12. Two projector diagrams are proposed: one has rays actually meeting on the screen, the other only has backward extensions meeting. Decide which can form the wall image and justify.

Rationale: A projector screen needs real rays to actually meet on the screen. Therefore the diagram with physical ray convergence can form the wall image; a backward-extension intersection represents a virtual image and cannot be projected there.

A. 18 cm
B. 24 cm
C. 48 cm
D. 12 cm
E. 36 cm
Answer: A — 18 cm
Work / rationale: $1/d_i=1/12-1/36=2/36=1/18$, so $d_i=18\text{ cm}$.

Work / rationale: A magnifier’s usual enlarged image is virtual; the eye locates it by back-tracing diverging outgoing rays.

Question 20

20. A ray diagram shows a parallel ray through a converging lens bending away from the far focal point. The best critique is

A. the standard construction rule sends that ray toward the far focal point
B. all lens rays bend away from the axis
C. the ray should reflect instead
D. focal points are used only for mirrors
E. parallel rays never change direction
Answer: A — the standard construction rule sends that ray toward the far focal point
Work / rationale: A standard converging-lens construction sends a ray initially parallel to the principal axis toward the far focal point.
