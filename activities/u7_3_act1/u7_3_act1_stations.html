Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. What electrical quantity describes opposition to charge motion through a device?

Answer / rationale: The quantity is resistance, measured in ohms. Resistance describes how strongly a device opposes charge motion.

Question 2

2. How does increasing wire length affect resistance when other variables stay fixed?

Answer / rationale: Increasing wire length increases resistance when material and cross-sectional area are unchanged because charge carriers travel through more material and experience more interactions.

Question 3

3. Which symbols appear in $V=IR$?

Answer / rationale: $V=IR$ relates voltage $V$, current $I$, and resistance $R$.

Station 2 Answers

Question 4

4. If voltage is doubled across an unchanged resistor, how does current change?

Answer / rationale: For unchanged resistance, $I=V/R$. Doubling voltage therefore doubles current.

Question 5

5. If resistance is doubled while voltage stays fixed, how does current change?

Answer / rationale: At fixed voltage, $I=V/R$. Doubling resistance therefore makes current one-half as large.

Question 6

6. A 24 V source produces 3 A through a resistor. Find the resistance.

Work: $R=V/I=24\text{ V}/3\text{ A}=8\ \Omega$. Answer: $8\ \Omega$.

7. A 4 Ω resistor carries 2 A. Calculate the voltage across it.

Work: $V=IR=(2\text{ A})(4\ \Omega)=8\text{ V}$. Answer: $8\text{ V}$.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. Use the hose comparison to explain why a wider conducting path can be associated with less opposition and a larger flow rate under the same driving difference.

Answer / rationale: A wider hose offers less restriction to water flow; similarly, a conductor with greater cross-sectional area provides more available pathways for mobile charge. Under the same driving voltage, lower resistance allows a larger current.

Question 9

9. Two copper wires have equal thickness but lengths of 1 m and 4 m. Predict which has higher resistance and explain the particle/path reason.

Answer / rationale: The 4 m wire has greater resistance. Charge carriers must move through four times the length of material, increasing the number of interactions that impede their drift.

Question 10

10. A resistor has constant resistance 8 Ω. Calculate the currents at 8 V and 24 V, then describe the pattern.

Work: At 8 V, $I=V/R=8/8=1\text{ A}$. At 24 V, $I=24/8=3\text{ A}$. Pattern: with constant resistance, tripling voltage triples current.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A classmate says resistance “uses up current” as charges pass through. Replace the statement with an Ohm’s Law explanation of how resistance affects the current established in the complete circuit.

Answer / rationale: Resistance does not consume current. In a complete circuit, the source voltage and total resistance determine the steady current through $I=V/R$; a larger resistance means a smaller current is established for the same voltage.

Question 12

12. A device heats during operation and its $V$–$I$ graph becomes progressively steeper. Explain how temperature-dependent resistance could account for the pattern.

Answer / rationale: On a $V$-versus-$I$ graph, slope is $V/I=R$. A progressively steeper graph means the effective resistance is increasing. Heating a filament can raise its resistance, so each additional ampere requires more voltage.

Question 16

16. An unchanged resistor carries 1 A at 5 V. What current should it carry at 15 V if it remains ohmic?

A. 15 A
B. 1/3 A
C. 1 A
D. 5 A
E. 3 A
Answer: E — 3 A
Work / rationale: The resistor is unchanged, so current is proportional to voltage: $15/5=3$, giving $I=3(1\text{ A})=3\text{ A}$.

Question 17

17. At a fixed 20 V, changing a resistor from 5 Ω to 10 Ω makes current

A. twice as large
B. four times as large
C. half as large
D. unchanged
E. zero by definition
Answer: C — half as large
Work / rationale: At fixed voltage, doubling resistance from 5 $\Omega$ to 10 $\Omega$ halves current because $I=V/R$.

Question 18

18. A straight $V$–$I$ line has a slope of 6 V/A. What physical quantity is 6?

A. voltage in volts
B. charge in coulombs
C. time in seconds
D. resistance in ohms
E. current in amperes
Answer: D — resistance in ohms
Work / rationale: On a $V$-versus-$I$ graph, slope is $V/I=R$; $6\text{ V/A}=6\ \Omega$.
