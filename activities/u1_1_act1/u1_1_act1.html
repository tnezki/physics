Whiteboard Indy
Multiple Representations of Relationships
Question sets: Set 1 / Set 2
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Individual
Materials: 1 whiteboard + marker per student
StructureIndividual → Independent practice
SetupEach student gets a whiteboard and one marker.
Directions
  1. Notes are welcome
  2. Try something.
  3. Write large and clearly — your work should be readable from across the room.
  4. Use your resources: ask your partner first, then the teacher. Do not copy.
  5. Mistakes are part of learning — fix them together and discuss what went wrong.
Goal: Individual accountability + low-stakes problem entry
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Every student begins the problem on their own board.
  • Work is large enough to read during a quick class scan.
  • Students use mistakes as a reason to revise and explain.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • Students wait for a nearby board before beginning.
  • Answers are copied without discussing the reasoning.
  • Boards are erased immediately when an answer is wrong.

Whiteboard Partners
Multiple Representations of Relationships
Question sets: Set 1 / Set 2
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Partners
Materials: 1 whiteboard + marker per pair
StructureFast partner practice
SetupWork with your partner. One whiteboard and one marker between you.
Directions
  1. Work with a partner.
  2. Both students stay engaged.
  3. Alternate who writes.
  4. Write clearly and large.
  5. Stay focused and collaborate.
  6. Fix mistakes and discuss.
Goal: Engagement + quick feedback
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Partners alternate who controls the marker from problem to problem.
  • Both partners explain or check the reasoning before showing an answer.
  • Pairs correct errors together rather than silently replacing an answer.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • One partner keeps the marker for every problem.
  • The non-writing partner watches without contributing.
  • Pairs copy another group instead of discussing their own work.

Rally Coach
Multiple Representations of Relationships
Question sets: Set 1 / Set 2
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Partners
Materials: 1 whiteboard + marker per student
StructurePartner explanation + alternating roles
SetupSit with your partner. Each person needs one whiteboard and one marker.
Directions
  1. Work with a partner
  2. Partner A explains, Partner B writes
  3. Switch roles
  4. Check answers with teacher
  5. Rotate partners
Goal: Verbal reasoning + procedural accuracy
✓ Looks Like Success
  • The explaining partner talks through the reasoning instead of taking the marker.
  • The writing partner records what is explained and asks questions when needed.
  • Partners switch roles so both students practice explaining and recording.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • The coach grabs the board and solves the problem for the writer.
  • Partners skip the explanation and only compare final answers.
  • The same student remains the coach for every round.

Speed Dating Math
Multiple Representations of Relationships
Question sets: Set 1 / Set 2
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Rotating partners
Materials: 1 whiteboard or paper per student
StructurePaired practice → Timed rotation → Strategy sharing
SetupPartners. One whiteboard or blank paper for each person
Directions
  1. Work with a partner on the projected problem — both students write on their own paper
  2. Compare strategies and methods with your partner before rotating
  3. After 3–4 minutes, one row shifts one seat to create a new pairing
  4. Bring your previous work and share your strategy with your new partner
  5. Repeat across multiple problems and partner pairings
Goal: Multiple representations + collaborative problem solving
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Both students record their own work before the rotation.
  • Partners compare methods, not just final answers.
  • Students carry prior reasoning forward and explain it to each new partner.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • Only one student in each pair does the writing.
  • Students rotate before comparing strategies.
  • A new pairing starts by copying previous work without explanation.

Showdown
Multiple Representations of Relationships
Question sets: Set 1 / Set 2
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Groups of 4
Materials: 1 whiteboard + marker per student
StructureIndy → Group comparison → Consensus
SetupGroups of 4. Each person has a whiteboard. Rotate the Captain each round.
Directions
  1. Each student solves the problem individually
  2. When prompted, students Showdown (reveal answers at the same time)
  3. Discuss differences and justify reasoning
  4. Group agrees on a correct final answer
  5. Repeat
Goal: Accountability + justification
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Every student commits to an answer before boards are revealed.
  • Boards are revealed together on the signal.
  • Groups discuss differences and justify the consensus answer.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • Students reveal early and others change answers to match.
  • One student announces an answer before everyone has worked.
  • The group votes on an answer without discussing evidence or reasoning.

Think, Trade, Agree
Multiple Representations of Relationships
Question sets: Set 1 / Set 2
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Groups of 4
Materials: 1 whiteboard + marker per student
StructureIndy → Group comparison → Consensus
SetupGroups of 4. Each person has a whiteboard
Directions
  1. Teacher presents problem
  2. Each student solves the problem individually
  3. When all, students done, exchange boards
  4. Discuss differences and justify reasoning
  5. Come to an agreement or ask teacher to help
  6. Repeat exchange with someone different
Goal: Accountability + justification
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Each student finishes an independent attempt before trading.
  • Students read and discuss the reasoning on the board they receive.
  • The group reaches agreement by using evidence and explanation.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • Boards are traded before everyone has attempted the problem.
  • Students erase a peer’s work without discussing it.
  • The group chooses the most common answer without justification.

Round Table
Multiple Representations of Relationships
Question sets: Set 1 / Set 2
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Groups of 4
Materials: 1 whiteboard + 1 marker per group
StructureOne step per student
SetupGroups of 4. One whiteboard and one marker per group.
Directions
  1. Groups of 4. One whiteboard and marker per group.
  2. Each student completes ONE step, then passes the board.
  3. Rotate until the problem is complete.
  4. Compare when the solution is shown.
  5. If everyone understands, celebrate.
Goal: Sequential reasoning + shared responsibility
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Each student adds one clear step, idea, or piece of reasoning before passing the board.
  • Students read the work already on the board before adding their contribution.
  • The group checks the completed response together when the solution is shown.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • One student keeps the board and completes the whole problem.
  • Students pass the board without reading or building on the previous work.
  • Group members copy the shown solution without discussing where their work differed.

Mathematical Hot Seat
Multiple Representations of Relationships
Question sets: Set 1 / Set 2
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Small groups
Materials: 1 whiteboard + marker per student
StructureVerbal description only → Blind solving → Role rotation
SetupGroups. One whiteboard and one marker per member.
Directions
  1. One student per team sits facing away from the screen (the Hot Seat)
  2. Teacher projects a problem — only teammates can see it
  3. Teammates describe the problem using words only — no gestures, no writing
  4. Hot Seat student solves based solely on the verbal description
  5. After solving, the student turns around and compares to the original problem
  6. Rotate the Hot Seat for each round
Goal: Mathematical vocabulary + precise communication
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Teammates use precise mathematical language.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • Teammates point, gesture, or write the problem.

Groups: Individual or partners
Materials: Computer/iPad
StructureIndividual or Partners → Teacher-paced investigation
SetupIndividual or Partner work, computer/iPad
Directions

  2. Work through each screen and at your own pace.
  3. Read each prompt carefully — some screens ask you to type, sketch, move sliders, or plot points.

Stations
Multiple Representations of Relationships
Question sets: Set 1 / Set 2
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Stations, max 4 students
Materials: Printed station pages + record sheet
StructureTimed rotations
Setup4 stations around the room. Pick up a record sheet before you begin.
Directions
  1. Get your record sheet.
  2. Choose a starting station (max 4 students).
  3. Work for 3 minutes.Rotate when teacher signals.
  4. Cannot all go to the same next station.
Goal: Repetition + varied problem exposure
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Groups begin at different stations and keep no more than four students at a station.
  • Students record work before rotating on the teacher signal.
  • Groups spread out to different next stations instead of moving as one cluster.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • Students crowd the same station while others remain empty.
  • Groups rotate early or stay after the signal without teacher direction.
  • Students copy an existing response instead of working the station problem.

Find Someone Who
Multiple Representations of Relationships
Question sets: Set 1 / Set 2
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Individual start, then rotating partners
Materials: Printable worksheet + pencil
StructureMovement + peer interaction
SetupYou will need your record sheet or worksheet and a pencil. Keep it with you as you move around the room.
Directions
  1. Work Alone (5 min): Skim every problem you can, then start solving any problem you can. Leave anything you’re stuck on blank for now.
  2. Find a Partner: Walk around and find someone who is ready to share. Compare your completed answers — discuss any that don’t match.
  3. Work Together (5 min): Choose one problem that either of you left blank. Work through it together. Both partners record the solution on their own sheet.
  4. Find Someone New: Thank your partner and repeat steps 2–3 with a different person each time.
  5. Return to your seat with every problem completed and understood — not just filled in.
Goal: Exposure to multiple explanations
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Students arrive at a partner with some independent work already attempted.
  • Partners compare reasoning and work through one unfinished problem together.
  • Students move to a different partner for the next collaboration.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • Students begin by circulating without attempting any problems.
  • One student supplies answers while the other only copies.
  • Students remain with the same partner for the entire activity.

Tarsia (Puzzle Activity)
Multiple Representations of Relationships
Source: Teacher-selected activity
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Groups of 4
Materials: Printed puzzle pieces
StructureMatching puzzle (self-checking)
SetupGroups of 4. Copies of puzzle
Directions
  1. Students match expressions, answers, or steps.
  2. Pieces form a complete shape (triangle/square).
  3. If correct, puzzle fits perfectly.
Goal: Error detection + pattern recognition
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Students justify each match.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • Pieces are forced without checking.
