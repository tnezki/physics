Multiple Representations of Relationships
Source: practice_set_1_1_2(1).html
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Teams of 3–4
Materials: Student whiteboards + markers
StructureIndividual whiteboard work with team reveal and compare.
SetupTeams of 3–4; each student needs a whiteboard, marker, and eraser.
Directions
  1. Teacher displays one problem page.
  2. Students solve independently on whiteboards without talking.
  3. On “Showdown,” everyone reveals boards at the same time.
  4. Teams compare strategies, correct errors, and prepare for the solution page.
  5. Teacher advances to the solution page for confirmation and discussion.
Goal: Every student commits to a solution before seeing peers’ work.
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Every student writes an answer before the reveal.
  • Teams compare differences after all boards are up.
  • Students revise using evidence from the displayed solution.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • One student writes while others wait to copy.
  • Boards are revealed before everyone has committed.
  • Teams erase mistakes without discussing the reasoning.

Solution 5
Mastery · DOK 1

Perform each rational-number operation, using fraction rules as needed.

  1. $\frac{2}{9}+\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)$
  2. $-\frac{6}{7}-\frac{3}{5}$
  3. $\frac{9}{10}\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)$
  4. $\frac{1}{4}\div\frac{2}{7}$
Solution:
  1. $\frac{2}{9}-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{4}{18}-\frac{9}{18}=-\frac{5}{18}$.
  2. $-\frac{6}{7}-\frac{3}{5}=-\frac{30}{35}-\frac{21}{35}=-\frac{51}{35}$.
  3. $\frac{9}{10}\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)=-\frac{18}{30}=-\frac{3}{5}$.
  4. $\frac{1}{4}\div\frac{2}{7}=\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{7}{2}=\frac{7}{8}$.

Solution 11
Mastery · DOK 3

Construct a step-by-step strategy for evaluating $\left|-4.5+\frac{7}{3}\right|-\left(-\frac{5}{6}\right)$. Your strategy must explain when to use absolute value and when to combine rational numbers.

Solution: First combine inside the absolute value: $-4.5+\frac{7}{3}=-\frac{9}{2}+\frac{7}{3}=-\frac{27}{6}+\frac{14}{6}=-\frac{13}{6}$. Then $\left|-\frac{13}{6}\right|=\frac{13}{6}$. Finally subtracting a negative means add: $\frac{13}{6}+\frac{5}{6}=3$.
