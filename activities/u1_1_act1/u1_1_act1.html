Teacher & Student Moves Guide

Use across the Activity options
I-Can Moves
I Can 1: I can distinguish between an observation, inference, hypothesis, law, and theory.

Teacher Moves

  • Press for specific physics evidence, force interactions, or representation features that support the claim.
  • Ask students to connect the answer to the exact I-can language before moving on.

Student Moves

  • State the physics claim and point to the evidence, force, vector, or model feature that supports it.
  • Use precise vocabulary and revise the explanation when a partner identifies stronger evidence.
I Can 2: I can explain why a claim must be testable to be scientific.

Participation-Structure Moves
Stations

Teacher Moves

  • Monitor for different models and recurring misconceptions.
  • Select useful work for a later whole-class connection.

Student Moves

  • Leave a readable physics explanation or model at each station.
  • Compare reasoning instead of only checking final answers.
Find Someone Who

Teacher Moves

  • Require explanation or checking before a signature.
  • Listen for copied answers without physics reasoning.

Student Moves

  • Explain or check one problem with each partner.
  • Record reasoning you understand, not just a final answer.
Partner / Whiteboard Structures

Teacher Moves

  • Coach with questions rather than giving the next step.
  • Protect individual think time before comparison.

Student Moves

  • Explain one reasoning step at a time.
  • Restate a partner’s reasoning before agreeing or revising.

Whiteboard Indy

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

Individual → Independent practice

Setup

Each student gets a whiteboard and one marker.

Directions
  1. Notes are welcome.
  2. Try something.
  3. Write large and clearly — your work should be readable from across the room.
  4. Use your resources: ask your partner first, then the teacher. Do not copy.
  5. Mistakes are part of learning — fix them together and discuss what went wrong.
Goal: Individual accountability + low-stakes problem entry
Question sets

Set 1 · Set 2

Looks Like Success
  • Everyone has visible work on a board.
  • Students try before asking for an answer.
  • Mistakes are corrected with an explanation.
Doesn’t Look Like
  • Blank boards while waiting for someone else.
  • Copying a neighbor’s work without discussing it.
  • Tiny work that cannot be checked from a distance.

Whiteboard Partners

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

Fast partner practice

Setup

Work with your partner. One whiteboard and one marker between you.

Directions
  1. Work with a partner.
  2. Both students stay engaged.
  3. Alternate who writes.
  4. Write clearly and large.
  5. Stay focused and collaborate.
  6. Fix mistakes and discuss.
Goal: Engagement + quick feedback
Question sets

Set 1 · Set 2

Looks Like Success
  • Both partners can explain the current step.
  • Writing duties alternate across problems.
  • Partners correct errors before moving on.
Doesn’t Look Like
  • One person does all of the writing and thinking.
  • Partners move on without checking disagreements.
  • The board is turned in without discussion.

Rally Coach

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

Partner explanation + alternating roles

Setup

Sit with your partner. Each person needs one whiteboard and one marker.

Directions
  1. Work with a partner.
  2. Partner A explains, Partner B writes.
  3. Switch roles.
  4. Check answers with teacher.
  5. Rotate partners.
Goal: Verbal reasoning + procedural accuracy
Question sets

Set 1 · Set 2

Looks Like Success
  • The explainer talks through the mathematics, not just the answer.
  • The writer records the partner’s reasoning and asks questions.
  • Roles actually switch.
Doesn’t Look Like
  • Both partners work silently and separately.
  • The writer takes over the explanation.
  • Students copy the shown solution without comparing reasoning.

Speed Dating Math

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

Paired practice → Timed rotation → Strategy sharing

Setup

Partners. One whiteboard or blank paper for each person.

Directions
  1. Work with a partner on the projected problem — both students write on their own paper.
  2. Compare strategies and methods with your partner before rotating.
  3. After 3–4 minutes, one row shifts one seat to create a new pairing.
  4. Bring your previous work and share your strategy with your new partner.
  5. Repeat across multiple problems and partner pairings.
Goal: Multiple representations + collaborative problem solving
Question sets

Set 1 · Set 2

Looks Like Success
  • Both partners have their own mathematical work.
  • Students compare methods before the rotation.
  • New partners can explain what they carried forward.
Doesn’t Look Like
  • Students rotate before discussing their strategies.
  • Only one person writes.
  • A new pairing begins by copying instead of explaining.

Showdown

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

Indy → Group comparison → Consensus

Setup

Groups of 4. Each person has a whiteboard. Rotate the Captain each round.

Directions
  1. Each student solves the problem individually.
  2. When prompted, students Showdown (reveal answers at the same time).
  3. Discuss differences and justify reasoning.
  4. Group agrees on a correct final answer.
  5. Repeat.
Goal: Accountability + justification
Question sets

Set 1 · Set 2

Looks Like Success
  • Boards stay hidden until the reveal.
  • Different answers trigger mathematical discussion.
  • The group can justify its final consensus.
Doesn’t Look Like
  • Students peek and change answers before the reveal.
  • The loudest answer becomes the group answer without evidence.
  • One student solves while others wait.

Think, Trade, Agree

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

Indy → Group comparison → Consensus

Setup

Groups of 4. Each person has a whiteboard.

Directions
  1. Teacher presents problem.
  2. Each student solves the problem individually.
  3. When all students are done, exchange boards.
  4. Discuss differences and justify reasoning.
  5. Come to an agreement or ask teacher to help.
  6. Repeat exchange with someone different.
Goal: Accountability + justification
Question sets

Set 1 · Set 2

Looks Like Success
  • Everyone attempts the problem before trading.
  • Students read and question another person’s reasoning.
  • Agreement is based on evidence, not speed.
Doesn’t Look Like
  • Boards are traded before individual thinking happens.
  • Students erase another person’s work without discussion.
  • The group agrees just to finish.

Round Table

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

One step per student

Setup

Groups of 4. One whiteboard and one marker per group.

Directions
  1. Groups of 4. One whiteboard and marker per group.
  2. Each student completes ONE step, then passes the board.
  3. Rotate until the problem is complete.
  4. Compare when the solution is shown.
  5. If everyone understands, celebrate.
Goal: Sequential reasoning + shared responsibility
Question sets

Set 1 · Set 2

Looks Like Success
  • Each pass adds one meaningful step.
  • Students check the previous step before adding their own.
  • Everyone can explain the finished solution.
Doesn’t Look Like
  • One student keeps the board for the whole problem.
  • Students add steps without reading what came before.
  • The group celebrates an answer nobody can explain.

Mathematical Hot Seat

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

Verbal description only → Blind solving → Role rotation

Setup

Groups. One whiteboard and one marker per member.

Directions
  1. One student per team sits facing away from the screen (the Hot Seat).
  2. Teacher projects a problem — only teammates can see it.
  3. Teammates describe the problem using words only — no gestures, no writing.
  4. Hot Seat student solves based solely on the verbal description.
  5. After solving, the student turns around and compares to the original problem.
  6. Rotate the Hot Seat for each round.
Goal: Mathematical vocabulary + precise communication
Question sets

Set 1 · Set 2

Looks Like Success
  • Teammates use precise mathematical language.
  • The Hot Seat student solves only from the verbal description.
  • Roles rotate so everyone practices describing and listening.
Doesn’t Look Like
  • Teammates point, gesture, or write the problem.
  • Someone simply says the answer.
  • The same student stays in the Hot Seat every round.

Rally Coach II

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

Teammate lists steps other 3 write

Setup

Group of 4. Each person needs one whiteboard and one marker.

Directions
  1. Pick Starter.
  2. Starter lists steps to solve; teammates write.
  3. Check answers with teacher.
  4. Rotate starter.
Goal: Verbal reasoning + procedural accuracy
Question sets

Set 1 · Set 2

Looks Like Success
  • The Starter gives mathematical steps in a usable order.
  • All three teammates write and ask clarifying questions.
  • The Starter rotates each round.
Doesn’t Look Like
  • The Starter grabs a board and does the work.
  • Teammates copy silently without listening.
  • The same person leads every problem.

Stations

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

Timed rotations

Setup

Use 6 stations with 4 questions per station. Students pick up a record sheet before beginning.

Directions
  1. Get the record sheet.
  2. Choose a starting station within the posted group-size limit.
  3. Work until the teacher signals rotation.
  4. Rotate as directed; do not crowd one station.
Goal: Repetition + varied problem exposure
Question sets

Set 1 Stations · Set 2 Stations

Looks Like Success
  • Groups spread across the six stations.
  • Students record work for all four problems at a station.
  • Groups rotate only when signaled.
Doesn’t Look Like
  • Several groups crowd one station.
  • Students skip work and copy answers.
  • Groups rotate early and interrupt others.

Find Someone Who

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

Movement + peer interaction

Setup

Students need the printable worksheet/record sheet and a pencil.

Directions
  1. Work alone first and attempt as many problems as possible.
  2. Find a partner and compare completed work.
  3. Work together on one unfinished or disagreed-on problem; both students record the reasoning.
  4. Find someone new and repeat.
  5. Return with the work completed and understood, not merely filled in.
Goal: Exposure to multiple explanations
Question sets

Set 1 Worksheet · Set 2 Worksheet

Looks Like Success
  • Students begin with independent attempts.
  • Partners discuss one unfinished or disputed problem.
  • Students meet more than one partner and keep their own reasoning.
Doesn’t Look Like
  • Walking around before attempting any mathematics.
  • Collecting names or answers without discussion.
  • One student fills in another student’s sheet.

Tarsia (Puzzle Activity)

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

Matching puzzle (self-checking)

Setup

Groups of 4. Copies of puzzle.

Directions
  1. Students match expressions, answers, or steps.
  2. Pieces form a complete shape.
  3. If correct, the puzzle fits.
Goal: Error detection + pattern recognition
Practice-owned source

Open the deployed Tarsia pair file for Section 6.1. Activities does not duplicate the Tarsia export.

Looks Like Success
  • Students justify each match before locking it in.
  • The group uses the completed shape as a self-check.
  • Disagreements are resolved by checking the mathematics.
Doesn’t Look Like
  • Pieces are forced together based only on shape.
  • One student assembles while others watch.
  • A mismatch is ignored instead of checked.
