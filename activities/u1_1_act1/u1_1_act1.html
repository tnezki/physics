Teacher & Student Moves Guide

Use across the Activity options
I-Can Moves
I Can 1: I can distinguish between an observation, inference, hypothesis, law, and theory.

Teacher Moves

  • Ask students to label a statement as observation, inference, hypothesis, law, or theory and justify the label from its role.
  • Press students to separate what was measured from what was concluded.

Student Moves

  • Name the scientific-thinking category before explaining.
  • Point to the feature of the statement that makes the classification fit.
I Can 2: I can explain why a claim must be testable to be scientific.

Teacher Moves

  • Ask what observation or measurement could support or challenge a claim.
  • Have students revise non-testable language into measurable variables.

Student Moves

  • Identify measurable variables and a possible test.
  • Revise vague claims so evidence could count for or against them.
I Can 3: I can use evidence from a short scenario to decide whether a physics explanation is supported and explain why formulas in physics are guides for thinking, not just tools for calculation.

Teacher Moves

  • Ask students to compute or compare the evidence pattern before choosing an explanation.
  • Press for what an equation reveals about the relationship, not just a numerical result.

Student Moves

  • Use the evidence first, then state the supported explanation.
  • Explain what the relationship or calculation means physically.
Participation-Structure Moves
Stations

Teacher Moves

  • Monitor for different models and recurring misconceptions.
  • Select useful work for a later whole-class connection.

Student Moves

  • Leave readable physics reasoning at each station.
  • Compare reasoning instead of only checking final answers.
Find Someone Who

Teacher Moves

  • Require explanation or checking before a signature.
  • Listen for copied answers without physics reasoning.

Student Moves

  • Explain or check one problem with each partner.
  • Record reasoning you understand, not just a final answer.
Partner / Whiteboard Structures

Teacher Moves

  • Coach with questions rather than giving the next step.
  • Protect individual think time before comparison.

Student Moves

  • Explain one reasoning step at a time.
  • Restate a partner’s reasoning before agreeing or revising.

Whiteboard Indy

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

Individual → Independent practice

Setup

Each student gets a whiteboard and one marker.

Directions
  1. Notes are welcome.
  2. Try something.
  3. Write large and clearly — your work should be readable from across the room.
  4. Use your resources: ask your partner first, then the teacher. Do not copy.
  5. Mistakes are part of learning — fix them together and discuss what went wrong.
Goal: Individual accountability + low-stakes problem entry
Question sets

Set 1 · Set 2

Looks Like Success
  • Students explain physics reasoning, not only final answers.
  • Representations and vocabulary are used precisely.
  • Errors are revised after discussion.
Doesn’t Look Like
  • Copying without explanation.
  • One student doing all of the thinking.
  • Moving on without resolving disagreement.

Speed Dating Math

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

Paired practice → Timed rotation → Strategy sharing

Setup

Partners. One whiteboard or blank paper for each person.

Directions
  1. Work with a partner on the projected problem — both students write on their own paper.
  2. Compare strategies and methods with your partner before rotating.
  3. After the teacher-set interval, one row shifts one seat to create a new pairing.
  4. Bring your previous work and share your strategy with your new partner.
  5. Repeat across multiple problems and partner pairings.
Goal: Multiple strategies/representations + collaborative problem solving

Showdown

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

Individual → Simultaneous reveal → Group comparison → Consensus

Setup

Groups of 4. Each student needs a whiteboard and marker; a captain may rotate.

Directions
  1. Solve the projected problem independently.
  2. Keep your work hidden until the captain calls “Showdown.”
  3. Reveal boards at the same time.
  4. Compare reasoning and justify differences.
  5. Reach consensus, revise if needed, and rotate the captain.
Goal: Individual accountability + justification

Structure

Individual → Exchange boards → Compare/justify → Consensus/revision

Setup

Directions

Round Table

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

One step per student

Setup

Groups of 4. One shared whiteboard and marker per group.

Directions
  1. Student 1 completes one reasoning step and passes the board.
  2. Each next student checks the prior work, adds one step, and passes.
  3. Continue until the response is complete.
  4. Compare the group solution when the teacher reveals/checks it.
Goal: Sequential reasoning + shared responsibility

Mathematical Hot Seat

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

Verbal description only → Blind solving → Role rotation

Setup

One student faces away from the projected problem. Teammates can see it; the Hot Seat student cannot.

Directions
  1. Teammates describe the problem using words only.
  2. The Hot Seat student solves from the verbal description.
  3. Compare with the projected problem/solution.
  4. Discuss which scientific language was precise or ambiguous.
  5. Rotate the Hot Seat role.
Goal: Precise mathematical/scientific language + reasoning

Rally Coach II

Thinking Like a Physicist
← Activity Options
Structure

One teammate states steps while the other three write → Rotate starter

Setup

Groups of 4. Each student needs a whiteboard and marker.

Directions
  1. One student states the first reasoning step aloud while all four write it.
  2. The next student states the next step while all four continue writing.
  3. Keep rotating the speaker until the problem is complete.
  4. Compare and correct together.
  5. Rotate who starts on the next problem.
Goal: Verbal reasoning + procedural accuracy
