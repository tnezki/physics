Find Someone Who — Set 1 — Section 7.2: Electric Potential and Current

1. What happens to electric potential energy when like charges are forced closer together?

2. Which unit measures electric potential difference?

3. Define current using charge and time.

4. What two conditions are needed for a steady current: a source of potential difference and what else?

5. Does a bulb use up electric charge, electrical energy, or both? State the better description.

6. A battery gives 75 J of energy to 5 C of charge. Determine its voltage.

7. A current of 3 A flows for 8 s. Determine how much charge passes a point.

8. Use the pump analogy to explain why a maintained pressure difference is more like a battery’s role than a one-time push of water.

9. Two equal charge amounts move through different potential differences, 4 V and 12 V. Compare the energy transferred per coulomb in the two cases.

10. A conducting loop is complete but has no battery, generator, or other source. Explain why a sustained current is not expected.

11. A classmate says current is “stored in the battery and released into the wire.” Critique the model using charges already present throughout the conductor and the source establishing a potential difference.

12. A circuit diagram shows moving charge but no indicated energy source. Explain what is missing from the model if the current is supposed to continue steadily.

13. A separated-charge system is capable of doing electrical work because it has

A. current stored in the wire
B. zero voltage
C. charge sign only
D. friction between objects
E. electric potential energy

14. A 12-V source supplies how much energy per coulomb?

A. 12 J/C
B. 12 C/s
C. 12 C total
D. 12 J total
E. 1/12 J/C

15. A meter reports 0.8 C passing each second. Which quantity is being measured?

A. voltage
B. electric potential energy
C. current
D. charge
E. electric force

16. Why does closing the last switch in a battery–lamp loop matter?

A. It creates the circuit’s electrons.
B. It completes the conducting route needed for sustained charge flow.
C. It makes all charges disappear.
D. It converts voltage into charge.
E. It destroys electric potential energy.

17. Which description best separates the two processes in a glowing lamp?

A. Charge is converted to light.
B. Voltage particles move through the filament.
C. Energy remains stored in the wire.
D. Charge circulates while electrical energy is transferred to light and thermal forms.
E. Current is destroyed inside the filament.

18. A source gives 56 J to 8 C. What is its potential difference?

A. 448 V
B. 48 V
C. 0.14 V
D. 64 V
E. 7 V

19. A 2.5 A current operates for 4 s. How much charge passes?

A. 10 C
B. 0.625 C
C. 6.5 C
D. 2.5 C
E. 40 C

20. A student says the battery “pumps voltage around the loop.” Which revision is best?

A. Voltage is the charge that moves.
B. Current is energy per charge.
C. The source maintains potential difference; charge flow is current and energy is transferred through the circuit.
D. The battery supplies new electrons to replace used ones.
E. Electric force is what flows back.
