Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. What happens to electric potential energy when like charges are forced closer together?

Answer / rationale: The electric potential energy increases. Work must be done against the repulsive electric force to push like charges closer together.

Question 2

2. Which unit measures electric potential difference?

Answer / rationale: Electric potential difference is measured in volts (V), where $1\text{ V}=1\text{ J/C}$.

Question 3

3. Define current using charge and time.

Answer / rationale: Current is the rate at which charge passes a point: $I=Q/t$. Its SI unit is the ampere, or coulomb per second.

Question 4

4. What two conditions are needed for a steady current: a source of potential difference and what else?

Answer / rationale: A steady current needs a source that maintains potential difference and a complete conducting path so charge can circulate continuously.

Question 6

6. A battery gives 75 J of energy to 5 C of charge. Determine its voltage.

Work: $V=E/Q=75\text{ J}/5\text{ C}=15\text{ V}$. Answer: $15\text{ V}$.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. Use the pump analogy to explain why a maintained pressure difference is more like a battery’s role than a one-time push of water.

Answer / rationale: A pump maintains a pressure difference so water can keep flowing through a complete loop; a battery similarly maintains an electric potential difference that can sustain charge flow and energy transfer. A one-time push would not maintain continuous flow.

Question 9

9. Two equal charge amounts move through different potential differences, 4 V and 12 V. Compare the energy transferred per coulomb in the two cases.

Answer / rationale: Voltage is energy per charge. The 4 V case transfers $4\text{ J/C}$, while the 12 V case transfers $12\text{ J/C}$, so the 12 V case transfers three times as much energy per coulomb.

Question 10

10. A conducting loop is complete but has no battery, generator, or other source. Explain why a sustained current is not expected.

Answer / rationale: A complete wire loop by itself does not maintain a potential difference. Without a battery, generator, or other source establishing an electric field and supplying energy, any brief charge redistribution dies out rather than producing sustained current.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A classmate says current is “stored in the battery and released into the wire.” Critique the model using charges already present throughout the conductor and the source establishing a potential difference.

Answer / rationale: Current is not stored inside the battery. Mobile charges are already present throughout the conductor; the battery maintains a potential difference/electric field that causes those charges to drift and transfers energy through the circuit.

Question 12

12. A circuit diagram shows moving charge but no indicated energy source. Explain what is missing from the model if the current is supposed to continue steadily.

Answer / rationale: The model is missing an energy source that maintains potential difference, such as a battery or generator. Without it, a steady current cannot continue indefinitely because no source is continually supplying electrical energy.

Question 20

A. Voltage is the charge that moves.

Work / rationale: The source maintains potential difference; current is charge flow and energy is transferred through the circuit. Voltage itself is not a substance that flows.
