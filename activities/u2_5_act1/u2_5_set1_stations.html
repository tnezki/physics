Problem 1

Draw a velocity vector for an object moving 6 m/s north. Your arrow must communicate both the direction and the relative magnitude.

Solution: Velocity vectors encode direction with arrow orientation and speed with arrow length/scale; labels make the magnitude explicit.

Problem 2

The dots mark equal time intervals. Describe the motion shown, connect the changing spacing to velocity, and critique the claim “equal time intervals always mean constant velocity.”

Solution: In a motion diagram made at equal time intervals, larger spacing means greater speed. Changing spacing is evidence of changing velocity.
