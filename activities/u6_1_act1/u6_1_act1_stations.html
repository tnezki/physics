Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. Explain why light can travel through empty space even though sound cannot.

Answer / rationale: Light is an electromagnetic wave, so it does not require a material medium and can propagate through a vacuum. Sound is a mechanical wave and needs interacting particles in a medium.

Question 2

2. Which quantity counts wave cycles per second, and which quantity measures spacing between corresponding points on a wave?

Answer / rationale: Frequency counts cycles per second (Hz). Wavelength is the spacing between corresponding points on successive cycles, such as crest-to-crest.

Question 3

3. Name one electromagnetic region with lower frequency than visible light and one with higher frequency.

Answer / rationale: One valid pair is infrared (lower frequency than visible) and ultraviolet (higher frequency than visible). Radio/microwave are also lower; X-rays/gamma rays are also higher.

Question 4

4. A shower curtain passes light but blurs shapes. Which material category best describes it?

Answer / rationale: Translucent. It transmits light but scatters it enough that a sharp image is not preserved.

Question 5

5. What is the difference between an umbra and a penumbra in a shadow?

Answer / rationale: The umbra is the fully shadowed region where the source is completely blocked. The penumbra is the partially shadowed region where only part of an extended source is blocked.

Station 3 Answers

Question 6

6. Use the shadow sequence to explain why moving an object farther from the wall can change the sharpness and size of the shadow region.

Rationale: With an extended source, moving the blocker changes which source-to-screen rays are blocked. As the blocker-wall spacing changes, the geometric spread of fully and partially blocked rays changes, so both shadow size and edge sharpness/penumbra width can change.

Question 7

7. A visible-light wave has frequency $6.0\times10^{14}$ Hz. Calculate its wavelength in vacuum.

Work: $\lambda=c/f=(3.0\times10^8)/(6.0\times10^{14})=5.0\times10^{-7}\text{ m}$. Answer: $5.0\times10^{-7}\text{ m}$ (500 nm).

Question 8

8. Sunlight takes about 500 s to reach Earth. Estimate the Sun–Earth distance using $d=ct$ and $c=3.0\times10^8$ m/s.

Work: $d=ct=(3.0\times10^8)(500)=1.5\times10^{11}\text{ m}$. Answer: about $1.5\times10^{11}\text{ m}$.

Question 9

9. Red light has a longer wavelength than violet light in vacuum. Compare their frequencies and explain without calculating.

Rationale: In vacuum both colors travel at the same speed, and $c=f\lambda$. A longer wavelength therefore requires a lower frequency, so red light has a lower frequency than violet light.

Question 10

10. A material transmits some light, scatters much of it, and prevents a clear image from being seen through it. Explain why “translucent” is a better description than “transparent.”

Rationale: Transparent materials transmit enough organized light to preserve image detail. This material transmits light but strongly scatters it, so blurred shapes are seen; that is the defining behavior of a translucent material.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A classmate says, “A shadow is a substance called darkness that travels from an object to the wall.” Diagnose the error and replace it with a blocked-ray model.

Rationale: A shadow is not a substance traveling to the wall. It is a region receiving less light because an opaque object blocks some source-to-wall ray paths. The ray model should show source rays traveling outward and the blocker preventing selected rays from reaching the screen.

Question 12

12. Two explanations are proposed for vision: one says the eye receives light; the other says the eye emits sensing rays. Identify which fits evidence such as needing a light source to see a non-glowing object, and defend your choice.

Rationale: The receiver model fits the evidence: light from a source reaches the object, reflects/scatters, and then enters the eye. If the eye emitted the sensing rays, turning off the room light would not make a non-glowing object difficult to see.

Question 14

14. For two light waves traveling at the same speed, the one with the longer wavelength must have

A. the lower frequency
B. the higher frequency
C. the same frequency
D. the greater amplitude
E. no measurable period
Answer: A — the lower frequency
Work / rationale: At fixed speed, $c=f\lambda$, so increasing wavelength requires decreasing frequency.

Question 15

15. Radio, visible, and ultraviolet radiation are grouped together because they are all

A. parts of the electromagnetic spectrum
B. mechanical waves
C. visible to the eye
D. longitudinal pressure waves
E. produced only by hot objects
Answer: A — parts of the electromagnetic spectrum
Work / rationale: Radio, visible, and ultraviolet are all electromagnetic-spectrum regions.

Question 19

19. If a light wave keeps the same speed while wavelength doubles, frequency

A. is cut in half
B. doubles
C. stays the same
D. quadruples
E. becomes zero
Answer: A — is cut in half
Work / rationale: With speed fixed in $c=f\lambda$, doubling $\lambda$ requires halving $f$.

Question 20

20. A panel transmits light but only blurred silhouettes are visible. Which evidence best supports “translucent”?

A. Light crosses the panel but is strongly scattered.
B. No light reaches the far side.
C. A sharp image is preserved.
D. The panel emits visible light.
E. The panel is metallic.
Answer: A — Light crosses the panel but is strongly scattered.
Work / rationale: Transmission plus strong scattering is direct evidence of translucency rather than opacity or transparency.
