Unit 6.1 · Activity 1 · Nature of LightSolution 1

DOK 1

1. Explain why light can travel through empty space even though sound cannot.

Answer / rationale: Light is an electromagnetic wave, so it does not require a material medium and can propagate through a vacuum. Sound is a mechanical wave and needs interacting particles in a medium.

6. Use the shadow sequence to explain why moving an object farther from the wall can change the sharpness and size of the shadow region.

Rationale: With an extended source, moving the blocker changes which source-to-screen rays are blocked. As the blocker-wall spacing changes, the geometric spread of fully and partially blocked rays changes, so both shadow size and edge sharpness/penumbra width can change.

7. A visible-light wave has frequency $6.0\times10^{14}$ Hz. Calculate its wavelength in vacuum.

Work: $\lambda=c/f=(3.0\times10^8)/(6.0\times10^{14})=5.0\times10^{-7}\text{ m}$. Answer: $5.0\times10^{-7}\text{ m}$ (500 nm).

9. Red light has a longer wavelength than violet light in vacuum. Compare their frequencies and explain without calculating.

Rationale: In vacuum both colors travel at the same speed, and $c=f\lambda$. A longer wavelength therefore requires a lower frequency, so red light has a lower frequency than violet light.

11. A classmate says, “A shadow is a substance called darkness that travels from an object to the wall.” Diagnose the error and replace it with a blocked-ray model.

Rationale: A shadow is not a substance traveling to the wall. It is a region receiving less light because an opaque object blocks some source-to-wall ray paths. The ray model should show source rays traveling outward and the blocker preventing selected rays from reaching the screen.
