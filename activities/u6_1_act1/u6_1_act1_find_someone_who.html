Find Someone Who — Set 1 — Section 6.1: Nature of Light

1. Explain why light can travel through empty space even though sound cannot.

2. Which quantity counts wave cycles per second, and which quantity measures spacing between corresponding points on a wave?

3. Name one electromagnetic region with lower frequency than visible light and one with higher frequency.

4. A shower curtain passes light but blurs shapes. Which material category best describes it?

5. What is the difference between an umbra and a penumbra in a shadow?

6. Use the shadow sequence to explain why moving an object farther from the wall can change the sharpness and size of the shadow region.

7. A visible-light wave has frequency $6.0\times10^{14}$ Hz. Calculate its wavelength in vacuum.

8. Sunlight takes about 500 s to reach Earth. Estimate the Sun–Earth distance using $d=ct$ and $c=3.0\times10^8$ m/s.

9. Red light has a longer wavelength than violet light in vacuum. Compare their frequencies and explain without calculating.

10. A material transmits some light, scatters much of it, and prevents a clear image from being seen through it. Explain why “translucent” is a better description than “transparent.”

11. A classmate says, “A shadow is a substance called darkness that travels from an object to the wall.” Diagnose the error and replace it with a blocked-ray model.

12. Two explanations are proposed for vision: one says the eye receives light; the other says the eye emits sensing rays. Identify which fits evidence such as needing a light source to see a non-glowing object, and defend your choice.

13. A student compares sound and light. Which feature belongs to light?

A. It can propagate through a vacuum as an electromagnetic wave.
B. It requires air molecules to travel.
C. It is always longitudinal.
D. It carries matter from source to observer.
E. It moves only in transparent solids.

14. For two light waves traveling at the same speed, the one with the longer wavelength must have

A. the lower frequency
B. the higher frequency
C. the same frequency
D. the greater amplitude
E. no measurable period

15. Radio, visible, and ultraviolet radiation are grouped together because they are all

A. parts of the electromagnetic spectrum
B. mechanical waves
C. visible to the eye
D. longitudinal pressure waves
E. produced only by hot objects

16. Wax paper passes light but destroys image detail. The best classification is

A. translucent
B. transparent
C. opaque
D. vacuum
E. reflective only

17. Why does a broad light source often make a fuzzy shadow edge?

A. Some source points are blocked while others still illuminate the edge region.
B. Darkness spreads sideways from the blocker.
C. The screen changes light frequency.
D. The blocker becomes transparent at the edge.
E. The eye sends fewer rays there.

18. Which path correctly explains seeing a chair?

A. lamp → chair → eye
B. eye → chair → lamp
C. chair → darkness → eye
D. eye → air → chair
E. wall → eye → chair

19. If a light wave keeps the same speed while wavelength doubles, frequency

A. is cut in half
B. doubles
C. stays the same
D. quadruples
E. becomes zero

20. A panel transmits light but only blurred silhouettes are visible. Which evidence best supports “translucent”?

A. Light crosses the panel but is strongly scattered.
B. No light reaches the far side.
C. A sharp image is preserved.
D. The panel emits visible light.
E. The panel is metallic.
