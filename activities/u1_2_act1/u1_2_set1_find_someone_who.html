Teacher Key — Activity Set 1
Conceptual Physics • Unit 1 • Section 1.2
1

A loaded grocery cart is harder to start or stop than an empty cart. Explain this using inertia.

Solution: The loaded cart has more mass and therefore more inertia, so it resists changes in velocity more strongly.
2

A parked car rests on level ground. Explain why “no forces act” is incorrect.

Solution: Weight acts downward and the ground’s support force acts upward; the forces balance, so the net force is zero even though forces act.
3

A lamp hangs motionless from a cord. Identify the main forces on the lamp and state the net-force condition.

Solution: Tension acts upward and weight acts downward. Because the lamp is motionless, they balance and the net force is zero.
4

A parachutist descends at terminal speed. Relate drag, weight, and net force.

Solution: At terminal speed, upward drag equals downward weight, so the net force is zero and velocity is constant.

9

A spacecraft drifts in deep space with negligible net force. Predict what happens next and justify with Newton’s First Law.

Solution: It continues moving with constant velocity (same speed and direction) because negligible net force means no acceleration.

  2. Only weight acts because the book is not moving.
  3. Only support force acts.
  4. Inertia acts upward to cancel gravity.
Solution: A. Weight acts downward and support force acts upward; they can balance.
11

An air-track glider moves left while the net force is zero. Predict what happens next and justify with Newton’s First Law.

Solution: It continues moving left at constant velocity while the net force remains zero.
12

A force diagram for a sliding cart includes a forward arrow labeled “inertia force.” Critique the diagram.

Solution: Inertia is not a force and should not be drawn as a force arrow. It is a property of matter that resists changes in velocity.

  1. Balanced; constant velocity means zero net force.
  2. A forward net force is required for motion.

14

A toy car moves on a low-friction track with zero net force. Explain the motion using Newton’s First Law.

Solution: With zero net force, the toy car keeps a constant velocity; no continuing forward net force is required.
15

Use the book-on-table diagram to name the interaction partner associated with each vertical force.

Solution: The downward weight is the Earth-on-book gravitational force; the upward support force is the table-on-book contact force.
16

When a bus stops suddenly, a passenger tends to continue forward. A student calls this a forward “inertia force.” Critique the explanation.

Solution: The passenger’s body tends to maintain its previous velocity because of inertia; there is no forward “inertia force.”

Compare a 12 kg cart and a 3 kg cart. Which has more inertia, and why?

Solution: The 12 kg cart has more inertia because inertia increases with mass.

Solution: Buoyant force acts upward and weight acts downward. They balance, so the net force is zero.

Solution: The individual forces are nonzero (weight down, support up), but their vector sum—the net force—is zero.

  1. The net force is zero; horizontal and vertical components balance.
  2. A forward net force is required for motion.

  4. Inertia is an additional balancing force.
Solution: A. The net force is zero; horizontal and vertical components balance.
