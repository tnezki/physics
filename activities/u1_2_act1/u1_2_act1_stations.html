Question 1

1. Complete this Newton’s First Law idea: unchanged motion continues unless ________. Explain what “unchanged motion” includes.

Work / rationale: Unless a nonzero net force acts. “Unchanged motion” means remaining at rest or moving with constant velocity in a straight line.

Question 2

2. Define inertia without using the word force.

Work / rationale: Inertia is a property of matter: the tendency to resist a change in motion. More mass means more inertia.

Question 3

3. A person stands still on a bathroom scale. Identify the two main vertical forces acting on the person.

Work / rationale: Weight (gravity) acts downward, and the scale’s support/normal force acts upward.

4. A moving object has several forces acting on it, but the forces balance. Does Newton’s First Law allow the object to keep moving? State the allowed motion.

Work / rationale: Yes. With zero net force, it can continue moving in a straight line at constant speed (constant velocity).

Work / rationale: The motion is changing because its direction changes. Therefore the net force is not zero.

Question 6

6. Use the coin-and-card demonstration from the notes. Explain why the card can move sideways while the coin tends to stay nearly above the glass until it begins to fall.

Work / rationale: The card receives a quick sideways force, while the coin receives little horizontal force. Because of inertia, the coin tends to keep its original horizontal motion, so it stays nearly above the glass and then falls when its support is removed.

Question 7

7. A loaded equipment cart and an empty cart are both at rest. A student says the loaded cart has more inertia only because it is harder to push. Explain the deeper relationship between amount of matter and inertia.

Work / rationale: Inertia is tied to the amount of matter (mass), not simply to whether something feels hard to push. The loaded cart has more mass, so it has more inertia and resists a change in motion more strongly.

Question 8

8. A hammer handle is moving downward and stops suddenly. Predict the hammerhead’s tendency immediately after the stop and explain why this is an inertia effect rather than an extra downward force.

Work / rationale: The hammerhead tends to continue moving downward when the handle stops. That tendency is inertia, not an extra downward “inertia force”; actual interactions with the handle are what eventually change the hammerhead’s motion.

Question 9

9. A scale shows 550 N while a person stands still. Explain how the scale can show a nonzero force even though the net force on the person is zero.

Work / rationale: The 550-N scale reading is an upward support force, not the net force. While the person stands still, an approximately 550-N downward weight balances it, so the net force is zero.

Question 10

10. A ball rolls across a floor and slows. Identify what observation shows the motion is changing and explain why that means the net force cannot be zero.

Work / rationale: Its speed is decreasing, so its velocity is changing. A change in motion means the net force is not zero; on a level floor, friction or another opposing interaction can provide that net force.

Question 11

11. A student draws an “inertia force” arrow backward on a passenger when a bus brakes. Critique the diagram and replace the explanation with one that uses Newton’s First Law and actual interactions.

Work / rationale: Inertia is not a force, so an “inertia force” arrow should not be drawn. When the bus brakes, the passenger tends to keep the previous forward velocity; actual forces from the seat, belt, floor, or other contacts act to change the passenger’s motion.

Question 12

12. Two explanations are offered for a puck gliding after leaving a stick. One says the stick leaves a forward force stored in the puck; the other says the puck can continue moving while net force is near zero. Evaluate both explanations using Newton’s First Law.

Work / rationale: The “stored forward force” explanation is incorrect. Newton’s First Law says a puck can keep moving at constant velocity when the net force is near zero; friction or drag, not the absence of a stored push, explains later changes in motion.

Question 13

13. A soccer ball is at rest. If the net force remains zero, what does Newton’s First Law predict?

  1. It remains at rest.
  2. It begins moving slowly.
  3. Its inertia disappears.
  4. Gravity must stop acting.
  5. Its support force becomes zero.
Answer: A. It remains at rest.
Rationale: Newton’s First Law says an object at rest remains at rest when the net force is zero. Individual forces may still act, but they balance.

Question 14

14. A cart already moves north at constant speed. If the net force stays zero, what happens?

  1. It stops.
  2. It speeds up.
  3. It continues north at the same speed.
  4. It turns east.
  5. It must gain a northward force.
Answer: C. It continues north at the same speed.
Rationale: Zero net force means no change in velocity. Since the cart is already moving north at constant speed, it continues with that same velocity.

Question 15

15. Which description treats inertia correctly?

  1. A force that pushes backward
  2. A property of matter related to resistance to motion change
  3. A force created only by high speed
  4. The upward force from a surface
  5. The total of all force vectors
Answer: B. A property of matter related to resistance to motion change.
Rationale: Inertia is not an interaction force. It is a property of matter describing resistance to a change in motion, and it increases with mass.

Question 16

16. Two carts move at different speeds. Cart X has twice as much matter as Cart Y. Which statement is best supported?

  1. Cart Y must have more inertia because it is moving.
  2. Cart X has greater inertia because it has more matter.
  3. Both have zero inertia while moving.
  4. Their inertia is determined only by net force.
  5. Speed alone determines inertia.
Answer: B. Cart X has greater inertia because it has more matter.
Rationale: Cart X has more matter, so it has more mass and therefore more inertia. The carts’ speeds do not determine which has more inertia.

Question 17

17. Which statement correctly distinguishes force from net force?

  1. Net force is one particular interaction.
  2. Force and net force always mean the same thing.
  3. A force is one interaction; net force is the vector combination of all forces on the object.
  4. Net force exists only when no forces act.
  5. Net force is another name for inertia.
Answer: C. A force is one interaction; net force is the vector combination of all forces on the object.
Rationale: A force is one interaction acting on the object. Net force is found by combining all of those force vectors, including their directions.

Question 18

18. A laptop sits motionless on a desk. Which statement is correct?

  1. There are no forces on it.
  2. Its downward weight and upward support force can cancel.
  3. Its weight is zero.
  4. The desk exerts friction upward.
  5. Inertia supplies the upward force.
Answer: B. Its downward weight and upward support force can cancel.
Rationale: The laptop is motionless because its downward weight and the desk’s upward support force can be equal and opposite, giving zero net force.

Question 19

19. Which force is produced by a stretched rope or cable?

  1. weight
  2. support force
  3. tension
  4. inertia
  5. net force
Answer: C. tension.
Rationale: A stretched rope or cable pulls on attached objects; that pulling interaction is called tension.

Question 20

20. Which force can act opposite sliding motion between two surfaces?

  1. weight
  2. friction
  3. inertia
  4. tension
  5. support force
Answer: B. friction.
Rationale: Friction is the contact force that can oppose relative sliding motion between surfaces.
