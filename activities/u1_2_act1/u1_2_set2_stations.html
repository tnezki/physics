Problem 1

When a tablecloth is pulled quickly, dishes tend to stay nearly at rest. Is inertia a force holding them in place?

Solution: No. Inertia is a property of matter, not a holding force.
Problem 2

Which has more inertia, a tennis ball or a bowling ball? Why?

Solution: A bowling ball has more inertia because it has more mass and resists changes in motion more strongly.

Problem 3

A lunchbox rests on a table. Draw or name the forces acting on the lunchbox only.

Solution: Weight acts downward; the table’s support force acts upward.

Problem 1

A book has nonzero upward and downward forces that are equal. Does zero net force mean no forces act?

Solution: No. Individual forces are nonzero, but their vector sum is zero.
Problem 2

A cart on a level low-friction track moves west at constant velocity. What does Newton’s First Law say about the net force while its velocity stays constant?

Solution: The net force is approximately zero while the velocity remains constant.

Set 2 — Station 5 Solutions

Problem 3

Rewrite this statement correctly: “The passenger moves forward because inertia shoves them.”

Solution: The passenger tends to keep the previous velocity because of inertia; inertia is not a shove or force.
Problem 4

For a box resting on a level floor, compare the roles of weight and support force.

Solution: Weight is Earth’s gravitational pull downward; support force is the floor’s contact push upward. They can balance while remaining different interactions.

Problem 3

Use the force diagram to verify the vertical net force.

Solution: 44 N upward − 44 N downward = 0 N; the vertical forces balance.
Problem 4

A sign weighs 95 N and hangs at rest from a vertical cable. Determine the cable force needed for equilibrium. The cable is then limited to 70 N upward. Does the sign remain in equilibrium?

Solution: At rest, cable force must be 95 N upward. With only 70 N upward, the net force is 25 N downward, so equilibrium is lost.
