Problem 11

A cart moves west at constant velocity with zero net force. Predict its motion a moment later.

Problem 12

Problem 13

When a tablecloth is pulled quickly, dishes tend to stay nearly at rest. Is inertia a force holding them in place?

Answer: No. Inertia is a property of matter, not a holding force.

Problem 14

Which has more inertia, a tennis ball or a bowling ball? Why?

Answer: A bowling ball has more inertia because it has more mass and resists changes in motion more strongly.

Problem 15

A lunchbox rests on a table. Draw or name the forces acting on the lunchbox only.

Answer: Weight acts downward; the table’s support force acts upward.

Problem 16

A cart moves left at constant speed. Which horizontal model is consistent: 14 N left and 14 N right, or a larger force left?

Answer: Equal opposite horizontal forces are consistent with constant velocity; an imbalance would change the velocity.

Problem 17

A book has nonzero upward and downward forces that are equal. Does zero net force mean no forces act?

Answer: No. Individual forces are nonzero, but their vector sum is zero.

Problem 18

A cart on a level low-friction track moves west at constant velocity. What does Newton’s First Law say about the net force while its velocity stays constant?

Answer: The net force is approximately zero while the velocity remains constant.

Problem 19

Rewrite this statement correctly: “The passenger moves forward because inertia shoves them.”

Answer: The passenger tends to keep the previous velocity because of inertia; inertia is not a shove or force.

Problem 20

For a box resting on a level floor, compare the roles of weight and support force.

Answer: Weight is Earth’s gravitational pull downward; support force is the floor’s contact push upward. They can balance while remaining different interactions.

Problem 21

A 115 N box rests on a level floor. What upward support force is needed for vertical balance?

Answer: 115 N upward.

Problem 22

A cart has a 24 N force left and zero net horizontal force. What horizontal force must act right?

Answer: 24 N right.

Problem 23

Use the force diagram to verify the vertical net force.

Answer: 44 N upward − 44 N downward = 0 N; the vertical forces balance.

Problem 24

A sign weighs 95 N and hangs at rest from a vertical cable. Determine the cable force needed for equilibrium. The cable is then limited to 70 N upward. Does the sign remain in equilibrium?

Answer: At rest, cable force must be 95 N upward. With only 70 N upward, the net force is 25 N downward, so equilibrium is lost.
