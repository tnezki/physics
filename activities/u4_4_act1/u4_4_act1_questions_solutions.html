1. What quantity changes when positive net work is done on an object?

Answer: Kinetic energy. Rationale: The work-energy theorem states $W_{net}=\Delta KE$, so positive net work increases kinetic energy.

Unit 4.4 · Activity 1 · Conservation of EnergyProblem 10
Problem 10
DOK 2

10. A 4 kg object slows from 6 m/s to 2 m/s. Calculate the change in kinetic energy and interpret the sign of net work.

Unit 4.4 · Activity 1 · Conservation of EnergySolution 10
Solution 10
DOK 2

10. A 4 kg object slows from 6 m/s to 2 m/s. Calculate the change in kinetic energy and interpret the sign of net work.

Work: $KE_i=\tfrac12(4)(6^2)=72\text{ J}$ and $KE_f=\tfrac12(4)(2^2)=8\text{ J}$. Thus $\Delta KE=8-72=-64\text{ J}$, so $W_{net}=-64\text{ J}$. Rationale: The negative sign means net work removed kinetic energy.

Unit 4.4 · Activity 1 · Conservation of EnergySolution 11
Solution 11
DOK 3

11. A student says, “Friction violates conservation because mechanical energy decreases.” Correct the reasoning using a broader energy account.

Rationale: Friction does not destroy energy. Mechanical energy can be transformed into thermal energy, sound, deformation, or energy transferred to the surroundings, while a complete total-energy account remains conserved.
