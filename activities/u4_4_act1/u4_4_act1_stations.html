Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. What quantity changes when positive net work is done on an object?

Answer: Kinetic energy. Rationale: The work-energy theorem states $W_{net}=\Delta KE$, so positive net work increases kinetic energy.

Question 2

2. Define energy transformation.

Answer / rationale: An energy transformation is a change from one form of energy to another while total energy remains conserved.

Question 3

3. Distinguish mechanical energy from total energy in a situation with friction.

Answer / rationale: Mechanical energy here is mainly kinetic plus gravitational potential energy. Total energy also includes nonmechanical forms such as thermal energy, so friction can reduce mechanical energy while total energy remains conserved.

Station 3 Answers

Question 6

6. A falling object has 70 J PE and 20 J KE at one instant. Ignoring other forms, what is the total mechanical energy?

Work: $E_{mech}=PE+KE=70+20=90\text{ J}$. Answer: $90\text{ J}$.

Question 7

7. At a later instant the same ideal object has 25 J PE. If the total remains 90 J, determine its KE.

Work: $KE=E_{total}-PE=90-25=65\text{ J}$. Answer: $65\text{ J}$.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. Use the infrared skid image to identify evidence that kinetic energy was transferred into another form as the bicycle slowed.

Rationale: The infrared image shows warmer regions after braking/skidding. That temperature increase is evidence that some kinetic energy was transformed/transferred into thermal energy as the bicycle slowed.

Question 9

9. A cart’s KE decreases by 48 J. What does the work-energy theorem imply about the net work?

Work: $W_{net}=\Delta KE=-48\text{ J}$. Answer: Net work is $-48\text{ J}$.

Question 10

10. A 4 kg object slows from 6 m/s to 2 m/s. Calculate the change in kinetic energy and interpret the sign of net work.

Work: $KE_i=\tfrac12(4)(6^2)=72\text{ J}$ and $KE_f=\tfrac12(4)(2^2)=8\text{ J}$. Thus $\Delta KE=8-72=-64\text{ J}$, so $W_{net}=-64\text{ J}$. Rationale: The negative sign means net work removed kinetic energy.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A student says, “Friction violates conservation because mechanical energy decreases.” Correct the reasoning using a broader energy account.

Rationale: Friction does not destroy energy. Mechanical energy can be transformed into thermal energy, sound, deformation, or energy transferred to the surroundings, while a complete total-energy account remains conserved.

Question 12

12. Two models describe the same ramp: Model A keeps total energy constant by adding thermal energy as KE and PE decline; Model B simply lowers the total. Defend which model is more complete and explain what evidence could distinguish them.

Rationale: Model A is more complete because it keeps the total account constant by including thermal energy as KE and PE decrease. Evidence such as a measured temperature increase of the surfaces would support that thermal-energy pathway.

16. An ideal coaster has 120 J PE at the top and 20 J PE lower down. If it began nearly at rest, the lower-point KE is approximately

A. 20 J
B. 80 J
C. 100 J

A. −30 J

E. +60 J
Answer: A — −30 J

Work / rationale: Thermal energy $=150-80=70\text{ J}$.
