Question 1

1. The work-energy theorem says net work changes

A. kinetic energy
B. mass
C. contact time
D. momentum conservation
Answer: A — kinetic energy
Work / rationale: The work-energy theorem states $W_{net}=\Delta KE$, so net work changes kinetic energy.

Question 2

2. Conservation of energy says total energy is

A. always kinetic
B. not created or destroyed
C. always mechanical
D. zero after friction
Answer: B — not created or destroyed
Work / rationale: Conservation of energy states that total energy is not created or destroyed.

Question 3

3. Friction often increases

A. mass
B. height
C. thermal energy
D. momentum without interaction
Answer: C — thermal energy
Work / rationale: Friction commonly transforms mechanical energy into thermal energy.

11. A chart shows PE down 40 J and KE up 25 J with no other change. Best critique?

Question 19

19. Which model best obeys conservation?

A. PE decreases and the total simply shrinks.
B. KE increases while no other form changes and total rises.
C. PE decreases while KE and thermal increase by matching total amounts.
D. Thermal energy is omitted because it is less useful.
Answer: C — PE decreases while KE and thermal increase by matching total amounts.
Work / rationale: A conserving model lets decreases in one form be matched by increases in other forms so the total remains constant.

Question 20

20. Why can a stopped car still be part of an energy-conservation story?

A. Zero speed means all energy vanished.
B. Stopping cancels conservation.
C. The car loses mass equal to its KE.
D. Its former kinetic energy has been transferred or transformed into other forms.
Answer: D — Its former kinetic energy has been transferred or transformed into other forms.
Work / rationale: When the car stops, its former kinetic energy has been transformed/transferred into thermal, sound, deformation, and other forms rather than disappearing.
