Problem 1

A cart travels 20 m in 4 s. Calculate its average speed and include units.

Solution: Average speed = distance ÷ time = 20 m ÷ 4 s = 5 m/s.

Problem 2

A cart travels 64 m in 8 s. Calculate its average speed and include units.

Solution: Average speed = distance ÷ time = 64 m ÷ 8 s = 8 m/s.

Problem 3

A robot moves at a steady 6 m/s north. Explain what makes this a velocity description rather than only a speed description, and state what would make the velocity change even if the speed stayed 6 m/s.

  1. It gives both speed (6 m/s) and direction (north). The velocity changes if the direction changes, even if the speed remains 6 m/s.
  2. Velocity is only the number on a speedometer.
  3. Velocity changes only when speed changes.
  4. Direction is irrelevant to velocity.

Solution: Velocity is a vector: magnitude and direction. A turn changes velocity even at constant speed.

Problem 4

During a 12-minute bicycle trip, the rider’s average speed is 7 m/s. At one instant, the speedometer reads 10 m/s. Distinguish the two measurements.

Solution: Average speed uses total distance and total time. Instantaneous speed describes the speed at one specific moment.
