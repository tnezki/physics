Problem 3

During a 12-minute bicycle trip, the rider’s average speed is 8 m/s. At one instant, the speedometer reads 11 m/s. Distinguish the two measurements.

  1. 8 m/s describes the whole trip average; 11 m/s is the instantaneous speed at that moment.
  2. Both numbers are instantaneous speeds.
  3. Both numbers are average speeds.
  4. The larger value must be velocity because it is larger.
Problem 4

A robot moves at a steady 9 m/s west. Explain what makes this a velocity description rather than only a speed description, and state what would make the velocity change even if the speed stayed 9 m/s.

  1. It gives both speed (9 m/s) and direction (west). The velocity changes if the direction changes, even if the speed remains 9 m/s.
  2. Velocity is only the number on a speedometer.
  3. Velocity changes only when speed changes.
  4. Direction is irrelevant to velocity.

Problem 1

A robot moves at a steady 8 m/s east. Explain what makes this a velocity description rather than only a speed description, and state what would make the velocity change even if the speed stayed 8 m/s.

Solution: Velocity is a vector: magnitude and direction. A turn changes velocity even at constant speed.

Problem 2

During a 12-minute bicycle trip, the rider’s average speed is 6 m/s. At one instant, the speedometer reads 9 m/s. Distinguish the two measurements.

Solution: Average speed uses total distance and total time. Instantaneous speed describes the speed at one specific moment.

Problem 7

A cart travels 49 m in 7 s. Calculate its average speed and include units.

  1. 7 m/s
  2. 56 m/s
  3. 49 m/s
  4. 7 m/s

Solution: Average speed = distance ÷ time = 49 m ÷ 7 s = 7 m/s.
