Question 4

4. What is the difference between constant speed and constant velocity?

Answer / work: Constant speed means the magnitude of motion stays the same. Constant velocity requires both speed and direction to stay the same.

Question 5

Answer / work: Vector, because the direction “east” is included along with the magnitude 22 m/s.

Question 8

8. Use the circular-track image. Explain how the car can keep the same speed while its velocity changes.

Answer / work: The car can keep the same speed while its direction changes around the circular track. Because velocity includes direction, the velocity changes.

Question 9

9. A cyclist averages 15.0 m/s over a 360-m route. Determine the travel time and show the relationship you used.

Answer / work: $t=d/v=(360\text{ m})/(15.0\text{ m/s})=24\text{ s}$.

Question 10

10. Two trips each cover 120 km in 2 h. One is nearly steady; the other includes stops and bursts of higher speed. Compare the average speeds and explain why their motion histories can still differ.

Answer / work: Both average speeds are $120\text{ km}/2\text{ h}=60\text{ km/h}$. Their instantaneous speeds can differ because one trip may include stops and faster bursts while still having the same total distance and time.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A student says, “The trip computer says 40 km/h, so the car must have traveled 40 km/h the whole time.” Evaluate the claim and describe one motion history consistent with a 40 km/h average.

Answer / work: The claim is incorrect because 40 km/h is an average, not necessarily the speed at every moment. For example, traveling 20 km in the first hour and 60 km in the second hour gives 80 km in 2 h, an average of 40 km/h, even though the speed was not constant.

Question 12

12. Two cars both maintain 15 m/s. Car A travels straight north; Car B follows a curved road. Compare their speed and velocity behavior and explain what evidence distinguishes the cases.

Answer / work: Both cars have the same speed, 15 m/s. Car A has constant velocity if it continues straight north. Car B’s velocity changes because its direction changes on the curve. Comparing velocity-vector directions distinguishes the cases.

Question 13

13. A runner covers 150 m in 30 s. Average speed is

A. 3 m/s
B. 5 m/s
C. 20 m/s
D. 120 m/s
E. 4500 m/s
Answer: B — 5 m/s
Rationale: Average speed is total distance divided by total time: $150\text{ m}/30\text{ s}=5\text{ m/s}$.

Question 14

14. A speedometer shows 63 km/h at one particular moment. That value is the car’s

A. instantaneous speed
B. average speed
C. total distance
D. velocity only if no direction is known
E. elapsed time
Answer: A — instantaneous speed
Rationale: A speedometer reports the speed at that instant, not an average over the whole trip.

Question 15

15. Which statement lacks the direction needed for velocity?

A. 12 m/s
B. 12 m/s south
C. 5 km/h east
D. 20 m/s north
E. 3 m/s west
Answer: A — 12 m/s
Rationale: Velocity requires both magnitude and direction; “12 m/s” gives only magnitude.

Question 16

16. Speed is called a scalar because it has

A. magnitude without required direction
B. direction without magnitude
C. no units
D. two components
E. only a graph
Answer: A — magnitude without required direction
Rationale: A scalar has magnitude but does not require direction, which is why speed is scalar.

Question 17

17. Which change guarantees a change in velocity?

A. a change in direction
B. a new paint color
C. a longer trip time with same motion
D. a different clock
E. a change in mass only
Answer: A — a change in direction
Rationale: Velocity includes direction, so any direction change guarantees a velocity change.

Question 18

18. Which situation best represents constant velocity?

A. moving straight west at 6 m/s steadily
B. circling at 6 m/s
C. speeding up west
D. turning north at the same speed
E. slowing to a stop
Answer: A — moving straight west at 6 m/s steadily
Rationale: Constant velocity requires unchanged speed and unchanged direction; steady straight-line motion satisfies both.

A. 40 km/h
B. 60 km/h
C. 80 km/h
D. 960 km/h
E. 244 km/h
Answer: B — 60 km/h
Rationale: $v_{avg}=d/t=240\text{ km}/4\text{ h}=60\text{ km/h}$.

Question 20

20. Which pair can have equal speeds but unequal velocities?

A. two cars at 10 m/s in opposite directions
B. two cars at rest side by side
C. two trains both 12 m/s east
D. two carts both 4 m/s north
E. one object compared with itself
Answer: A — two cars at 10 m/s in opposite directions
Rationale: The speeds can match while the directions differ, so the velocity vectors are unequal.
