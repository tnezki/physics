Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. A reflected sound heard after the original is called what?

Answer / rationale: An echo is reflected sound that arrives after the original sound.

Question 2

2. When sound bends because its speed changes across air layers, what phenomenon is occurring?

Answer / rationale: Refraction. Sound bends when its propagation speed changes from one region/layer to another.

Question 3

3. What causes an object to undergo forced vibration?

Answer / rationale: Forced vibration occurs when one vibrating or periodically driven system exerts a repeating force on another object, causing it to vibrate.

Question 6

6. The resonance-response graph shows vibration amplitude at several driving frequencies. Identify the strongest-response frequency and explain why that value is evidence for a natural frequency.

Answer / rationale: The strongest response occurs at $6 ext{ Hz}$, where the plotted amplitude is largest (about $6.5 ext{ cm}$). A sharp response maximum at that driving frequency is evidence that $6 ext{ Hz}$ is near a natural frequency of the system.

Question 7

7. A sonar-like sound pulse returns after 0.80 s. If the wave speed is 340 m/s, calculate the reflector distance using the round trip.

Work: Total round-trip distance $=vt=(340)(0.80)=272\text{ m}$. One-way reflector distance $=272/2=136\text{ m}$. Answer: $136\text{ m}$.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. During a cool night, sound speed differs in layers of air and the path curves. Explain how this is an example of refraction rather than reflection.

Rationale: The path changes direction gradually because sound speed differs between air layers. That is refraction. Reflection would require the wave to bounce from a boundary rather than bend while continuing through the medium.

Question 9

9. A music-box mechanism sounds louder when pressed against a tabletop. Explain how forced vibration of the table can increase the radiating surface.

Rationale: The music-box mechanism drives the tabletop, causing forced vibration. The larger tabletop area then pushes on more air than the small mechanism alone, so it radiates sound more effectively and is heard as louder.

Question 10

10. Two identical tuning forks are close together. One is struck and the other begins vibrating. Explain why matching natural frequencies matters.

Rationale: The struck fork drives air and the nearby fork. Because the forks have matching natural frequencies, the second fork responds strongly to that driving frequency, producing resonance/strong sympathetic vibration.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A student argues that resonance means an object creates energy from nothing. Critique the claim by identifying the driving source and the effect of repeated in-step energy transfers.

Rationale: Resonance does not create energy. The driver supplies energy on each cycle; when the timing is near a natural frequency, successive transfers add coherently so the oscillator stores more mechanical energy and reaches a larger amplitude.

Question 12

12. A sound ray bends downward through layered air. Evaluate the statement “the wave must have reflected from an invisible wall” and replace it with a speed-change explanation.

Rationale: An invisible reflecting wall is unnecessary. If sound speed changes with height, different portions of the wavefront travel at different speeds, causing the path to bend downward by refraction.
