Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. Define constructive sound interference.

Answer / rationale: Constructive sound interference occurs when overlapping pressure/displacement variations reinforce, producing a larger resultant amplitude at that location.

Question 2

2. Define destructive sound interference.

Answer / rationale: Destructive sound interference occurs when overlapping variations oppose one another, reducing the resultant amplitude and possibly producing near-cancellation.

Question 3

3. What does a repeating loud-soft-loud-soft pattern from two close tones indicate?

Answer / rationale: Beats. Two close but unequal frequencies alternate between reinforcing and opposing as their relative phase changes, producing a loud-soft pattern.

Station 3 Answers

Question 6

6. The graph shows measured beat rate for several frequency separations. Describe the relationship and use one point as evidence.

Rationale: Beat rate increases as the frequency separation increases; the graph should show approximately $f_{beat}=|f_1-f_2|$. For example, a point with a $4\text{ Hz}$ separation should correspond to about $4$ beats per second.

Question 7

7. Two tones are 256 Hz and 260 Hz. Calculate the beat frequency.

Work: $f_{beat}=|260-256|=4\text{ Hz}$. Answer: $4$ beats/s.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. A pair of strings produces 3 beats per second. After an adjustment, it produces 1 beat per second. Explain what happened to the frequency difference.

Rationale: The frequency difference decreased from $3\text{ Hz}$ to $1\text{ Hz}$. The two string frequencies moved closer together, which is why the beats became slower.

Question 9

9. A headphone cancels a steady low-frequency hum well but not a rapidly changing sound. Explain why a fixed opposite pattern is harder to match to a changing signal.

Rationale: Strong cancellation requires the canceling signal to match both the incoming frequency content and its phase/timing. A rapidly changing sound changes too quickly for one fixed opposite waveform to stay matched.

Question 10

10. Two equal-frequency speaker signals arrive exactly in phase at a point. Predict whether the pressure variations reinforce or cancel and explain.

Rationale: They reinforce. Equal-frequency signals arriving in phase have pressure variations with the same sign at the same time, so superposition produces a larger resultant amplitude.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A student claims any two sounds played together make beats. Evaluate the claim and identify the frequency condition that produces a slow, noticeable beat pattern.

Rationale: The claim is too broad. Noticeable slow beats require two frequencies that are close but not identical, so their relative phase drifts slowly and the resultant amplitude rises and falls periodically.

Question 12

12. A model of noise cancellation shows an “opposite” wave but does not specify timing. Explain why the model is incomplete and what phase information is needed.

Rationale: Saying only “opposite” is insufficient. For strong cancellation, the canceling waveform must arrive approximately $180^\circ$ out of phase with the unwanted sound at the listener and must track its timing/frequency so the opposing pressure variations coincide.
