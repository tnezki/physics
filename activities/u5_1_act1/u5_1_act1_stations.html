Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. A tuning fork prong repeatedly moves away from and back toward its starting position. Identify the repeated-motion idea this demonstrates.

Answer / rationale: A vibration is repeated back-and-forth motion about an equilibrium position. The tuning-fork prong repeatedly moves away from and back toward its starting position.

Question 2

2. Define a wave in a way that includes both a disturbance and energy transfer.

Answer / rationale: A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy from one place to another without transporting the medium as a whole from source to receiver.

Question 3

3. What two matching locations on neighboring cycles could be used to measure wavelength on a transverse wave?

Answer / rationale: Measure between matching points on adjacent cycles, such as crest-to-crest or trough-to-trough.

Question 4

4. In a longitudinal wave, are the particles displaced mainly parallel or perpendicular to the direction the disturbance travels?

Answer / rationale: Parallel. In a longitudinal wave, medium particles vibrate back and forth mainly parallel to the direction the disturbance travels.

Question 5

5. Write the reciprocal relationship between frequency and period.

Answer / rationale: $f=1/T$ and $T=1/f$. Frequency and period are reciprocals.

Question 6

6. Use the slinky image to compare particle motion in the transverse and longitudinal examples. Describe the motion direction relative to wave travel.

Rationale: In the transverse example, the slinky particles move perpendicular to the direction the wave travels. In the longitudinal example, the particles move parallel to the wave-travel direction, forming compressions and rarefactions.

Question 7

7. A wave travels 21 m in 3.0 s. Determine its speed, then find its wavelength if the frequency is 2.0 Hz.

Work: $v=d/t=21/3.0=7.0\text{ m/s}$. Then $\lambda=v/f=7.0/2.0=3.5\text{ m}$. Answer: speed $7.0\text{ m/s}$; wavelength $3.5\text{ m}$.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. A 0.25 s period corresponds to what frequency?

Work: $f=1/T=1/0.25=4.0\text{ Hz}$. Answer: $4.0\text{ Hz}$.

Question 9

9. A wave speed is 18 m/s and wavelength is 6.0 m. Find the frequency.

Work: $f=v/\lambda=18/6.0=3.0\text{ Hz}$. Answer: $3.0\text{ Hz}$.

Question 10

10. At constant wave speed, a student shortens the wavelength from 4 m to 2 m. Predict and calculate how the frequency changes.

Work / rationale: At constant speed, $f=v/\lambda$. If $\lambda$ changes from $4$ m to $2$ m, it is halved, so frequency doubles. Numerically, $f_1=v/4$ and $f_2=v/2=2f_1$.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A classmate labels the distance from crest to trough as one wavelength. Explain the error and give a correct wavelength measurement.

Rationale: Crest-to-trough spans only half of one cycle, so it is $\lambda/2$, not one wavelength. A correct wavelength is measured crest-to-next-crest or trough-to-next-trough.

Question 12

12. A student claims that a larger-amplitude rope wave must also have a larger wavelength. Explain why amplitude and wavelength describe different features and what evidence would be needed to compare them.

Rationale: Amplitude is the maximum displacement from equilibrium; wavelength is the horizontal/spatial distance between matching points on successive cycles. A larger amplitude does not require a larger wavelength. To compare them, measure vertical displacement for amplitude and matching-point spacing for wavelength on the same scaled diagram or data set.
