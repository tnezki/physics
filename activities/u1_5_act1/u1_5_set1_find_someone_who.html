Teacher Key — Activity Set 1
Conceptual Physics • Unit 1 • Section 1.5
1

A cart moves right at constant speed. A student says, “The net force must point right because the cart is moving right.” Critique and revise the claim.

Solution: At constant speed in a straight line, acceleration is zero, so the net force is zero. Motion to the right does not require a rightward net force.
2

A puck moves left while the net force points right. Predict its motion over time.

Solution: The rightward net force gives rightward acceleration. While the puck is moving left it slows, then can reverse and speed up right if the force continues.
3

Use the constant-speed diagram to connect balanced forces with unchanged velocity.

Solution: Balanced forces give zero net force, so acceleration is zero and the object’s velocity remains unchanged.
4

A cart is moving left while 30 N acts right and 12 N acts left. A student says the net force points left because the cart is moving left. Critique the reasoning.

Solution: The net force is 30−12=18 N to the right. Net-force direction comes from the force sum, not from the cart’s current leftward velocity.

5

Use the supplied mismatch diagram to critique the claim “An object must move in the direction of the largest force arrow.”

Solution: The largest force arrow determines the direction of acceleration/net force, not necessarily the current motion direction; velocity and net force can point differently.
6

A sign hangs at rest from two angled cables. Draw a force diagram for the sign.

Solution: Sign: weight downward plus two tension forces directed along the two cables away from the sign.
7

Use the supplied mismatch diagram to distinguish velocity arrows from force arrows and revise a confused explanation.

Solution: Velocity arrows describe motion; force arrows describe interactions. Revise the explanation by summing only real forces to find the net force, then use it to predict the change in velocity.
8

Inspect the representation-mismatch diagram. Identify the force-model mismatch with the stated constant-speed motion and explain the needed revision.

Solution: Constant-speed motion requires zero net force, so unequal/opposing force arrows are inconsistent; revise the force model so opposing components balance.

9

Use the supplied mismatch diagram to explain why motion direction alone cannot justify a free-body diagram.

Solution: Motion direction is described by velocity, while a free-body diagram contains real interaction forces. The force model must come from interactions, not from which way the object is moving.
10

A crate is pushed left at constant speed across a rough floor. Draw a force diagram for the crate.

Solution: Crate: weight down, support up, applied push left, friction right; horizontal forces balance at constant speed.
11

A book rests on a level table. Draw a force diagram for the book only.

Solution: Book: weight downward and table support force upward; equal in magnitude for rest.
12

Use the opposing-force diagram. State the condition required for the shown forces to be balanced.

Solution: The opposing forces are balanced only when they are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction, giving zero net force.

13

A sled is pulled to the right across rough snow and is speeding up. Draw a force diagram for the sled.

Solution: Sled: weight down, support up, pull right, friction left; because it speeds up right, the rightward force exceeds the leftward friction.
14

A foam block floats motionless in water. Draw a force diagram for the block.

Solution: Foam block: weight downward and buoyant force upward; equal in magnitude while floating motionless.
15

A block rests on a frictionless incline. Draw a force diagram for the block.

Solution: Block: weight vertically downward and normal/support force perpendicular to the incline; no friction force on a frictionless incline.
16

A sled moves right but is slowing. A student says the net force must point right. Critique the statement.

Solution: If the sled is moving right but slowing, its acceleration—and therefore its net force—points left, opposite the current velocity.

A car travels to the right at constant speed on a level road. Draw a force diagram for the car.

Solution: Car: weight down, road support up, forward driving/traction force, backward resistance; forces balance at constant speed.

Solution: A force arrow represents an interaction acting on the object; a motion/velocity arrow represents how the object is moving. A velocity arrow is not an extra force.

21

An elevator moves upward at constant speed. Draw a force diagram for the elevator car.

Solution: Elevator car: weight downward and cable/support force upward; equal in magnitude at constant speed.
22

Equal 20 N forces act left and right on a moving cart. A student predicts the cart must stop. Critique the prediction.

Solution: Equal 20 N opposite forces give zero net force, so the cart keeps its current constant velocity; zero net force does not require it to stop.
23

Use the constant-speed box diagram. Explain what the force arrows must show if the motion is truly constant in a straight line.

Solution: Opposing horizontal forces must be equal, and opposing vertical forces must be equal, so the net force is zero and velocity remains unchanged.
24

A cart is moving right while a net force acts left. Predict how its motion changes if that force continues.

Solution: The cart initially slows while moving right. If the leftward net force continues long enough, it can stop and then speed up to the left.
