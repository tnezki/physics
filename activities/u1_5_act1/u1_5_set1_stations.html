Station 1 Answers

Question 1

What does a solution to an equation represent in a context?

Solution: A solution represents the value of the variable that makes the equation true in the situation. In context, it answers the question being modeled.

Question 2

Check whether $n=6$ satisfies $4n=24$.

Solution: Substitute $n=6$: $4(6)=24$. Since both sides are $24$, $n=6$ satisfies the equation.

Question 3

Solve $\frac{x}{5}+7=13$ and interpret what the first step does to the equation.

Solution: $\frac{x}{5}+7=13$ gives $\frac{x}{5}=6$ after subtracting $7$ from both sides. Multiplying by $5$ gives $x=30$. The first step removes the added $7$ while keeping the equation balanced.

Question 4

A concert ticket costs \$18 plus a \$4 service fee. A student writes $18+4t=90$ to find the number of tickets bought with \$90. Decide whether the model is reasonable and revise it if needed.

Solution: The model is not reasonable if $t$ is the number of tickets, because the \$4 fee is one-time. The revised model is $18t+4=90$. Then $18t=86$, so $t=\frac{86}{18}$, not a whole number; with \$90, the student could buy $4$ tickets and have \$14 left.

Station 2 Answers

Question 5

Solve each equation.

  1. $6-x-3=10$
  2. $100x+300=200$
  3. $36-2x=-x+2$
Solution:
  1. $6-x-3=10$ gives $3-x=10$, so $x=-7$.
  2. $100x+300=200$ gives $100x=-100$, so $x=-1$.
  3. $36-2x=-x+2$ gives $34=x$, so $x=34$.

Question 6

Write an equation for the sentence and solve: six more than twice a number is $20$.

Solution: Let $x$ be the number. Equation: $2x+6=20$. Then $2x=14$ and $x=7$.

Question 7

A recipe makes $3$ dozen muffins and uses $16$ ounces of oats. A club needs $8$ dozen muffins. At the same rate, how many ounces of oats are needed? Round to the nearest ounce and explain the operation you used.

Solution: Oats per dozen: $\frac{16}{3}$ ounces. For $8$ dozen: $8\cdot\frac{16}{3}=\frac{128}{3}\approx42.67$, so about $43$ ounces.

Question 8

Two workers can assemble $40$ kits in $3$ days. At the same rate, how many workers are needed to assemble $60$ kits in $1$ day? Show how the rational-number rate is used.

Solution: Two workers for $3$ days is $6$ worker-days for $40$ kits, so one worker-day makes $\frac{40}{6}$ kits. To make $60$ kits in $1$ day requires $60\div\frac{40}{6}=9$ workers.

Question 9

A diver begins at $1.5$ meters above sea level, descends $6.75$ meters, rises $2\frac{1}{4}$ meters, then descends another $1.1$ meters. Find the diver's final elevation and describe its meaning.

Solution: $1.5-6.75+2.25-1.1=-4.1$. The diver ends $4.1$ meters below sea level.

Question 10

Construct a strategy for deciding whether $3(2x-5)+4$ and $6x-11$ are equivalent for all $x$. Your strategy must include more than testing one value.

Solution: Distribute: $3(2x-5)+4=6x-15+4=6x-11$. Since this matches $6x-11$, the expressions are equivalent for all $x$. Testing can support the result, but simplifying proves it.

Question 11

The area model is intended to represent $(x+2)(x+3)$, but one cell is incorrect. Identify the mismatch, revise the model, and explain how the product should expand.

Solution: For $(x+2)(x+3)$, the four products are $x^2,3x,2x,6$. The incorrect cell is $5$, which should be $6$. The product expands to $x^2+5x+6$.

Question 12

Design a short distance model that uses both $|a-b|$ and signed addition to answer the same question. Include the numbers, the context, and a justification that both representations agree.

Solution: Example: A person moves from $-4$ to $3$ on a path. Distance is $|3-(-4)|=7$. Signed addition from $-4$ by $+7$ lands at $3$. Both models agree that the separation is $7$ units.

Station 4 Answers

Question 13

State the rate of change for a pattern that adds ${7}$ tiles each figure.

Solution: The rate of change is $7$ tiles per figure.

Question 14

A graph is a straight line rising from left to right. Is its rate of change positive, negative, or zero?

Solution: A straight line rising from left to right has a positive rate of change.

Question 15

Write an input-output rule for “a quiz has ${20}$ points, and a student loses ${2}$ points for each mistake.” Then find the score for ${6}$ mistakes.

Solution: Let $m$ be mistakes. The rule is $S=20-2m$. For $6$ mistakes, $S=20-12=8$ points.

Question 16

Construct a strategy for comparing a graph that passes through $({0},{6})$ and $({4},{18})$ with a table that shows $x$-values ${0}$, ${2}$, ${4}$ and outputs ${10}$, ${16}$, ${22}$. Your strategy must account for starting value, rate of change, and at least one common input.

Solution: Graph: starts at $6$ and has rate $\frac{18-6}{4-0}=3$. Table: starts at $10$ and has rate $3$ because outputs rise by $6$ when $x$ rises by $2$. At $x=4$, graph output is $18$ and table output is $22$, so they have the same rate but different starting values.
