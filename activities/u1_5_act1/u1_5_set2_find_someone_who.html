Problem 13

A force diagram for the book includes “table pushes floor downward” as a force on the book. Diagnose the system error.

Answer: That interaction statement describes a force on another object, not necessarily on the chosen book. A force diagram must include only forces acting on the chosen system.

Problem 14

Classify the horizontal force model as balanced or unbalanced.

Answer: Unbalanced; net horizontal force is 7 N right.

Problem 15

Rank the three force models by net-force magnitude, greatest to least.

Answer: A=7 N, B=0 N, C=7 N; ranking A > C > B.

Problem 16

A sled has the force model shown while it is described as moving right at constant speed. Are the force model and motion description consistent?

Answer: No. The net force is 5 N left, so velocity must change rather than remain constant.

Problem 17

A flawed force model for a hanging sign shows only weight downward. What evidence shows the model is incomplete?

Answer: Because the sign remains at rest, an upward tension force must also act.

Problem 18

Revise this explanation using net force: “The box slows down because friction is present.”

Answer: The box slows because the net force is opposite its velocity; friction contributes to that net force.

Problem 19

a sled is pulled left while friction acts right. Describe the chain from real situation to force model to physical interpretation.

Answer: Choose the moving object as the system, represent the 12 N driving force left and 5 N opposing force right (plus vertical forces when relevant), then interpret the nonzero net force as a velocity change toward left.

Problem 20

A dog pulls a sled across snow. What should you decide before drawing a force model, and why?

Answer: Choose the system/object first (for example, the sled). That choice determines which interactions count as forces on the object and prevents including forces acting on other objects.

Problem 21

After selecting the object as the system, use the force model to calculate whether the horizontal forces balance.

Answer: Net force = 0 N, so the horizontal forces balance.

Problem 22

A box moves straight at constant velocity. The model shows 14 N right. What force must be present left for horizontal balance?

Answer: 14 N left.

Problem 23

Complete the quantitative force model by finding the horizontal net force.

Answer: 11 N left.

Problem 24

A wagon model has 16 N left and 5 N right. First determine the net force. Then decide whether a claim of “constant velocity” is consistent with the model.

Answer: Net force = 11 N left. Constant velocity is not consistent because a nonzero net force changes velocity.
