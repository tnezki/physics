Question 1

1. Which light interaction is most important in a camera lens?

Answer / rationale: Refraction through the camera lens changes ray directions so they converge on the image sensor.

Question 2

2. Where should the eye’s optical system bring rays from a viewed object to focus?

Answer / rationale: The eye should focus rays onto the retina, the light-sensitive receiving surface.

Question 3

3. In a fiber, does the guided ray curve continuously or travel in segments between boundary interactions?

Answer / rationale: In a ray model the guided light travels in approximately straight segments between boundary interactions; it is redirected at the core-cladding boundary rather than smoothly curving with the fiber.

Question 6

6. Use the fiber figure to trace one guided ray and name the boundary condition that keeps it from escaping the core.

Rationale: The guided ray travels inside the higher-index core and repeatedly reaches the lower-index boundary at an incidence angle greater than the critical angle. That condition produces total internal reflection and keeps the ray in the core.

Question 7

7. A farsighted eye needs incoming rays to begin converging sooner. Explain why a converging corrective lens can provide that change.

Rationale: A converging corrective lens refracts incoming rays so they are already converging before entering the eye. That gives the eye’s own optics a head start and shifts the final focus forward toward the retina for farsighted correction.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. Compare a camera and the human eye: identify one optical similarity and one important difference in where the focused image is received.

Rationale: Similarity: both use refracting optical surfaces to bring incoming rays to a focused image on a light-sensitive surface. Difference: the eye receives the image on the retina, while a camera receives it on an electronic sensor/film and focuses by different mechanical/biological adjustments.

Question 9

9. A prism pair redirects a ray inside glass. Explain how total internal reflection can turn the path with little transmission through the reflecting face.

Rationale: At a glass-to-lower-index boundary, if the internal incidence angle exceeds the critical angle, no transmitted refracted ray forms; the ray reflects internally. A prism can use this TIR condition to redirect the beam efficiently.

Question 10

10. A coating passes 92% of visible light at one wavelength but only 70% at another. Explain why one “high transmission” number is not enough to characterize the device.

Rationale: The coating response depends on wavelength. A single 92% value would hide the fact that another wavelength transmits only 70%, so performance must be characterized across the wavelengths/conditions named in the claim.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A website says an optical fiber has zero loss no matter how tightly it bends. Evaluate the claim using critical angle and changing incidence angle.

Rationale: Tight bending changes the geometry of ray encounters with the core-cladding boundary. Some incidence angles can fall below the critical-angle requirement, allowing light to escape, so “zero loss at any bend” is not physically justified.

Question 12

12. A display window looks clear from straight ahead but produces strong reflections from the side. Explain why this does not contradict the first observation and what variable changed.

Rationale: Reflection and transmission depend on incidence/viewing angle. Straight ahead and oblique viewing are different geometries, so strong side reflections can coexist with high head-on transmission without contradiction.
