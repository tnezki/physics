Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. What kind of interference reduces the resulting displacement when overlapping waves have opposite signs?

Answer / rationale: Destructive interference. Opposite-sign displacements partially or completely cancel when they overlap.

Question 2

2. When two waves overlap, are their displacements added permanently or only while they share the same region?

Answer / rationale: Only while the waves share the same region. Superposition adds their displacements during overlap; afterward ordinary pulses continue traveling.

Question 3

3. What is an antinode in a standing wave?

Answer / rationale: An antinode is a location in a standing wave where the oscillation amplitude is greatest.

Question 4

4. As a source moves away from a listener, does the observed frequency increase or decrease?

Answer / rationale: It decreases. A receding source produces more widely spaced wavefront arrivals, so the listener detects a lower frequency.

Answer / rationale: A standing wave, with a fixed repeating pattern of nodes and antinodes.

Question 6

6. Use the standing-wave image to identify where nodes and antinodes would be located and explain how the number of segments changes between patterns.

Rationale: Nodes occur at the fixed points of minimal displacement, including the ends in these string patterns; antinodes lie midway between neighboring nodes where motion is greatest. As the mode number increases, the number of segments/loops increases and the node-antinode pattern becomes more closely spaced.

Work: $+5\text{ cm}+(-2\text{ cm})=+3\text{ cm}$. Answer: $+3\text{ cm}$.

Question 8

8. Two equal upward pulses meet. If each has amplitude 2 cm at the overlap point, what is the temporary combined displacement?

Work: $+2\text{ cm}+(+2\text{ cm})=+4\text{ cm}$. Answer: temporary displacement $+4\text{ cm}$; the pulses reinforce constructively.

Question 9

9. A listener stands ahead of a moving source where wavefront spacing is 0.70 m and behind where it is 1.10 m. Which side has the higher observed frequency? Explain using arrival spacing.

Rationale: The front side has the higher observed frequency because its wavefronts are spaced more closely ($0.70$ m versus $1.10$ m). With essentially the same propagation speed in the medium, closer spacing means a greater arrival rate.

Question 10

10. A rope is driven at a frequency that produces three loops in a standing wave. Describe what remains stationary and what oscillates most strongly.

Rationale: The nodes remain at fixed locations with minimal motion, while antinodes between them oscillate most strongly. Three loops means three antinodal regions separated by nodes.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A student calls every overlap of waves “constructive interference.” Explain how the signs and sizes of displacements determine whether the result is constructive, destructive, or partial.

Rationale: Add the signed displacements. Same-sign displacements reinforce and give constructive interference; equal opposite signs can cancel completely; unequal opposite signs produce partial destructive interference. The result depends on both sign and magnitude.

Question 12

12. A classmate says the Doppler effect is caused by a change in wave speed. Explain why a spacing/arrival-rate model is better when source and observer move through the same medium.

Rationale: In one unchanged medium, wave speed can stay essentially constant. Relative motion changes the spacing of arriving wavefronts: compressed spacing gives a higher arrival rate and stretched spacing gives a lower one. That explains the Doppler shift without requiring the medium-wave speed itself to change.
