1. A compass needle is itself a tiny magnet. What two pole regions does it have?

Answer: A compass needle has a north-seeking pole region and a south-seeking pole region. Rationale: A compass is a tiny magnetic dipole, so both pole types are present.

2. State the interaction rule for like magnetic poles and for unlike magnetic poles.

Answer: Like poles repel; unlike poles attract. Rationale: N–N and S–S push apart, while N–S pull together.

4. Why does cutting a magnet not produce a north-only piece and a south-only piece?

Answer: Each smaller piece still contains aligned magnetic dipoles/domains, so each fragment develops both north and south pole regions. Cutting does not isolate a single pole.

Answer: Strongly magnetized iron has many more domains aligned in a common direction. Ordinary iron has domains oriented more randomly, so their effects largely cancel.
