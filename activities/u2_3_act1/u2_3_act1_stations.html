Question 4

4. Can an object accelerate while its speed stays constant? Give the type of motion that makes this possible.

Answer / work: Yes. An object moving at constant speed around a curve or circle accelerates because its velocity direction changes.

Question 5

Answer / work: $\Delta v$ means change in velocity: $v_f-v_i$.

Station 3 Answers

Question 6

6. A runner changes from 2 m/s to 14 m/s in 6 s. Determine the average acceleration.

Answer / work: $a=(14-2)\text{ m/s}/6\text{ s}=2\text{ m/s}^2$.

Question 7

7. A bicycle moving east slows from 10 m/s to 4 m/s in 2 s. Take east as positive and calculate acceleration.

Answer / work: $a=(4-10)\text{ m/s}/2\text{ s}=-3\text{ m/s}^2$. The negative sign means the acceleration points west when east is positive.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. Use the circular-track figure. Explain why equal speed around the track does not mean zero acceleration.

Answer / work: Even with equal speed, the velocity direction changes continuously around the track. A changing velocity means nonzero acceleration.

Question 9

9. A cart has velocities 0, 3, 6, 9, 12 m/s at successive one-second times. Calculate acceleration and explain what stays constant in the pattern.

Answer / work: Velocity increases by 3 m/s every 1 s, so $a=3\text{ m/s}^2$. The acceleration stays constant.

Question 10

10. A velocity–time graph slopes downward from +16 m/s at 0 s to 0 m/s at 4 s. Determine the acceleration and describe the motion.

Answer / work: $a=(0-16)\text{ m/s}/4\text{ s}=-4\text{ m/s}^2$. The object moves in the positive direction while slowing uniformly to rest.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A classmate claims, “If the velocity arrows are the same length, acceleration must be zero.” Evaluate the claim and explain what additional vector feature must be checked.

Answer / work: The claim is incomplete. Equal-length velocity arrows show equal speed, but their directions must also be checked. If the arrows point in different directions, velocity changes and acceleration is not zero.

Question 12

12. Two cars both read 20 m/s on their speedometers. Car A travels straight east; Car B follows a curved exit ramp. Compare their accelerations and defend the difference using velocity change.

Answer / work: Car A has zero acceleration if it continues straight east at constant speed. Car B has nonzero acceleration because the curved ramp changes the direction of its velocity even though its speedometer still reads 20 m/s.

Question 16

16. Which situation can have constant speed and nonzero acceleration?

A. a car rounding a curve
B. a train moving straight steadily
C. a parked bicycle
D. a book on a desk
E. a cart with constant velocity
Answer: A — a car rounding a curve
Rationale: Turning changes the direction of velocity even if the speed stays constant, so the object accelerates.

Question 17

17. A flat velocity–time graph indicates

A. zero acceleration
B. increasing acceleration
C. decreasing time
D. changing direction automatically
E. free fall
Answer: A — zero acceleration
Rationale: Constant velocity means $\Delta v=0$, so acceleration is zero.

Question 18

18. A straight upward-sloping velocity–time graph indicates

A. constant positive acceleration
B. constant velocity
C. zero velocity
D. negative time
E. no displacement
Answer: A — constant positive acceleration
Rationale: Equal velocity increases in equal time intervals indicate constant positive acceleration.

Rationale: Equal speed does not guarantee equal velocity; a direction change changes velocity and therefore implies acceleration.
