C. 240 J
D. 120 J
Answer: D — 120 J
Work / rationale: $PE_g=mgh=(3)(10)(4)=120\text{ J}$.

Question 9

9. A 2 kg cart at 5 m/s has kinetic energy

A. 25 J
B. 10 J
C. 20 J
D. 50 J
Answer: A — 25 J
Work / rationale: $KE=\tfrac12mv^2=\tfrac12(2)(5^2)=25\text{ J}$.

Question 10

10. If the speed of the same object doubles, kinetic energy becomes

A. twice as large
B. four times as large
C. half as large
D. unchanged
Answer: B — four times as large
Work / rationale: Because $KE\propto v^2$, doubling speed multiplies kinetic energy by $2^2=4$.

Question 19

19. Which situation clearly involves positive work by the stated force?

C. A forward force moves a cart forward.
D. A force is applied but displacement is zero.

Question 20

20. Why can higher speed greatly increase stopping-energy demands?

A. Power equals speed.
B. Momentum has no mass term.
C. Potential energy depends on contact time.
D. Kinetic energy depends on the square of speed.
Answer: D — Kinetic energy depends on the square of speed.
Work / rationale: Kinetic energy contains a speed-squared term, $KE=\tfrac12mv^2$, so increasing speed greatly raises stopping-energy demands.
