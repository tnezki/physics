Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. Classify this situation as work or no work on the wall: a student pushes hard but the wall does not move.

Answer / rationale: No mechanical work is done on the wall in this model because the wall has zero displacement. With $d=0$, $W=Fd\cos\theta=0$.

Question 2

2. What quantity tells how quickly work is done?

Answer: Power. Rationale: Power measures the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred.

Question 3

3. Distinguish kinetic energy from gravitational potential energy in words.

Answer / rationale: Kinetic energy is energy associated with motion. Gravitational potential energy is energy associated with position/height in a gravitational interaction relative to a chosen reference level.

Station 3 Answers

Question 6

6. A 35 N pull moves a sled 4 m in the pull direction. Calculate the work.

Work: $W=Fd=(35\text{ N})(4\text{ m})=140\text{ J}$. Answer: $140\text{ J}$.

Question 7

7. Two machines each do 600 J of work. Machine A takes 12 s; Machine B takes 6 s. Calculate both powers and compare.

Work: $P_A=600/12=50\text{ W}$ and $P_B=600/6=100\text{ W}$. Answer / rationale: Machine B has twice the power because it does the same work in half the time.

Question 8

8. Use the wall figure to explain why a large force does not guarantee mechanical work on the object being pushed.

Rationale: A large force alone does not guarantee work. In the figure the wall does not move, so its displacement is zero and the mechanical work on the wall is zero.

Question 9

9. A 6 kg box is placed on a shelf 1.5 m above the floor. Using $gpprox10$ m/s², calculate its gravitational potential energy relative to the floor.

Work: $PE_g=mgh=(6\text{ kg})(10\text{ m/s}^2)(1.5\text{ m})=90\text{ J}$. Answer: $90\text{ J}$.

Question 10

10. A 7 kg inspection robot rolls at 3 m/s. Use the kinetic-energy model to determine the energy associated with its motion.

Work: $KE=\tfrac12mv^2=\tfrac12(7)(3^2)=31.5\text{ J}$. Answer: $31.5\text{ J}$.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A student says, “Power tells how much total energy a machine has.” Diagnose the mistake and compare two machines that do the same work in different times.

Rationale: Power is a rate, not the total energy a machine “has.” For equal work, the machine that completes the work in less time has greater power because $P=W/t$.

Question 12

12. A cart’s speed triples while its mass is unchanged. Explain how its kinetic energy changes and why a speed-squared model matters for safety.

Work / rationale: $KE=\tfrac12mv^2$, so tripling speed multiplies kinetic energy by $3^2=9$. This matters for safety because stopping-energy demands rise much faster than speed.

A. 6 J

C. 18 J
D. 36 J
E. 72 J
Answer: C — 18 J
Work / rationale: $KE=\tfrac12mv^2=\tfrac12(4)(3^2)=18\text{ J}$.

Question 20

20. Which statement best describes energy change as a swing moves downward?

A. Gravitational PE decreases while KE increases.
B. Both PE and KE must decrease.
C. KE becomes mass.
D. Power is stored as height.
E. Work is a substance contained in the swing.
Answer: A — Gravitational PE decreases while KE increases.
Work / rationale: As an ideal swing moves downward, gravitational potential energy decreases while kinetic energy increases.
