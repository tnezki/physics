Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. What is the difference between static friction and sliding friction?

Answer / rationale: Static friction acts while the surfaces are not sliding relative to one another and can adjust to oppose attempted slipping. Sliding friction acts once the surfaces are sliding past each other.

Question 2

2. A tire pushes backward on the road at the contact point. Friction from the road on the tire points in which horizontal direction?

Answer / rationale: The road exerts friction on the tire forward, opposite the tire’s backward push on the road.

Question 3

3. A cup is at rest on a tray that is gently tilted but the cup has not slipped. Which type of friction prevents slipping?

Answer: Static friction. Rationale: The cup has not begun to slide, so friction is preventing attempted slipping.

Station 2 Answers

Question 4

4. State one key difference between mass and weight, including units.

Answer / rationale: Mass measures inertia/amount of matter and is measured in kilograms (kg). Weight is the gravitational force on the object and is measured in newtons (N).

Question 5

5. A lamp rests on a table. Identify the downward force and the upward surface force.

Answer: Downward weight (gravity) and upward normal/support force from the table. Rationale: The lamp is supported vertically, so the table’s normal force acts upward against the downward gravitational force.

Station 3 Answers

Question 6

6. A 50-N push acts right on a box while 18 N of friction acts left. Find the horizontal net force.

Work: $F_{net}=50\text{ N right}-18\text{ N left}=32\text{ N right}$.

Question 7

7. Use the source figure of a foot pressing on a block. Identify which forces are vertical and explain why the block can have zero vertical acceleration even though forces act.

Rationale: The vertical forces include the block’s weight downward, the foot’s downward contact force, and the surface’s upward support/normal force. Zero vertical acceleration is possible when the upward support force equals the total downward force, so $F_{net,y}=0$.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. A 9-kg suitcase is on Earth. Calculate its weight using $gpprox10\text{ m/s}^2$. Explain what would happen to mass if the suitcase were moved to a weaker gravitational field.

Work: $W=mg=(9\text{ kg})(10\text{ m/s}^2)=90\text{ N}$. Rationale: Moving to a weaker gravitational field changes weight because $g$ changes, but the suitcase’s mass remains $9\text{ kg}$.

Question 9

9. A 4-kg crate is pushed right with 26 N while friction is 10 N left. Calculate net force and acceleration.

Work: $F_{net}=26-10=16\text{ N right}$. Then $a=F_{net}/m=16/4=4\text{ m/s}^2$ right.

Question 10

10. A student increases a horizontal push from 5 N to 9 N on a box that still does not move. Explain what static friction must do during this interval.

Rationale: Because the box remains at rest, the net horizontal force stays zero. Static friction must increase from $5\text{ N}$ to $9\text{ N}$ opposite the push, as long as the maximum static-friction value has not been exceeded.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A classmate says, “Static friction is always the same fixed value.” Critique this claim using a box that remains at rest under several different small pushes.

Rationale: The claim is incorrect because static friction is not one fixed value. If a stationary box is pushed with $2\text{ N}$, $5\text{ N}$, then $7\text{ N}$, static friction can match each push with $2\text{ N}$, $5\text{ N}$, then $7\text{ N}$ in the opposite direction, up to its maximum.

Question 12

12. An astronaut takes the same wrench from Earth to the Moon. Defend the statement that the wrench’s mass remains unchanged while its weight changes.

Rationale: Mass measures the wrench’s inertia and does not depend on location. Weight is $W=mg$, so a smaller local $g$ on the Moon produces a smaller weight while the mass remains unchanged.

Question 19

19. Which property of an object is unchanged by moving it from Earth to the Moon?

A. mass
B. weight
C. gravitational force
D. local $g$
E. support force from a scale
Answer: A — mass
Work / rationale: Mass is an intrinsic measure of inertia and does not change when the gravitational field changes.

Question 20

20. Reducing friction while keeping the same applied force usually makes the net force

A. larger in the applied-force direction
B. smaller in every case
C. zero
D. equal to weight
E. independent of the push
Answer: A — larger in the applied-force direction
Work / rationale: With the same applied force, reducing an opposing friction force makes the net force larger in the applied-force direction.
