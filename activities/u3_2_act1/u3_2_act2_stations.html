Question 1

1. Which force opposes sliding or attempted sliding between surfaces?

A. friction
B. mass
C. weight
D. acceleration
Answer: A — friction
Work / rationale: Friction is the contact force that opposes sliding or attempted sliding between surfaces.

Question 2

2. A crate is pushed but does not move. Which friction type can balance the push?

A. sliding friction
B. static friction
C. weight
D. air resistance
Answer: B — static friction
Work / rationale: Because the crate has not begun to slide, static friction can adjust to balance the push.

Question 3

3. A block slides right. Friction on the block points

A. right
B. up
C. left
D. down only
Answer: C — left
Work / rationale: Sliding friction opposes the relative sliding motion, so a block sliding right experiences friction to the left.

Question 11

11. A student says mass and weight are the same because both describe “how heavy” something is. Which correction is best?

Question 12

Answer: D — The box with less friction has larger net force and larger acceleration.
Work / rationale: With the same mass and applied push, less friction means a larger net force, so $a=F_{net}/m$ is larger.

Question 13

13. Which statement about friction is correct?

Work / rationale: The correct SI units are mass in kilograms and weight in newtons.

Question 19

19. A box is at rest while push increases from 3 N to 7 N. Static friction during that change

A. must stay at 3 N
B. must become zero
C. can increase to keep the net force zero
D. must point with the push
Answer: C — can increase to keep the net force zero
Work / rationale: As long as the box remains at rest, static friction can increase from $3\text{ N}$ to $7\text{ N}$ to keep net force zero.

Question 20

20. An object moves from Earth to a weaker gravitational field. Which statement is correct?

A. Its mass decreases while weight stays the same.
B. Both mass and weight stay the same.
C. Its mass becomes a force.
D. Its mass stays the same while its weight decreases.
Answer: D — Its mass stays the same while its weight decreases.
Work / rationale: Mass stays unchanged, but $W=mg$ decreases when the local gravitational field strength $g$ is smaller.
