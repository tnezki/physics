Question 8

8. Use the book-and-spring support-force image. Explain how a rigid-looking table can exert an upward force and why that force can balance weight.

Work / rationale: The table deforms by a tiny amount, like a very stiff spring. Its material pushes upward on the book through contact; for a book at rest, that support force can equal the downward weight so the net vertical force is zero.

Question 9

9. A 72-kg person stands still on a level scale. Use $g=10\,\text{N/kg}$ to determine weight and then the scale’s support force.

Work / rationale: Weight: W = mg = (72 kg)(10 N/kg) = 720 N downward. Because the person is at rest, the scale’s support force is 720 N upward.

Question 10

10. A bicycle moves east at constant speed while its drive force is 90 N east. Determine the total westward resistance and state the net horizontal force.

Work / rationale: 90 N westward resistance. Equal opposite horizontal forces give a net horizontal force of 0 N.

Question 11

11. A student says, “The scale reads 700 N, so the person accelerates upward with a 700-N net force.” Analyze the error and provide a complete equilibrium explanation.

Work / rationale: The 700-N scale reading is one force: the upward support force. It is not the net force. For a person standing still, the downward weight is about 700 N, so the forces balance and the net force is zero.

Question 12

12. Two students define equilibrium. Student A says only objects at rest are in equilibrium. Student B says zero net force allows either rest or constant straight-line motion. Evaluate the definitions and defend the stronger one.

Work / rationale: Student B is correct. Equilibrium means zero net force, which allows either rest (static equilibrium) or constant straight-line motion (dynamic equilibrium).

Question 13

13. The equilibrium rule is

  1. $\Sigma F=0$
  2. $W=mg$
  3. $F=ma$
  4. $v=d/t$
  5. $F_N=W+mg$ always
Answer: A. $\Sigma F=0$.
Rationale: Equilibrium means all force vectors balance, so their vector sum—the net force—is zero: ΣF = 0.

Question 14

14. Which object is NOT in equilibrium?

  1. A sign hanging still
  2. A train moving straight at constant speed
  3. A book resting on a table
  4. A skateboard speeding up
  5. A cart rolling straight at steady speed
Answer: D. A skateboard speeding up.
Rationale: Speeding up means velocity is changing, so acceleration is nonzero and the net force cannot be zero. Therefore the skateboard is not in equilibrium.

Question 15

15. A book at rest has weight 18 N. With no other vertical forces, the support force is

  1. 0 N
  2. 9 N
  3. 18 N
  4. 36 N
  5. greater than 18 N always
Answer: C. 18 N.
Rationale: The book is at rest with no other vertical forces, so the upward support must balance the 18 N downward weight: F_N = 18 N.

Question 16

16. A person stands still on a scale. Why is the net force zero?

  1. The scale reading is zero.
  2. Support force and weight balance.
  3. Gravity stops acting.
  4. Inertia acts upward.
  5. The person has no mass.
Answer: B. Support force and weight balance.
Rationale: Standing still means zero acceleration and zero net force. The upward scale/support force balances the downward weight.

Question 17

17. Which motion can represent dynamic equilibrium?

  1. Speeding up straight
  2. Turning at steady speed
  3. Moving straight at constant speed
  4. Falling faster
  5. Slowing down
Answer: C. Moving straight at constant speed.
Rationale: Dynamic equilibrium is motion with zero net force, so velocity must remain constant in both speed and direction: straight-line constant-speed motion.

Question 18

18. A car cruises straight at constant speed. The forward force is 400 N. The total backward force must be

  1. 0 N
  2. 200 N
  3. 400 N
  4. 800 N
  5. greater than 400 N
Answer: C. 400 N.
Rationale: Constant straight-line speed means zero net horizontal force. Therefore the backward forces must total the same 400 N as the forward force.

Question 19

19. A 500-N load is supported by two vertical ropes. One provides 170 N. The other provides

  1. 170 N
  2. 250 N
  3. 330 N
  4. 500 N
  5. 670 N
Answer: C. 330 N.
Rationale: For equilibrium the two upward rope forces must total 500 N. The missing support is 500 N − 170 N = 330 N.

Question 20

20. If a person shifts closer to one support while a platform stays in equilibrium, what can happen?

  1. Both supports must remain equal.
  2. One support can increase while the other decreases, with the total still balancing the load.
  3. The net force must become nonzero.
  4. Weight disappears.
  5. Both supports become zero.
Answer: B. One support can increase while the other decreases, with the total still balancing the load.
Rationale: Equilibrium requires the total support to continue balancing the load. Shifting the person can redistribute that total so one support increases while the other decreases.
