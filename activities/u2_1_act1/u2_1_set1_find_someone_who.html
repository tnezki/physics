Problem 1

A description says, “The cup stays beside Leo on the seat while roadside signs move backward past the window.” Identify the reference frame used when the cup is described as staying beside Leo, and cite the cue.

Answer: A reference frame is what positions are measured relative to. “Stays beside the passenger on the seat” identifies the moving vehicle frame.

Problem 2

A rider on a train says a bag on the floor is at rest. An observer standing on the ground says the bag is moving with the train. Compare the two descriptions and explain why Earth’s surface is a convenient frame for everyday travel.

Answer: Motion is relative. The bag is at rest in the vehicle frame but moving in the Earth frame; neither description is contradictory when the frame is stated.

Problem 3

A description says, “The cup stays beside Leo on the seat while roadside signs move backward past the window.” Identify the reference frame used when the cup is described as staying beside Leo, and cite the cue.

Answer: A reference frame is what positions are measured relative to. “Stays beside the passenger on the seat” identifies the moving vehicle frame.

Problem 4

Leo walks toward the front of a train moving east at 8 m/s. Leo walks east at 2 m/s relative to the train. Explain how Leo can have one velocity relative to the train and a different velocity relative to the ground.

  1. Leo must have the same velocity in every frame.
  2. The train is the only valid frame.
  3. Changing reference frame changes the object’s mass.
  4. Relative to the train, Leo moves east at 2 m/s; relative to the ground, Leo moves east at 10 m/s. Motion depends on the chosen reference frame.

Answer: Keep the reference frame explicit. Because both velocities point east, the ground-frame speed is the train speed plus the walking speed.
