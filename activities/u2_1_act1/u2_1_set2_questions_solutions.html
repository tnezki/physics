Q1

A cyclist reports 6 m/s east relative to the road while a rider beside the cyclist says the cyclist is nearly at rest relative to the moving group. Compare the descriptions and identify why Earth’s surface is often the convenient everyday frame.

S1

A cyclist reports 6 m/s east relative to the road while a rider beside the cyclist says the cyclist is nearly at rest relative to the moving group. Compare the descriptions and identify why Earth’s surface is often the convenient everyday frame.

Answer: Both descriptions can be valid because velocity is frame-dependent; Earth’s surface gives a stable shared reference for ordinary locations and travel.
Reasoning: Reference frames change measured motion, not the object itself.
Q2

A motion description says, “The backpack remains beside Zoe on the seat while trees move backward past the window.” Identify the reference frame used when the backpack is described as remaining beside Zoe, and cite the cue.

  1. no reference frame is needed
  2. the vehicle/passenger frame
  3. the trees outside
  4. the backpack itself only
S2

A motion description says, “The backpack remains beside Zoe on the seat while trees move backward past the window.” Identify the reference frame used when the backpack is described as remaining beside Zoe, and cite the cue.

Answer: The vehicle/passenger frame; the backpack stays at a fixed position relative to the passenger/seat.
Reasoning: A reference frame is the object or coordinate system used to judge position and motion. “Beside the passenger on the seat” fixes the backpack relative to the vehicle.
Q3

A cup sits on a tray inside a bus moving east. The cup does not slide on the tray. Explain how the cup can be at rest in the bus frame but moving in the Earth frame.

S3

A cup sits on a tray inside a bus moving east. The cup does not slide on the tray. Explain how the cup can be at rest in the bus frame but moving in the Earth frame.

Answer: Its position is constant relative to the bus, but changes relative to Earth as the bus moves.
Reasoning: Motion depends on the reference frame; the object can be at rest in one frame and moving in another.
