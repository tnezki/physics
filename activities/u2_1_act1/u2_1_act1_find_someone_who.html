Find Someone Who — Set 1 — Section 2.1: Motion and Reference Frames

1. What does the word rest mean when a particular reference frame has been chosen?

2. A laptop stays on a tray table while an airplane taxis. Describe the laptop relative to the tray and relative to the airport terminal.

3. Identify the reference frame in this description: “The elevator rises past the third-floor doorway.”

4. A student sits at a desk. Why can the student reasonably be called at rest in class even though Earth moves around the Sun?

5. Complete this idea: a motion statement becomes physically meaningful when the object’s change in position is compared with ________.

6. A boat passenger watches a cooler stay beside the same bench while a person on a dock watches the cooler move downstream. Compare the two descriptions and name the frame used by each observer.

7. Use the Earth–Sun figure. Explain how a chair can be at rest relative to the room while still moving relative to the Sun.

8. A moving walkway carries a standing traveler through an airport. Write two complete motion descriptions of the traveler, one using the walkway and one using the airport floor.

9. Choose a useful reference frame for each purpose: describing a swimmer’s motion inside a moving boat, describing the boat’s motion along a river, and describing Earth’s motion in the solar system. Justify each choice briefly.

10. The sentence “The package moved 20 m” appears in a lab report. Explain what information about the reference frame must be stated or implied before the claim can be interpreted.

11. Two observers report opposite answers to “Is the cart moving?” Observer A stands on the cart; Observer B stands beside the track. Explain how both answers could be correct without either observer making a measurement error.

12. A student argues that the ground frame is always the “real” frame and any vehicle frame is wrong. Evaluate the argument and give an example showing why multiple frames can be useful.

13. A backpack stays on a train seat while the train moves. Relative to the seat, the backpack is

A. moving backward
B. moving forward
C. at rest
D. accelerating
E. in free fall

14. The same backpack is moving relative to

A. the seat cushion
B. a passenger sitting beside it
C. the train floor
D. a station platform
E. its zipper

15. Which phrase explicitly names a reference frame?

A. moves quickly
B. is at rest relative to the dock
C. travels far
D. changes position
E. moves for ten seconds

16. A description says, “The elevator moves upward past floor 4.” What frame is implied?

A. the elevator car
B. the building
C. a passenger’s shoes
D. the elevator motor
E. a cloud

17. Two observers can correctly report different motion for the same object when

A. they use different reference frames
B. one observer is always wrong
C. the object has two masses
D. time stops
E. the object has no position

18. Which comparison best describes a person standing still on a moving walkway?

A. at rest relative to walkway, moving relative to floor
B. moving relative to walkway, at rest relative to floor
C. at rest relative to every frame
D. moving relative to every frame
E. moving only relative to the Sun

19. Why does “a car travels 25 m/s” usually mean relative to the road?

A. Road motion is the conventional nearby frame.
B. Cars cannot be reference frames.
C. The road has zero motion in every frame.
D. Velocity is independent of frames.
E. The driver cannot observe motion.

20. Which situation most clearly requires stating a non-ground reference frame to avoid confusion?

A. a runner on a moving train
B. a parked bicycle beside a tree
C. a book on a desk
D. a mailbox beside a road
E. a cone on a field
