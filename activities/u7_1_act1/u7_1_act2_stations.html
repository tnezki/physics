Question 1

1. An object with an excess of electrons is

A. positively charged
B. negatively charged
C. neutral
D. an insulator by definition
Answer: B — negatively charged
Work / rationale: An excess of electrons means there is more negative charge than positive charge, so the object is negatively charged.

Question 2

2. Two positive charges will

A. attract
B. become neutral automatically
C. have no electric interaction
D. repel
Answer: D — repel
Work / rationale: Like positive charges repel, so each force points away from the other charge.

Question 3

3. A neutral object has

A. balanced total positive and negative charge
B. no charged particles
C. only neutrons
D. more electrons than protons
Answer: A — balanced total positive and negative charge
Work / rationale: Neutral means the total positive and negative charges balance so net charge is zero.

Question 6

6. If distance between two charges becomes $2r$ with charges unchanged, the force becomes

A. $F/4$
B. $2F$
C. $F/2$
D. $4F$
Answer: A — $F/4$
Work / rationale: Coulomb force follows $F\propto1/r^2$. With $r\to2r$, $F_{new}=F/2^2=F/4$.

Question 7

7. If one charge magnitude triples at fixed distance, the force magnitude becomes

A. $F/3$
B. $9F$
C. unchanged
D. $3F$
Answer: D — $3F$
Work / rationale: Coulomb force is proportional to the product of charge magnitudes. Tripling one charge gives $F_{new}=3F$.

Answer: A — A nearby charge can redistribute mobile charge before any contact occurs.

Question 17

A. $3F$
B. $F/9$

Answer: B — $F/9$
Work / rationale: With distance changing from $r$ to $3r$, $F_{new}=F/3^2=F/9$.

Question 18

18. Both charge magnitudes double while distance also doubles. Compared with the original force, the new force is

A. twice as large
B. four times as large
C. one-fourth as large
D. the same
Answer: D — the same
Work / rationale: Doubling both charges multiplies $q_1q_2$ by 4, while doubling distance multiplies $r^2$ by 4; the factors cancel, so the force is unchanged.
