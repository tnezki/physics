Find Someone Who — Set 1 — Section 7.1: Electric Charge and Forces

1. An object has more protons than electrons. What is its net charge sign?

2. A positive sphere and a negative sphere are released near one another. Predict the direction of their interaction.

3. A glass rod is rubbed with cloth and loses electrons. Identify the rod’s final charge sign.

4. Classify copper wire and dry rubber as conductor or insulator.

5. A charged sphere physically touches a neutral metal sphere and charge redistributes. Name the charging process.

6. Use the electron-transfer figure to identify the direction electrons moved and explain the signs left on the two materials.

7. The force between two charged particles is 18 N. Their distance is doubled with charge amounts unchanged. Determine the new force.

8. At the same distance, both charge magnitudes are doubled. By what factor does the electric-force magnitude change?

9. A metal sphere and a plastic sphere each receive extra electrons at one spot. Compare how the added charge is expected to distribute and explain why.

10. A grounded conductor is near a charged object. Explain how the Earth connection can allow electrons to enter or leave the conductor during induction.

11. A classmate argues that attraction proves two objects have opposite net charges. Explain why a charged object can also attract a neutral conductor and what additional evidence is needed.

12. A lab group reports that doubling distance doubled the electric force. Use Coulomb’s law to critique the claim and describe a result that would be more consistent with the model.

13. A lab card says an object has equal total positive and negative charge. Which label fits?

A. positive
B. neutral
C. negative
D. grounded
E. induced

14. A pair of known negative pith balls swings apart after release. What interaction is shown?

A. attraction
B. polarization
C. grounding
D. contact charging
E. repulsion

15. A cloth loses six electrons while a strip gains six. Which conclusion follows?

A. Charge was transferred while the isolated pair kept the same total charge.
B. Six protons moved into the strip.
C. Twelve new charges were created.
D. Both materials must be positive.
E. Charge conservation does not apply to rubbing.

16. Why is a metal pan better than a plastic ruler for demonstrating charge redistribution?

A. Metal contains no electrons.
B. Plastic contains only neutral particles.
C. Metal always has a positive net charge.
D. Mobile charge can move throughout the metal more readily.
E. Plastic destroys excess electrons.

17. A charged rod is brought near two touching metal spheres; they are separated before the rod is removed. Which process is being used?

A. friction
B. direct contact charging
C. induction
D. grounding
E. polarization only

18. The same two charges are moved from distance $r$ to $4r$. The force becomes

A. $4F$
B. $F/16$
C. $F/4$
D. $16F$
E. unchanged

19. A neutral aluminum sphere shifts electrons to its far side when a negative rod approaches. What explains the attraction?

A. Positive charge is created near the rod.
B. Protons leave the sphere.
C. The sphere becomes a permanent negative charge.
D. All electrons jump to Earth.
E. The near side becomes relatively positive while the object remains neutral overall.

20. Which response best repairs the claim “friction produces charge”?

A. Friction can move electrons between materials; it does not create net charge in an isolated pair.
B. Friction changes electrons into protons.
C. The statement is correct whenever rubber is used.
D. Only conductors conserve charge.
E. Friction removes charge conservation.
