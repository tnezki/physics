Question 1

1. An object has more protons than electrons. What is its net charge sign?

Answer / rationale: The object is positive. More protons than electrons means the positive charge exceeds the negative charge.

Question 2

2. A positive sphere and a negative sphere are released near one another. Predict the direction of their interaction.

Answer / rationale: The spheres attract. Opposite net charges exert forces toward one another.

Question 3

3. A glass rod is rubbed with cloth and loses electrons. Identify the rod’s final charge sign.

Answer / rationale: The rod becomes positive. Losing electrons removes negative charge while the number of protons stays essentially fixed.

Station 2 Answers

Question 4

4. Classify copper wire and dry rubber as conductor or insulator.

Answer / rationale: Copper wire is a conductor; dry rubber is an insulator. Mobile electrons can redistribute readily in copper but not through dry rubber.

Question 5

5. A charged sphere physically touches a neutral metal sphere and charge redistributes. Name the charging process.

Answer / rationale: This is charging by contact (conduction). Physical contact allows mobile charge to redistribute between the metal spheres.

Question 6

6. Use the electron-transfer figure to identify the direction electrons moved and explain the signs left on the two materials.

Answer / rationale: The figure shows electrons moving from the fur to the rod. The rod gains electrons and becomes negative; the fur loses electrons and becomes positive. Total charge is transferred, not created.

Question 7

7. The force between two charged particles is 18 N. Their distance is doubled with charge amounts unchanged. Determine the new force.

Work: Coulomb force varies as $1/r^2$. Doubling distance gives $F_{new}=18/(2^2)=18/4=4.5\text{ N}$. Answer: $4.5\text{ N}$.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. At the same distance, both charge magnitudes are doubled. By what factor does the electric-force magnitude change?

Work / rationale: Coulomb force is proportional to $|q_1q_2|$. Doubling both charges multiplies the product by $2\times2=4$. Answer: the force becomes 4 times as large.

Question 9

9. A metal sphere and a plastic sphere each receive extra electrons at one spot. Compare how the added charge is expected to distribute and explain why.

Answer / rationale: On the metal sphere, added electrons can move and redistribute over the conducting surface. On the plastic sphere, excess charge stays much more localized because the material strongly restricts charge motion.

Question 10

10. A grounded conductor is near a charged object. Explain how the Earth connection can allow electrons to enter or leave the conductor during induction.

Answer / rationale: Grounding connects the conductor to the Earth, a huge charge reservoir. Depending on the nearby charge, electrons can flow from Earth into the conductor or from the conductor into Earth, allowing induction to leave a net charge after the ground is removed.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A classmate argues that attraction proves two objects have opposite net charges. Explain why a charged object can also attract a neutral conductor and what additional evidence is needed.

Answer / rationale: Attraction alone does not prove opposite net charges because a charged object can polarize a neutral conductor, bringing opposite charge slightly closer and producing net attraction. Additional evidence such as repulsion with a known charge or a sign-sensitive measurement is needed.

Question 12

12. A lab group reports that doubling distance doubled the electric force. Use Coulomb’s law to critique the claim and describe a result that would be more consistent with the model.

Work / rationale: Coulomb’s law gives $F\propto1/r^2$, so doubling distance should make the force one-fourth as large, not twice as large. From 18 N, a consistent result would be about $18/4=4.5\text{ N}$.

Answer: E — repulsion
Work / rationale: Both pith balls are negative, so like charges repel and the balls swing apart.

Work / rationale: The six electrons lost by one material are exactly the six gained by the other, demonstrating transfer with conservation of total charge.

A. friction
B. direct contact charging
C. induction
D. grounding
E. polarization only
Answer: C — induction
Work / rationale: A nearby charge separates charge in the touching conductors and they are separated before the external rod is removed; that is charging by induction.

Work / rationale: Coulomb force varies as $1/r^2$, so $F_{new}=F/4^2=F/16$.
