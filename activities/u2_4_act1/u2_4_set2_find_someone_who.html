Problem 1

An object starts from rest and falls freely for 2 s. Using g ≈ 10 m/s², determine the speed gained.

Answer: For an object starting from rest in ideal free fall, v = gt ≈ (10 m/s²)(2 s) = 20 m/s downward.

Problem 2

Use the ideal free-fall table to compare how distance fallen changes from one second to the next. Then explain why a sheet of paper falling through air may not follow this pattern.

Time (s)Distance fallen (m)
15
220
345

Answer: Ideal free fall follows d = ½gt². Air resistance can reduce acceleration below the ideal value and alter the pattern.

Problem 3

An object starts from rest and falls freely for 4 s. Using g ≈ 10 m/s², determine the speed gained.

Answer: For an object starting from rest in ideal free fall, v = gt ≈ (10 m/s²)(4 s) = 40 m/s downward.

Problem 4

Two compact objects of masses 3 kg and 7 kg are dropped together in a vacuum from the same height. Predict their accelerations and explain why one does not “fall faster” simply because it is heavier.

Answer: In ideal free fall, gravity produces the same acceleration for all objects regardless of mass.
