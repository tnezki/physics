Teacher Key — Page 1

1. 40 m/s downward — Speed gained = g t = (10 m/s²)(4 s) = 40 m/s. Direction is downward.
2. With air resistance removed, both are in ideal free fall and have the same gravitational acceleration near Earth. — In ideal free fall, gravitational acceleration is independent of object mass.
3. After 3 s: 45 m; after 4 s: 80 m. The ideal distance grows with t². A filter with significant air resistance is not in ideal free fall and can reach nearly constant speed. — d=5t² gives 45 m and 80 m. Significant air resistance changes acceleration, so the ideal free-fall model no longer applies.
4. They reach the floor together if released from the same height with the same initial velocity. With air resistance ignored, both have the same downward acceleration g. — In ideal free fall, all objects near Earth share the same gravitational acceleration regardless of mass.
