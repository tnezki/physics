Problem 5

An object starts from rest and falls freely for 3 s. Using g ≈ 10 m/s², determine the speed gained.

  1. about 30 m/s downward
  2. about 3 m/s downward
  3. about 13 m/s downward
  4. about 30 m/s upward

Answer: For an object starting from rest in ideal free fall, v = gt ≈ (10 m/s²)(3 s) = 30 m/s downward.

Problem 6

Use the ideal free-fall table to compare how distance fallen changes from one second to the next. Then explain why a sheet of paper falling through air may not follow this pattern.

Time (s)Distance fallen (m)
15
220
345
  1. Distance increases by progressively larger amounts because speed is increasing; a sheet of paper can deviate because air resistance is significant.
  2. Equal distances are covered each second, and air has no effect.
  3. Distance decreases each second because gravity weakens.
  4. Any falling object must exactly match the table.

Answer: Ideal free fall follows d = ½gt². Air resistance can reduce acceleration below the ideal value and alter the pattern.
