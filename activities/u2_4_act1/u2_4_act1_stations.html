1. State what must be true about air resistance for the simple ideal free-fall model to apply.

Answer / work: Air resistance must be negligible or ignored so gravity is the only significant influence.

Station 3 Answers

Question 6

6. A dropped ball has been falling for 5 s. Determine its speed using $g=10\text{ m/s}^2$.

Answer / work: $v=gt=(10\text{ m/s}^2)(5\text{ s})=50\text{ m/s}$.

Question 7

7. A rock falls from rest for 2.5 s. Use $d=\frac12gt^2$ to determine its distance fallen.

Answer / work: $d=\frac12gt^2=\frac12(10)(2.5)^2=31.25\text{ m}$, about 31 m.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. Use the graph. Compare the total free-fall distance at 2 s and 4 s and explain why the graph becomes steeper.

Answer / work: At 2 s the graph shows about 20 m; at 4 s it shows about 80 m. The graph becomes steeper because the object’s speed increases with time, so it covers more distance during each equal time interval.

Question 9

9. A ball’s speed changes from 0 to 30 m/s during 3 s of free fall. Determine its average velocity magnitude for the interval, then calculate the distance traveled.

Answer / work: For constant acceleration from 0 to 30 m/s, the average velocity magnitude is $(0+30)/2=15\text{ m/s}$. The distance is $d=v_{avg}t=(15)(3)=45\text{ m}$.

Question 10

10. Total free-fall distances are 5, 20, 45, and 80 m at 1, 2, 3, and 4 s. Determine the distance traveled during the fourth second alone.

Answer / work: During the fourth second, the object goes from 45 m total to 80 m total: $80-45=35\text{ m}$.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A coin and flat sheet of paper reach the floor at different times in air. Explain why that observation alone does not show different gravitational accelerations.

Answer / work: The different fall times can be caused by different air-resistance effects, especially because the flat paper has much more drag relative to its weight. The observation in air does not isolate gravitational acceleration.

Question 12

12. Compare a coin and feather falling in air with the same pair falling in vacuum. Use the contrast to separate the effects of gravity and air resistance.

Answer / work: In air, the feather is slowed much more by air resistance, so the coin arrives first. In vacuum, air resistance is absent and both objects accelerate at the same gravitational rate, so they fall together.

Rationale: Near Earth, speed changes by about $10\text{ m/s}$ each second in ideal free fall.

Station 7 Answers

Question 16

16. Ignoring air resistance, a heavy ball and a light ball have

A. equal acceleration due to gravity
B. different g values
C. zero acceleration at release
D. equal mass
E. no velocity change
Answer: A — equal acceleration due to gravity
Rationale: In a vacuum, different masses fall with the same gravitational acceleration because air resistance is absent.

Question 17

17. Distance fallen from rest in 2 s is about

A. 10 m
B. 20 m
C. 30 m
D. 40 m
E. 50 m
Answer: B — 20 m
Rationale: From rest, $d=\tfrac12gt^2=\tfrac12(10)(2^2)=20\text{ m}$.

Question 18

18. Which interval has the greatest distance for ideal free fall from rest?

A. the fourth one-second interval
B. the first one-second interval
C. all one-second intervals are equal
D. the second only
E. none; distance decreases
Answer: A — the fourth one-second interval
Rationale: Because the object speeds up, it covers the greatest distance during the latest equal-time interval.

Station 8 Answers

Question 19

19. A ball moving upward after release can still be in free fall because

A. gravity can be the only significant influence even while velocity is upward
B. free fall requires zero acceleration
C. upward motion removes gravity
D. air resistance must push it up
E. mass becomes zero
Answer: A — gravity can be the only significant influence even while velocity is upward
Rationale: After release, gravity can remain the only significant influence even while the ball is still moving upward.

Question 20

20. At the very top of an ideal thrown ball’s path, the ball has

A. zero speed but nonzero downward acceleration
B. zero speed and zero acceleration
C. maximum upward speed
D. upward acceleration
E. no gravity
Answer: A — zero speed but nonzero downward acceleration
Rationale: At the highest point the instantaneous velocity is zero, but gravitational acceleration is still downward and nonzero.
