Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. Free fall is motion under the significant influence of

A. gravity alone
B. air resistance alone
C. a motor
D. support force
Answer: A — gravity alone
Work / rationale: In the ideal free-fall model, gravity is the only significant force/influence on the motion.

Question 2

2. The approximate value of $g$ used here is

A. 10 m/s
B. 10 m/s²
C. 5 m/s²
D. 100 m/s²
Answer: B — 10 m/s²
Work / rationale: Near Earth this course uses $g\approx10\text{ m/s}^2$.

Question 3

3. A ball thrown upward after leaving the hand can still be in free fall if

A. its speed is increasing upward
B. gravity is absent
C. air resistance is ignored
D. it has zero mass
Answer: C — air resistance is ignored
Work / rationale: After release, a rising ball is still in free fall if air resistance is neglected and gravity is the only significant influence.

Answer: B — 30 m/s

Question 8

8. A dropped object reaches 40 m/s after 4 s. Its average velocity magnitude over the interval is

A. 10 m/s
B. 30 m/s
C. 40 m/s
D. 20 m/s
Answer: D — 20 m/s
Work / rationale: For constant acceleration from 0 to 40 m/s, $v_{avg}=(0+40)/2=20\text{ m/s}$.

Question 9

9. Total distances at 1, 2, 3 s are 5, 20, 45 m. Distance during the third second is

A. 25 m
B. 15 m
C. 20 m
D. 45 m
Answer: A — 25 m
Work / rationale: Distance during the third second is $45\text{ m}-20\text{ m}=25\text{ m}$.

Question 10

10. Why do free-fall distances grow by larger amounts each second?

A. gravity decreases each second
B. speed increases continuously under gravity
C. time intervals become longer
D. mass increases
Answer: B — speed increases continuously under gravity
Work / rationale: Gravity continuously increases the object’s speed, so it covers more distance during each later equal-time interval.

Work / rationale: A feather is strongly affected by drag, so air resistance makes its motion differ from the ideal free-fall model.

B. upward acceleration
C. zero acceleration
D. increasing mass
Answer: A — downward gravitational acceleration
Work / rationale: While the ball moves upward, gravity accelerates it downward, reducing the upward velocity.

Work / rationale: The later one-second interval covers more distance, which is the pattern expected for an object speeding up under gravity.
