Find Someone Who — Set 1 — Section 3.1: Force and Acceleration

1. A skateboard rolls north with constant velocity. What can you conclude about its net force and acceleration?

2. A box has 6 N left and 15 N right acting on it. State the net force and the acceleration direction.

3. Complete the pattern: same mass, three times the net force → ______ acceleration.

4. Complete the pattern: same net force, twice the mass → ______ acceleration.

5. An object moves west while the net force points north. Which direction does its acceleration point?

6. A 9-kg cart is pulled with a net force of 27 N east. Calculate its acceleration.

7. A 42-N net force causes 7 m/s² acceleration. Calculate the mass.

8. A 4-kg drone accelerates upward at 2.5 m/s². Determine the upward net force.

9. Use the source image showing equal force applied to different amounts of mass. Explain why the larger mass has the smaller acceleration.

10. Trial A uses a 3-kg cart with 12 N net force. Trial B uses the same cart with 30 N net force. Find both accelerations and compare the factor of change.

11. A classmate argues, “A large force always means a large acceleration.” Explain what important variable is missing from that claim and give a numerical counterexample.

12. A cart is moving left but accelerating right. Explain how this is possible and describe what its speed initially does if the rightward net force continues.

B. force direction
