1. A ball is dropped inside a vacuum chamber. What significant force acts after release?

Answer: Gravity (weight) is the only significant force after release in the vacuum chamber. Rationale: The vacuum removes significant air resistance, and no support force remains after release.

2. Why does a free-falling object accelerate rather than move at constant speed?

Rationale: Gravity gives the object a nonzero downward net force. By $F_{net}=ma$, a nonzero net force produces acceleration rather than constant velocity.

Work: With $100\text{ N}$ drag: $F_{net}=800-100=700\text{ N down}$. With $500\text{ N}$ drag: $F_{net}=800-500=300\text{ N down}$. Rationale: As air resistance grows, the downward net force and therefore the downward acceleration decrease.

Unit 3.3 · Activity 1 · Free Fall and Nonfree FallSolution 9
Solution 9
DOK 2

9. A 2-kg and a 6-kg object fall in a vacuum. Compare the gravitational force on each, then explain why their accelerations can still be equal.

Work: $W=mg$. The $2\text{ kg}$ object has $20\text{ N}$ weight; the $6\text{ kg}$ object has $60\text{ N}$ weight. Yet $a=F/m=mg/m=g$, so both accelerate at about $10\text{ m/s}^2$ downward in vacuum.

Unit 3.3 · Activity 1 · Free Fall and Nonfree FallSolution 11
Solution 11
DOK 3

11. A student predicts a heavier vacuum-dropped ball must fall faster because gravity pulls harder on it. Explain why the greater force does not produce greater $g$ when mass is also greater.

Rationale: A heavier object does feel a larger gravitational force, $F_g=mg$, but it also has proportionally greater inertia. Using $a=F/m=(mg)/m=g$, the mass cancels, so all objects have the same free-fall acceleration in vacuum.

DOK 3

12. A parachutist changes from a tucked position to a spread-out position. Build a force-and-motion explanation for the immediate change in air resistance, net force, and acceleration.

Rationale: Spreading out increases cross-sectional area, so air resistance increases immediately. It can become greater than weight, making the net force and acceleration upward while the parachutist is still moving downward; that upward acceleration reduces the downward speed.
