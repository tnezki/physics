Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. A ball is dropped inside a vacuum chamber. What significant force acts after release?

Answer: Gravity (weight) is the only significant force after release in the vacuum chamber. Rationale: The vacuum removes significant air resistance, and no support force remains after release.

Question 2

2. Why does a free-falling object accelerate rather than move at constant speed?

Rationale: Gravity gives the object a nonzero downward net force. By $F_{net}=ma$, a nonzero net force produces acceleration rather than constant velocity.

Question 3

3. A coffee filter falls through air and air resistance matters. Classify the motion as free fall or nonfree fall.

Answer: Nonfree fall. Rationale: Air resistance is significant in addition to gravity.

Station 2 Answers

Question 4

4. What happens to acceleration when upward air resistance grows closer to the downward weight?

Rationale: As upward air resistance grows toward the downward weight, the downward net force becomes smaller, so the downward acceleration decreases toward zero.

Question 5

5. What is the net force at terminal speed?

Answer: $F_{net}=0$. Rationale: At terminal speed, upward air resistance equals downward weight, so acceleration is zero while the object can continue moving downward at constant speed.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. A skydiver’s weight is 800 N. Air resistance increases from 100 N to 500 N. Calculate the downward net force in each case and compare the acceleration trend.

Work: With $100\text{ N}$ drag: $F_{net}=800-100=700\text{ N down}$. With $500\text{ N}$ drag: $F_{net}=800-500=300\text{ N down}$. Rationale: As air resistance grows, the downward net force and therefore the downward acceleration decrease.

Question 9

9. A 2-kg and a 6-kg object fall in a vacuum. Compare the gravitational force on each, then explain why their accelerations can still be equal.

Work: $W=mg$. The $2\text{ kg}$ object has $20\text{ N}$ weight; the $6\text{ kg}$ object has $60\text{ N}$ weight. Yet $a=F/m=mg/m=g$, so both accelerate at about $10\text{ m/s}^2$ downward in vacuum.

Question 10

10. A falling object moves downward at constant speed. What must be true about weight and air resistance? Explain using net force.

Rationale: Constant downward speed means $a=0$, so $F_{net}=0$. Therefore the upward air resistance must equal the downward weight.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A student predicts a heavier vacuum-dropped ball must fall faster because gravity pulls harder on it. Explain why the greater force does not produce greater $g$ when mass is also greater.

Rationale: A heavier object does feel a larger gravitational force, $F_g=mg$, but it also has proportionally greater inertia. Using $a=F/m=(mg)/m=g$, the mass cancels, so all objects have the same free-fall acceleration in vacuum.

Question 12

12. A parachutist changes from a tucked position to a spread-out position. Build a force-and-motion explanation for the immediate change in air resistance, net force, and acceleration.

Rationale: Spreading out increases cross-sectional area, so air resistance increases immediately. It can become greater than weight, making the net force and acceleration upward while the parachutist is still moving downward; that upward acceleration reduces the downward speed.

B. an open parachute descending
C. a leaf fluttering through air
D. a paper sheet drifting
E. a balloon rising
Answer: A — a coin falling in an evacuated tube
Work / rationale: An evacuated tube makes air resistance negligible, so the coin is closest to ideal free fall.

Question 14

14. When air resistance is ignored, all freely falling objects near Earth have

A. the same acceleration $g$
B. the same weight
C. the same mass
D. zero net force
E. the same speed at all times
Answer: A — the same acceleration $g$
Work / rationale: With air resistance neglected, every object near Earth accelerates at the same $g$ regardless of mass.

Question 15

A. 300 N downward
B. 600 N downward
C. 300 N upward
D. 450 N upward
E. zero
Answer: A — 300 N downward
Work / rationale: $F_{net}=450\text{ N down}-150\text{ N up}=300\text{ N down}$.
