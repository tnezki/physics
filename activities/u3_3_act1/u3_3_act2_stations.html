C. 1 m/s² downward
D. zero
Answer: B — 10 m/s² downward
Work / rationale: Near Earth this course uses $g\approx10\text{ m/s}^2$ downward.

Work / rationale: An open parachute creates significant air resistance, so the motion is nonfree fall.

Answer: C — continues downward at constant velocity
Work / rationale: Equal upward and downward forces give $F_{net}=0$, so the falling object continues downward at constant velocity rather than stopping instantly.

Question 8

8. Why do a heavy and light object have equal free-fall acceleration in vacuum?

D. The gravitational force increases with mass in the same proportion that inertia does.

Work / rationale: The gravitational force increases in direct proportion to mass, while inertia also increases in direct proportion to mass, so $a=F/m=g$ for both.

Question 9

A. It decreases.
B. It increases without limit.

D. It becomes mass.
Answer: A — It decreases.
Work / rationale: As air resistance grows upward, it subtracts more from weight, so the downward net force decreases.

Question 10

A. the compact object
B. the wide flat object
C. both must have $g$ exactly

Answer: B — the wide flat object
Work / rationale: At the same mass and speed, the wide flat object generally has greater air resistance and therefore a smaller downward net force and acceleration.
