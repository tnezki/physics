Question 1

1. Identify the vibrating source in this situation: a loudspeaker cone moves back and forth and a tone is heard.

Answer / rationale: The loudspeaker cone is the vibrating source. Its back-and-forth motion drives neighboring particles and launches the sound disturbance.

Question 2

2. What is a compression in a longitudinal sound wave?

Answer / rationale: A compression is a region of a longitudinal wave where medium particles are closer together than average, so density/pressure is higher.

Question 3

3. What does the term medium mean for a sound wave?

Answer / rationale: The medium is the matter whose particles interact to transmit the mechanical disturbance from place to place.

Question 6

6. The chart is a relative-speed model based on the section reading. Describe the ordering of sound propagation through gases, liquids, and solids, and explain what the model says about sound speed depending on the medium.

Rationale: The model orders sound speed as gases slowest, liquids faster, and solids generally fastest. The key conclusion is that sound speed depends on the properties of the transmitting medium rather than having one universal value.

Question 7

7. A sound pulse travels 1020 m in 3.0 s through room-temperature air. Determine its speed and compare it with the class approximation of 340 m/s.

Work: $v=d/t=1020/3.0=340\text{ m/s}$. Answer: $340\text{ m/s}$, which matches the class approximation.

Question 8

8. A listener is 850 m from a sound source. Estimate the travel time through air using 340 m/s.

Work: $t=d/v=850/340=2.5\text{ s}$. Answer: about $2.5\text{ s}$.

Question 9

9. A person places an ear on a long solid beam while another person taps the far end. Explain why the beam can transmit sound even though it does not flow like air.

Rationale: The solid does not need to flow. Its atoms vibrate about local positions and transfer the disturbance to neighboring atoms through their interactions, allowing sound energy to propagate through the beam.

Question 10

10. Two sounds have equal amplitude but frequencies 250 Hz and 500 Hz. Compare their loudness (from the given amplitude information) and pitch.

Rationale: Equal amplitude suggests similar loudness under the simplified model. The $500$ Hz tone has the higher pitch because pitch primarily tracks frequency; the $250$ Hz tone has the lower pitch.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A student draws air particles traveling from the speaker all the way to the listener. Explain how the particle model should be revised to show local vibration and energy transfer.

Rationale: Air particles should be shown oscillating back and forth around local equilibrium positions, creating moving compressions and rarefactions. The disturbance and energy travel to the listener; the same particles do not travel the full source-to-listener distance.

Question 12

12. A claim says sound can cross interplanetary space because radio signals can. Explain why this comparison confuses mechanical sound waves with a different kind of wave.

Rationale: Ordinary sound is a mechanical wave and needs matter to transmit particle-to-particle motion, so it cannot cross a vacuum directly. Radio waves are electromagnetic waves and can propagate through vacuum; they are a different wave type.

A. loudness
B. sound speed in every medium

Answer: C — pitch
Work / rationale: Pitch primarily tracks frequency, so increasing frequency raises pitch when amplitude is unchanged.
