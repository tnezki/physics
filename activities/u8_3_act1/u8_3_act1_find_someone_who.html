Find Someone Who — Set 1 — Section 8.3: Electromagnets and Magnetic Forces

1. How does a current-carrying coil differ from a permanent magnet in how its magnetism is produced?

2. What happens qualitatively to electromagnet strength when the current is increased?

3. Why is soft iron often placed inside an electromagnet coil?

4. In $F=qvB$, what does $B$ represent?

5. In $F=BIL$, what does $L$ represent?

6. Use the force-versus-current graph for a fixed wire in a fixed field. Describe the relationship and estimate the force when the current is $6\text{ A}$.

7. Two identical electromagnets differ only in current: one carries 1.5 A and the other 3.0 A. Predict the stronger one and explain why.

8. A moving charge has $q=1.2\text{ C}$, $v=5.0\text{ m/s}$, and $B=0.80\text{ T}$ with perpendicular motion. Calculate the force magnitude.

9. A straight wire segment $0.40\text{ m}$ long carries $2.5\text{ A}$ in a $1.2\text{ T}$ field perpendicular to the wire. Calculate the magnetic force.

10. A $0.50\text{ m}$ wire carrying $4.0\text{ A}$ experiences $3.0\text{ N}$ of magnetic force perpendicular to the field. Determine the field strength.

11. A crane operator can release scrap metal by switching off an electromagnet. Explain the behavior using current-produced field and iron-core/domain ideas rather than saying the magnet “runs out.”

12. Two designs have the same current and number of turns, but one includes a soft-iron core. Explain what evidence would support the claim that the core improves the lifting electromagnet.

13. An electromagnet is produced most directly by

  1. a wooden block
  2. a glass lens
  3. a neutral object with no current
  4. a stationary unconnected wire
  5. electric current through a coil

14. Which combination is most likely to make a stronger lifting electromagnet?

  1. an open circuit and no core
  2. fewer turns, smaller current, and no core
  3. less current and fewer turns
  4. more coil turns, larger current, and a suitable iron core
  5. zero current with many turns

15. In $B=F/(qv)$, the equation is useful for finding

  1. electric power
  2. resistance
  3. voltage ratio
  4. magnetic-field strength
  5. coil turns

16. A $2\text{ C}$ charge moves at $3\text{ m/s}$ through a $0.5\text{ T}$ field perpendicularly. The force magnitude is

  1. 1 N
  2. 0.33 N
  3. 12 N
  4. 3 N
  5. 5 N

17. A $0.50\text{ m}$ wire carries $4\text{ A}$ in a $0.25\text{ T}$ perpendicular field. The force magnitude is

  1. 4.75 N
  2. 8 N
  3. 0.125 N
  4. 0.50 N
  5. 2 N

18. A magnetic force on a moving charge can be perpendicular to its motion. This means the force can

  1. change the direction of motion without simply acting as a forward push
  2. only increase speed
  3. remove the magnetic field
  4. only decrease mass
  5. make the charge stationary instantly

19. A speaker coil moves because changing current in a magnetic field produces

  1. free electrical energy
  2. a changing magnetic force on the coil
  3. no interaction
  4. an isolated pole
  5. gravitational attraction only

20. What is a key advantage of an electromagnet over a permanent magnet in a crane?

  1. It has only one pole.
  2. Its field is unrelated to current.
  3. It requires no energy input.
  4. It cannot be turned off.
