Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. How does a current-carrying coil differ from a permanent magnet in how its magnetism is produced?

Answer: A current-carrying coil produces controllable magnetism from electric current; a permanent magnet retains magnetization because of persistent domain alignment without needing circuit current.

Question 2

2. What happens qualitatively to electromagnet strength when the current is increased?

Answer: Increasing current generally strengthens the electromagnet’s magnetic effect, all else equal.

Question 3

3. Why is soft iron often placed inside an electromagnet coil?

Answer: Soft iron has domains that align readily with the coil’s field, concentrating and strengthening the electromagnet while current flows.

Question 4

4. In $F=qvB$, what does $B$ represent?

Answer: $B$ represents magnetic-field strength (magnetic flux density), measured in tesla (T).

Question 5

5. In $F=BIL$, what does $L$ represent?

Answer: $L$ is the length of wire segment that lies in the magnetic field, measured in meters.

Question 8

8. A moving charge has $q=1.2\text{ C}$, $v=5.0\text{ m/s}$, and $B=0.80\text{ T}$ with perpendicular motion. Calculate the force magnitude.

Work: $F=qvB=(1.2)(5.0)(0.80)=4.8\text{ N}$. Answer: $4.8\text{ N}$.

Question 9

9. A straight wire segment $0.40\text{ m}$ long carries $2.5\text{ A}$ in a $1.2\text{ T}$ field perpendicular to the wire. Calculate the magnetic force.

Work: $F=BIL=(1.2)(2.5)(0.40)=1.2\text{ N}$. Answer: $1.2\text{ N}$.

Question 10

10. A $0.50\text{ m}$ wire carrying $4.0\text{ A}$ experiences $3.0\text{ N}$ of magnetic force perpendicular to the field. Determine the field strength.

Work: $B=F/(IL)=3.0/[(4.0)(0.50)]=1.5\text{ T}$. Answer: $1.5\text{ T}$.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A crane operator can release scrap metal by switching off an electromagnet. Explain the behavior using current-produced field and iron-core/domain ideas rather than saying the magnet “runs out.”

Answer: Switching off current collapses the coil’s controllable magnetic field. The soft-iron core’s domains are no longer strongly aligned by that field, so the lifting attraction drops and the scrap can be released.

Question 12

12. Two designs have the same current and number of turns, but one includes a soft-iron core. Explain what evidence would support the claim that the core improves the lifting electromagnet.

Answer: Test both designs under the same current, turns, geometry, gap, and load conditions, then measure a consistent strength indicator such as maximum supported mass. Greater lift for the iron-core design would support the claim.
