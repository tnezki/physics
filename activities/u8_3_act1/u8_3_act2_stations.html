Question 1

1. What makes an ordinary wire coil an electromagnet?

  1. the wire color
  2. electric current through the coil
  3. a permanent isolated north pole
  4. gravity
Answer: B — electric current through the coil
Work / rationale: Current through a coil produces the controllable magnetic field that makes it an electromagnet.

Question 2

2. Which change usually strengthens an electromagnet?

  1. opening the circuit
  2. removing all turns
  3. reducing current to zero
  4. adding more turns to the coil
Answer: D — adding more turns to the coil
Work / rationale: Adding turns usually strengthens the coil field because more turn contributions can reinforce.

Question 3

3. Why can an iron core improve an electromagnet?

  1. its magnetic domains can align with the coil field
  2. it stops the current
  3. it removes magnetic fields
  4. it creates free energy
Answer: A — its magnetic domains can align with the coil field
Work / rationale: Soft iron domains align with the coil field and concentrate/strengthen the electromagnet.

Work / rationale: $F=BIL$ depends on $B$, $I$, and $L$; the charge of one particle is not in the wire-force relationship.

  3. double
  4. halve
Answer: C — double
Work / rationale: With $q$ and $B$ fixed, $F=qvB$ is directly proportional to speed, so doubling $v$ doubles $F$.
