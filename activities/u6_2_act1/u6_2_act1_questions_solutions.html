6. Use the plane-mirror figure to explain why the image is located behind the mirror even though no light travels there.

Rationale: Real reflected rays leave the mirror and reach the observer. The brain/diagram back-traces those rays in straight lines; their backward extensions intersect behind the mirror, which defines the apparent virtual-image location even though no light travels through that point.

8. An object is 0.80 m from a plane mirror. Describe the apparent image location and explain why the image cannot be caught on a screen behind the mirror.

Rationale: A plane-mirror image appears the same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front, so it appears $0.80\text{ m}$ behind the mirror. It cannot be caught there because no real reflected rays converge at that location; only backward extensions do.

DOK 2

9. A periscope ray must turn at two plane mirrors. Explain how the law of reflection constrains the orientation of each mirror.

Rationale: At each mirror, the surface orientation fixes the local normal. The mirror must be angled so the incoming and outgoing path segments make equal angles with that normal. Applying $\theta_i=\theta_r$ at both reflections sets the periscope path.

Problem 10

10. A rough wall and a smooth window both reflect light. Explain why one usually gives a clearer image for a fixed observer.

Unit 6.2 · Activity 1 · Reflection and MirrorsSolution 10
Solution 10
DOK 2

10. A rough wall and a smooth window both reflect light. Explain why one usually gives a clearer image for a fixed observer.

Rationale: On a smooth surface, neighboring local normals are nearly aligned, so rays from a given object remain organized after reflection and can form a clear image. A rough wall has many differently oriented normals, spreading reflected rays in many directions (diffuse reflection).

Unit 6.2 · Activity 1 · Reflection and MirrorsSolution 11
Solution 11
DOK 3

11. Someone claims diffuse reflection breaks the law of reflection. Evaluate the claim by considering the many differently oriented microscopic surface normals.

Rationale: Diffuse reflection does not break the law. Each microscopic patch obeys $\theta_i=\theta_r$ about its own local normal; the reflected directions differ because the local normals differ across the rough surface.
