Question 8

8. Why does rough paper still reflect light?

A. Rough surfaces stop obeying reflection laws.
B. Each small surface region reflects according to its own local normal.
C. Paper creates new light.
D. The reflected rays all remain parallel.
Answer: B — Each small surface region reflects according to its own local normal.
Work / rationale: Each microscopic patch has its own local normal and obeys the law of reflection; varying normals spread the rays.

Question 9

9. A ray strikes exactly along the normal. What happens?

A. It reflects 90° sideways.
B. It reflects back along the same line.
C. It becomes refracted only.
D. It forms no reflection.
Answer: B — It reflects back along the same line.
Work / rationale: At $0^\circ$ to the normal, the reflected ray retraces the incoming line in the opposite direction.

Question 10

10. Why can a virtual mirror image be seen but not projected behind the mirror?

A. No light reaches the eye.
B. The mirror emits an image but no rays.
C. The image absorbs all screen light.
D. Real reflected rays reach the eye, but only their backward extensions meet at the apparent image.
Answer: D — Real reflected rays reach the eye, but only their backward extensions meet at the apparent image.
Work / rationale: Real reflected rays reach the eye, but their backward extensions—not the actual rays—intersect at the virtual image.
