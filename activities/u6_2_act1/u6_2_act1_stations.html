Question 1

1. What does it mean for light to reflect?

Answer / rationale: Reflection is the return of light from a surface back into the original medium after it strikes the boundary.

Question 2

2. Which ray is traveling toward the mirror before the interaction?

Answer / rationale: The incident ray is the ray traveling toward the mirror before the interaction.

Question 3

3. Which ray leaves the mirror after the interaction?

Answer / rationale: The reflected ray is the ray traveling away from the mirror after the interaction.

Question 4

4. From what line—not from the mirror surface—are incidence and reflection angles measured?

Answer / rationale: Both angles are measured from the normal, the line perpendicular to the mirror at the point where the ray strikes.

Question 5

5. Is a plane-mirror image real or virtual?

Answer / rationale: A plane mirror forms a virtual image because the reflected rays do not actually meet at the apparent image location behind the mirror.

Question 6

6. Use the plane-mirror figure to explain why the image is located behind the mirror even though no light travels there.

Rationale: Real reflected rays leave the mirror and reach the observer. The brain/diagram back-traces those rays in straight lines; their backward extensions intersect behind the mirror, which defines the apparent virtual-image location even though no light travels through that point.

Question 7

7. A ray leaves a plane mirror at 57° from the normal. Determine its angle of incidence.

Work / rationale: The law of reflection gives $\theta_i=\theta_r$. Therefore $\theta_i=57^\circ$. Answer: $57^\circ$ from the normal.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. An object is 0.80 m from a plane mirror. Describe the apparent image location and explain why the image cannot be caught on a screen behind the mirror.

Rationale: A plane-mirror image appears the same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front, so it appears $0.80\text{ m}$ behind the mirror. It cannot be caught there because no real reflected rays converge at that location; only backward extensions do.

Question 9

9. A periscope ray must turn at two plane mirrors. Explain how the law of reflection constrains the orientation of each mirror.

Rationale: At each mirror, the surface orientation fixes the local normal. The mirror must be angled so the incoming and outgoing path segments make equal angles with that normal. Applying $\theta_i=\theta_r$ at both reflections sets the periscope path.

Question 10

10. A rough wall and a smooth window both reflect light. Explain why one usually gives a clearer image for a fixed observer.

Rationale: On a smooth surface, neighboring local normals are nearly aligned, so rays from a given object remain organized after reflection and can form a clear image. A rough wall has many differently oriented normals, spreading reflected rays in many directions (diffuse reflection).

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. Someone claims diffuse reflection breaks the law of reflection. Evaluate the claim by considering the many differently oriented microscopic surface normals.

Rationale: Diffuse reflection does not break the law. Each microscopic patch obeys $\theta_i=\theta_r$ about its own local normal; the reflected directions differ because the local normals differ across the rough surface.

Question 12

12. A classmate draws reflected rays physically continuing behind a mirror to the virtual image. Critique the model and distinguish real rays from backward extensions.

Rationale: The physical reflected rays remain on the observer side of the mirror. Lines drawn behind the mirror are only backward extensions used to locate the virtual image; they are not actual light paths.

Question 20

A. each microscopic surface region still follows equal angles about its own local normal
B. rough surfaces do not reflect
C. the reflected rays all have equal global direction
D. diffuse reflection is actually refraction

Work / rationale: Each microscopic patch still obeys equal-angle reflection about its own local normal; roughness only varies those normals.
