Question 1

1. Momentum conservation refers to the

A. total momentum of a chosen system
B. momentum of each object separately
C. kinetic energy of each object
D. largest momentum only
Answer: A — total momentum of a chosen system
Work / rationale: Conservation of momentum applies to the total vector momentum of a chosen system.

Question 2

2. A force between two objects both inside the chosen system is

A. external
B. internal
C. zero by definition
D. always friction
Answer: B — internal
Work / rationale: A force between two objects that are both inside the chosen system is internal.

Question 3

3. Objects that stick together undergo an

A. elastic collision
B. explosion
C. inelastic collision
D. free-fall event
Answer: C — inelastic collision
Work / rationale: When objects stick together after collision, the collision is inelastic.

Question 6

6. A 2 kg cart at +4 m/s and 1 kg cart at −3 m/s have total momentum

A. +11 kg·m/s
B. +5 kg·m/s
C. −5 kg·m/s
D. +1 kg·m/s
Answer: B — +5 kg·m/s
Work / rationale: $p_{total}=(2)(+4)+(1)(-3)=8-3=+5\text{ kg\cdot m/s}$.

Question 7

7. A 1 kg cart at +6 m/s sticks to a 2 kg cart at rest. Their common speed is

A. 3 m/s
B. 6 m/s
C. 2 m/s
D. 9 m/s
Answer: C — 2 m/s
Work / rationale: Initial momentum is $(1)(6)=6$. After sticking, total mass is $3\text{ kg}$, so $v_f=6/3=2\text{ m/s}$.

Question 19

19. Which observation most threatens momentum conservation for a chosen cart system during collision?

A. the carts exert large forces on each other
B. the carts have unequal masses
C. a strong outside force acts during the collision
D. one cart reverses direction
Answer: C — a strong outside force acts during the collision
Work / rationale: A strong force from outside the chosen cart system gives external impulse and can change the system's total momentum.

Question 20

20. A rebound can give a larger momentum change than merely stopping because

A. mass doubles during rebound
B. the time must be zero
C. momentum has no direction
D. the momentum reverses direction
Answer: D — the momentum reverses direction
Work / rationale: Reversing direction changes momentum from one sign to the opposite sign, which can produce a larger $|\Delta p|$ than stopping at zero.
