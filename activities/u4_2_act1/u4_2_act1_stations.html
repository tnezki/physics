Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. State the before-and-after momentum relationship used for an isolated or nearly isolated system.

Answer / rationale: For an isolated or nearly isolated system, total momentum before equals total momentum after: $\vec p_{total,before}=\vec p_{total,after}$, provided net external impulse is negligible.

Question 2

2. Define external force relative to a chosen system.

Answer / rationale: An external force is a force exerted by something outside the chosen system on something inside it; it crosses the system boundary.

Question 3

3. In an elastic collision, do the objects necessarily stick together or rebound?

Answer / rationale: In an ideal elastic collision, the objects rebound rather than sticking together. Momentum is conserved for an isolated system, and kinetic energy is also conserved in the ideal elastic model.

Station 2 Answers

Question 4

4. What is recoil?

Answer / rationale: Recoil is backward motion of one part of a system as another part gains momentum in the opposite direction, such as a cannon moving backward when a cannonball moves forward.

Question 5

5. Why must momentum direction be tracked in collision problems?

Answer / rationale: Momentum is a vector, so direction determines sign and must be included when adding the momenta of different objects.

Station 3 Answers

Question 6

6. A 4 kg cart moves at +2 m/s and a 2 kg cart moves at −3 m/s. Determine the system’s total momentum.

Work: $p_{total}=(4)(+2)+(2)(-3)=+8-6=+2\text{ kg\cdot m/s}$. Answer: $+2\text{ kg\cdot m/s}$.

Question 7

7. A 1 kg cart at +8 m/s sticks to a 3 kg cart at rest. Find their common velocity immediately after the collision.

Work: $p_i=(1)(+8)+(3)(0)=8\text{ kg\cdot m/s}$. After sticking, $(1+3)v_f=8$, so $v_f=2\text{ m/s}$. Answer: $+2\text{ m/s}$.

Question 8

8. Use the collision figure to explain what can remain conserved even though the two objects move together after impact.

Rationale: The objects can stick and move together while the total momentum of the isolated two-object system remains conserved. Some kinetic energy can transform into thermal energy, sound, or deformation even though total momentum is unchanged.

Question 9

9. Before an isolated collision the total momentum is −6 kg·m/s. Afterward one object has +4 kg·m/s. Determine the momentum of the other object.

Work: $-6=(+4)+p_2$, so $p_2=-10\text{ kg\cdot m/s}$. Answer: $-10\text{ kg\cdot m/s}$.

Question 10

10. A ball approaches a paddle at +5 kg·m/s momentum and leaves with −3 kg·m/s. Find its momentum change and explain why reversing direction matters.

Work: $\Delta p=p_f-p_i=(-3)-(+5)=-8\text{ kg\cdot m/s}$. Rationale: Reversing direction matters because final momentum has the opposite sign, making the momentum change larger in magnitude than merely stopping.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A driver says, “The car stopped in the crash, so the car’s momentum was not conserved.” Explain why conservation cannot be judged until the system and external impulse are specified.

Rationale: Momentum conservation applies to the total momentum of a defined system, not automatically to one car. Whether the chosen system conserves momentum depends on the net external impulse during the crash; the system boundary and outside interactions must be identified first.

Question 12

12. A small cart and a massive cart exert equal interaction forces during a collision. Explain how their momentum changes are related while their velocity changes can differ.

Rationale: The carts exert equal-and-opposite forces for the same interaction time, so their impulses and momentum changes are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. Because $\Delta v=\Delta p/m$, the smaller-mass cart can have the larger velocity change.

Answer: A — something outside the chosen system

D. have zero final speed

Answer: A — rebound without lasting deformation or major thermal production
Work / rationale: An ideal elastic collision involves rebound with little lasting deformation/thermal production, rather than sticking.

Answer: A — backward motion associated with another part gaining forward momentum
Work / rationale: Recoil is opposite-direction motion of one part as another part gains momentum in the other direction.

Question 19

A. zero
B. positive
C. equal to their total mass
D. equal to their total kinetic energy
E. greater than before
Answer: A — zero
Work / rationale: The object starts at rest, so its initial total momentum is zero; the vector sum of fragment momenta must remain zero.

Question 20

A. Momentum can remain conserved for an isolated system even though kinetic energy changes form.
B. Momentum is never conserved when objects stick.
C. Objects must rebound with equal speed.
D. No internal forces act.
E. The system must have zero momentum.
Answer: A — Momentum can remain conserved for an isolated system even though kinetic energy changes form.
Work / rationale: In an isolated inelastic collision, total momentum can remain conserved even while kinetic energy transforms into other forms.
