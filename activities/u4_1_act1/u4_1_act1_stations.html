Station 1 Answers

Question 1

1. Define momentum without using the phrase “amount of motion.” Include both quantities that determine it.

Answer / rationale: Momentum is the vector quantity $\vec p=m\vec v$. It depends on both mass and velocity, including the velocity direction.

Question 2

2. If velocity is chosen as negative, what sign does momentum have for a positive mass?

Answer / rationale: Negative. Since mass is positive and $p=mv$, momentum has the same sign/direction as velocity.

Question 3

3. What is impulse: something an object possesses while moving, or something it experiences during an interaction?

Answer / rationale: Impulse is something an object experiences during an interaction. It depends on force and interaction time and changes the object's momentum.

Station 2 Answers

Question 4

4. State the impulse–momentum relationship.

Answer / rationale: $J=F_{avg}\Delta t=\Delta p$. The impulse delivered to an object equals its change in momentum.

Question 5

5. Name one safety feature whose purpose is to lengthen stopping time.

Answer / rationale: Example: a crumple zone, air bag, padded mat, or moving a catcher's glove backward. Each increases stopping time so the same momentum change can occur with a smaller average force.

Question 6

6. A 0.40 kg ball moves west at 15 m/s. Calculate its momentum, using west as negative.

Work: $p=mv=(0.40\text{ kg})(-15\text{ m/s})=-6.0\text{ kg\cdot m/s}$. Answer: $-6.0\text{ kg\cdot m/s}$ (west).

Question 7

7. A 45 N force acts for 0.16 s. Find the impulse delivered.

Work: $J=F\Delta t=(45\text{ N})(0.16\text{ s})=7.2\text{ N\cdot s}$. Answer: $7.2\text{ N\cdot s}$.

Station 4 Answers

Question 8

8. Use the figure to compare the two pushes. If the force is the same in both cases but one lasts twice as long, explain how the impulses compare.

Work / rationale: $J=F\Delta t$. With the same force, doubling the interaction time doubles the impulse. The longer push delivers twice the impulse of the shorter push.

Question 9

9. A cart’s momentum changes from −6 kg·m/s to +2 kg·m/s. Calculate the impulse, including sign.

Work: $J=\Delta p=p_f-p_i=(+2)-(-6)=+8\text{ kg\cdot m/s}$. Answer: $+8\text{ N\cdot s}$.

Question 10

10. A stopping interaction requires 20 N·s of impulse. Find the average force if the stop lasts 0.10 s, then predict the force if the time is doubled.

Work: $F_{avg}=J/\Delta t=20/0.10=200\text{ N}$. If time doubles to $0.20\text{ s}$, $F_{avg}=20/0.20=100\text{ N}$. Answer: $200\text{ N}$, then $100\text{ N}$.

Station 5 Answers

Question 11

11. A student claims, “If two objects have equal speed, the heavier one always has exactly twice the momentum.” Identify what information is missing and repair the statement.

Rationale: Equal speed alone is not enough to claim “exactly twice”; the mass ratio is missing. At equal speed, momentum magnitude is proportional to mass. A correct repair is: if one object has twice the mass of the other and both have the same speed, it has twice the momentum magnitude.

Question 12

12. A catcher moves the glove backward while catching a fast ball. Explain the effect on stopping time, average force, and change in momentum.

Rationale: Moving the glove backward increases the stopping time. The ball still undergoes essentially the same change in momentum from its incoming value to rest, but $F_{avg}=\Delta p/\Delta t$ is smaller when $\Delta t$ is larger.

Question 16

A. The force acts for the longest time.
B. The force acts for the shortest time.
C. The object has the greatest mass.

Answer: A — change in momentum
Work / rationale: The impulse-momentum theorem is $J=\Delta p$.
