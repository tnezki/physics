The Equilibrium Rule

A motionless hanging object can have two real forces acting on it: an upward tension force and a downward weight. If those forces are equal and opposite, their vector sum is zero and the object remains at rest.

When the net force on an object is zero, the object is in mechanical equilibrium. The equilibrium rule is written ΣF = 0, where Σ means “the vector sum of” and F represents the forces.

For a suspended or supported object, the rule can be applied by direction. Upward force totals must balance downward force totals, and leftward totals must balance rightward totals, if the net force is zero.

Stop & Discuss
  1. What forces are actually acting even though the object is at rest?
  2. What must be true about upward and downward totals?
  3. What does ΣF = 0 say—and not say—about whether forces exist?

Reading Force Balance in a Scaffold Model

The scaffold diagram makes equilibrium visible. Several downward weight forces act on the system while the supporting ropes exert upward tension forces. The individual forces do not vanish; instead, the upward total equals the downward total.

This kind of diagram can be used in reverse. If the total downward force is known and one support force is known, the equilibrium rule lets you infer the missing support force. The reasoning begins with the physical condition—zero net force—not with an isolated arithmetic trick.

Stop & Discuss
  1. Which arrows/totals must match for vertical equilibrium?
  2. If one upward support decreases, what must happen elsewhere to keep ΣF = 0?
  3. What evidence in the diagram shows equilibrium?

Physics NoteStatic and Dynamic Equilibrium

Zero net force can describe an object at rest or an object moving with constant velocity in a straight line. Equilibrium means no change in velocity, not “not moving.”

Stop & Discuss

  2. What must remain constant for moving equilibrium?
  3. Why would changing direction break the equilibrium claim even if speed is unchanged?

Vocabulary

mechanical equilibriumA condition in which the vector sum of the forces is zero.
static equilibriumMechanical equilibrium while the object is at rest.
dynamic equilibriumMechanical equilibrium while the object moves with constant velocity in a straight line.
balanced forcesForces whose vector sum is zero.
constant velocityMotion with unchanging speed and unchanging direction.

Summary

  • Mechanical equilibrium means the vector sum of forces is zero.
  • Objects at rest can have multiple real forces that balance.
  • Static equilibrium is rest with zero net force; dynamic equilibrium is constant straight-line velocity with zero net force.
  • The equilibrium rule can be used separately for up/down and left/right force totals.

Common Mistakes

Common mistakeBetter physics thinking
Equating equilibrium with rest onlyEquilibrium also includes constant straight-line motion.
Assuming constant speed always means equilibrium when direction changesConstant velocity requires both constant speed and constant direction.
Assuming forces disappear at equilibriumForces may be present; they simply sum to zero.
