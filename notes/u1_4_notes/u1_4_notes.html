The equilibrium rule

Mechanical equilibrium means the net force on the chosen system is zero. A hanging bag, a supported scaffold, or a book on a table can all have several individual forces acting while the vector sum remains zero.

The compact relationship is \(\Sigma \vec F=0\). For a vertical situation, total upward force must balance total downward force. The equation is a statement about the model, not merely an instruction to subtract numbers.

Formula / Relationship

Equilibrium rule: \(\Sigma \vec F=0\). In one axis, total positive-direction force equals total negative-direction force.

The rule applies to static and dynamic equilibrium.

Example 1

A cart rolls in a straight line at constant speed while all forces balance. What kind of equilibrium is this?

You Try It 1

A person stands still on a scale with balanced vertical forces. Classify the equilibrium.

Balanced does not mean force-free

An object at rest can have weight downward and one or more support or tension forces upward. Those interactions remain present. Rest tells us the velocity is not changing; with the equilibrium model, that means the forces balance.

When a load shifts, the individual support forces can change even though their total still balances the downward load.

You Try It 2

The scaffold is at rest. Use the upward support arrows and downward load arrows to explain how several forces can act while the scaffold remains in equilibrium.

  1. The opposing forces can balance so their vector sum is zero even though individual forces are present.
  2. An object at rest cannot have any forces acting.
  3. Inertia creates an upward force that removes all other forces.
  4. Only gravitational force matters when speed is zero.

Static and dynamic equilibrium

Static equilibrium is equilibrium at rest. Dynamic equilibrium is equilibrium while moving with constant velocity. In both cases, the net force is zero. The difference is the motion state, not whether forces exist.

Constant velocity means constant speed in a straight line. An object moving in a curve changes direction, so its velocity changes and it is not in translational equilibrium.

Stop & Discuss

How can two situations have the same net force of zero but different motion states?

Use balance to find an unknown force

Because equilibrium constrains the vector sum, a missing support force can be found from the others. If downward forces total 1000 N, then all upward supports together must total 1000 N. If one support provides 170 N, the other must provide the remaining 830 N.

The same reasoning works left/right: a constant-speed box can have a rightward push and leftward friction of equal magnitude.

Example 3

A suspended light is in vertical equilibrium. One cable pulls upward with 27 N and a second cable pulls upward with 45 N while the light’s weight is 72 N downward. Use \(\Sigma F_y\) to determine the net force.

You Try It 3

Motion evidence and force evidence must agree

A force model and a motion description are two representations of the same system. If the object moves with constant velocity, the force diagram must have zero resultant. If the force diagram has a nonzero resultant, the motion should be changing. Checking consistency between representations is a powerful way to catch errors.

Summary

  • Mechanical equilibrium means the vector sum of forces is zero.
  • Equilibrium can be static (at rest) or dynamic (constant velocity).
  • The equilibrium rule can be used to reason about unknown upward/downward and left/right force components.

Common Mistakes

  • Equating equilibrium with rest only.
  • Assuming constant speed means equilibrium even when the direction is changing.
  • Assuming individual forces disappear when the net force is zero.
