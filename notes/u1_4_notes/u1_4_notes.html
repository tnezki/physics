Mechanical Equilibrium Is a Zero-Net-Force State

When the vector sum of all forces on an object is zero, the object is in mechanical equilibrium. A hanging load can have tension upward and weight downward while remaining in equilibrium because the forces cancel.

If the object is at rest, we call the situation static equilibrium. If the object moves with constant velocity in a straight line while the net force is still zero, it is dynamic equilibrium. Both are Newton’s First Law situations.

Formula / RelationshipThe equilibrium rule

Mechanical equilibrium requires \(\Sigma \vec F=0\). Zero net force means velocity does not change; it does not require velocity to be zero.

Rest Does Not Mean Forces Disappear

A gymnast hanging from rings is motionless, yet gravity pulls downward and the supports pull upward. The scale readings can change as the gymnast shifts position, but the total force still balances when the gymnast remains at rest.

The same idea applies to books, signs, people on scales, and suspended lights. A force diagram should include the real interactions even when the object has zero speed.

Use the Equilibrium Rule by Direction

For the scaffold, the sum of upward support forces equals the total downward load. We can analyze vertical and horizontal directions separately because each direction must have zero net force in equilibrium.

The Practicing Physics scaffold examples show that one support can carry more while another carries less. What stays fixed is the requirement that the vector sum match the total load so the net force remains zero.

Formula / RelationshipVertical and horizontal balance

At equilibrium, \(\Sigma F_x=0\) and \(\Sigma F_y=0\). Missing support forces can be found by requiring the signed forces in that direction to sum to zero.

Dynamic Equilibrium: Motion Can Continue While Forces Balance

Mechanical equilibrium is not limited to motionless objects. Newton’s First Law gives the key connection: when net force is zero, velocity does not change. That includes an object already moving with constant velocity in a straight line.

Therefore, an object can move at constant speed in a straight line and still be in equilibrium. If a box moves right at constant speed while a push acts right and friction acts left, the horizontal forces balance. Motion is present; acceleration is not.

Summary

  • Mechanical equilibrium means the vector sum of forces is zero.
  • Static equilibrium is at rest; dynamic equilibrium is constant-velocity motion.
  • Objects in equilibrium can still have multiple nonzero forces acting.
  • Use ΣF=0 separately by direction to reason about missing or balancing forces.
