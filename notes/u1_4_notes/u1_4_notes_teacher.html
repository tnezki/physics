The equilibrium rule

An object is in mechanical equilibrium when the vector sum of the forces on it is zero: \(\Sigma F=0\). The forces do not have to disappear. A hanging object can have an upward tension and a downward weight that are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.

If the object is at rest with zero net force, it is in static equilibrium. The word equilibrium describes the force balance and the unchanged state of motion—not the absence of forces.

Example

A lamp hangs motionless from the ceiling. Is this static equilibrium, dynamic equilibrium, or neither? Explain briefly.
Answer / acceptable reasoning: Static equilibrium: it is at rest and the net force is zero.

You Try It

A train moves in a straight line at constant speed while its forces balance. Classify the equilibrium state.
Answer / acceptable reasoning: Dynamic equilibrium: it is moving with constant velocity while the net force is zero.

Example

A book is at rest on a table. Explain why “it is at rest, so no forces act” is incorrect.
Answer / acceptable reasoning: Weight acts downward and the table’s support force acts upward. The forces can be nonzero but balanced, so the book remains at rest.

You Try It

A hanging sign is motionless. Explain why it can still have forces acting on it.
Answer / acceptable reasoning: Its weight downward and tension/support upward are nonzero forces that balance, giving zero net force.

Several forces can still balance

Equilibrium can involve more than two forces. On a supported scaffold, several upward tensions can balance several downward weights. What matters is the vector sum: the total upward contribution and total downward contribution must combine to zero.

Example

A 75 N sign hangs at rest from one vertical cable. What upward force must the cable exert, and what does that tell you about the sign’s motion?
Answer / acceptable reasoning: 75 N upward. The net force is zero, so the sign can remain at rest.

You Try It

A 140 N light fixture hangs motionless. What upward support force is required for equilibrium?
Answer / acceptable reasoning: 140 N upward; the forces balance and velocity remains zero.

Dynamic equilibrium

Zero net force also allows motion. An object moving at constant speed in a straight line has constant velocity, so its motion is not changing. If the forces on it balance, the object is in dynamic equilibrium. Static and dynamic equilibrium have the same net-force condition; they differ in whether the unchanged velocity is zero or nonzero.

Example

A cart moves straight right at constant speed. Its diagram shows 10 N right and 10 N left. Is the motion consistent with equilibrium? Explain.
Answer / acceptable reasoning: Yes. The horizontal net force is 0 N, so constant straight-line motion is consistent with dynamic equilibrium.

You Try It

A conveyor box moves straight left at constant speed while equal horizontal forces act in opposite directions. Is it in equilibrium?
Answer / acceptable reasoning: Yes. Equal opposite forces give zero net force; constant straight-line motion is dynamic equilibrium.
Stop & Discuss

Compare the scaffold and constant-speed box. What is the same about their net force, and what is different about their motion?

Listen for:
  • Students identify zero net force in both cases.
  • Students distinguish rest from constant straight-line motion.
  • Students state that equilibrium means unchanged motion, not necessarily zero speed.

Summary

  • Mechanical equilibrium means the net force is zero.
  • Static equilibrium describes an object at rest with zero net force.
  • Dynamic equilibrium describes constant straight-line motion with zero net force.
  • Balanced forces can be nonzero individually; their vector sum is zero.
  • The equilibrium rule can be used to infer missing upward/downward or left/right forces.
