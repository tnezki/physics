Conceptual Physics • Unit 2

Motion and Reference Frames

Section 2.1

Motion only makes sense after you decide what you are measuring it relative to.

Learning Target

Students will explain that motion is relative and describe an object’s motion using an appropriate frame of reference.

  • I can explain why an object can be moving relative to one reference frame but not another.
  • I can identify the reference frame used in a motion description.
  • I can compare motion from two different points of view and explain why everyday motion is usually described relative to Earth’s surface.

A motion description is never complete until you know what the position is being compared with. The object or coordinate system used for that comparison is the reference frame. An object may be at rest in one frame and moving in another at the same time.

Imagine a backpack resting on the seat of a moving bus. Relative to the seat and passengers, the backpack does not change position. Relative to the road, the backpack travels with the bus. Neither description is wrong; they answer different questions because they use different reference frames.

Motion is relative. “Moving” and “at rest” always mean moving or at rest relative to a chosen reference frame.
Stop and Reflect

A passenger says a cup on the dashboard is at rest. A person standing on the sidewalk says the cup is moving. What reference frame is each person using?

Identifying the Reference Frame

Clues in a motion statement often reveal the frame. Words such as “beside the passenger,” “relative to the train,” or “past the window” tell you what positions are being compared. A strong motion statement names the frame explicitly: “The backpack is at rest relative to the bus,” or “The backpack moves east relative to the road.”

Worked Example 1

Situation: Kai walks toward the front of a train moving east at 9 m/s. Kai walks at 1 m/s relative to the train. Explain how Kai can be moving relative to the ground while also being described using a different motion relative to the train.

  1. Use one reference frame at a time.
  2. Relative to the train, Kai moves east at 1 m/s.
  3. Relative to the ground, the train motion and walking motion are both east, so Kai moves east at 10 m/s.
  4. Both descriptions are valid because the reference frames are different.
You Try It 1

Zoe walks toward the front of a train moving east at 9 m/s. Zoe walks at 3 m/s relative to the train. Explain how Zoe can be moving relative to the ground while also being described using a different motion relative to the train.

Why Earth Is the Everyday Default

For ordinary travel, Earth’s surface is a convenient shared frame because roads, buildings, addresses, and most everyday locations are fixed relative to it. Using the Earth frame does not make it more physically correct than every other frame; it simply gives people a common reference for describing motion.

Worked Example 2

Situation: A motion description says, “The backpack remains beside Sam on the seat while trees move backward past the window.” Identify the reference frame used when the backpack is described as remaining beside Sam, and cite the cue.

  1. The frame is the vehicle/passenger frame.
  2. The cue is that the backpack stays at a fixed position beside Sam on the seat.
You Try It 2

A motion description says, “The backpack remains beside Ari on the seat while trees move backward past the window.” Identify the reference frame used when the backpack is described as remaining beside Ari, and cite the cue.

Worked Example 3

Situation: A passenger on a bus says a phone on the seat is at rest. A person on the sidewalk says the phone is moving east with the bus. Compare the two descriptions and explain why using Earth’s surface is convenient for ordinary travel descriptions.

  1. Both descriptions can be correct because they use different frames.
  2. The phone is at rest in the bus frame and moving in the Earth/sidewalk frame.
  3. Earth’s surface is a convenient shared frame for everyday locations and travel.
You Try It 3

A rider says a bag is at rest on the train floor while a station observer says the bag is moving west. Explain how both statements can be correct and identify the reference frame for each statement.
