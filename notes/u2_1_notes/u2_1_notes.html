Motion and Reference Frames
The same object can have different motion descriptions without any observer being wrong.

Mathematical idea: A motion statement becomes meaningful only when the comparison frame is clear.

You will use everyday vehicles, Earth–Sun motion, and source diagrams to identify reference frames and compare descriptions from different viewpoints.

Reading 1: Motion Needs a Comparison

Motion describes a change in position, but a motion statement is incomplete until we say what the position is compared with. The textbook points out that even a person sitting in a chair is moving with Earth around the Sun. The same person can therefore be described as moving or not moving, depending on the comparison being made.

A reference frame is the object, surface, or viewpoint used as the comparison for describing position and motion. A chair can be a useful reference frame for a person sitting in it; the floor of a bus can be a useful reference frame for a passenger; the road can be a useful reference frame for the whole bus.

The important physics idea is not that one observer is right and another is wrong. Each description can be correct if the reference frame is stated clearly. The words “moving” and “not moving” tell us about a relationship between an object and a chosen frame.

Image read: Circle the object you would use as the reference frame for each statement: “the chair is not moving” and “the chair is moving through space.” Add a short note explaining why both statements can be reasonable.

The textbook uses this Earth–Sun comparison to make a broader point: appearances of rest do not mean an object has zero motion relative to every possible frame. A useful motion description always tells, or lets the reader infer, the frame being used.

Stop and Discuss

  1. Using the Earth–Sun image, explain how the same chair can have zero speed relative to one frame and a large speed relative to another.
  2. What information is missing from the sentence “The student is moving”? Name a reference frame that could make the sentence more precise.

Example 1: Passenger on a Moving Bus

A student stands still in the aisle of a bus while the bus moves along a road.

  1. Describe the student’s motion relative to the bus floor.
  2. Describe the student’s motion relative to the road.
  3. Explain why the two descriptions do not contradict each other.

You Try It 1: Backpack on a Train

A backpack rests on a seat while a train moves past a station.

  1. Is the backpack moving relative to the seat? Explain.
  2. Is the backpack moving relative to the station platform? Explain.
  3. Write one complete motion statement that names its reference frame.

Reading 2: Relative Motion and Everyday Frames

Relative motion means that motion is described relative to something else. The textbook example of walking down the aisle of a moving bus shows why this matters: your speed relative to the bus floor can be very different from your speed relative to the road.

An object is at rest relative to a chosen frame when its position does not change in that frame. Rest is therefore also relative. A passenger sitting in a moving vehicle may be at rest relative to the seat but moving relative to a person standing beside the road.

For everyday motion, people usually use Earth’s surface as the unstated reference frame. When a racing car is reported to travel at 300 km/h, the intended comparison is normally the track. When a runner is said to be moving at 5 m/s, the intended comparison is usually the ground.

The choice of reference frame becomes especially important when two motions occur at once. A person can move on a train while the train moves on the tracks. The source review figure below shows several combinations of a runner’s motion and a flatcar’s motion, so the ground and the car can give different descriptions.

When two people seem to disagree about whether something is moving, first ask what each person is using as a reference frame. Making that frame explicit often resolves the disagreement.

  1. Why is Earth’s surface a convenient reference frame for most classroom and roadway motion descriptions?
  2. Choose one flatcar situation in the image. Describe how the runner’s motion would be reported from the flatcar and from the ground.

Example 2: Two Observers, One Drone

A drone hovers above the center of a moving boat. A passenger on the boat sees the drone stay over the same spot on the deck while a person on shore watches the boat and drone move downstream together.

  1. Identify the reference frame used by the passenger.
  2. Identify the reference frame used by the shore observer.
  3. Explain how both observations can be correct.

You Try It 2: Walking Through a Moving Airport

A traveler walks forward on a moving walkway while another traveler stands on the floor beside it.

  1. Name two useful reference frames for describing the walking traveler.
  2. Explain how the traveler can have one motion relative to the walkway and a different motion relative to the floor.
  3. Write a sentence that makes the reference frame explicit.

Vocabulary: Define the Terms

You have now seen these words in the reading, images, and practice. Use this table to consolidate the physics meaning of each term.

Vocabulary termDefinition
motionA change in position relative to a chosen reference frame.
reference frameThe object, surface, or viewpoint used as the comparison for describing position and motion.
relative motionMotion described with respect to another object or reference frame.
restA condition in which position does not change relative to the chosen reference frame.

Summary

  • Motion and rest are always described relative to a reference frame.
  • The same object can be moving relative to one frame and at rest relative to another.
  • Everyday motion is usually described relative to Earth’s surface unless another frame is stated.
  • Apparent disagreements about motion can often be resolved by naming each observer’s reference frame.
