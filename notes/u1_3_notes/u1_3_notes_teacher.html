Close Reading

Net force is the combined force

A force is a push or pull. When more than one force acts on the same object, the important quantity is the net force: the vector sum of those forces. Same-direction forces reinforce one another. Equal opposite forces cancel. Unequal opposite forces produce a resultant in the direction of the larger force.

This is directional reasoning, not just arithmetic. Saying “5 N” is incomplete if the direction matters.

Formula / Relationship

Relationships: Same-direction forces add; opposite-direction forces subtract. For perpendicular components, \(R=\sqrt{X^2+Y^2}\).

Direction is part of the relationship, not an optional label.

Teacher guidance: Ask students to connect the paragraph, the representation, and the current I-can rather than treating the figure as decoration.

Force arrows carry information

Force is a vector quantity because a complete description requires both magnitude and direction. In a scaled vector diagram, the arrow points in the force direction and its length represents the force magnitude. A longer arrow represents a stronger force when the same scale is used.

The resultant is the one vector that represents the combined effect of the component vectors.

Teacher guidance: Ask students to connect the paragraph, the representation, and the current I-can rather than treating the figure as decoration.

Example 2

The diagram shows the idea of a resultant. For a cart with 9 N right and 4 N left, determine the one-dimensional resultant force and its direction.

Answer: 5 N to the right

Solution / look-for: Opposite-directed force magnitudes subtract, and the resultant points toward the larger force: 9−4=5 N right.

You Try It 2

The diagram illustrates that component vectors combine to make a resultant. In a separate one-dimensional case, a 12 N force acts right and a 5 N force acts left. What is the resultant?

  1. 17 N right
  2. 7 N right
  3. 7 N left
  4. 0 N

Answer: 7 N right

Solution / look-for: Opposite directions subtract: 12 N − 5 N = 7 N toward the larger rightward force.

Perpendicular vectors combine geometrically

When two force vectors are perpendicular, they cannot be combined by simply adding or subtracting their magnitudes. The parallelogram rule places the vectors as adjacent sides; the diagonal is the resultant.

For perpendicular components, the magnitude relationship is \(R=\sqrt{X^2+Y^2}\). A 3-N horizontal component and 4-N vertical component therefore produce a 5-N resultant.

Teacher guidance: Ask students to connect the paragraph, the representation, and the current I-can rather than treating the figure as decoration.

Stop & Discuss

If two force arrows have the same length but point in different directions, what information is the diagram giving you—and what information is it not giving you?

Look-for: Equal length means equal magnitude on the chosen scale; direction still determines how the vectors combine. The diagram alone may not identify the physical source unless labeled.

The resultant predicts the combined effect

A force-vector model should make the combined effect visible. In one dimension, opposite forces subtract and the resultant points toward the larger magnitude. In two dimensions, the diagonal direction shows how the component effects combine.

You Try It 3

In the hanging-person vector construction, explain how the two angled support tensions combine to balance the downward weight vector.

Answer: The horizontal components of the two tensions oppose each other, while their upward components combine to produce an upward resultant that balances the downward weight.

Solution / look-for: The horizontal components of the two tensions oppose each other, while their upward components combine to produce an upward resultant that balances the downward weight.

Support forces can also be added as vectors

Tension forces often act at angles. In the hanging-person example, the two rope tensions have horizontal and vertical effects. Their horizontal effects oppose, while their upward effects combine. For equilibrium, the combined support effect must balance the downward weight.

Reading arrow length and direction is therefore not cosmetic—it is evidence about which force is larger and how the forces combine.

Vocabulary Definitions

TermPhysics meaning
vectorA quantity represented by both magnitude and direction.
magnitudeThe size or strength of a quantity.
directionThe orientation in which a vector points.
resultantThe single vector equal to the combined effect of two or more vectors.
net forceThe resultant vector formed by adding all forces acting on an object.

Summary

  • Force is a vector: both magnitude and direction matter.
  • Same-direction forces add, opposite-direction forces subtract, and the resultant points in the combined direction.
  • Arrow length and direction are evidence in a vector model, not decoration.

Common Mistakes

  • Adding force magnitudes while ignoring direction.
  • Treating arrow length as decoration instead of evidence about magnitude.
  • Pointing the resultant opposite the direction of the larger unbalanced effect.
