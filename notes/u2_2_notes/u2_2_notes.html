Conceptual Physics • Unit 2

Speed and Velocity

Section 2.2

Speed tells how fast. Velocity tells how fast and which way.

Learning Target

Students will distinguish between speed and velocity and use distance, time, and direction to describe motion.

  • I can calculate average speed from distance and time.
  • I can distinguish instantaneous speed from average speed.
  • I can explain that velocity includes both speed and direction and compare examples of constant speed, constant velocity, and changing velocity.

Speed describes how fast an object moves. Average speed uses the total distance traveled over the total elapsed time. It summarizes an entire trip, including faster parts, slower parts, and stops.

A speedometer instead shows instantaneous speed—the speed at a particular moment. A trip can have an average speed of 10 m/s even though the speedometer reads 6 m/s at one time and 14 m/s at another.

Average speed summarizes a whole interval. Instantaneous speed describes one moment. Velocity adds direction to the speed description.
Stop and Reflect

A cyclist’s speedometer reads 12 m/s right now, but the cyclist’s average speed for the entire trip is 8 m/s. What does each number describe?

Average Speed

\[v_{avg}=\frac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total elapsed time}}\]

Average Speed

Average speed is calculated from total distance and total elapsed time. If a rider travels 200 m during a 25 s trip, the average speed is 8 m/s. If the rider stops during the trip, the stop time still counts in the total elapsed time.

Worked Example 1

Situation: A cart travels a total distance of 120 m in 10 s. Calculate its average speed.

  1. Use average speed = total distance ÷ total time.
  2. 120 m ÷ 10 s = 12 m/s.
  3. Answer: 12 m/s.
You Try It 1

Velocity Includes Direction

Velocity is a vector quantity: it includes both speed and direction. Two objects can have the same speed but different velocities if they move in different directions. An object moving around a curve at constant speed has changing velocity because its direction changes.

Worked Example 2

Situation: On an airport tram, a rider’s speedometer reads 12 m/s at one instant. The rider’s average speed over the entire trip is 10 m/s. Explain what each value describes.

  1. 12 m/s is the instantaneous speed at that moment.
  2. 10 m/s summarizes the whole trip: total distance divided by total elapsed time.
You Try It 2

On a moving walkway, a rider’s speedometer reads 13 m/s at one instant. The rider’s average speed over the entire trip is 10 m/s. Explain what each value describes.

Worked Example 3

Situation: A robot moves at a constant speed of 5 m/s. At first it moves east, then it turns and moves north while keeping the same speed. Compare its speed and velocity before and after the turn.

  1. The speed remains 5 m/s.
  2. The direction changes from east to north.
  3. Because velocity includes direction, the velocity changes.
