Acceleration

Section 2.3

Acceleration is about changing velocity, not simply going fast.

Designed for about 20–30 minutes of reading, reflection, and practice.

Learning Target

Students will explain acceleration as the rate at which velocity changes and identify acceleration from motion descriptions, data, and graphs.

  • I can explain that acceleration can involve speeding up, slowing down, or changing direction.

Velocity describes speed and direction. Acceleration describes how velocity changes. Because velocity can change in more than one way, acceleration is broader than “speeding up.” An object accelerates when it speeds up, slows down, or changes direction.

Acceleration is the rate at which velocity changes. A change in speed, a change in direction, or both can produce acceleration.

For straight-line motion, average acceleration compares the change in velocity with the time interval over which that change occurs.

Average Acceleration

\(a=\dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}=\dfrac{v_f-v_i}{\Delta t}\). The unit \(\text{m/s}^2\) means the velocity changes by a certain number of meters per second each second.

Stop and Reflect

A car’s velocity changes by 6 m/s every second for several seconds. Explain what the “per second each second” idea means in ordinary language.

Speeding Up and Slowing Down Are Both Acceleration

If an object moves east and its eastward speed increases, the velocity is changing eastward. If the object still moves east but slows down, its velocity is also changing. In the second case, the acceleration points opposite the velocity. This is why the sign of acceleration alone does not tell you whether an object is speeding up or slowing down; you must compare the directions of velocity and acceleration.

In one-dimensional motion we often choose one direction as positive. East or right might be positive, making west or left negative. With that choice, acceleration can be positive, negative, or zero. The sign tells direction relative to the chosen axis, not whether the acceleration is “good” or “bad.”

Worked Example 1

Situation: A cart’s velocity changes from 1 m/s to 7 m/s in 3 s. Calculate its average acceleration.

  1. Find the change in velocity: \(7-1=6\text{{ m/s}}\).
  2. Divide by the time interval: \(6\text{{ m/s}}/3\text{{ s}}=2\text{{ m/s}}^2\).
  3. The positive result means acceleration is in the chosen positive direction.

Motion Diagrams Reveal Changing Speed

A motion diagram marks an object’s position at equal time intervals. Equal spacing between dots represents constant speed. Increasing spacing means the object covers more distance in each equal time interval and is speeding up. Decreasing spacing means it covers less distance in each interval and is slowing down.

Changing Direction Is Also Acceleration

An object moving at constant speed can still accelerate if its direction changes. On a curved path, the velocity vector points in a different direction from one moment to the next. Since velocity changes, acceleration is present even if the speedometer reading stays constant.

This is a common misconception because everyday speech often uses “accelerate” to mean “go faster.” Physics uses a more precise meaning: any change in velocity.

Worked Example 2

Situation: A skateboarder moves east while slowing down. State the direction of velocity and acceleration.

  1. The velocity points east because the skateboarder is moving east.
  2. To reduce an eastward velocity, acceleration points west, opposite the velocity.
  3. The skateboarder is accelerating because velocity magnitude is changing even though speed decreases.
You Try It 2

Velocity–Time Graphs: Slope Tells Acceleration

On a velocity–time graph, the vertical axis is velocity and the horizontal axis is time. The slope tells how quickly velocity changes. A line sloping upward has positive acceleration. A horizontal line has zero acceleration because velocity is constant. A line sloping downward has negative acceleration.

Do not confuse the height of the graph with acceleration. A point high on the graph means the velocity value is high; acceleration comes from the slope. A steep slope means velocity changes quickly. A shallow slope means it changes more slowly.

Stop and Reflect

On a velocity–time graph, what would a horizontal line below zero mean? Describe both the motion and the acceleration.

Worked Example 3

Situation: A velocity–time graph rises steadily with a slope of 2 m/s². Identify the acceleration and justify from the graph.

  1. The slope of a velocity–time graph equals acceleration.
  2. The line rises as time increases, so the slope is positive.
  3. The stated slope is 2 m/s², so the acceleration is +2 m/s².
You Try It 3

A velocity–time graph is horizontal at 6 m/s for 5 s. Identify the acceleration and explain your reasoning from the slope.
