Acceleration

Section 2.3

Acceleration is about changing velocity—not just going faster.

Learning Target

Students will explain acceleration as the rate at which velocity changes and identify acceleration from motion descriptions, data, and graphs.

  • I can calculate acceleration from change in velocity and time.
  • I can explain that acceleration can involve speeding up, slowing down, or changing direction.
  • I can identify positive, negative, or zero acceleration from a motion situation and interpret simple motion graphs and connect them to velocity or acceleration.

Acceleration describes how quickly velocity changes. Because velocity includes both speed and direction, acceleration can happen in several ways: an object can speed up, slow down, or change direction.

Average acceleration compares the change in velocity with the time interval over which that change occurs. The sign of acceleration depends on the chosen positive direction, so “positive acceleration” does not automatically mean speeding up.

An object accelerates whenever its velocity changes—through a change in speed, a change in direction, or both.

Calculating Average Acceleration

Average acceleration is the change in velocity divided by the elapsed time. You must compare the final and initial velocities before dividing by time. Units of m/s² mean that velocity changes by some number of meters per second during each second.

Worked Example 1

Situation: A cart’s velocity changes from 1 m/s to 7 m/s in 3 s. Calculate its average acceleration.

  1. Find the change in velocity: 7 − 1 = 6 m/s.
  2. Divide by the time interval: 6 m/s ÷ 3 s = 2 m/s².
  3. Answer: 2 m/s².

Reading a Velocity–Time Graph

The slope of a velocity–time graph represents acceleration. An upward-sloping line has positive acceleration, a downward-sloping line has negative acceleration, and a horizontal line has zero acceleration. The graph describes velocity over time; it is not a picture of the object’s path.

Worked Example 2

Situation: A skateboarder moves east while slowing down. State the direction of the skateboarder’s velocity and acceleration, and explain why the skateboarder is accelerating even though speed decreases.

  1. Velocity points east because that is the direction of motion.
  2. Acceleration points west, opposite the velocity, because the skateboarder is slowing down.
  3. A decrease in velocity magnitude is still a change in velocity, so it is acceleration.
