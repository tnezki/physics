Preview the situation before solving.

Mark the givens, the representation, and the relationship each part will need.

A cart moves to the right at constant speed while two horizontal forces act on it. Use the diagram to unpack the situation. (a) Identify what the equal arrow lengths tell you. (b) State the net horizontal force. (c) Predict the cart’s motion one second later if the forces stay the same. (d) Explain which part of Newton’s First Law supports your prediction.
Answer / acceptable reasoning: (a) The horizontal forces have equal magnitude and opposite direction. (b) The net horizontal force is 0 N. (c) The cart continues to the right at the same speed. (d) With zero net force, an object already moving maintains constant velocity.

Newton’s First Law

Newton’s First Law connects motion to net force. If the net force is zero, an object keeps doing what it is already doing: an object at rest remains at rest, and a moving object continues at constant speed in a straight line. A change in velocity requires a nonzero net force.

Example

A hockey puck glides straight across nearly frictionless ice at constant speed. What does Newton’s First Law predict if the net force remains zero?
Answer / acceptable reasoning: It continues moving in a straight line at constant speed because zero net force means its velocity does not change.

You Try It

A spacecraft coasts far from planets with its engines off. What does Newton’s First Law predict if the net external force is essentially zero?
Answer / acceptable reasoning: It continues with constant velocity—same speed and direction—until a net external force acts.

Inertia is a property, not a force

Inertia is the tendency of matter to resist a change in motion. When a tablecloth is pulled quickly, the cloth changes motion sharply while the dishes tend to keep their previous state of rest. Inertia does not push the dishes forward or hold them back; it describes their resistance to changing velocity.

Example

A student says, “The bowling ball has more inertia force than the tennis ball.” Correct the statement using the physics meaning of inertia.
Answer / acceptable reasoning: Inertia is not a force. The bowling ball has more inertia because it has more matter/mass and is harder to change its motion.

You Try It

A student says, “The loaded cart pushes forward with inertia after the person lets go.” Correct the statement.
Answer / acceptable reasoning: Inertia is not a forward push. It is the cart’s tendency to keep its current motion unless a net external force changes it.
Stop & Discuss

In the tablecloth example, what changes rapidly and what tends to keep its prior state? Explain without calling inertia a force.

Listen for:
  • Students use “resists change in motion” or equivalent.
  • Students do not invent an inertia force.
  • Students connect a change in motion to net force rather than to motion itself.

Support force and weight

A book resting on a table is not force-free. Earth pulls downward on the book with its weight, while the table pushes upward with a support force (normal force). If the book remains at rest, these forces balance so their vector sum is zero.

The spring comparison makes the contact force easier to picture: compressed material can push back. The table’s atoms deform slightly under the book and provide the upward support force.

Example

A book rests on a table. In the diagram, arrow A points upward and arrow B points downward. Identify the two forces acting on the book.
Answer / acceptable reasoning: A is the table’s support force on the book; B is the book’s weight (Earth’s gravitational force) downward.

You Try It

A plant pot rests on a shelf. Arrow A points upward and arrow B points downward. Identify the two forces acting on the pot.
Answer / acceptable reasoning: A is the shelf’s support force on the pot; B is the pot’s weight downward.

Example

A box moves right at constant speed while the force diagram shows 12 N left and 12 N right. Does “constant speed” mean no forces act? Explain.
Answer / acceptable reasoning: No. Nonzero forces act, but they are balanced, so the net force is 0 N and the velocity remains constant.

You Try It

A sled moves left at constant speed while its diagram shows equal forces left and right. Distinguish “no forces acting” from “no net force acting” for this sled.
Answer / acceptable reasoning: Forces do act on the sled, but their vector sum is zero. “No net force” means balanced forces, not necessarily no individual forces.

A quick scale connection

A bathroom scale is another support-force situation. When a person stands still, the scale and floor transmit an upward support force while gravity acts downward. The forces can be large even though the net force is zero.

Summary

  • Newton’s First Law says rest or constant straight-line motion continues when net force is zero.
  • Inertia is a property of matter that describes resistance to changes in motion; it is not a force.
  • An object can have several forces acting on it while the net force is zero.
  • Support force and weight are common forces that can balance on supported objects.
