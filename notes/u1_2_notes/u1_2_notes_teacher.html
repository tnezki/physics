Close Reading

Newton’s First Law changes the question

Earlier thinkers often assumed that continued motion needed a continued force. Galileo and Newton reversed that idea. Newton’s First Law says that an object continues at rest or continues with constant speed in a straight line unless a nonzero net external force acts.

The key word is continues. Forces are needed to change velocity—not to “use up” motion. If the net force is zero, velocity does not change.

Formula / Relationship

Relationship: \(\Sigma \vec F=0\) means the vector sum of forces is zero.

Zero net force means velocity remains unchanged.

Teacher guidance: Ask students to connect the paragraph, the representation, and the current I-can rather than treating the figure as decoration.

Example 1

A spacecraft coasts far from large external forces. Its velocity remains unchanged unless a net external force acts. Name the principle.

Answer: Newton’s First Law

Solution / look-for: The situation describes unchanged velocity when net force is zero, which is Newton’s First Law.

Inertia is a property of matter

Inertia is the tendency of an object to resist a change in velocity. It is not an extra interaction and should not be drawn as an arrow on a force diagram. More mass means more inertia, but inertia itself is not a force pushing an object forward or holding it back.

Tablecloth, coin, hammer, and string demonstrations all make the same idea visible: when one part of a situation changes quickly, another object tends to keep doing what it was already doing.

Teacher guidance: Ask students to connect the paragraph, the representation, and the current I-can rather than treating the figure as decoration.

Stop & Discuss

Why is “inertia force” a misleading phrase? Use one source phenomenon to explain the difference between a property and an interaction.

Look-for: Inertia describes resistance to velocity change; it is not a separate interaction arrow. The source phenomenon should be used as evidence.

Zero net force does not mean zero forces

Several forces may act at the same time and still add to zero. A moving box can have a push to the right and friction to the left. A hanging or supported object can have upward forces and weight downward. When opposing effects balance, the net force is zero even though the individual interactions remain real.

This is why the statement “constant speed means no forces act” is wrong. The correct statement is that constant straight-line velocity requires zero net force.

Teacher guidance: Ask students to connect the paragraph, the representation, and the current I-can rather than treating the figure as decoration.

Support force comes from contact

A book at rest on a table has weight downward. Because it remains at rest, there must also be an upward force that balances that weight. The table provides this support force (often called the normal force). At the microscopic level, compressed atoms in the surface behave a little like springs pushing back.

For a simple supported object in vertical equilibrium, the upward and downward forces add to zero: \(\Sigma F_y=0\).

A scale reading is a force, but not usually the net force

When a person stands motionless on a bathroom scale, gravity pulls downward and the scale pushes upward. The scale reports the support force. If the person is in equilibrium, that support force has the same magnitude as the person’s weight, while the net force is zero.

Always keep the object and the quantity straight: an individual force can be large even when the vector sum of all forces is zero.

Vocabulary Definitions

TermPhysics meaning
forceA push or pull—an interaction that can affect an object’s motion.
inertiaThe tendency of matter to resist a change in velocity; a property, not a force.
Newton’s First LawAn object keeps its state of rest or constant straight-line velocity unless a nonzero net force acts.
net forceThe vector sum of all forces acting on the chosen object.
support forceThe contact force a surface exerts perpendicular to itself; also called the normal force.
weightThe gravitational force acting on an object.

Summary

  • Newton’s First Law connects zero net force with unchanged velocity.
  • Inertia is a property of matter, not a force.
  • Several forces can act and still balance to zero net force; support force and weight are common examples.

Common Mistakes

  • Calling inertia a force.
  • Assuming an object must have a net forward force simply because it is moving.
  • Confusing “no forces acting” with “the forces add to zero.”
