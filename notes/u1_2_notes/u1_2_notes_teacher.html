A rolling equipment cart moves to the right at a steady speed across a level hallway. Its force model is:

180 N support ↑
35 N ← [cart] → 35 N
180 N weight ↓

(a) Identify the object being modeled and list the forces acting on it.
(b) Determine the horizontal and vertical net force.
(c) Explain why ‘forces are acting’ and ‘net force is zero’ can both be true.

Newton’s First Law of Motion and Inertia

Galileo challenged the old idea that a moving object needs a continuing force simply to keep moving. Newton refined that insight into the First Law: every object continues in a state of rest or of uniform speed in a straight line unless acted on by a nonzero net force.

The key word is continues. If an object is at rest, it tends to remain at rest. If it is already moving, it tends to keep the same speed and direction. A change in velocity requires a nonzero net force.

Inertia is the name for this resistance to a change in motion. It is a property of matter, not a force. In the tablecloth demonstration, the dishes tend to keep their state of rest while the cloth is pulled quickly away. Nothing called an “inertia force” pushes the dishes backward or forward.

Stop & Discuss
  1. What would the dishes/coin do if the supporting object moves quickly?
  2. Which actual interaction forces act, and which “inertia force” should not be drawn?
  3. What does “continues” mean in Newton’s First Law?
Listen for: Students predict unchanged motion until a real interaction changes velocity and describe inertia as a property, not a force arrow.
Physics NoteInertia Is Not a Force

Inertia belongs to an object because it has matter/mass. A force is an interaction between objects. Do not draw an arrow labeled “inertia”; draw only actual interaction forces.

Example 1

A hockey puck glides across nearly frictionless ice with zero net force. Predict its motion and state Newton’s First Law in everyday language.
Answer: It keeps moving in a straight line at constant speed; motion changes only when a nonzero net force acts.
Zero net force means zero change in velocity. Newton’s First Law says an object keeps its state of rest or constant straight-line motion unless a net force changes it.

You Try It 1

A space probe coasts far from strong interactions. Its net force is zero. What happens to its velocity? Explain with Newton’s First Law.
Answer: Its velocity stays constant.
With no net force, there is no reason for the velocity to change; it continues at the same speed and direction.

Example 2

A student says, ‘When a bus stops, passengers lurch forward because an inertia force pushes them forward.’ Critique the statement and replace it with a correct explanation.
Answer: There is no inertia force; inertia is the property of matter that resists changes in velocity, so passengers tend to keep moving forward while the bus slows.
Inertia is not an interaction force. The passenger’s body tends to maintain its previous motion until actual forces from the seat, belt, or floor change it.

You Try It 2

A student says, ‘A book resists being pushed because inertia pulls backward on it.’ Correct the explanation.
Answer: Inertia does not pull backward; it is the book’s tendency to resist a change in motion because it has mass.
A backward arrow labeled inertia would be a fake force. Inertia describes resistance to changing velocity, not an interaction.

Support Force: A Real Interaction That Can Balance Weight

Consider a book resting on a table. Earth pulls downward on the book with the force we call weight. If that were the only force, the book’s motion would change downward. Instead, the table pushes upward on the book with a support force.

The table can push because its atoms compress slightly, much like tiny springs. A compressed spring pushes back; the table does the same. For a book at rest on a level table, the upward support force and downward weight can be equal in size and opposite in direction.

That means real forces are acting even though the net force is zero. “No forces act” and “the forces add to zero” are completely different physical claims.

Stop & Discuss
  1. Which force is Earth-on-book?
  2. Which force is table-on-book?
  3. How can both forces be present while the book remains at rest?
Listen for: Students identify weight and support as real forces and explain that equal/opposite totals yield zero net force.

Example 3

A 20 N book rests on a level table. Draw a force diagram for the book only. Label each force and explain why the diagram can contain forces even though the book is not accelerating.
Answer: Draw 20 N weight downward and 20 N support force upward; the forces are real but balance to zero net force.
The Earth pulls down on the book and the table pushes up. Equal opposite forces cancel, so the book has zero net force without having ‘no forces.’

You Try It 3

A hanging sign weighs 90 N and is motionless on a vertical cable. Draw a force diagram for the sign only and label the forces.
Answer: Draw 90 N weight downward and 90 N cable tension upward.
The sign is the object of interest. Earth’s weight force points down and the cable’s tension points up. Because it is motionless, these forces balance.

Support Force on a Bathroom Scale

A bathroom scale is another support-force situation. When you stand still on it, gravity pulls you downward while the scale pushes upward. The scale’s internal spring-like mechanism responds to the support interaction.

If you are in equilibrium, the upward and downward force totals balance. The scale can show a substantial force even while the net force on you is zero. A zero net force never means that every individual force has disappeared.

Stop & Discuss
  1. If ΣF = 0, must the object be at rest?
  2. Which motion descriptions are consistent with zero net force?
  3. Which statement confuses “no force” with “no net force”?
Listen for: Students allow both rest and constant straight-line velocity for zero net force and reject “no forces act” when interactions are present.

Example 4

Force model: 50 N ← [crate] → 50 N. Motion description: the crate moves right at a steady 1.5 m/s. Can the model and motion description represent the same situation? Explain.
Answer: Yes; the horizontal forces balance, so net force is zero and constant rightward velocity is possible.
Balanced forces do not imply zero velocity. They imply no change in velocity, which is consistent with steady straight-line motion.

You Try It 4

Force model: 12 N ← [cart] → 12 N. Motion description: the cart is speeding up to the right. Are the two descriptions consistent? Explain.
Answer: No; the balanced model gives zero net force, so it cannot account for a change in velocity.
A cart speeding up requires nonzero net force in the direction of the velocity change. The shown forces cancel.

Vocabulary

forceA push or pull caused by an interaction.
inertiaThe tendency of matter to resist a change in velocity; a property of matter, not a force.
Newton’s First LawAn object keeps its state of rest or constant straight-line motion unless a nonzero net force changes it.
net forceThe vector sum of all forces acting on the chosen object.
support forceThe contact force a surface exerts perpendicular to itself on an object it supports.
weightThe gravitational force acting on an object.

Summary

  • Newton’s First Law describes unchanged velocity when net force is zero.
  • Inertia is a property of matter, not a force.
  • Support force and weight are real interactions that can balance.
  • “No forces” and “zero net force” are different claims.

Common Mistakes

Common mistakeBetter physics thinking
Calling inertia a forceInertia is a property of matter; draw only forces produced by interactions.
Assuming motion requires a net forward forceConstant velocity is possible with zero net force.
Confusing no force with zero net forceSeveral real forces can cancel so their vector sum is zero.
