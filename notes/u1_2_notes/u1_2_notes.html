Newton’s First Law of Motion and Inertia

Galileo challenged the old idea that a moving object needs a continuing force simply to keep moving. Newton refined that insight into the First Law: every object continues in a state of rest or of uniform speed in a straight line unless acted on by a nonzero net force.

The key word is continues. If an object is at rest, it tends to remain at rest. If it is already moving, it tends to keep the same speed and direction. A change in velocity requires a nonzero net force.

Inertia is the name for this resistance to a change in motion. It is a property of matter, not a force. In the tablecloth demonstration, the dishes tend to keep their state of rest while the cloth is pulled quickly away. Nothing called an “inertia force” pushes the dishes backward or forward.

Stop & Discuss
  1. What would the dishes/coin do if the supporting object moves quickly?
  2. Which actual interaction forces act, and which “inertia force” should not be drawn?
  3. What does “continues” mean in Newton’s First Law?
Physics NoteInertia Is Not a Force

Inertia belongs to an object because it has matter/mass. A force is an interaction between objects. Do not draw an arrow labeled “inertia”; draw only actual interaction forces.

Support Force: A Real Interaction That Can Balance Weight

Consider a book resting on a table. Earth pulls downward on the book with the force we call weight. If that were the only force, the book’s motion would change downward. Instead, the table pushes upward on the book with a support force.

The table can push because its atoms compress slightly, much like tiny springs. A compressed spring pushes back; the table does the same. For a book at rest on a level table, the upward support force and downward weight can be equal in size and opposite in direction.

That means real forces are acting even though the net force is zero. “No forces act” and “the forces add to zero” are completely different physical claims.

Stop & Discuss
  1. Which force is Earth-on-book?
  2. Which force is table-on-book?
  3. How can both forces be present while the book remains at rest?

Support Force on a Bathroom Scale

A bathroom scale is another support-force situation. When you stand still on it, gravity pulls you downward while the scale pushes upward. The scale’s internal spring-like mechanism responds to the support interaction.

If you are in equilibrium, the upward and downward force totals balance. The scale can show a substantial force even while the net force on you is zero. A zero net force never means that every individual force has disappeared.

Stop & Discuss
  1. If ΣF = 0, must the object be at rest?
  2. Which motion descriptions are consistent with zero net force?
  3. Which statement confuses “no force” with “no net force”?

Vocabulary

forceA push or pull caused by an interaction.
inertiaThe tendency of matter to resist a change in velocity; a property of matter, not a force.
Newton’s First LawAn object keeps its state of rest or constant straight-line motion unless a nonzero net force changes it.
net forceThe vector sum of all forces acting on the chosen object.
support forceThe contact force a surface exerts perpendicular to itself on an object it supports.
weightThe gravitational force acting on an object.

Summary

  • Newton’s First Law describes unchanged velocity when net force is zero.
  • Inertia is a property of matter, not a force.
  • Support force and weight are real interactions that can balance.
  • “No forces” and “zero net force” are different claims.

Common Mistakes

Common mistakeBetter physics thinking
Calling inertia a forceInertia is a property of matter; draw only forces produced by interactions.
Assuming motion requires a net forward forceConstant velocity is possible with zero net force.
Confusing no force with zero net forceSeveral real forces can cancel so their vector sum is zero.
