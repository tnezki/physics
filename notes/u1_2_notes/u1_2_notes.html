Forces and Newton’s First Law

Section 1.2

Motion does not need a force to continue; a change in motion does.

Learning Target

Students will explain Newton’s First Law and use the idea of inertia to predict whether an object’s motion will stay the same or change.

  • I can state Newton’s First Law in everyday language.
  • I can explain that inertia is a property of matter, not a force.
  • I can distinguish between “no forces acting” and “no net force acting” and identify support force, weight, and other common forces in simple situations.

For a long time, people followed Aristotle’s idea that a moving object needed a continuing force to keep moving. Galileo challenged that view. He reasoned that when friction and other resisting forces become smaller, an object keeps moving for a longer time. Newton organized that idea into his first law.

Every object continues in its state of rest or continues moving at constant speed in a straight line unless a nonzero net force acts on it.

The key idea is continues. An object does not need a net force simply to remain at rest or to keep moving with constant velocity. A nonzero net force is needed to change motion. Everyday experience can hide this rule because friction is almost always present. A sliding book slows down not because motion naturally dies away, but because friction produces a net force opposite its motion.

The tendency of an object to resist a change in its motion is called inertia. Inertia is not a force. It is a property of matter. A moving object tends to keep moving the same way, and an object at rest tends to remain at rest, unless interactions with other objects create a nonzero net force.

Stop and Reflect

A hockey puck slides much farther on ice than it does on a wooden floor. Which surface gives the puck a smaller friction force? How does that observation support Newton’s First Law?

Worked Example 1

Situation: A space probe is moving at 14 km/s in a straight line. The net external force on it is essentially zero. Predict its motion.

  1. Newton’s First Law applies because the net force is zero.
  2. Zero net force means the probe’s velocity does not change.
  3. Answer: It continues at 14 km/s in the same straight-line direction.
You Try It 1

A curling stone is moving at constant velocity. Imagine all horizontal forces suddenly become zero. What happens to its speed and direction? Explain using Newton’s First Law.

Inertia in Action

Inertia explains many familiar demonstrations. If a card is snapped sideways from under a coin, the card changes motion quickly while the coin tends to remain where it was. Once the card is gone, gravity pulls the coin downward into the glass. A hammerhead can tighten on its handle for a similar reason: when the handle stops suddenly, the massive hammerhead tends to keep moving.

Mass is a measure of inertia: objects with more mass are harder to accelerate, slow down, or turn. That does not mean they have an “inertia force.” Forces come from interactions such as gravity, a surface, a rope, a push, or friction. Inertia describes how strongly an object resists a change in velocity.

Stop and Reflect

When a car stops suddenly, why does a passenger’s body tend to continue forward? In your explanation, use the word inertia without calling inertia a force.

Worked Example 2

Situation: A student says, “The coin falls into the glass because an inertia force holds it still while the card moves.” Correct the statement.

  1. Inertia is a property, not a force.
  2. The sideways force acts mainly on the card, so the card accelerates away.
  3. The coin tends to remain in its original state of rest; then gravity pulls it downward.
  4. Corrected idea: The coin’s inertia explains its resistance to the sudden sideways change.
You Try It 2

A passenger leans backward when a bus suddenly accelerates forward. Explain the observation using inertia. Identify the real interaction force that eventually accelerates the passenger with the bus.

Support Force

Now consider a book resting on a table. Gravity pulls the book downward. That gravitational force is the book’s weight. The table also pushes upward on the book. This upward contact force is the support force, often called the normal force because it acts perpendicular to the supporting surface.

Because the book remains at rest, its velocity is not changing. Newton’s First Law tells us that the net force must therefore be zero. The downward weight and the upward support force have equal magnitudes and opposite directions.

The table is not perfectly rigid at the microscopic level. The book compresses the material slightly, and the table pushes back. That push is a real force. The same idea explains a bathroom scale: the scale is squeezed and its internal mechanism measures the support force.

This leads to an important distinction. No forces acting means there are literally no forces on the object. No net force acting means the forces that are present add to zero. A book on a table, a person standing still on a scale, and a sign hanging motionless can all have several forces acting while still having zero net force.

Stop and Reflect

A book sits motionless on a desk. A student says, “Nothing is moving, so no forces are acting.” What evidence shows that statement is wrong? Name the two main forces on the book and describe their relationship.

Worked Example 3

Situation: A book has a weight of 80 N and is resting on a horizontal table. Find the support force and the net vertical force.

  1. The book is at rest, so its velocity is unchanged.
  2. Newton’s First Law requires a net force of zero.
  3. The table’s support force must balance the 80 N weight.
  4. Answer: support force = 80 N upward; net vertical force = 0 N.
You Try It 3

A student whose weight is 620 N stands motionless on a bathroom scale. What upward support force acts on the student? What is the net force on the student?

Newton’s First Law is therefore a rule for reading motion. If velocity is unchanged, the forces are balanced and the net force is zero. If velocity changes—speeding up, slowing down, or changing direction—the net force is not zero. The motion of an object gives evidence about the combined effect of the forces acting on it.
