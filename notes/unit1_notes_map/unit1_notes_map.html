Section 1.1

Thinking Like a Physicist

Learning target: Students will explain how physics uses observation, measurement, evidence, models, and testable claims to understand the natural world.

Exact I-can targets

  • U1-S1-IC1 I can distinguish between an observation, inference, hypothesis, law, and theory.
  • U1-S1-IC2 I can explain why a claim must be testable to be scientific.
  • U1-S1-IC3 I can use evidence from a short scenario to decide whether a physics explanation is supported and explain why formulas in physics are guides for thinking, not just tools for calculation.

Misconception inventory

  • Treating an inference as an observation
  • Assuming an explanation is scientific because it sounds plausible
  • Treating a formula as a rule with no physical meaning

Section 1.2

Forces and Newton’s First Law

Learning target: Students will explain Newton’s First Law and use the idea of inertia to predict whether an object’s motion will stay the same or change.

Exact I-can targets

  • U1-S2-IC1 I can state Newton’s First Law in everyday language.
  • U1-S2-IC2 I can explain that inertia is a property of matter, not a force.
  • U1-S2-IC3 I can distinguish between “no forces acting” and “no net force acting.” and identify support force, weight, and other common forces in simple situations.

Misconception inventory

  • Calling inertia a force
  • Assuming motion requires a net forward force
  • Confusing no force with zero net force

Section 1.3

Net Force and Vectors

Learning target: Students will represent forces as vectors and determine the direction and relative size of the net force in one-dimensional and simple two-dimensional situations.

Exact I-can targets

  • U1-S3-IC1 I can explain why force is a vector quantity.
  • U1-S3-IC2 I can combine forces acting in the same direction or opposite directions.
  • U1-S3-IC3 I can identify the resultant force from a simple vector diagram and use arrow length and direction to compare force strength and direction.

Misconception inventory

  • Adding vector magnitudes without direction
  • Treating arrow length as decorative
  • Reversing resultant direction

Section 1.4

Equilibrium and Balanced Motion

Learning target: Students will determine whether an object is in mechanical equilibrium and explain how balanced forces relate to rest or constant straight-line motion.

Exact I-can targets

  • U1-S4-IC1 I can recognize static equilibrium and dynamic equilibrium.
  • U1-S4-IC2 I can explain why an object at rest can still have forces acting on it.
  • U1-S4-IC3 I can explain why an object moving at constant speed in a straight line can be in equilibrium and use the equilibrium rule to reason about upward/downward and left/right forces.

Misconception inventory

  • Equating equilibrium with rest only
  • Assuming constant speed always means equilibrium when direction changes
  • Assuming forces disappear at equilibrium

Build story

Section 1.5

Modeling Forces in the Real World

Learning target: Students will create and critique force models for real-world situations involving inertia, support force, net force, and equilibrium.

Exact I-can targets

  • U1-S5-IC1 I can draw a force diagram for an object in a real-world situation.
