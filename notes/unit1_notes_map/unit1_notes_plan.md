# Unit 1 Notes Plan

## Unit Goals
Unit 1 should build one coherent model: physicists begin with observations and evidence, describe interactions with force vectors, combine those vectors into net force, recognize equilibrium as the zero-net-force case, and finally encode the whole analysis in force diagrams. The essential question is: **How do forces affect the motion of objects, and how can we represent those forces?**

## Section-by-Section Build

### U1-S1 — 1.1 The Language of Physics
Build from observation vs. inference into testability, evidence, variables/controls, then law/theory/model. End by framing formulas as compact relationships that describe a phenomenon, not merely answer machines. Use the official LT and U1-S1-IC1 through IC3 verbatim from the framework.

### U1-S2 — 1.2 Newton’s First Law
Start with the distinction between motion and **change in motion**. Establish inertia as a property, then Newton’s First Law, zero net force, constant velocity, common forces, and the crucial difference between “no forces” and “no net force.”

### U1-S3 — 1.3 Describing and Combining Forces
Make force explicitly vector-valued. Teach arrow direction and length before arithmetic. Progress through same-direction and opposite-direction combinations to resultant/net force and simple diagram interpretation.

### U1-S4 — 1.4 Equilibrium and Mechanical Equilibrium
Treat equilibrium as the special zero-net-force case. Put static and dynamic equilibrium side by side so “equilibrium = stopped” never becomes the governing model. Introduce ΣF = 0 as a statement of balance, not a calculation trick.

### U1-S5 — 1.5 Drawing Force Diagrams
Integrate the unit through a repeatable workflow: choose the system → identify external interactions → draw and label force arrows → determine balance/net force → explain or predict motion. Include critique/revision of an incorrect force explanation.

## Item Lens Plan
- **S1:** observation vs. inference; testability; evidence supports a model; formulas express relationships.
- **S2:** constant velocity does not mean no forces; inertia is not a force; zero net force is the no-change condition.
- **S3:** vectors carry magnitude and direction; direction controls how forces combine.
- **S4:** equilibrium can be static or dynamic; rest does not imply absence of forces.
- **S5:** force arrows represent external interactions **on the chosen system**; force ownership and labels must be physically correct.

## Teacher-Only Notes
Keep misconception prompts, solution commentary, and WTC interpretation out of student-facing prose. Especially watch for these recurring ideas: theory = guess; inertia = force; moving objects require a forward force; net zero = no forces; equilibrium = rest only; object names used as force labels; and forces exerted **by** the system mistakenly drawn on its own diagram.

For worked Examples, narrate the reasoning that connects the physical situation to the representation. For You Try and WTC items, preserve answer visibility: do not add labels, arrows, captions, or annotations that reveal what students are meant to identify or infer.
