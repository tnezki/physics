One force-modeling toolkit

Newton’s First Law, force vectors, net force, and equilibrium work together. A force model should help answer one central question: do the forces on the chosen object combine to zero, or is there a nonzero resultant that changes the object’s motion?

A three-step modeling routine

1. Define the system. Choose the object you are analyzing. 2. Identify and represent interactions. Draw only forces acting on that system, with arrow direction and relative magnitude carrying meaning. 3. Connect net force to motion. Balanced forces mean unchanged velocity; an unbalanced net force means velocity changes in the net-force direction.

Example

A backpack rests on the floor. Define the backpack as the system, then name the forces that belong on its force diagram.

You Try It

A picture frame hangs motionless from a vertical cord. Define the frame as the system and name the forces acting on it.

A force diagram for a cart shows 9 N right and 9 N left. Are the horizontal forces balanced or unbalanced?

Real situations can hide forces

Motion by itself does not tell you which individual forces are present. A cart moving at constant velocity may have an applied force balanced by friction. A hanging sign can have several nonzero tensions and a weight while remaining motionless. Use evidence about motion together with interactions to decide whether a force model is plausible.

Example

A cart’s model shows a larger force to the right than to the left. What motion change does the model predict?

You Try It

A sled’s model shows a larger leftward force than rightward force. What motion change does the model predict?

Critique the model, not just the picture

A force model is a claim about a chosen system. Check that every arrow is a force acting on that system, then compare the vector sum with the motion evidence. If a constant-velocity object is shown with an unbalanced force diagram, the representation and evidence conflict. A strong critique identifies the conflict and states what must be revised.

Example

A diagram for a book on a table includes a downward arrow labeled “book pushes table.” Critique the model and state the correct downward force on the book.

You Try It

A cart force diagram includes an arrow for the force the cart exerts on the person pulling it. Critique the model.
Stop & Discuss

What feature of the flawed cart model conflicts with the stated constant-speed motion? What should be revised so the model and motion evidence agree?

Vocabulary

force modela representation showing the forces acting on a chosen object or system
systemthe specific object or group of objects chosen for analysis
interactiona mutual influence between objects that can produce forces
net forcethe vector sum of all forces acting on the chosen object
equilibriuma condition in which the net force is zero so velocity does not change
evidenceobservations, measurements, or representations used to evaluate a model or claim

Summary

  • A force model begins by defining the system and including only forces acting on that object.
  • Force arrows represent interactions with direction and relative magnitude; together they determine net force.
  • Balanced forces mean zero net force and unchanged motion; unbalanced forces mean motion changes.
  • A good force model must agree with the observed motion/evidence and can be revised when it does not.

Common Mistakes
