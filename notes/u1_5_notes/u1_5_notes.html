Close Reading

Start by choosing the system

A useful force model begins with a clear choice: What object are you analyzing? Draw only forces acting on that object. Weight acts on the object because Earth interacts with it. A table may exert a support force. A cord may exert tension. A push, pull, or friction force belongs on the diagram only when that interaction acts on the chosen object.

Do not add a force merely because the object is moving. Motion is not an interaction.

Formula / Relationship

Model check: \(\vec F_{net}=\Sigma \vec F\). If \(\vec F_{net}=0\), velocity is unchanged; if it is nonzero, velocity changes.

Use the relationship to test whether force and motion representations agree.

Example 1

A book rests on a horizontal table. Draw a force diagram for the book only.

You Try It 1

A lamp hangs motionless from a single vertical cord. Draw a force diagram for the lamp only.

Build the diagram before judging the motion

Represent each interaction with an arrow that starts from the object, points in the force direction, and is labeled. Relative arrow length should match relative force strength when that comparison is known. Then combine the arrows conceptually to decide whether the net force is zero or nonzero.

Balanced forces mean zero net force. Unbalanced forces mean a nonzero resultant.

Example 2

The motion description says the crate moves at constant speed, but the supplied force diagram is unbalanced. Are the representations consistent? Identify the mismatch and state one correction.

You Try It 2

The supplied diagram shows a box moving at constant speed with opposing horizontal forces. Decide whether the motion description and force model are consistent and explain why.

Use net force to predict change

Newton’s First Law gives the consistency check. Zero net force means velocity remains unchanged: an object at rest stays at rest, and an object already moving continues with constant velocity. A nonzero net force means velocity changes in the direction of the net force.

This lets a model make a prediction instead of merely labeling a picture.

Example 3

A crate is initially at rest and experiences a nonzero net force to the right. Predict its motion. Justify using net force.

You Try It 3

A puck moves left while the net force is zero. Predict its motion over the next interval. Justify using net force.

Critique models by comparing representations

Real physics work often involves finding what is wrong with a model. Compare the force diagram with the motion description. A constant-speed straight-line cart should not have an unbalanced horizontal force diagram. A speeding-up box should not have equal opposing horizontal arrows.

When you revise a flawed model, change the smallest feature needed and explain why the revision makes the force evidence and motion evidence agree.

Stop & Discuss

What is the fastest consistency check you can make between a force diagram and a motion description?

A complete explanation connects model, evidence, and vocabulary

A strong force explanation names the system, identifies the interactions, describes whether the forces are balanced, states the net-force condition, and connects that condition to the motion. Vocabulary such as inertia, support force, resultant, and equilibrium is useful only when it clarifies the physical reasoning.

Example 4

A sled is pulled to the right across a rough floor at constant speed. Draw a force diagram for the sled only.

You Try It 4

A box is pushed to the right and speeds up on a level floor. Draw a force diagram for the box.

Vocabulary Definitions

TermPhysics meaning
force modelA representation of the forces acting on a chosen object or system.
systemThe object or group of objects selected for analysis.
interactionA push or pull between objects that can be represented as a force.
net forceThe vector sum of the forces acting on the chosen system.
equilibriumA zero-net-force state in which velocity does not change.
evidenceObservations, measurements, or representations used to support or challenge a model.

Summary

  • A force model begins by choosing the system and drawing only forces acting on it.
  • Balanced forces produce zero net force; unbalanced forces produce a nonzero net force.
  • A valid force model and motion description must agree, and flawed models should be revised using evidence and precise physics vocabulary.

Common Mistakes

  • Drawing forces that act on some other object instead of on the chosen system.
  • Omitting weight or a support force from a supported object.
  • Matching a balanced force diagram to a motion description that says the object is speeding up, slowing down, or turning.
