Build a Force Model for One Chosen Object

A force diagram starts by choosing the system—the object whose motion you want to explain. Then identify interactions with other objects: Earth can exert weight, a surface can exert support and friction, a rope can exert tension, and a person can exert an applied push or pull.

Draw only forces acting on the chosen object. Do not add arrows for forces the object exerts on something else, and do not draw inertia as a force. Direction and relative magnitude should carry physical meaning.

Example 1

A book rests on a horizontal table. Draw a force diagram for the book only.

You Try It 1

A lamp hangs motionless from a single vertical cord. Draw a force diagram for the lamp only.

Check Whether the Model Is Balanced

After the forces are drawn, compare them by direction. Balanced forces have a zero resultant. Unbalanced forces have a nonzero resultant. The force model and motion description must tell the same story.

A crate described as moving with constant velocity cannot be paired with a diagram showing a larger horizontal force in one direction. Either the arrows must be revised to balance or the motion description must indicate changing velocity.

Formula / RelationshipModel consistency check

Compare the force diagram with the motion evidence. Constant velocity ↔ \(\Sigma \vec F=0\). A nonzero net force ↔ changing velocity in the net-force direction.

Net Force Predicts Change in Motion

A nonzero net force does not simply mean “the object moves.” It means the velocity changes. An object initially at rest can begin speeding up in the net-force direction; a moving object can speed up, slow down, or turn depending on the direction of the net force.

If the net force is zero, velocity remains unchanged. A puck already moving left continues left at constant velocity in the idealized zero-net-force model.

Example 3

A crate is initially at rest and experiences a nonzero net force to the right. Predict its motion. Justify using net force.

You Try It 3

A puck moves left while the net force is zero. Predict its motion over the next interval. Justify using net force.

Critique and Revise Models with Evidence

A useful critique names a specific mismatch: a missing interaction, an extra non-force arrow, unequal arrows paired with constant velocity, or a diagram that includes forces acting on the wrong object. Then revise the model so it agrees with the evidence and Newton’s First Law.

The final explanation should connect the arrows to net force and connect net force to the observed or predicted motion. That connection turns a picture into a physics model.

Stop and Discuss

Draw the Complete Interaction Story

A complete force diagram should make the interaction story visible without drawing the motion as a force. For a sled moving at constant speed on a rough floor, show weight down, support up, pull in the direction of the applied interaction, and friction opposite the sliding interaction. Balanced arrows match unchanged velocity.

If a box speeds up to the right on a level floor, the vertical forces can still balance while the rightward applied force is larger than the leftward friction. The horizontal imbalance is what connects the force model to the change in motion.

Example 4

A sled is pulled to the right across a rough floor at constant speed. Draw a force diagram for the sled only.

Vocabulary Reference

TermMeaning in this section
force modelA representation of the forces acting on a chosen system.
systemThe object or collection of objects selected for analysis.
interactionA physical relationship between objects that can produce a force.
net forceThe vector sum of the forces acting on the system.
equilibriumA zero-net-force condition with no change in velocity.
evidenceObservations or measurements used to evaluate whether a model fits the physical situation.

Summary

  • Choose one system and draw only the forces acting on that system.
  • Check whether the force model is balanced or unbalanced.
  • Zero net force means unchanged velocity; nonzero net force means changing velocity.
  • Critique models by naming a mismatch, revising it, and connecting the revision to motion evidence.
