The supplied diagram is intended to represent a cart moving to the right at constant speed, but the force model is flawed. Use the same diagram for all parts.

(a) Identify the feature of the force diagram that conflicts with constant-speed straight-line motion.

(b) Based on the unequal horizontal arrows, what direction of net force does the flawed diagram imply?

(c) Describe how you would revise the horizontal force arrows so the model matches constant-speed motion.

(d) Explain how your revised net force predicts the cart’s motion and why that prediction is consistent with Newton’s First Law.

Answer / Acceptable Reasoning

  • (a) The unequal horizontal force arrows conflict with the stated constant-speed straight-line motion.
  • (b) The flawed model implies a nonzero net force in the direction of the longer horizontal arrow.
  • (c) Revise the horizontal arrows so the opposing force magnitudes are equal.
  • (d) With zero net force, the cart’s velocity remains constant, which agrees with Newton’s First Law.

  • Have students name the smallest diagram change that restores consistency.

Student Moves

  • Identify the unbalanced horizontal arrows as the mismatch.
  • Revise to equal opposing horizontal forces and connect zero net force to constant velocity.

I can draw a force diagram for an object in a real-world situation.

Start by choosing the system and the interactions

A useful force model begins with a clear choice: what object or system are you analyzing? Draw only forces acting on that chosen system. Weight belongs on the object because Earth interacts with it. A table can exert a support force. A cord can exert tension. A push, pull, or friction force belongs only when that interaction acts on the chosen object.

Motion itself is not an interaction, and inertia is not a force. The familiar demonstrations below are reminders to separate a property of matter from an actual push or pull. When you build a force diagram, every arrow should have an interaction behind it.

Example 1: Build a Force Diagram

A book rests on a horizontal table. Draw a force diagram for the book only.

Answer / Acceptable Reasoning

  • For the book only: draw weight downward and the table’s support force upward.
  • Because the book is at rest on a level table, the two vertical force magnitudes balance.

Teacher Moves

  • Ask, “What object is the system, and what interaction creates each arrow?”
  • For critique tasks, ask for the smallest feature that would make the model physically informative.

Student Moves

  • Draw or describe only forces acting on the chosen system.
  • Label directions/interactions and make relative arrow lengths consistent with the situation.

Listen For / Likely Misconception

  • Listen for inertia or motion drawn as a force, or forces placed on the diagram that act on another object.

I can decide whether the forces are balanced or unbalanced.

Balanced or unbalanced comes from the vector sum

After the interactions are identified, represent each force with an arrow that points in the force direction and is labeled. Relative arrow length should match relative strength when that information is known. Then combine the vectors conceptually. Balanced forces have zero resultant; unbalanced forces have a nonzero resultant.

Vector models make this check visible. Same-direction components reinforce, opposite-direction components oppose, and angled forces combine according to their components. The question is not whether forces are present—it is what their vector sum is.

Formula / RelationshipForce-model consistency check

\(\vec F_{net}=\Sigma \vec F\)

Physical meaning: If \(\vec F_{net}=0\), velocity is unchanged. If \(\vec F_{net}\neq 0\), velocity changes in the direction of the net force.

Example 2: Check Representation Consistency

The motion description says the crate moves at constant speed, but the supplied force diagram is unbalanced. Are the representations consistent? Identify the mismatch and state one correction.

Answer / Acceptable Reasoning

  • The representations are inconsistent: constant velocity requires zero net force, but the supplied force diagram is unbalanced.
  • Correct the diagram by balancing the opposing forces, or change the motion description so it matches the nonzero net force.

Teacher Moves

  • Ask students to compare the motion sentence and force arrows side by side.
  • Have them name one minimal correction and explain why it works.

Student Moves

  • Identify the mismatch between net force and motion.
  • Revise either the force representation or the motion description so the two agree.

Listen For / Likely Misconception

  • Listen for students who judge consistency from the direction of motion rather than from whether velocity is changing.

I can explain how the net force affects the object’s motion and critique an incorrect force explanation and revise it using physics vocabulary.

Make the force model agree with the motion evidence

Newton’s First Law turns a force model into a prediction. Zero net force means velocity remains unchanged: an object at rest stays at rest, and an object already moving continues with constant velocity. A nonzero net force means the velocity changes. That gives you a fast consistency check between a motion description and a force diagram.

When a model is flawed, revise the smallest feature needed and explain why. A constant-speed straight-line cart should not have unequal opposing horizontal arrows. A supported object should not omit weight or support. A strong explanation names the system, identifies the interactions, states whether the forces balance, and connects the net-force condition to the observed motion.

Example 3: Predict from Net Force

A crate is initially at rest and experiences a nonzero net force to the right. Predict its motion. Justify using net force.

Answer / Acceptable Reasoning

  • The crate’s velocity begins changing to the right; from rest, it starts moving and speeding up to the right.
  • The nonzero net force points right, so the change in velocity is to the right.

Teacher Moves

  • Ask for the net-force condition first, then the velocity prediction.
  • Press students to distinguish current direction of motion from direction of velocity change.

Student Moves

  • State whether net force is zero or nonzero and its direction.
  • Use Newton’s First Law/net-force reasoning to predict how velocity changes or remains unchanged.

Listen For / Likely Misconception

  • Listen for the idea that zero net force makes a moving object stop.

Critique and revise with precise physics language

Evidence can come from the motion description, a force diagram, a measurement, or another representation. The model earns trust when these sources agree. If they do not, the mismatch tells you what to investigate or revise.

Terms such as inertia, support force, resultant, net force, and equilibrium are useful when they clarify the reasoning. They should help explain the physical interaction or motion—not simply decorate the answer.

Example 4: Draw a Real-World Force Model

A sled is pulled to the right across a rough floor at constant speed. Draw a force diagram for the sled only.

Answer / Acceptable Reasoning

  • For the sled: weight downward, support force upward, pull to the right, and friction to the left.
  • At constant velocity, the vertical forces balance and the horizontal pull and friction balance.

You Try It 4: Draw a Real-World Force Model

Answer / Acceptable Reasoning

  • For the box: weight downward and support force upward on the level floor; applied push to the right and friction to the left if the floor is rough.
  • Because the box speeds up to the right, the rightward force must exceed the leftward force, producing a net force to the right.

Vocabulary with Definitions / Reference

TermPhysics meaning
force modelA representation of the forces acting on a chosen object or system.
systemThe object or group of objects selected for analysis.
interactionA push or pull between objects that can be represented as a force.
net forceThe vector sum of the forces acting on the chosen system.
equilibriumA zero-net-force state in which velocity does not change.
evidenceObservations, measurements, or representations used to support or challenge a model.

Summary

  • A force model begins by choosing the system and drawing only forces acting on it.
  • Balanced forces produce zero net force; unbalanced forces produce a nonzero net force.
  • A valid force model and motion description must agree, and flawed models should be revised using evidence and precise physics vocabulary.

Common Mistakes

  • Drawing forces that act on some other object instead of on the chosen system.
  • Omitting weight or a support force from a supported object.
  • Matching a balanced force diagram to a motion description that says the object is speeding up, slowing down, or turning.
