1. Kai walks toward the front of a train moving east at 9 m/s. Kai walks at 2 m/s relative to the train. Explain how Kai can be moving relative to the ground while also being described using a different motion relative to the train.

  1. Kai is at rest relative to the ground because the train is the only valid frame.
  2. Reference frames change the object’s mass, which changes its motion.
  3. Relative to the train, Kai moves east at 2 m/s; relative to the ground, Kai moves east at 11 m/s. Motion depends on the chosen reference frame.
  4. Kai must have the same velocity in every reference frame.

2. A motion description says, “The backpack remains beside Zoe on the seat while trees move backward past the window.” Identify the reference frame used when the backpack is described as remaining beside Zoe, and cite the cue.

  1. no reference frame is needed
  2. the vehicle/passenger frame
  3. the trees outside
  4. the backpack itself only

3. A passenger on a bus says a phone on the seat is at rest. A person on the sidewalk says the phone is moving east with the bus. Compare the two descriptions and explain why using Earth’s surface is convenient for ordinary travel descriptions.

  1. Both descriptions can be correct because they use different reference frames. The phone is at rest in the bus frame and moving in the Earth/sidewalk frame. Earth’s surface is a convenient shared everyday frame.
  2. Both must measure zero velocity because the phone is not sliding.
  3. Only the sidewalk observer can be correct because Earth is the unique true frame.
  4. Only the passenger can be correct because the phone is touching the seat.

4. A cart travels a total distance of 70 m in 9 s. Calculate its average speed.

  1. 630 m/s
  2. 39.5 m/s
  3. 0.13 m/s
  4. 7.77778 m/s

5. On a train platform, a rider’s speedometer reads 14 m/s at one instant. The rider’s average speed over the entire trip is 8 m/s. Explain what each value describes.

  1. 14 m/s is instantaneous speed at that moment; 8 m/s is total distance divided by total trip time.
  2. Both values are instantaneous because both use m/s.
  3. Both values are average because every speed is averaged over time.
  4. The larger value is average and the smaller value is instantaneous by definition.

6. A robot moves at a constant speed of 4 m/s. At first it moves east, then it turns and moves north while keeping the same speed. Compare its speed and velocity before and after the turn.

  1. The speed remains 4 m/s, but the velocity changes because its direction changes from east to north.
  2. Both speed and velocity remain constant because the speedometer value is unchanged.
  3. Velocity has no direction, so only position changes.
  4. The speed changes but the velocity does not.

7. A cart’s velocity changes from 2 m/s to 18 m/s in 4 s. Calculate its average acceleration.

  1. 64 m/s²
  2. 4 m/s²
  3. 5 m/s²
  4. 16 m/s²

8. A skateboarder moves east while slowing down. State the direction of the skateboarder’s velocity and acceleration, and explain why the skateboarder is accelerating even though speed decreases.

  1. Velocity and acceleration are both east because the skateboarder is moving east.
  2. Acceleration is zero whenever speed decreases.
  3. Velocity is east; acceleration is west. Acceleration is any change in velocity, including a decrease in speed.
  4. Velocity is west and acceleration is east because slowing reverses velocity instantly.

9. Use the velocity–time graph to identify whether the acceleration is positive, negative, or zero and justify your answer from the graph slope.

  1. positive acceleration
  2. negative acceleration
  3. zero acceleration because velocity is shown on the vertical axis
  4. acceleration cannot be determined from a velocity–time graph

10. Two compact objects of different masses are released together in a vacuum chamber near Earth. Predict which reaches the floor first and explain using the meaning of free fall.

  1. Neither accelerates because a vacuum removes gravity.
  2. The lighter object arrives first because it has less inertia.
  3. The heavier object arrives first because gravity gives it a larger acceleration.
  4. They reach the floor together if released from the same height with the same initial velocity. With air resistance ignored, both have the same downward acceleration g.

11. Ignoring air resistance, an object is dropped from rest. Using g ≈ 10 m/s², determine the speed it has gained after 2 s.

  1. 10 m/s downward
  2. 20 m/s upward
  3. 20 m/s downward
  4. 12 m/s downward

12. For ideal free fall from rest, use d = ½gt² with g ≈ 10 m/s². Compare the total distance fallen after 1 s and 2 s. Then explain why a coffee filter falling at terminal speed would not follow this same pattern.

  1. The filter follows exactly the same t² pattern because all falling objects are always in free fall.
  2. After 1 s: 5 m; after 2 s: 20 m. The ideal distance grows with t². A filter with significant air resistance is not in ideal free fall and can reach nearly constant speed.
  3. Distance is proportional to 1/t².
  4. Distance increases by the same amount each second because acceleration is constant.
