How We Work in STEM

The habits that make engineering possible

The Foundation of Great Engineering

We need a room where people can think, build, test, ask questions, and improve safely.

Be Ready

Be on time, have what you need, and get started when class begins.

Be Respectful

Respect people, ideas, materials, tools, and the learning happening around you.

Be Safe

Follow directions and use materials and equipment the way they are intended.

Be Involved

Think, build, test, record, and contribute. Engineering is something you do.

Be Curious & Persistent

Ask questions. Try ideas. Expect some things not to work. Use what you learn to make the next attempt better.

The important part: it loops.

Testing is not the end. A test gives you information. Engineers use that information to revise the design and test again.

Engineering Mindset

How to think when the answer is not obvious

Test Ideas

Do not argue forever about what might work. Build something testable and collect evidence.

Failure Gives Information

A weak joint, a bend, or a collapse can tell you exactly what needs attention.

Record Your Thinking

A quick sketch, measurement, or note helps you make a smarter second version.

Improve One Thing at a Time

Make purposeful changes so you can tell what actually helped.

First tries do not need to be perfect.

The goal is to learn enough from the first version that the next version is better.

Mini Challenge: Slowest Marble

Design • Build • Test • Improve

Make the Marble Take the Longest Time.

Design and build a free-standing cardboard structure that makes a marble take as long as possible to travel from the designated start to the finish using only gravity.

Success

The marble must move continuously, stay on the designed path, remain untouched after release, and reach the finish.

The Test

Each version gets 3 official trials with the same teacher-provided marble. Time begins at release and ends when the marble crosses the finish. Your longest successful run is your score.

Engineering focus

Path design • motion • reliability • testing • evidence • iteration

Material Budget & Rules

Use the limits to drive your design decisions

Unlimited
cardboard
48 in
masking tape in the final structure
1
standard teacher-provided marble

Requirements

  • Entire structure must stay within a 12 in × 12 in base area
  • Maximum height: 36 inches
  • Structure must be free-standing
  • Only cardboard and masking tape may be part of the final structure
  • After release, the marble may move only because of gravity
  • No touching, blowing, shaking, tilting, or other interaction after release

Performance

  • The marble must remain continuously in motion
  • The marble must stay on the designed path and reach the finish
  • Complete 3 official trials for each version
  • Primary result: longest successful travel time
  • The same teacher-provided marble is used for every official test

Important: Cardboard is unlimited and may be reused. The 48-inch tape limit applies to the final structure, not cumulatively across all three days.

Three-Day Engineering Sprint

Design, test, learn, and improve

Day 1 — Design & Start Building

  1. Identify the problem and requirements.
  2. Sketch at least two genuinely different path ideas.
  3. Choose one and explain why it is a promising starting point.
  4. Build and test enough of Version 1 to learn whether the basic idea works.

Day 2 — Build, Test & Learn

  1. Finish Version 1.
  2. Complete 3 official trials.
  3. Record your best successful time and what you notice about the run.
  4. Plan one or two evidence-based improvements and begin Version 2.

Day 3 — Improve & Retest

  1. Finish the redesign using Day 2 evidence.
  2. Complete 3 final official trials.
  3. Compare Version 1 and Version 2 and calculate your improvement.
  4. Share your result and what you learned.

The Challenge

Design and build a free-standing cardboard structure that makes a marble take as long as possible to travel from the designated start to the finish using only gravity.

Material Budget

  • Unlimited cardboard
  • Maximum 48 inches of masking tape in the final structure
  • One standard teacher-provided marble for official tests
  • No other materials may be part of the final structure

Cardboard may be reused during redesign.

Requirements

  • Entire structure stays within a 12 in × 12 in base area.
  • Maximum height is 36 inches.
  • Structure must be free-standing.
  • After release, the marble moves only because of gravity.
  • No touching, blowing, shaking, tilting, or other interaction after release.
  • The marble must stay on the designed path, keep moving, and reach the finish.

Testing Procedure

  1. Verify that the completed structure meets the base, height, material, and tape limits.
  2. Place the teacher-provided marble at the designated starting point.
  3. Release the marble without giving it a push.
  4. Time the run from release until the marble crosses the designated finish.
  5. A trial counts only if the marble moves continuously, stays on the designed path, remains untouched, and reaches the finish.
  6. Complete 3 official trials. Record the longest successful time as the version score.
  7. Use what you learned to improve the design, then repeat the same test for Version 2.

Success Measure

Primary: Longest successful travel time.    Also track: Improvement from Version 1 to Version 2.
