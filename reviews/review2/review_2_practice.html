Unit 2 Review Practice Problems

2.1 Motion and Reference Frames

1. In one sentence, define a reference frame as it is used to describe motion.

2. A phone lies on the seat of a train moving through a station. Describe the phone’s motion relative to the seat and relative to the station platform.

3. A drone stays directly above the center of a ferry while the ferry crosses a river. Compare how an observer on the ferry and an observer on shore describe the drone’s motion. Name each reference frame.

4. One observer says a backpack on a moving train has not moved; another says it traveled hundreds of meters. Reconcile both claims using reference frames.

2.2 Speed and Velocity

5. Explain the difference between speed and velocity. Include the extra information that velocity requires.

6. A delivery robot travels $180\text{ m}$ in $15\text{ s}$. Calculate its average speed and interpret the result in words.

7. Trip A covers $90\text{ km}$ in $1.5\text{ h}$ without stopping. Trip B covers the same $90\text{ km}$ in $1.5\text{ h}$ but includes a 15-minute stop. Compare their average speeds and explain what must differ about at least some instantaneous speeds.

8. A cyclist rides $6\text{ m/s}$ east and later $6\text{ m/s}$ west. Compare the cyclist’s speeds and velocities in the two parts of the trip.

2.3 Acceleration

9. Define acceleration in terms of velocity and time.

10. A cart changes from $3\text{ m/s}$ to $15\text{ m/s}$ in $4\text{ s}$. Calculate its acceleration.

11. A velocity table lists $2, 5, 8,\text{ and }11\text{ m/s}$ at $0,1,2,\text{ and }3\text{ s}$. Determine the acceleration and explain the pattern that shows it is constant.

12. A car moves around a circular track at constant speed. Defend the claim that it is accelerating by connecting direction, velocity, and the definition of acceleration.

2.4 Free Fall Motion

13. Define free fall in terms of the significant influence acting on an object.

14. A ball is dropped from rest for 3.5 s. Use $g=10\text{ m/s}^2$ to determine its speed.

15. A stone is dropped from rest for 3 s. Use $d=\frac{1}{2}gt^2$ with $g=10\text{ m/s}^2$ to determine the total distance fallen.

16. A student says, “Heavier objects fall faster because they have more mass.” Evaluate the claim for ideal free fall and explain what air resistance can change in real observations.

2.5 Velocity Vectors and Motion Modeling

17. Two velocity arrows use the same scale and have equal length but point in different directions. Compare the speeds and velocities they represent.

18. A motion table shows distances of $0,2,6,12,\text{ and }20\text{ m}$ at one-second intervals. Describe what the changing distance increments indicate about the object’s speed.

19. A distance–time graph becomes progressively steeper. Describe the motion in words and state the dot-spacing pattern you would expect in a motion diagram made at equal time intervals.

20. A table increases by equal distances each second and its distance–time graph is a straight line, but the written story says the object is speeding up. Identify the inconsistent representation and revise it so all three agree.
