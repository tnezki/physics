At a Glance

Motion is always described relative to a frame. Speed describes how fast; velocity adds direction. Acceleration is change in velocity. Near Earth, ideal free fall has nearly constant downward acceleration. Motion models must agree across words, vectors, tables, graphs, and equations.

Vocabulary

Reference frame
The object or coordinate system used to judge position and motion.
Average speed
Total distance divided by total time.
Velocity
Speed with direction.
Acceleration
Change in velocity per time.
Free fall
Motion when gravity is the dominant influence.

Rules & Structures

Average speed: total distance ÷ total time.

Average acceleration: Δv ÷ Δt.

Ideal free fall near Earth: speed changes by about 10 m/s each second; from rest, d ≈ 5t².

Graphs: position–time slope is velocity; velocity–time slope is acceleration.

Choose the Frame First

Describe motion relative to a chosen reference frame and compare how the same motion is described from different viewpoints.

  • I can explain why an object can be moving relative to one reference frame but not another.
  • I can identify the reference frame used in a motion description.
  • I can compare motion from two different points of view and explain why everyday motion is usually described relative to Earth’s surface.

Separate Speed from Velocity

Distinguish speed from velocity and use distance, time, and direction quantitatively and conceptually.

  • I can calculate average speed from distance and time.
  • I can distinguish instantaneous speed from average speed.
  • I can explain that velocity includes both speed and direction and compare examples of constant speed, constant velocity, and changing velocity.

Track Changes in Velocity

Explain acceleration as a change in velocity and analyze free-fall motion when gravity is the dominant influence.

  • I can calculate acceleration from change in velocity and time.
  • I can explain that acceleration can involve speeding up, slowing down, or changing direction.
  • I can identify positive, negative, or zero acceleration from a motion situation and interpret simple motion graphs and connect them to velocity or acceleration.
  • I can explain why objects in free fall accelerate at the same rate when air resistance is ignored.
  • I can use g ≈ 10 m/s² to determine speed gained during free fall.
  • I can use free-fall patterns to compare distance fallen during different time intervals and distinguish free fall from falling with significant air resistance.

Make Representations Agree

Build and critique multi-representation motion models using vectors, diagrams, tables, graphs, and equations.

  • I can represent velocity with arrows that show both speed and direction.
  • I can compare velocity vectors to determine whether an object’s velocity is changing.
  • I can connect motion diagrams, data tables, and graphs to written descriptions of motion and critique an incorrect motion model and revise it using evidence.

DOK Perspective

Be ready not only to calculate or identify, but also to justify a frame, connect representations, critique an incorrect model, and use evidence to revise it.

Final Check

  • Can I name the reference frame before comparing motion?
  • Can I distinguish scalar speed from vector velocity?
  • Can I use slopes and equations as evidence rather than decoration?
  • Can I explain when the ideal free-fall model applies?
