At a Glance

Motion and Kinematics

Motion descriptions depend on a reference frame. Speed describes how fast position changes, velocity adds direction, and acceleration describes how velocity changes. Free fall is a special accelerated-motion model, and motion can be represented consistently with words, data, graphs, equations, diagrams, and vectors.

Rules & Structures

Relationships That Must Agree

Average speed

$v=\frac{d}{t}$

Use total distance and total elapsed time; report the units.

Average acceleration

$a=\frac{\Delta v}{t}$

Acceleration depends on how velocity changes, not just on whether speed is large.

Ideal free fall

$v=gt$,   $d=\frac12gt^2$

Near Earth use $g\approx10\,\text{m/s}^2$ when the course model says air resistance is ignored.

Model check: A turn can change velocity even when speed stays constant. In free fall, mass does not change the ideal gravitational acceleration.
Review Focus 1

Reference Frames

Mastery Goal: Describe motion relative to a chosen reference frame and compare how the same motion can be described from different viewpoints.

  • I can explain why an object can be moving relative to one reference frame but not another.
  • I can identify the reference frame used in a motion description.
  • I can compare motion from two different points of view and explain why everyday motion is usually described relative to Earth’s surface.

Study connection

Keep the reference frame named throughout the explanation. Two observers can report different velocities for the same object without either description being wrong.

Review Focus 2

Speed and Velocity

Mastery Goal: Distinguish speed from velocity and use distance, time, and direction to describe motion quantitatively and conceptually.

  • I can calculate average speed from distance and time.
  • I can distinguish instantaneous speed from average speed.
  • I can explain that velocity includes both speed and direction and compare examples of constant speed, constant velocity, and changing velocity.

Study connection

Separate “how fast” from “how fast and in what direction.” Average speed summarizes an interval; instantaneous speed refers to one moment.

Review Focus 3

Acceleration and Free Fall

Mastery Goal: Explain acceleration as change in velocity and analyze free-fall motion when gravity is the dominant force.

  • I can calculate acceleration from change in velocity and time.
  • I can explain that acceleration can involve speeding up, slowing down, or changing direction.
  • I can identify positive, negative, or zero acceleration from a motion situation and interpret simple motion graphs and connect them to velocity or acceleration.
  • I can explain why objects in free fall accelerate at the same rate when air resistance is ignored.
  • I can use g ≈ 10 m/s² to determine speed gained during free fall.
  • I can use free-fall patterns to compare distance fallen during different time intervals and distinguish free fall from falling with significant air resistance.

Study connection

Acceleration is a change in velocity. Use signs and directions consistently, and distinguish ideal free fall from situations where air resistance matters.

Review Focus 4

Motion Models

Mastery Goal: Build and critique multi-representation motion models using vectors, diagrams, tables, graphs, and equations.

  • I can explain why an object can be moving relative to one reference frame but not another.
  • I can identify the reference frame used in a motion description.
  • I can compare motion from two different points of view and explain why everyday motion is usually described relative to Earth’s surface.
  • I can calculate average speed from distance and time.
  • I can distinguish instantaneous speed from average speed.
  • I can explain that velocity includes both speed and direction and compare examples of constant speed, constant velocity, and changing velocity.
  • I can calculate acceleration from change in velocity and time.
  • I can explain that acceleration can involve speeding up, slowing down, or changing direction.
  • I can identify positive, negative, or zero acceleration from a motion situation and interpret simple motion graphs and connect them to velocity or acceleration.
  • I can explain why objects in free fall accelerate at the same rate when air resistance is ignored.
  • I can use g ≈ 10 m/s² to determine speed gained during free fall.
  • I can use free-fall patterns to compare distance fallen during different time intervals and distinguish free fall from falling with significant air resistance.
  • I can represent velocity with arrows that show both speed and direction.
  • I can compare velocity vectors to determine whether an object’s velocity is changing.
  • I can connect motion diagrams, data tables, and graphs to written descriptions of motion and critique an incorrect motion model and revise it using evidence.

Study connection

Translate carefully between representations. Arrow direction and length, table trends, graph slope, and written descriptions must describe the same motion.

DOK Perspective

How the Thinking Can Deepen

DOK describes the kind of thinking a task requires; it is not a proficiency rank. In this unit, the same concept can appear in different ways.

Recall / recognize

Name the idea

  • Identify a reference frame.
  • Recognize velocity as speed plus direction.
Apply / interpret

Use a relationship

  • Calculate average speed or acceleration.
  • Interpret a velocity vector or simple data table.
Reason / justify

Connect evidence

  • Explain why two reference-frame descriptions can both be valid.
  • Critique a motion model using graph, table, or vector evidence.
Extended evidence

Synthesize a model

  • Design, defend, or critique an unfamiliar multi-representation motion model over sustained work.
