Unit 3 Review Practice Problems

3.1 Force and Acceleration

1. A puck is already moving east at constant velocity on nearly frictionless ice. Explain why a continuing eastward net force is not required to keep it moving.

2. Two identical carts have the same mass. Cart B experiences twice the net force of Cart A. Compare their accelerations and explain the relationship.

3. A $6\text{ kg}$ cart has a net force of $24\text{ N}$. Use $a=\frac{F_{\text{net}}}{m}$ to determine its acceleration.

4. The same $18\text{ N}$ net force acts on a $2\text{ kg}$ cart and a $6\text{ kg}$ cart. Calculate both accelerations and explain why they differ.

3.2 Friction and Real-World Forces

5. Explain the difference between static friction and sliding friction. Include when each one acts.

6. A student pushes a crate with $70\text{ N}$ to the right while friction is $25\text{ N}$ to the left. Determine the net force and the direction of acceleration.

7. A box remains at rest while a student pushes horizontally with $30\text{ N}$. Explain how static friction can allow the box to remain at rest.

8. A $5\text{ kg}$ object is near Earth. Use $W=mg$ with $g\approx10\text{ m/s}^2$ to determine its weight, then explain how mass and weight are different quantities.

3.3 Free Fall and Nonfree Fall

9. A rock is released in a vacuum chamber. Explain why this motion is classified as free fall and identify the significant force acting.

10. A falling object has weight $80\text{ N}$ downward and air resistance $30\text{ N}$ upward. Determine the net force and explain why its acceleration is less than $g$.

11. A skydiver is moving downward at terminal speed and has a weight of $600\text{ N}$. Determine the air-resistance force and the acceleration, then describe the skydiver’s velocity.

12. Two parachutes carry equal weights, but one has a much larger frontal area. Predict which experiences more air resistance at the same speed and explain how that changes the net force.

3.4 Newton’s Third Law and Interactions

13. A student pushes on a wall. Name the two forces in the Newton’s Third Law pair using “object on object” language.

14. A car and a much heavier truck collide. Explain why the interaction forces are equal in magnitude but the accelerations can be very different.

15. Explain why an action-reaction pair does not cancel when calculating the net force on one of the two interacting objects.

16. A paddler pushes water backward and the boat moves forward. If the system is the boat plus paddler, decide whether the water’s force on the paddle is internal or external to the system and justify your choice.

3.5 Projectile Motion

17. A ball rolls horizontally off a table. Describe what happens to its horizontal velocity and vertical velocity after it leaves the table when air resistance is ignored.

18. A projectile has horizontal velocity $v_x=6\text{ m/s}$ and is airborne for $3\text{ s}$. Use $x=v_xt$ to determine its horizontal displacement.

19. A ball is dropped from rest for $2\text{ s}$. Use $y=\frac{1}{2}gt^2$ with $g\approx10\text{ m/s}^2$ to determine its vertical drop. Explain why a horizontal launch speed would not change that vertical calculation.

20. A student draws a projectile with horizontal velocity arrows getting shorter and says a forward force is needed to keep it moving. Critique both parts of the model for ideal projectile motion.
