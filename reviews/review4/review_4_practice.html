Unit 4 Review Practice Problems

4.1 Momentum and Impulse

1. A small fast ball and a large slow cart can have the same momentum. Explain how this is possible using the quantities in $p=mv$.

2. A $4\text{ kg}$ cart moves east at $3\text{ m/s}$. Determine its momentum and include the direction.

3. A $50\text{ N}$ force acts on a ball for $0.20\text{ s}$. Use $J=Ft$ to determine the impulse and state what quantity that impulse changes.

4. Two identical moving carts are brought to rest, so each has the same change in momentum. One stops in $0.05\text{ s}$ and the other in $0.25\text{ s}$. Explain which experiences the smaller average force and why.

4.2 Conservation of Momentum

5. A student chooses “cart A only” as the system during a collision with cart B. Explain why the force from cart B is external to that system and how choosing both carts would change the classification.

6. A $2\text{ kg}$ cart moves right at $4\text{ m/s}$ while a $1\text{ kg}$ cart moves left at $3\text{ m/s}$. Take right as positive and determine the total momentum of the two-cart system.

7. A $1\text{ kg}$ cart moving right at $6\text{ m/s}$ sticks to a $2\text{ kg}$ cart initially at rest. Using momentum conservation, determine the velocity of the combined carts after the collision.

8. A student says, “Momentum cannot be conserved in a crash because the vehicles slow down or stop.” Critique the statement by referring to system momentum before and after the interaction.

4.3 Work and Energy

9. Classify each as work or no work on the object and explain briefly: (a) holding a heavy box motionless, (b) lifting the box upward, and (c) pushing on a wall that does not move.

10. A student pushes a cart with a $20\text{ N}$ force through $5\text{ m}$ in the same direction as the force. Determine the work done on the cart.

11. A machine does $900\text{ J}$ of work in $15\text{ s}$. Determine its power.

12. A $2\text{ kg}$ object is lifted $6\text{ m}$ above a reference level. Using $g\approx10\text{ m/s}^2$, determine its gravitational potential energy. Then determine the kinetic energy of a $2\text{ kg}$ object moving at $6\text{ m/s}$.

4.4 Conservation of Energy

13. A net force does positive work on a cart and the cart speeds up. Explain the connection between net work and the cart’s change in kinetic energy.

14. An energy bar chart for a system shows $70\text{ J}$ of gravitational potential energy and $30\text{ J}$ of kinetic energy with negligible thermal energy. If total energy is conserved, what total energy should the system have at another point in the motion? Explain.

15. A sliding object slows because of friction. Describe what happens to its kinetic energy and where that energy goes.

16. A student says, “When a roller coaster stops, its energy is gone.” Revise the statement using conservation of energy and at least two possible forms or destinations for the energy.

4.5 Energy in Society

17. For a hydrogen fuel-cell system, distinguish the energy source, the hydrogen energy carrier, and one energy transformation that occurs when the fuel cell produces electricity.

18. A community is comparing solar and geothermal electricity. Explain why availability, efficiency, environmental impact, and reliability should all be considered rather than choosing based on only one criterion.

19. A device converts electrical energy into useful motion but also becomes warm. Explain why the thermal energy does not violate conservation of energy and how it relates to efficiency.

20. A company claims its generator runs forever after one initial push and continuously supplies extra electrical energy with no continuing input. Critique the claim and name the energy measurements needed to evaluate it.
