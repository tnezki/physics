Conceptual Physics — Unit 1 Review Practice

Practice the ideas that will appear on the Unit 1 summative.

Vocabulary to know: observation, inference, hypothesis, scientific law, scientific theory, Newton’s First Law, inertia, force, weight, support force, applied force, net force, equilibrium, static equilibrium, dynamic equilibrium, vector, component, resultant.

Scientific Reasoning: Observation, Inference, Hypothesis, Law & Theory

1. During a cart trial, a student records, “The cart traveled 2.4 m in 4.0 s, so the wheels must have very little friction.” Identify the observations and the inference.

2. A student says, “The metal ball is colder because it contains less thermal energy.” The only direct evidence is that a thermometer reads 12°C. Which part is observation and which part is inference? Explain the difference.

3. Match each description to hypothesis, scientific law, or scientific theory: (a) a testable proposed explanation or prediction, (b) a well-supported explanation for how or why a broad set of phenomena occurs, (c) a concise description of a consistent pattern in nature.

4. A student says, “A scientific theory becomes a law after enough evidence proves it.” Critique the statement and replace it with a more accurate explanation of how laws and theories differ.

5. A class proposes: “If the mass of a cart increases while the same push is used, the cart’s motion will change less.” Explain why this can function as a scientific hypothesis and name one measurement that could test it.

6. Write one observation and one reasonable inference for this situation: after a push, a cart travels 3.2 m before stopping.

Newton’s First Law, Inertia & Common Forces

7. Explain Newton’s First Law in everyday language. Your explanation should include both an object at rest and an object already moving.

8. A hockey puck slides across nearly frictionless ice after the stick is no longer touching it. Predict its motion and explain the prediction using Newton’s First Law.

9. A student draws a forward arrow labeled “inertia force” on a moving cart. Explain why the arrow should be removed and what inertia actually describes.

10. The same brief push is given to an empty cart and a heavily loaded cart. Which cart has more inertia? Explain without describing inertia as a force.

11. A book rests on a level table. Identify the forces acting on the book, name the object causing each force, and give each force’s direction.

12. A student pushes a cart to the right across a rough level floor. Identify the main forces acting on the cart. Do not include velocity, inertia, or forces the cart exerts on other objects.

Equilibrium, Net Force & Motion

13. Classify each situation as static equilibrium, dynamic equilibrium, or not equilibrium: (a) a backpack resting on the floor, (b) a train moving straight at constant speed, (c) a bicycle speeding up, (d) a car turning at constant speed.

14. A hanging sign is motionless while an upward tension of 180 N balances its 180 N weight. Explain why it is incorrect to say “no forces act on the sign.”

15. A cart moves east at constant speed in a straight line while several forces act on it. Explain why the cart can still be in equilibrium and identify whether that equilibrium is static or dynamic.

16. A crate moves right at constant speed. A 42 N applied force acts right. Use the equilibrium rule to determine the horizontal resistive force, including its direction.

17. A 140 N equipment case is motionless on a floor while a person also pulls upward on it with 35 N. Use vertical equilibrium to determine the upward support force from the floor.

18. Compare two objects with zero net force: Object A is at rest; Object B moves north at 5 m/s in a straight line. Explain what will happen to each object’s velocity if the net force remains zero.

Force Models, Critique & Resultant Vectors

19. A laptop rests on a horizontal desk. Draw a force diagram for the laptop only. Label each force and then state the net force.

20. A crate moves right at constant speed while a worker pushes it across a rough level floor. Draw a force diagram and explain what must be true about the left/right forces and the up/down forces.

21. A student says, “The crate is moving right, so the rightward force must be greater than the leftward force.” The crate actually moves right at constant speed. Critique the claim and revise it using net force, equilibrium, and constant velocity.

22. Two nonparallel forces act on an object: $\vec F_1=\langle 6,2\rangle\,\text{N}$ and $\vec F_2=\langle -2,5\rangle\,\text{N}$. Draw both vectors head-to-tail on the grid. Then determine the resultant x-component, y-component, magnitude, and general direction.

23. Two nonparallel forces act on a cart: $\vec F_1=\langle -4,3\rangle\,\text{N}$ and $\vec F_2=\langle 7,-1\rangle\,\text{N}$. Find the resultant components and magnitude. State whether the forces are balanced or unbalanced.

24. A puck experiences two nonparallel forces: $\vec F_1=\langle 3,4\rangle\,\text{N}$ and $\vec F_2=\langle -6,2\rangle\,\text{N}$. Determine the resultant vector and its magnitude. If the puck is initially moving east, explain what the nonzero resultant tells you about how its velocity will change.
