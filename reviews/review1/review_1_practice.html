Unit 1 Review Practice Problems

1.1 Thinking Like a Physicist

1. A student writes: “The pillar casts a 42 cm shadow at noon, so the Sun is not directly overhead.” Identify the observation/measurement and the inference.

2. Write one physics claim that is testable. Then name one observation or measurement that would count against the claim.

3. At the same time of day, two cities measure different shadow angles from vertical posts. Explain why repeated angle measurements are evidence that can be used to evaluate a model of Earth.

4. Two students use the same formula. Student A immediately plugs in numbers. Student B first identifies what each quantity represents, where the measurements came from, and what assumptions make the relationship useful. Evaluate both approaches and defend which one shows stronger scientific reasoning.

1.2 Forces and Newton’s First Law

5. State Newton’s First Law in everyday language without using the phrase “an object in motion stays in motion.”

6. A student says, “A loaded cart has more inertia force than an empty cart.” Correct the statement and explain what inertia actually is.

7. A puck glides straight at nearly constant speed after the stick is no longer touching it. Explain why this does not require a continuing forward force.

8. A book rests on a level table. Name the two main vertical forces acting on the book and identify the interaction that causes each force.

1.3 Net Force and Vectors

9. Why is force a vector quantity? Your answer must name the two pieces of information a force vector communicates.

10. A cart has $24\text{ N}$ right, $8\text{ N}$ right, and $13\text{ N}$ left acting horizontally. Determine the net force and its direction.

11. Two perpendicular forces act on an object: $30\text{ N}$ right and $40\text{ N}$ up. Determine the magnitude and general direction of the resultant force.

12. On the same scale, Force A is shown by a 3 cm arrow left and Force B by a 5 cm arrow right. Compare their strengths and state the direction of the net force.

1.4 Equilibrium and Balanced Motion

13. Classify each as static equilibrium, dynamic equilibrium, or not in equilibrium: (a) a sign hanging motionless, (b) a train moving straight at constant speed, (c) a skateboarder speeding up.

14. A $18\text{ N}$ book rests on a level table. What upward support force is required for equilibrium? Explain why forces can act even though the book remains at rest.

15. A cart moves straight at constant speed. One horizontal force is $32\text{ N}$ right. What must the total horizontal force to the left be? Explain using net force.

16. A $45\text{ N}$ sign hangs motionless from one vertical cable. Determine the cable tension and distinguish the tension force from the net force on the sign.

1.5 Modeling Forces in the Real World

17. Draw a force diagram for a person standing still on a level bathroom scale. Label the source/type of each main force acting on the person.

18. A box slides right at constant speed while friction is $12\text{ N}$ left. Determine the required horizontal applied force and explain why the forces are balanced.

19. A student draws a rightward arrow labeled “motion force” and a leftward friction arrow for a book sliding right at constant speed. Identify the conceptual error and describe a corrected force model.

20. A cart is described as moving straight at constant speed, but its force model shows $20\text{ N}$ right and $10\text{ N}$ left. Identify the conflict. Revise either the force model or the motion description so the evidence is consistent.
