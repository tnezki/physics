Conceptual Physics — Unit 1 Review Practice

Practice the skills and understandings from your Unit 1 Mastery Goals.

Mastery Goal 1: Evidence, Models & Testable Explanations

1.

What scientific-thinking term means “a tentative, testable explanation for observations”?

2.

A student says, “If we double the ramp height, the cart will reach the bottom sooner,” and proposes timing repeated trials. Evaluate the claim’s testability and explain what would need to be measured.

3.

A student uses F_net = F_right − F_left and substitutes two force magnitudes without first deciding which direction is positive. Critique the reasoning and state how the relationship should guide the thinking.

4.

A class models average speed with speed = distance/time. One student claims a cart that travels farther is always faster, even when the travel times differ. Critique the reasoning and state how the relationship should guide the thinking.

Mastery Goal 2: Newton's First Law & Equilibrium

5.

A spacecraft coasts far from large external forces. Its velocity remains unchanged unless a net external force acts. Name the principle.

6.

A book remains at rest on a desk because the forces on it balance. Which law connects zero net force with unchanged motion?

7.

A student labels an arrow “inertia force” on a moving cart. Explain why that arrow should be removed and what inertia actually describes.

8.

A 4 kg book is resting on a level table. Predict what happens to its motion while the upward support force remains equal to its downward weight. Justify using net force.

Mastery Goal 3: Net Force & Vector Balance

9.

In the supplied vector model, explain what changing an arrow’s length would mean physically and what rotating the arrow would mean.

10.

The diagram shows equal perpendicular vectors. Explain what the equal arrow lengths mean and what the different arrow directions communicate.

11.

The diagram shows the idea of a resultant. For a cart with 9 N right and 4 N left, determine the one-dimensional resultant force and its direction.

12.

Use the opposing-force vector diagram. Explain how arrow direction and relative length determine the resultant: when is it zero, and when does it point left or right?

Mastery Goal 4: Build and Critique Force Models

13.

A book rests on a horizontal table. Draw a force diagram for the book only.

14.

The motion description says the crate moves at constant speed, but the supplied force diagram is unbalanced. Are the representations consistent? Identify the mismatch and state one correction.

15.

A crate is initially at rest and experiences a nonzero net force to the right. Predict its motion. Justify using net force.

16.

A puck moves left while the net force is zero. Predict its motion over the next interval. Justify using net force.
