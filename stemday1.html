How We Work in STEM

The habits that make engineering possible

The Foundation of Great Engineering

We need a room where people can think, build, test, ask questions, and improve safely.

Be Ready

Be on time, have what you need, and get started when class begins.

Be Respectful

Respect people, ideas, materials, tools, and the learning happening around you.

Be Safe

Follow directions and use materials and equipment the way they are intended.

Be Involved

Think, build, test, record, and contribute. Engineering is something you do.

Be Curious & Persistent

Ask questions. Try ideas. Expect some things not to work. Use what you learn to make the next attempt better.

The important part: it loops.

Testing is not the end. A test gives you information. Engineers use that information to revise the design and test again.

Engineering Mindset

How to think when the answer is not obvious

Test Ideas

Do not argue forever about what might work. Build something testable and collect evidence.

Failure Gives Information

A weak joint, a bend, or a collapse can tell you exactly what needs attention.

Record Your Thinking

A quick sketch, measurement, or note helps you make a smarter second version.

Improve One Thing at a Time

Make purposeful changes so you can tell what actually helped.

First tries do not need to be perfect.

The goal is to learn enough from the first version that the next version is better.

Mini Challenge: Long Span

Design • Build • Test • Improve

Span the Greatest Distance.

Design and build a free-standing spanning structure that reaches across the greatest unsupported distance while supporting a teacher-provided hanging load.

Success

Your structure must remain free-standing and support the teacher-provided hanging load for 10 seconds across the greatest possible clear span.

The Test

The teacher provides the hanging load. The supports are moved farther apart to determine the greatest clear span your structure can hold successfully.

Engineering focus

Structure • joints • bending • bracing • load paths • efficient use of limited materials

Material Budget & Rules

Limited materials force design decisions

50
7-inch cocktail straws
30
toothpicks
36 in
masking tape

Requirements

  • Goal: create the greatest clear unsupported span
  • Span is measured between the inside edges of the two supports
  • Structure may rest on the test supports but cannot be taped or attached to them
  • Nothing may touch the floor, wall, chair, or another support
  • Teacher-provided load must hang near the center of the span

Performance

  • Primary result: greatest clear span held for 10 seconds
  • Tiebreaker: fewer materials used
  • The same teacher-provided hanging load is used for every test
  • You may use less than the full budget

Important: Tape is part of the structure. It may connect or reinforce materials, but it may not anchor the structure to the test supports.

Three-Day Engineering Sprint

Design, test, learn, and improve

Day 1 — Design & Start Building

  1. Identify the problem and requirements.
  2. Sketch at least two possible spanning-structure ideas.
  3. Choose one and explain why.
  4. Begin prototype construction.

Day 2 — Build & Test

  1. Finish the first prototype.
  2. Complete the first load test.
  3. Record maximum successful span and where failure began.
  4. Plan one purposeful improvement.

Day 3 — Improve & Retest

  1. Revise the structure using test evidence.
  2. Complete the final load test.
  3. Compare Version 1 and Version 2.
  4. Record what changed and what you learned.

The Challenge

Design and build a free-standing spanning structure that reaches across the greatest unsupported distance while supporting a teacher-provided hanging load.

Material Budget

  • Maximum 50 cocktail straws (about 7 in each)
  • Maximum 30 toothpicks
  • Maximum 36 inches masking tape
  • Teacher provides the hanging load; the same load is used for every test

You may use less than the maximum.

Requirements

  • Create the greatest clear unsupported span possible.
  • Span is measured between the inside edges of the two supports.
  • Structure must be free-standing.
  • Do not tape the structure to the supports.
  • Nothing may touch the floor, wall, chair, or another support.
  • The teacher-provided load must hang near the center of the span.

Testing Procedure

  1. Place the completed structure across the two test supports.
  2. Teacher attaches the provided hanging load near the center of the span.
  3. Begin at a reasonable clear span between supports and measure the clear distance.
  4. If the load is held for 10 seconds, move the supports farther apart and test again.
  5. Record the greatest successful clear span and what happened at failure.
  6. Improve the design and repeat the same test.

Success Measure

Primary: Greatest clear span that supports the hanging load for 10 seconds.    Tiebreaker: Fewer materials used.
