Form: V1
Directions: Answer questions 1–16 on the bubble sheet. Choose the best answer for each question. You may write on this test copy.
1. During a cart investigation, a student writes: “The cart traveled 1.8 m in 3.0 s, so the wheels must have very little friction.” Which part is an inference?
A. The cart traveled 1.8 m.
B. Both measured values are observations.
C. The time was 3.0 s.
D. The wheels must have very little friction.
2. Which statement best distinguishes a scientific law from a scientific theory?
A. A theory describes what happens but never explains why.
B. A law is a theory that has been proven and can never change.
C. A law describes a consistent pattern in nature; a theory explains why or how patterns occur using a large body of evidence.
D. A theory is a guess, while a law is always an equation.
3. A class proposes: “If we increase the mass of a cart while using the same push, its motion will change less.” What makes this statement a scientific hypothesis?
A. It is a testable prediction that can be supported or challenged by evidence.
B. It has already been confirmed in every possible situation.
C. It summarizes a pattern without making a testable prediction.
D. It explains all motion and therefore cannot be tested.
4. A hockey puck glides across nearly frictionless ice. After the player stops pushing it, the puck continues moving straight at nearly constant speed. Which explanation best matches Newton’s First Law?
A. With very little net force, the puck’s velocity changes very little.
B. Objects naturally slow down even when no net force acts.
C. A forward force called motion keeps the puck moving.
D. The puck keeps moving because its weight points forward.
5. A student says, “Inertia is the forward force that keeps the cart moving.” Which revision is most accurate?
A. Inertia points opposite the direction of motion.
B. Inertia is a force that acts only while an object is moving.
C. Inertia and net force are two names for the same thing.
D. Inertia is a property of matter that resists changes in velocity; it is not a force.
6. A student pushes a cart across the hallway while it stays in contact with the floor. Which set lists forces that can act on the cart?
A. weight, support force, applied push, and possible friction/resistance
B. support force only, because the object stays on the surface
C. weight, inertia, motion force, and speed
D. applied push and the force the object exerts back on the person
7. Which situation is an example of dynamic equilibrium?
A. A train moves in a straight line at constant speed while the forces on it balance.
B. A bicycle speeds up on a straight road.
C. A car turns a corner at constant speed.
D. A backpack rests motionless on the floor.
8. Which situation is an example of static equilibrium?
A. A hanging sign remains motionless while upward tension balances its weight.
B. A ball changes direction while moving in a circle.
C. A cart speeds up while being pushed.
D. A puck glides east at constant speed.
9. A cart is at rest on a level floor. Its weight is 80 N downward, and the floor pushes upward with 80 N. Why is the net force zero?
A. The upward force removes gravity.
B. No forces actually act because the object is at rest.
C. The nonzero forces are opposite and equal, so their vector sum is zero.
D. The two forces cancel only because the object has no inertia.
10. A hanging light is motionless. Its weight is 80 N downward. What upward tension is required for equilibrium?
A. 80 N downward
B. 160 N upward
C. 80 N upward
D. 0 N
11. A cart moves at constant speed in a straight line. A 27 N applied force acts right. What horizontal resistance is required for equilibrium?
A. 54 N left
B. 0 N
C. 27 N left
D. 27 N right
12. A cart is moving to the right when the net force becomes zero. What does Newton’s First Law predict?
A. It must reverse direction because balanced forces oppose motion.
B. It immediately stops because no forward force remains.
C. It continues moving right at constant velocity unless a nonzero net force acts.
D. It speeds up because inertia becomes larger.
13. A book is at rest and the net force on it is zero. Which statement is correct?
A. Zero net force means every individual force is zero.
B. A hidden inertia force must be holding it still.
C. The book must begin moving because balanced forces produce motion.
D. Its velocity remains zero unless a nonzero net force acts.
14. A cart moves right at constant speed. The horizontal forces are 27 N right and 27 N left; the vertical forces also balance. Which statement best connects the force model to the motion?
A. The net force is zero, so constant straight-line velocity is possible.
B. The motion requires an extra forward inertia force.
C. The object must stop because the forces are balanced.
D. The net force points in the direction of motion because the object is moving.
15. A person pulls upward on a handle attached to a cart while it remains on the floor. The floor’s support force is now smaller than the object’s weight. Which explanation is best?
A. Support force must always equal weight no matter what other vertical forces act.
B. The upward pull shares the job of balancing the downward weight, so less support force is needed.
C. The object’s weight becomes smaller whenever someone pulls upward.
D. Inertia becomes an upward force.
16. A student explains a cart moving at constant speed: “The applied force is larger than resistance because the object is moving forward, and inertia is another forward force.” Which revision is best?
A. Only inertia matters once an object is moving.
B. For constant straight-line speed, the net force is zero; applied force can balance resistance, and inertia is a property rather than a force.
C. The student is correct because all moving objects need a forward net force.
D. The resistance must be zero whenever speed is constant.
