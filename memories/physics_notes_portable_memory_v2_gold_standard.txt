PART 2 — GOLD-STANDARD INSTRUCTIONAL DESIGN
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

AUDIENCE AND PURPOSE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Audience: grades 8–9 Conceptual Physics.

The notes are intended for class use and later annotation in Notability on an
iPad. Wasted white space is acceptable. Readability, annotation room, and clear
visual flow matter more than minimizing page count.

Reading Apprenticeship goals:
  • preview before close reading
  • notice vocabulary before definitions
  • skim headings, pictures, captions, diagrams, formulas, and tables
  • make predictions and questions
  • annotate while reading
  • connect words to images and models
  • return to evidence during discussion
  • apply an idea soon after reading it
  • leave visible space for students to think and write

READING DENSITY — GOLD STANDARD
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

These notes should feel like a guided, student-readable adaptation of the
extracted textbook, NOT a short study guide followed by a worksheet.

Use substantially more source-grounded reading than the original prototype.
Typical gold-standard rhythm for a normal section:
  • 2–4 instructional reading cycles
  • 3 cycles is a strong default when the source supports it
  • each reading cycle usually contains several short paragraphs, often about
    4–6, plus one or more purposeful source images when available
  • paragraph count is NOT a quota; preserve the conceptual sequence and include
    enough explanation for the physics to make sense

Use clear 8th–9th grade prose while preserving correct physics vocabulary.
Paraphrase the textbook; do not copy long source passages verbatim.

The reading should explain:
  • what students are looking at
  • what physical quantities/interactions matter
  • how evidence supports a claim
  • how the figure, diagram, measurement, or formula connects to the words
  • what changes and what stays the same across examples

IMAGE-DRIVEN INSTRUCTION
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Extracted textbook images are instructional assets, not decoration.

When relevant images are available, use them frequently and let them help drive
the surrounding prose. The final Unit 1 gold standard commonly used several
source images per section (often about 5–6), but source availability and
instructional value decide the count.

Good image use:
  • introduce a phenomenon through the image
  • explain a source diagram in the nearby paragraphs
  • ask students to label arrows, compare positions, identify measured features,
    or connect a sentence to evidence in the figure
  • use a second image to extend or contrast the first
  • return to a figure during Stop and Discuss or an Example when useful

Bad image use:
  • decorative image with no instructional purpose
  • dumping many images at the end
  • a caption that gives away all thinking
  • redrawing a supplied source image unnecessarily

Use a plain-text image annotation cue such as:
  Image read: Compare the two positions. Mark the evidence that shows what
  changed and what stayed the same.

Do NOT bold “Image read:” inside instructional reading. In reading passages,
bold is reserved for the first meaningful introduction/definition of vocabulary.

INTERLEAVED FLOW — NON-NEGOTIABLE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Do NOT place all reading first and all Examples/YTIs at the end.

Use this flow:

  Reading + source image(s)
        ↓
  Reading Stop and Discuss + workspace
        ↓
  Math Notes if the next application needs a new representation/formula
        ↓
  Example + workspace
        ↓
  You Try It + workspace
        ↓
  MORE reading + source image(s)
        ↓
  next Reading Stop and Discuss
        ↓
  next Example/YTI
        ↓
  repeat as needed

There is NO automatic Stop and Discuss after a You Try It.
Stop and Discuss belongs to the reading/image/evidence cycle, where students
need to process meaning, compare evidence, or explain a representation.

EXAMPLE/YTI COUNT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Use 1–4 Example/YTI pairs per section. Four is a CEILING, not a target.

Gold-standard default:
  • 3 pairs when the section naturally has three conceptual/application cycles
  • 2 pairs for a shorter section
  • 1 pair if that is truly enough
  • 4 only when the source and assessment targets genuinely require it

Never add a pair just to fill a template.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7. VOCABULARY BOLDING — STRICT GOLD-STANDARD RULE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Inside instructional reading-box prose:

  ✓ Bold a vocabulary term at its FIRST meaningful introduction and/or definition.
  ✓ If the word appears incidentally before it is explained, wait to bold until
    the meaningful introduction/definition.
  ✓ After that first bold use, all later uses in reading are normal text.
  ✓ A vocabulary term may be bold again in the Vocabulary Definitions table;
    that table is not instructional reading and does not violate this rule.

Do NOT:
  ✗ bold every later occurrence of the term
  ✗ bold non-vocabulary emphasis inside reading
  ✗ bold “Image read:” or other reading cues
  ✗ bold a formula explanation simply for emphasis
  ✗ use <strong> in reading for ordinary emphasis

Private QA requirement:
  For each vocabulary term, count <strong> uses inside instructional reading.
  Maximum = 1, and that one use must be the meaningful intro/definition.

GRAPH TOOL — AUTHORITATIVE FOR NEWLY GENERATED GRAPHS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

All NEWLY GENERATED graphs/visuals that graph_tool.py can produce must use the
currently uploaded graph_tool.py.

Do not:
  ✗ draw manual Matplotlib figures outside graph_tool.py
  ✗ use ASCII diagrams as a substitute
  ✗ use inline SVG as a substitute
  ✗ independently restyle graph-tool output

If the needed source image already exists and is appropriate, use the source
image instead of regenerating it.

If a required NEW graph/visual type does not exist in graph_tool.py, stop that
part of the build and report the exact missing type rather than inventing a new
rendering system.

GRAPH_TOOL.PY INTEGRITY RULE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

When graph_tool.py is needed:

1. Read the complete file.
2. Copy the complete file verbatim as generate_graphs.py.
3. Never modify, refactor, reorder, delete, or insert into existing source lines.
4. Find the LAST occurrence of:

     # PASTE YOUR GRAPH CODE BELOW THIS LINE

5. Append generation calls ONLY after that final marker.
6. Do not add imports, OUTPUT_DIR, os.makedirs, matplotlib.use(), style overrides,
   or replacement helper functions in the appended block.
7. Use only existing public functions and valid parameters.
8. Use title='' or a neutral title that does not reveal an answer.
9. Review every appended block for syntax, function names, signatures, brackets,
   and top-level indentation.
10. Run generate_graphs.py.
11. Verify every expected PNG exists.
12. Include generated PNGs in the Physics notes figures/ folder.
13. Include generate_graphs.py in the final section folder.

IMPORTANT: current graph_tool_v12 contains more than one append marker. The
FINAL/LAST marker is authoritative.

PART 9 — MATHJAX RULES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Rules:
  • config before CDN script
  • cdn.jsdelivr.net
  • inline math: $...$
  • display math: $$...$$

PART 16 — GOLD-STANDARD READING QUALITY CHECK
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Before finalizing each section, read it as if it were the student’s replacement
for the extracted textbook section.

Ask:
  1. Is there enough reading to understand WHY the physics works, not merely name
     the term or formula?
  2. Does each major figure have a reason to be there?
  3. Could a student annotate both the words and the images?
  4. Do the reading cycles preserve the source’s conceptual development?
  5. Does the Example/YTI arrive soon enough after the idea it applies?
  6. Is there more reading after that application so the section continues to
     develop rather than becoming a worksheet?
  7. Are vocabulary words bolded exactly once at their meaningful introduction?
  8. Is the final vocabulary table a consolidation, not the first time students
     see the meaning?
  9. Does the Summary capture the section without replacing the reading?
 10. Is there enough physical white space for Notability annotation?

If the answer to 1–6 is NO, the notes are probably too thin or too worksheet-like.
Revise before QA.
