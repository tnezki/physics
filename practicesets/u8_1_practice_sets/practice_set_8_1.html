Practice Set 1 — Section 8.1: Magnetism and Magnetic Fields

1. Name the two pole types found on every ordinary magnet.

2. Describe how two north poles interact compared with a north pole facing a south pole.

3. What does the arrow direction on a magnetic-field line represent?

4. If a bar magnet is broken into two pieces, what pole pattern should each piece have?

5. What is a magnetic domain in a magnetic material?

6. Use the iron-filings figure to compare the field near the ends of the magnet with the field farther away. Cite a visible feature as evidence.

7. A field map shows lines 2 mm apart near one pole and 9 mm apart far from the magnet. Compare the relative field strengths of the two regions.

8. A small compass is moved around a bar magnet. Explain how the compass orientation can be used to map local field direction.

9. A piece of iron begins with domains pointing many directions. After exposure to a strong magnet, many domains point similarly. Explain how its overall magnetic behavior changes.

10. Two magnets are arranged so field lines connect smoothly across the gap from one facing pole to the other. What interaction should the magnets have, and what feature supports the prediction?

11. A student argues that because magnetic field lines can be drawn on paper, the lines must be physical strands in space. Critique the claim using what a field-line model actually represents.

12. A classmate says, “The north pole of a magnet attracts any magnetic pole.” Diagnose the error and give a rule that works for both attraction and repulsion.

13. Which statement correctly describes ordinary magnetic poles?

  1. A magnet has both north and south pole regions.
  2. Magnetic poles exist only while a magnet is moving.
  3. Pole labels indicate electric charge signs.
  4. Only electromagnets have poles.
  5. A magnet can normally have a north pole with no south pole.

14. Which pair of facing poles tends to repel?

  1. a north pole facing unmagnetized iron only
  2. north facing north
  3. either unlike-pole pair
  4. south facing north
  5. north facing south

15. On a field-line model, closely spaced lines represent

  1. a relatively stronger magnetic field
  2. isolated magnetic charge
  3. a faster-moving magnet
  4. a weaker magnetic field
  5. a region with no magnetic effect

16. If a bar magnet is cut in half, each half is expected to

  1. become a single isolated pole
  2. have its own north and south poles
  3. contain only the original center pole
  4. become electrically charged
  5. lose all magnetic behavior immediately

17. A strongly magnetized iron sample is best modeled by domains that are

  1. converted into electric charges
  2. all absent
  3. more nearly aligned in a common direction
  4. separated into north-only atoms
  5. randomly oriented with perfect cancellation

18. A compass placed near a magnet is useful because its north end

  1. points opposite every field arrow
  2. measures field strength by changing mass
  3. creates the field around the magnet
  4. always points toward the closest object
  5. turns to align with the local magnetic-field direction

19. Which observation is strongest evidence that a magnetic field is stronger near one location than another?

  1. The paper is darker there.
  2. The arrow labels use a bigger font.
  3. The location is farther from both poles.
  4. The magnet is drawn larger there.
  5. Field lines are more densely packed there on a consistently drawn map.

20. A north pole is brought near a south pole. The most likely interaction is

  1. attraction
  2. conversion into electric charge
  3. repulsion
  4. no interaction
  5. loss of both poles
