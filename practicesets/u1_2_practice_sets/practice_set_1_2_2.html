Practice Set 2 — Section 1.2: Forces and Newton’s First Law

1. Complete this Newton’s First Law idea: unchanged motion continues unless ________. Explain what “unchanged motion” includes.

2. Define inertia without using the word force.

3. A person stands still on a bathroom scale. Identify the two main vertical forces acting on the person.

4. A moving object has several forces acting on it, but the forces balance. Does Newton’s First Law allow the object to keep moving? State the allowed motion.

5. A skateboarder turns a corner at steady speed. Is the motion unchanged or changing? What does Newton’s First Law imply about the net force?

6. Use the coin-and-card demonstration from the notes. Explain why the card can move sideways while the coin tends to stay nearly above the glass until it begins to fall.

7. A loaded equipment cart and an empty cart are both at rest. A student says the loaded cart has more inertia only because it is harder to push. Explain the deeper relationship between amount of matter and inertia.

8. A hammer handle is moving downward and stops suddenly. Predict the hammerhead’s tendency immediately after the stop and explain why this is an inertia effect rather than an extra downward force.

9. A scale shows 550 N while a person stands still. Explain how the scale can show a nonzero force even though the net force on the person is zero.

10. A ball rolls across a floor and slows. Identify what observation shows the motion is changing and explain why that means the net force cannot be zero.

11. A student draws an “inertia force” arrow backward on a passenger when a bus brakes. Critique the diagram and replace the explanation with one that uses Newton’s First Law and actual interactions.

12. Two explanations are offered for a puck gliding after leaving a stick. One says the stick leaves a forward force stored in the puck; the other says the puck can continue moving while net force is near zero. Evaluate both explanations using Newton’s First Law.

13. A soccer ball is at rest. If the net force remains zero, what does Newton’s First Law predict?

  1. It remains at rest.
  2. It begins moving slowly.
  3. Its inertia disappears.
  4. Gravity must stop acting.
  5. Its support force becomes zero.

14. A cart already moves north at constant speed. If the net force stays zero, what happens?

  1. It stops.
  2. It speeds up.
  3. It continues north at the same speed.
  4. It turns east.
  5. It must gain a northward force.

15. Which description treats inertia correctly?

  1. A force that pushes backward
  2. A property of matter related to resistance to motion change
  3. A force created only by high speed
  4. The upward force from a surface
  5. The total of all force vectors

16. Two carts move at different speeds. Cart X has twice as much matter as Cart Y. Which statement is best supported?

  1. Cart Y must have more inertia because it is moving.
  2. Cart X has greater inertia because it has more matter.
  3. Both have zero inertia while moving.
  4. Their inertia is determined only by net force.
  5. Speed alone determines inertia.

17. Which statement correctly distinguishes force from net force?

  1. Net force is one particular interaction.
  2. Force and net force always mean the same thing.
  3. A force is one interaction; net force is the vector combination of all forces on the object.
  4. Net force exists only when no forces act.
  5. Net force is another name for inertia.

18. A laptop sits motionless on a desk. Which statement is correct?

  1. There are no forces on it.
  2. Its downward weight and upward support force can cancel.
  3. Its weight is zero.
  4. The desk exerts friction upward.
  5. Inertia supplies the upward force.

19. Which force is produced by a stretched rope or cable?

  1. weight
  2. support force
  3. tension
  4. inertia
  5. net force

20. Which force can act opposite sliding motion between two surfaces?

  1. weight
  2. friction
  3. inertia
  4. tension
