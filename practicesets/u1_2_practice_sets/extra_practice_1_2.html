Extra Practice — Section 1.2: Forces and Newton’s First Law

1. State Newton’s First Law and give one example of an object at rest and one example of an object moving with unchanged motion.

2. What does it mean to say that inertia is a property of matter?

3. Name the force Earth exerts downward on a standing student and the force the floor exerts upward.

4. Can an object have several forces acting on it and still have zero net force? Answer yes or no and give a one-sentence example.

5. A bicycle moves straight at constant speed. What does its motion tell you about the horizontal net force?

6. Use the ball-between-strings image. Explain how the demonstration shows resistance to a sudden change while the actual forces are tensions in the strings, not “inertia forces.”

7. An empty cart and a heavily loaded cart receive similar brief pushes. Compare the expected change in motion qualitatively and connect the difference to inertia.

8. A book presses on a table so slightly that the deformation cannot be seen. Explain why the table can still exert an upward support force.

9. A moving toy car crosses a rough patch and slows. Explain how Newton’s First Law helps identify that a nonzero net force is acting during the slowdown.

10. A person standing still on a scale says, “Because the scale reads 620 N, my net force is 620 N upward.” Explain the mistake using weight, support force, and net force.

11. A passenger says, “When the bus turns, inertia pulls me outward.” Critique the wording. Explain the passenger’s tendency and identify that a real interaction is needed to change direction.

12. Compare these claims: (1) “No force is needed for constant straight-line motion when net force is zero.” (2) “A constant forward force is always needed to keep motion going.” Defend the better claim with Newton’s First Law and a real example.
