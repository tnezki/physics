Practice Set 1 — Section 1.2: Forces and Newton’s First Law

1. State Newton’s First Law in everyday language. Include what happens to an object at rest and to an object already moving when the net force is zero.

2. A student says, “Inertia is the force that keeps a moving object going.” Name the physics idea the student is confusing with a force, and state what inertia actually is.

3. A book rests on a table. Name the downward force on the book and the upward force from the table.

4. In one sentence, distinguish “no forces act on the object” from “the net force on the object is zero.”

5. A hockey puck moves in a straight line at constant speed. According to Newton’s First Law, what must be true about its net force?

6. Use the tablecloth image. Explain why the dishes can remain nearly in place when the cloth is pulled quickly. Identify the interaction that changes the cloth’s motion and explain why inertia should not be drawn as another force on the dishes.

7. Two identical carts are pushed in the same way, but one is loaded with books. Compare their inertia and predict which cart is harder to start, stop, or redirect. Explain why.

8. A coin rests on a card over a glass. The card is snapped sideways. Predict the coin’s motion and explain which force changes the card’s horizontal motion and why the coin does not immediately follow.

9. A 20-N book rests on a level table with no other vertical forces. Describe the two main vertical forces and explain why their presence is consistent with zero net force.

10. A puck eventually stops on ordinary ice. Explain why this does not contradict Newton’s First Law. Include the role of friction and what would happen on a lower-friction surface.

11. Critique this statement: “A faster object has more inertia because inertia is the force of motion.” Rewrite it using the correct relationship between inertia and matter, and explain what must happen for motion to change.

12. Student A says the dishes in a tablecloth trick stay put because “inertia pushes backward.” Student B says the dishes tend to keep their state of rest and only real interactions can provide forces. Decide which explanation is stronger and defend your choice.

13. Which statement best expresses Newton’s First Law?

  1. An object changes motion only when a nonzero net force acts.
  2. Every moving object needs a forward force to keep moving.
  3. Objects at rest have no forces acting on them.
  4. Inertia is a force that prevents motion.
  5. A force is required only to keep an object at rest.

14. A cart moves straight at constant speed. Which conclusion is most consistent with Newton’s First Law?

  1. A forward force must be larger than every backward force.
  2. Its net force is zero.
  3. No forces can be acting.
  4. Its inertia has become zero.
  5. Its weight must be zero.

15. Which statement about inertia is correct?

  1. Inertia is a hidden force opposite motion.
  2. Inertia increases only when speed increases.
  3. Inertia is a property of matter that resists changes in motion.
  4. Inertia is the vector sum of forces.
  5. Inertia disappears when an object stops.

16. Which object has greater inertia?

  1. A 1-kg cart moving fast
  2. A 1-kg cart at rest
  3. A 5-kg cart, regardless of whether it is moving or at rest
  4. Any object with a larger net force
  5. Any object moving in a straight line

17. A book rests on a table. Which statement is best?

  1. No forces act on the book.
  2. Only gravity acts on the book.
  3. Only the table acts on the book.
  4. Weight and support force can balance so the net force is zero.
  5. The book’s inertia pushes upward.

18. Which situation is the best example of “no net force” rather than “no forces”?

  1. A book resting on a table
  2. A hypothetical object in empty space with no interactions
  3. A ball speeding downward
  4. A cart turning a corner
  5. A sled with 20 N right and 5 N left

19. What is the downward force on a backpack near Earth called?

  1. support force
  2. tension
  3. weight
  4. friction
  5. inertia

20. A person stands still on a scale. What is the upward force exerted by the scale on the person?

  1. weight
  2. support force
  3. inertia
  4. net force
  5. friction
