Check Your Understanding — Section 1.1: Thinking Like a Physicist

1. Classify this statement: “The mark is 8.4 cm from the edge.” Then explain what makes it that type of statement.

2. Write one scientific claim that could be tested and name the evidence you would collect.

3. A student sees a repeated pattern in measurements and proposes an explanation. Identify which part is evidence and which part is an inference or hypothesis.

4. Explain why a model may omit realistic details yet still be useful for answering a particular physics question.

5. A calculation agrees with one trial but disagrees with four careful repeats. Explain what should be checked before deciding whether the model is supported.

6. Design a brief test of the claim “A longer pendulum takes more time for one cycle.” State the independent measurement you would change, what you would measure, and one result that would make you question the claim.
