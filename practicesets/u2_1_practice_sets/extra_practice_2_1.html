Extra Practice — Section 2.1: Motion and Reference Frames

1. Explain the difference between motion and relative motion in one or two sentences.

2. A dog lies in the back of a pickup truck traveling along a highway. State the dog’s motion relative to the truck bed and relative to a highway sign.

3. A ferry passes a lighthouse. Which object is the reference frame in the statement “the ferry moves past the lighthouse”?

4. Why is “the student is moving” incomplete as a physics statement?

5. Name one reference frame other than Earth’s surface that would be useful in an everyday motion description.

6. A cyclist carries a water bottle fixed to the bicycle frame. Compare the bottle’s motion relative to the bicycle and relative to the road, and explain what changes between the two descriptions.

7. A drone maintains a fixed position over the deck of a ship moving east. Write the drone’s motion description from the ship frame and from a pier frame.

8. Suppose a student says a classroom desk is “truly at rest.” Use the Earth–Sun idea to explain why the statement needs a reference frame.

9. Use situation C in the flatcar figure. Identify two possible frames and explain how the runner’s motion description changes when the frame changes.

10. A camera is mounted inside a moving bus. Another camera is fixed to a building. Explain why videos from the two cameras can show different motion for the same passenger.

11. A traveler says, “My suitcase never moved during the trip because it stayed beside me.” A station worker says, “That suitcase moved through the entire station.” Build a short argument showing how both statements can be valid.

12. Evaluate this rule: “Always use the ground frame because it is simpler.” Give one case where the ground frame is convenient and one case where another frame gives a more useful description.
