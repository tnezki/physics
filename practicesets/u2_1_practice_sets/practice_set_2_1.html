Practice Set 1 — Section 2.1: Motion and Reference Frames

1. In one sentence, define a reference frame as it is used to describe motion.

2. A child sits on a city bus that is moving down the street. State the child’s motion relative to the seat and relative to a lamppost beside the road.

3. A coffee mug does not change position on a classroom desk. Name the reference frame that makes “the mug is at rest” a precise statement.

4. Why is the ground normally the unstated reference frame when someone says, “The cyclist is moving 7 m/s”?

5. Rewrite “The suitcase is moving” so the sentence explicitly names a reference frame.

6. Use situation B in the flatcar figure. Compare the runner’s motion relative to the flatcar with the runner’s motion relative to the ground. Explain why the two descriptions can differ.

7. A phone lies on the seat of a train moving through a station. Explain which comparison makes the phone at rest and which comparison makes it moving.

8. Two students watch a drone that stays directly above the center of a ferry while the ferry crosses a river. One student is on the ferry and one is on shore. Predict how each student describes the drone’s motion and identify each frame.

9. For each statement, name a reasonable reference frame: (a) “the runner is at rest,” (b) “the runner is moving,” and (c) “the classroom is moving through space.” Explain why more than one statement can be true.

10. A classmate says, “If an object is moving, every observer must agree that it is moving.” Use a passenger in a moving vehicle to explain why that rule fails.

11. A backpack remains on a train seat as the train passes a platform. One observer says the backpack has not moved; another says it moved hundreds of meters. Reconcile the two claims by naming the frames and explaining what each observer measures.

12. Critique the statement “There is one absolutely correct answer to whether the student is moving.” Replace it with a more precise physics statement and support it with a concrete example.

13. A rider is seated on a moving subway car. Relative to which frame is the rider most clearly at rest?

  1. the subway seat
  2. a station wall
  3. the tracks
  4. a building outside
  5. the Sun

14. Which statement best shows that motion is relative?

  1. An object can be at rest in one frame and moving in another.
  2. Every observer measures the same motion.
  3. Only Earth can be a reference frame.
  4. Moving objects cannot be used as frames.
  5. Rest means zero motion relative to all possible frames.

15. In the sentence “The car moves east past the school,” which object is the most natural reference frame?

  1. the car engine
  2. the school and ground
  3. a passenger in the car
  4. the steering wheel
  5. another moving car

16. Which revision makes “The ball is not moving” most precise?

  1. The ball is not moving very much.
  2. The ball is at rest relative to the table.
  3. The ball has no mass.
  4. The ball is not moving because it is indoors.
  5. The ball is at rest relative to every object.

17. A traveler walks forward inside a train. Which comparison is most useful for describing the traveler from the conductor’s viewpoint?

  1. traveler relative to train floor
  2. traveler relative to a distant star
  3. train relative to Sun
  4. station relative to Earth center
  5. track relative to Moon

18. A platform observer and a train passenger disagree about whether a suitcase is moving. What should be checked first?

  1. the suitcase color
  2. the mass of the suitcase
  3. the reference frame each observer uses
  4. the time of day
  5. the train manufacturer

19. Why is Earth’s surface usually used in everyday motion descriptions?

  1. It is the only valid frame.
  2. It is a convenient common comparison for nearby motion.
  3. It never moves in any larger frame.
  4. It makes all speeds zero.
  5. It removes the need to state direction.

20. Which everyday statement most reasonably assumes the ground as its reference frame?

  1. The runner travels 5 m/s down the track.
  2. The passenger is still in her seat.
  3. The book is at rest on the shelf.
  4. The cup is fixed in the holder.
  5. The astronaut floats beside the spacecraft.
