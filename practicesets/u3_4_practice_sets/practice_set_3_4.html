Practice Set 1 — Section 3.4: Newton’s Third Law and Interactions

1. When one object exerts a force on a second object, what does Newton’s Third Law say the second object does?

2. A hand pushes on a wall. Name the two objects in the interaction.

3. A bat exerts a force on a ball. Identify the reaction force.

4. State the relationship between the magnitudes and directions of a Third-Law force pair.

5. Why do the two forces in a Third-Law pair not cancel on one object?

6. Use the hammer-and-stake image. Identify the force on the stake and the Third-Law partner force on the hammer.

7. Two skaters push apart. Skater A is 45 kg and Skater B is 75 kg. They exert equal-magnitude forces on each other. Which skater has the greater acceleration? Explain using Newton’s Second and Third Laws.

8. A truck and a small car collide. During contact the force on the truck from the car is 8,000 N. What is the force on the car from the truck, including direction relationship?

9. Choose the system as “person + skateboard,” excluding the wall. Is the wall’s force on the person internal or external to the chosen system? Explain.

10. Choose the system as two carts plus the compressed spring between them. Classify the spring forces between the carts as internal or external to the whole system.

11. A student says, “The truck hits the car harder than the car hits the truck because the truck is heavier.” Critique the claim and explain why their accelerations can still differ.

12. A person in a boat pushes water backward with a paddle. Define a system as person + boat. Explain why the water’s force can accelerate the system even though the Third-Law partner force is equal and opposite.
