Practice Set 1 — Section 2.4: Free Fall Motion

1. Define free fall in terms of the significant influence acting on the object.

2. When air resistance is ignored near Earth, what approximate acceleration magnitude is used for free fall?

3. A ball is moving upward after being thrown. Is it still in free fall after it leaves the hand if air resistance is ignored? Explain briefly.

4. Classify each as free fall or not free fall: a rock dropped in vacuum; a skydiver under an open parachute.

5. At the highest point of a thrown ball’s path, what are the ball’s instantaneous speed and acceleration status?

6. Use the falling-rock figure. Describe the speed-gain pattern from one second to the next and use $g \approx 10\text{ m/s}^2$ to predict the speed after 4 s.

7. A ball is dropped from rest. Use $v=gt$ with $g=10\text{ m/s}^2$ to determine its speed after 3.5 s.

8. A stone is dropped from rest for 3 s. Use $d=\frac12gt^2$ to determine the total distance fallen.

9. A dropped object falls 5 m by 1 s, 20 m by 2 s, and 45 m by 3 s. Determine the distance traveled during each one-second interval and describe the pattern.

10. A rock is dropped for 4 s. Determine its final speed, then use $v_{avg}=(v_i+v_f)/2$ to determine how far it falls. Report one final distance.

11. A student says, “Heavier objects fall faster because they have more mass.” Evaluate the claim for ideal free fall and explain what air resistance can change in real observations.

12. A student claims that a ball thrown upward has zero acceleration at the top because its velocity is momentarily zero. Critique the claim using gravity and free-fall reasoning.

  3. larger acceleration for the lighter ball
