Why did anyone care about tangent lines?

The mathematical problem came from trying to describe change in a curved world.

How can we describe the rate of change at one exact moment when the graph is curved?
MotionA falling object changes speed. A projectile rises, slows, stops for an instant, and falls.
AstronomyPlanets move along curved paths. Their direction and rate of motion change continuously.
GrowthPopulation, disease spread, and investment growth rarely follow straight lines for long.
OptimizationEngineers and scientists care about moments when a changing quantity reaches a high point, low point, or changes direction.

The tangent-line idea

For a straight line, slope describes its rate of change everywhere. For a curved graph, mathematicians needed a way to describe the rate of change right now. Your secant experiment suggests one route: use slopes through two nearby points and move the second point closer and closer.
