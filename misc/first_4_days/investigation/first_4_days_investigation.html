Ball Drop Video Analysis: Build a CER from Data

Track a falling ball in Vernier Video Analysis, compare equal-time intervals, and use your measurements as evidence.

Question
1

Predict before measuring

What happens to the ball’s speed while it falls?

Write a prediction and one reason for your thinking. Your prediction is not evidence yet.

Video Analysis Setup
  1. Open Vernier Video Analysis and import the teacher-provided ball-drop video.
  2. Set the scale using the known-length object visible in the video.
  3. Set an origin and choose one consistent point on the ball to track.
  4. Mark that same point at every frame, or at the same fixed frame step, so your time intervals stay consistent.
  5. Display the vertical position–time data. Keep the app’s vertical sign convention; either direction convention can work.
Quality check: Compare your first two or three marked points with your partner. Are you clicking the same place on the ball and using a consistent frame step?

What pattern do you notice? During about the same amount of time, does the ball move the same distance, a greater distance, or a smaller distance later in the fall?

CER

Explain the motion

Claim: Answer the question: What happens to the ball’s speed while it falls?

Evidence: Use the two interval comparisons above. Include specific measured values.

Reasoning: Explain why your evidence supports your claim. Connect distance moved during equal time intervals to speed.

Partner check before you finish: Point to the exact numbers in your evidence, then read your reasoning aloud. Does the reasoning explain why those numbers support the claim?

Claim = answer · Evidence = measured data · Reasoning = why the data support the answer
