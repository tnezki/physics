Bowling Ball Drop: What Happens to Velocity?

Make a claim first. Then use your own video and Vernier Video Analysis to decide whether the evidence supports it.

Names: ________________________________ ________________________________
Learning Target: I can make a claim about a physical phenomenon, examine data from an investigation, and use evidence and reasoning to support or revise my thinking.
1 · Claim — Before Any Data

What do you think will happen?

What do you think will happen to the bowling ball's velocity as it falls?

Write your claim before we record or analyze anything. Explain what you currently think and why.

2 · Record the Phenomenon

Capture three drops

Work with your partner to record the bowling-ball drop. One designated person will make each drop; pairs record.

  • We will record three drops.
  • Keep all three videos so your pair can choose the clearest one for analysis.
  • Follow the teacher's positioning and safety directions while recording.
Before going back inside: Make sure your pair has at least one video that clearly shows the ball through the useful part of the fall.

Analyze Your Video

Choose the best of your three recordings and build a useful data set from it.

3 · Watch the Vernier Demonstration

Learn the tools — not the answer

Your teacher will demonstrate the Vernier Video Analysis features you will use, including tracking motion, position-time and velocity-time graphs, Examine, and regression.

Watch for how to use the tools. The class will not interpret the bowling-ball result during the demonstration.

If you need a reminder later: use the Vernier Hints document in Student Resources.
4 · Pair Analysis

Replicate the analysis with your own video

With your partner, choose the clearest of your three drops and analyze that video in Vernier Video Analysis.

Your job is not to make your graph look a certain way. Your job is to represent what your video actually shows.

5 · Examine

Organize what your data shows

Use the graph tools, Examine, and regression as useful. Record patterns, values, or features that you think matter for the original velocity question.

Reason From the Evidence

Evidence Check

What seems most important?

Which pieces of your analysis seem most useful for deciding what happened to the bowling ball's velocity?

6 · Revisit the Claim

Keep it, revise it, or replace it

After analyzing your video, what is your claim now about the bowling ball's velocity as it falls?

7 · Evidence

Select the strongest evidence

Choose the data, graph features, regression results, or comparisons that best support your final claim.

8 · Reasoning

Connect the evidence to the claim

Explain why the evidence you selected supports your final claim about velocity.
