Ball Drop Video Analysis: Build a CER from Data

Use Vernier Video Analysis to track a falling ball, then use the data as evidence.
Names: ____________________________ ____________________________
Learning Target: I can successfully participate in physics class by following course procedures and using evidence-based reasoning to explain physical phenomena.
I Can Statements:
  • I can follow the procedures and routines used in physics class.
  • I can make a clear claim that answers a question about a physical phenomenon.
  • I can select and use relevant observations or data as evidence to support my claim.
  • I can explain how my evidence supports my claim using scientific reasoning.
Question
1
What happens to the ball’s speed while it falls?

Write a prediction before collecting data. A prediction is not yet evidence.

Video Analysis Setup
  1. Open Vernier Video Analysis and import the teacher-provided ball-drop video.
  2. Set the scale using a known-length object visible in the video.
  3. Set an origin and choose a consistent point on the ball to track.
  4. Step through the video and mark that same point on the ball across frames.
  5. Display the position–time data/graph. Keep the vertical direction convention consistent.
Procedure check: Before collecting the full set, compare your first two points with your partner. Are you clicking the same location on the ball?

Notice the Pattern
2
Evidence hunt

Use the graph and table. During equal or nearly equal time steps, do the position changes stay about the same, become larger, or become smaller?

3
Choose relevant evidence

Write two specific observations/data comparisons that could support a claim about whether the ball’s speed changes. Do not use the ball’s color, size, or another irrelevant detail.

CER

Explain the motion

Claim: Answer the question: What happens to the ball’s speed while it falls?

Evidence: Use at least two relevant observations or values from your video analysis.

Reasoning: Explain why the pattern in position change over equal time intervals supports your claim about speed.

Keep the CER simple: claim = answer; evidence = what you observed/measured; reasoning = why those observations support the answer.
