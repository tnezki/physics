Physics · First 4 Days · Teacher

Exit Ticket Keys

Keys below are for the local Version 1 source sets. Versions 2–4 preserve the same evidence roles through direct parallels/variations.

Portfolio Baseline 1

Anchor: Your group measures a toy car. One student records the distance immediately; another says, “I’ll remember it later.” Which procedure better protects the scientific record? Explain why.
Answer / evidence target: Record the measurement immediately; this preserves the observation while it is fresh and makes the record more trustworthy.
Parallel A: A battery-powered bug crosses a desk. A student writes, “The bug moved faster on the smooth desk than on the towel.” Is this sentence a Claim, Evidence, or Reasoning? Explain the job of that CER part.
Answer / evidence target: Claim; it answers what happened in the physical situation.
Parallel B: A wind-up car travels 38 cm across tile in 2 s and 21 cm across carpet in 2 s. Write one clear claim: On which surface did the car move faster?
Answer / evidence target: The object moved faster on the surface where it traveled farther during the same time.
Variation: A student claims a rolling ball moved faster on Ramp A than Ramp B. Which is better evidence: Ramp A was blue, or the ball traveled 1.4 m in 2 s on A and 0.9 m in 2 s on B? Explain why.
Answer / evidence target: The distance-and-time comparison is relevant evidence; color/appearance is not evidence about speed.

Portfolio Baseline 2

Anchor: A toy car is watched for three equal 1-second intervals. It travels 12 cm, then 20 cm, then 31 cm. Write one evidence sentence that supports the claim: The car sped up.
Answer / evidence target: It traveled 12 cm, then 20 cm, then 31 cm in equal 1-second intervals; the equal-time distance increased.
Parallel A: A cart travels 24 cm during each of four equal 1-second intervals. A student claims the cart moved at a steady speed. Write one reasoning sentence connecting the measurements to the claim.
Answer / evidence target: Equal distance in equal time means the same speed, supporting steady speed.
Parallel B: Claim: A marble rolled faster on the smooth board than on felt. The group recorded board color, a favorite surface, and distances of 82 cm on smooth board and 47 cm on felt during the same 2 s. Identify the relevant evidence and explain why it belongs.
Answer / evidence target: Use the distance measurements because they directly describe the motion under the same time condition; the other details are irrelevant.
Variation: A student writes, “The ball sped up because the distances got bigger.” Strengthen the reasoning: what does a larger distance during the same amount of time tell you about speed?
Answer / evidence target: More distance in the same amount of time means greater speed, so increasing equal-time distances support that it sped up.

Portfolio Baseline 3

Anchor: A wind-up bug travels 9 cm during the first second, 16 cm during the next second, and 25 cm during the third second. Write a complete CER answering: Did the bug speed up?
Answer / evidence target: Claim: yes. Evidence: 9 cm, 16 cm, and 25 cm in equal 1-second intervals. Reasoning: more distance in the same time means greater speed.
Parallel A: A toy car crosses the same 2 m path in two trials. Trial A takes 3 s; Trial B takes 5 s. Write a claim and one evidence sentence answering which trial was faster.
Answer / evidence target: The faster trial takes less time to cover the same distance; cite both times for the same path.
Parallel B: Claim: The cart moved faster on tile. Evidence: It traveled 1.2 m on tile and 0.7 m on carpet in the same 2 s. Reasoning: “The numbers prove it.” Rewrite only the Reasoning so it connects the evidence to the claim.
Answer / evidence target: Because the time is the same, the greater distance means greater speed on the named surface.
Variation: During video analysis, one student clicks a different point on the ball in every frame. Describe one procedure change that would make the data more trustworthy and explain why.
Answer / evidence target: Use one consistent tracking point/scale/frame interval/recording system. Consistency makes measurements comparable, repeatable, and traceable.
