Physics · First 4 Days

Day 1 Warm-Up

Familiar thinking + opening scientific reasoning
4 prompts
1. Brain teaser

A physics teacher has three identical opaque boxes. One contains a bell that rings when the box is tilted, one contains a sliding metal block, and one contains nothing. Without opening the boxes, what observations could help you identify all three?

2. Formula thinking

A foam alien is dropped from the school bleachers and falls 18 m in 2.0 s. Use $d=\tfrac12at^2$ to estimate its acceleration. Rearrange the relationship for $a$ before substituting the measurements.

3. General science

Two groups measure the same lab table as 1.48 m and 1.50 m. What is one reasonable scientific response to the small difference before deciding either group is wrong?

4. Course routine

Your teacher has just demonstrated how today’s file should be opened, named, and saved. Record the three steps you need to repeat next time without help.

Physics · First 4 Days

Day 2 Warm-Up

Familiar thinking + opening scientific reasoning
4 prompts
1. Brain teaser

Eight identical-looking steel balls sit on a lab cart. One is heavier. With a balance scale and only two weighings, how could you identify the heavier ball?

2. Formula thinking

A remote-control car travels 12 m down the hallway in 3.0 s. Use $v=d/t$ to determine its average speed and include units. Then state what the number means in words.

3. General science

A motion graph has time on the horizontal axis and position on the vertical axis. What does one plotted point tell you about the object?

4. CER focus

A cart moves 0.25 m, then 0.50 m, then 0.75 m during three equal time intervals. A student says, “The cart sped up.” Identify the claim and the observation that could serve as evidence.

Physics · First 4 Days

Day 3 Warm-Up

Familiar thinking + opening scientific reasoning
4 prompts
1. Brain teaser

A student says, “I know the scooter was moving faster because the graph looks steeper.” What follow-up question would make that statement more precise and evidence-based?

2. Formula thinking

A small robotic bug speeds up from 2 m/s to 10 m/s while crossing the classroom in 4 s. Use $a=\Delta v/\Delta t$ to determine its average acceleration. Explain what the sign and units tell you.

3. General science

Why should every measurement in a data table carry a unit instead of leaving the unit only in the title?

4. CER focus

A dropped ball covers a larger vertical distance during each successive tenth of a second. Write one clear claim about how the ball’s motion changes.

Physics · First 4 Days

Day 4 Warm-Up

Familiar thinking + opening scientific reasoning
4 prompts
1. Brain teaser

A class repeats the same ramp trial four times and gets nearly the same result each time. Why is that stronger evidence than a single successful trial?

2. Formula thinking

A mystery robot cart accelerates at 3 m/s² when the fan pushes it with 18 N. Use $F=ma$ to determine the cart’s mass. First rearrange the relationship so the unknown is isolated.

3. General science

One position measurement is far from every other point in a video-tracking data set. Name one thing you should check before deleting it.

4. CER focus

A student writes, “The cart sped up because the distances got bigger.” Rewrite the sentence so the reasoning explains why larger distances during equal time intervals mean greater speed.
