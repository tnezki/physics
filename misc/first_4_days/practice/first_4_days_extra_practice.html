First 4 Days — Extra Practice

Physics · First 4 Days · I can successfully participate in physics class by following course procedures and using evidence-based reasoning to explain physical phenomena.

I can follow the procedures and routines used in physics class.

1. A team records a great trial but saves the graph under the previous group’s filename. What should they do immediately, and what scientific problem could the naming error create?

2. You are unsure whether a force sensor should be zeroed before or after attaching it. What is the strongest procedure-based move?

3. Two group members both type into separate data tables. Name one routine that would prevent conflicting records.

4. A classmate finishes a CER but cannot find it the next day. Explain how a consistent save/export routine protects learning evidence.

I can identify the parts of a scientific explanation: Claim, Evidence, and Reasoning (CER).

5. A fan cart accelerates. A student writes: “The cart accelerated. Its velocity changed from 0.4 m/s to 1.1 m/s. Acceleration describes a change in velocity over time.” Identify C, E, and R.

6. A flashlight spot moves across a wall. Which CER part should include the measured positions? Which part should explain what those positions mean about motion?

7. A student writes only measurements and never answers the investigation question. Which CER part is missing?

8. A student states a claim and repeats the same measurement as the explanation. Which CER part is weak or missing?

I can make a clear claim that answers a question about a physical phenomenon.

9. A model car covers the same 0.50 m during every 0.25 s interval. Write a claim answering whether its speed changed.

10. A basketball rises, stops momentarily, then falls. Write a claim answering whether its velocity remained constant.

11. Two fans push identical carts. Cart A reaches 2.0 m/s in 2 s; Cart B reaches 2.0 m/s in 5 s. Write a claim answering which cart had the greater average acceleration.

12. A thermal camera shows one side of a metal plate warming while the other remains nearly unchanged. Write a claim that directly answers which side warmed more.

I can select and use relevant observations or data as evidence to support my claim.

13. A rolling ball’s trial sheet includes mass, color, start time, and distances traveled during equal intervals. Which data would you use to support a claim about changing speed?

14. A student claims a ramp angle affects cart acceleration. Which is stronger evidence: one final speed from one angle, or acceleration measurements from several controlled angles? Explain.

15. A cart’s speeds are 0.6, 0.9, 1.2 m/s. Write one concise evidence sentence for the claim that it sped up.

16. For the claim “the object stayed at rest,” choose stronger evidence: “it looked still” or “its measured position stayed at 1.25 m for six consecutive readings.” Explain.

I can explain how my evidence supports my claim using scientific reasoning.

17. A cart covers equal distances in equal time intervals. Explain why that pattern supports a claim of constant speed.

18. A dropped ball covers larger distances during equal video-frame intervals. Explain why the pattern supports a claim that it accelerates downward.

19. A fan pushes two carts with the same force, but the lighter cart accelerates more. Use $F=ma$ qualitatively to explain why that can happen.

20. A car travels the same 20 m route in 4 s on one trial and 8 s on another. Use the meaning of $v=d/t$ to explain which trial is faster.
