First 4 Days — Practice

Physics · First 4 Days · I can successfully participate in physics class by following course procedures and using evidence-based reasoning to explain physical phenomena.

I can follow the procedures and routines used in physics class.

1. Your group opens a shared video file, but two people begin renaming separate copies. What should the group do before collecting any measurements, and how does the routine protect the record of the trial?

2. A motion sensor is still connected when the bell rings. Describe the procedure-focused response that protects the equipment and the next class instead of simply walking away.

3. You miss a day and return to find several files with similar names. What should you use to identify the correct resource before starting work?

4. A lab partner writes measurements on scrap paper but never enters them in the group record. Explain why the data-recording routine matters scientifically, not just organizationally.

I can identify the parts of a scientific explanation: Claim, Evidence, and Reasoning (CER).

5. A toy car moves down a ramp. A student writes: “The car sped up. It traveled 0.20 m, 0.35 m, and 0.55 m during equal time intervals. Greater distance in the same amount of time means greater speed.” Label each sentence as Claim, Evidence, or Reasoning.

6. A dropped foam ball is farther from its starting point in each successive video frame. Which CER part should directly answer the question “Did the ball speed up?” What jobs do the other two parts perform?

7. A student says, “The metal washer became warmer because the thermometer rose from 21°C to 38°C.” Identify the claim/evidence pieces and describe the reasoning idea that is still needed.

8. During a cart investigation, sort these jobs into Claim, Evidence, or Reasoning: answers whether the cart accelerated; cites the changing velocity measurements; explains that acceleration is a change in velocity over time.

I can make a clear claim that answers a question about a physical phenomenon.

9. A battery-powered bug travels 0.15 m, 0.24 m, and 0.36 m during equal half-second intervals. Write only a clear claim answering: “How did the bug’s speed change?”

10. Three insulated cups cool for the same amount of time. Cup A ends at 42°C, Cup B at 48°C, and Cup C at 45°C. Write only the claim that answers, “Which cup kept the water warmest?”

11. A skateboarder turns around while keeping a speed of 3 m/s. Write a claim answering, “Did the skateboarder’s velocity change?”

12. A cart’s position relative to the lab table remains unchanged for 30 s. Write a precise claim answering, “Was the cart at rest relative to the table?”

I can select and use relevant observations or data as evidence to support my claim.

13. A hallway cart moves 0.30 m during the first second, 0.60 m during the next second, and 0.90 m during the third. For the claim “the cart sped up,” select the two most useful measurements and write them as evidence.

14. A paper airplane trial records launch color, flight time, hallway temperature, and horizontal distance. The claim is “Plane B stayed in the air longer than Plane A.” Which measurements are relevant evidence?

15. A student uses the cart color, the operator’s name, and frame-to-frame position changes as evidence that a cart sped up. Which information belongs in the evidence and which does not?

16. A ball moves 0.08 m between two early video frames and 0.21 m between two later frames; the frame intervals are equal. Write one evidence sentence supporting “the ball moved faster later.”

I can explain how my evidence supports my claim using scientific reasoning.

17. A cart travels farther during each equal time interval. Complete the reasoning for the claim “the cart sped up” without simply repeating the data.

18. Cup B finishes at 48°C while Cups A and C finish at 42°C and 45°C after the same cooling time. Explain why those measurements support the claim that Cup B kept the water warmest.

19. A student writes, “The ball sped up because the distances got bigger.” Strengthen the reasoning by naming the physical relationship that makes the distance changes meaningful.

20. A scooter changes from 3 m/s east to 3 m/s west. Explain why this supports the claim that its velocity changed even though the speed stayed 3 m/s.
