Physics · First 4 Days · Teacher

First 4 Days — Practice + Extra Practice Key

Learning Target: I can successfully participate in physics class by following course procedures and using evidence-based reasoning to explain physical phenomena.

1. OPEN-IC1

Your group opens a shared video file, but two people begin renaming separate copies. What should the group do before collecting any measurements, and how does the routine protect the record of the trial?

Answer / target: Confirm the class naming/location routine and work from the correct agreed copy; consistency prevents duplicate/mismatched evidence.

2. OPEN-IC1

A motion sensor is still connected when the bell rings. Describe the procedure-focused response that protects the equipment and the next class instead of simply walking away.

Answer / target: Follow the demonstrated shutdown/disconnect/return routine; ask if uncertain rather than improvise.

3. OPEN-IC1

You miss a day and return to find several files with similar names. What should you use to identify the correct resource before starting work?

Answer / target: Use the agenda/course location/file naming convention and the exact class routine; ask when uncertain.

4. OPEN-IC1

A lab partner writes measurements on scrap paper but never enters them in the group record. Explain why the data-recording routine matters scientifically, not just organizationally.

Answer / target: A shared durable record makes the evidence traceable, comparable, and less likely to be lost or altered.

5. OPEN-IC2

A toy car moves down a ramp. A student writes: “The car sped up. It traveled 0.20 m, 0.35 m, and 0.55 m during equal time intervals. Greater distance in the same amount of time means greater speed.” Label each sentence as Claim, Evidence, or Reasoning.

Answer / target: Claim; Evidence; Reasoning.

6. OPEN-IC2

A dropped foam ball is farther from its starting point in each successive video frame. Which CER part should directly answer the question “Did the ball speed up?” What jobs do the other two parts perform?

Answer / target: Claim answers; evidence reports relevant observations/data; reasoning explains why the evidence supports the claim.

7. OPEN-IC2

A student says, “The metal washer became warmer because the thermometer rose from 21°C to 38°C.” Identify the claim/evidence pieces and describe the reasoning idea that is still needed.

Answer / target: Claim: washer became warmer. Evidence: temperature rose 21 to 38 C. Reasoning: temperature is a measure related to thermal state; rising measured temperature indicates warming.

8. OPEN-IC2

During a cart investigation, sort these jobs into Claim, Evidence, or Reasoning: answers whether the cart accelerated; cites the changing velocity measurements; explains that acceleration is a change in velocity over time.

Answer / target: Claim; Evidence; Reasoning.

9. OPEN-IC3

A battery-powered bug travels 0.15 m, 0.24 m, and 0.36 m during equal half-second intervals. Write only a clear claim answering: “How did the bug’s speed change?”

Answer / target: The bug sped up.

10. OPEN-IC3

Three insulated cups cool for the same amount of time. Cup A ends at 42°C, Cup B at 48°C, and Cup C at 45°C. Write only the claim that answers, “Which cup kept the water warmest?”

Answer / target: Cup B kept the water warmest.

11. OPEN-IC3

A skateboarder turns around while keeping a speed of 3 m/s. Write a claim answering, “Did the skateboarder’s velocity change?”

Answer / target: Yes, the velocity changed because the direction changed.

12. OPEN-IC3

A cart’s position relative to the lab table remains unchanged for 30 s. Write a precise claim answering, “Was the cart at rest relative to the table?”

Answer / target: Yes, the cart was at rest relative to the table during that interval.

13. OPEN-IC4

A hallway cart moves 0.30 m during the first second, 0.60 m during the next second, and 0.90 m during the third. For the claim “the cart sped up,” select the two most useful measurements and write them as evidence.

Answer / target: Any comparison showing greater distance in equal later time, e.g., 0.30 m first second vs 0.90 m third second.

14. OPEN-IC4

A paper airplane trial records launch color, flight time, hallway temperature, and horizontal distance. The claim is “Plane B stayed in the air longer than Plane A.” Which measurements are relevant evidence?

Answer / target: The flight-time measurements for A and B.

15. OPEN-IC4

A student uses the cart color, the operator’s name, and frame-to-frame position changes as evidence that a cart sped up. Which information belongs in the evidence and which does not?

Answer / target: Position changes belong; color/operator name do not unless the investigation specifically makes them relevant.

16. OPEN-IC4

A ball moves 0.08 m between two early video frames and 0.21 m between two later frames; the frame intervals are equal. Write one evidence sentence supporting “the ball moved faster later.”

Answer / target: The ball covered 0.21 m in an equal frame interval later compared with 0.08 m earlier.

17. OPEN-IC5

A cart travels farther during each equal time interval. Complete the reasoning for the claim “the cart sped up” without simply repeating the data.

Answer / target: Because speed describes distance traveled per unit time, greater distance during equal time means greater speed.

18. OPEN-IC5

Cup B finishes at 48°C while Cups A and C finish at 42°C and 45°C after the same cooling time. Explain why those measurements support the claim that Cup B kept the water warmest.

Answer / target: Higher final temperature after equal time means it retained more thermal energy/experienced less cooling under the comparison.

19. OPEN-IC5

A student writes, “The ball sped up because the distances got bigger.” Strengthen the reasoning by naming the physical relationship that makes the distance changes meaningful.

Answer / target: For equal time intervals, greater distance traveled means greater average speed.

20. OPEN-IC5

A scooter changes from 3 m/s east to 3 m/s west. Explain why this supports the claim that its velocity changed even though the speed stayed 3 m/s.

Answer / target: Velocity includes direction; reversing direction changes velocity even at the same speed.

21. OPEN-IC1

A team records a great trial but saves the graph under the previous group’s filename. What should they do immediately, and what scientific problem could the naming error create?

Answer / target: Correct the filename/location using the class routine; the error can attach evidence to the wrong trial/group.

22. OPEN-IC1

You are unsure whether a force sensor should be zeroed before or after attaching it. What is the strongest procedure-based move?

Answer / target: Use the demonstrated procedure/instructions or ask; do not guess because inconsistent zeroing changes measurements.

23. OPEN-IC1

Two group members both type into separate data tables. Name one routine that would prevent conflicting records.

Answer / target: Designate/agree on one official record or synchronize using the class procedure.

24. OPEN-IC1

A classmate finishes a CER but cannot find it the next day. Explain how a consistent save/export routine protects learning evidence.

Answer / target: Makes work retrievable and preserves the link between data and explanation.

25. OPEN-IC2

A fan cart accelerates. A student writes: “The cart accelerated. Its velocity changed from 0.4 m/s to 1.1 m/s. Acceleration describes a change in velocity over time.” Identify C, E, and R.

Answer / target: Claim; Evidence; Reasoning.

26. OPEN-IC2

A flashlight spot moves across a wall. Which CER part should include the measured positions? Which part should explain what those positions mean about motion?

Answer / target: Evidence; Reasoning.

27. OPEN-IC2

A student writes only measurements and never answers the investigation question. Which CER part is missing?

Answer / target: Claim.

28. OPEN-IC2

A student states a claim and repeats the same measurement as the explanation. Which CER part is weak or missing?

Answer / target: Reasoning.

29. OPEN-IC3

A model car covers the same 0.50 m during every 0.25 s interval. Write a claim answering whether its speed changed.

Answer / target: Its speed remained approximately constant.

30. OPEN-IC3

A basketball rises, stops momentarily, then falls. Write a claim answering whether its velocity remained constant.

Answer / target: No, its velocity changed.

31. OPEN-IC3

Two fans push identical carts. Cart A reaches 2.0 m/s in 2 s; Cart B reaches 2.0 m/s in 5 s. Write a claim answering which cart had the greater average acceleration.

Answer / target: Cart A had the greater average acceleration.

32. OPEN-IC3

A thermal camera shows one side of a metal plate warming while the other remains nearly unchanged. Write a claim that directly answers which side warmed more.

Answer / target: The first side warmed more.

33. OPEN-IC4

A rolling ball’s trial sheet includes mass, color, start time, and distances traveled during equal intervals. Which data would you use to support a claim about changing speed?

Answer / target: Distances during equal intervals (with their timing).

34. OPEN-IC4

A student claims a ramp angle affects cart acceleration. Which is stronger evidence: one final speed from one angle, or acceleration measurements from several controlled angles? Explain.

Answer / target: Several controlled angle/acceleration measurements because they test the relationship and allow comparison.

35. OPEN-IC4

A cart’s speeds are 0.6, 0.9, 1.2 m/s. Write one concise evidence sentence for the claim that it sped up.

Answer / target: Measured speed increased from 0.6 to 1.2 m/s over the observations.

36. OPEN-IC4

For the claim “the object stayed at rest,” choose stronger evidence: “it looked still” or “its measured position stayed at 1.25 m for six consecutive readings.” Explain.

Answer / target: Measured constant position is stronger because it is specific and directly bears on rest relative to the reference.

37. OPEN-IC5

A cart covers equal distances in equal time intervals. Explain why that pattern supports a claim of constant speed.

Answer / target: Speed is distance per time; equal distance in equal time gives the same speed.

38. OPEN-IC5

A dropped ball covers larger distances during equal video-frame intervals. Explain why the pattern supports a claim that it accelerates downward.

Answer / target: Greater distance per equal time means increasing speed; changing velocity over time is acceleration.

39. OPEN-IC5

A fan pushes two carts with the same force, but the lighter cart accelerates more. Use $F=ma$ qualitatively to explain why that can happen.

Answer / target: For the same force, a smaller mass corresponds to a larger acceleration because a=F/m.

40. OPEN-IC5

A car travels the same 20 m route in 4 s on one trial and 8 s on another. Use the meaning of $v=d/t$ to explain which trial is faster.

Answer / target: Same distance in less time means greater speed; 5 m/s vs 2.5 m/s.
